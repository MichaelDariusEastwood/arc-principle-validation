    What limits any system that improves itself, whatever it is
                           made of?
The founding paper of Recursive Dynamics: the field as a whole, with the five laws of the ARC
                                 Theory as laws under test

                                            Michael Darius Eastwood
                              Independent Researcher, London, United Kingdom
                      ORCID 0009-0004-3222-7442 michael@michaeldariuseastwood.com



                                                        Abstract
            Machines that improve themselves using their own outputs now exist and work, and there is no
       accepted science of what limits them. This paper founds that science, Recursive Dynamics, and stakes
       its claim the way thermodynamics was staked in 1824: by computing a bound that any such machine
       must obey if the claim is right, publishing the instruments that measure its terms, and preregistering
       by timestamp the experiments whose nulls favour the rival view. The field takes the self-improvement
       loop as its native object and names five state variables, conjectured to be substrate-independent:
       capability, recursive depth, correction rate, drift and correction leverage. It carries five laws as named
       conjectures under test, with statuses declared. One, the ARC Ceiling, is derived in full from two
       lines of assumption; two, added on 24 September 2026 and registered as laws on 15 October 2026 in
       version 1.103 of the theory registration, are not yet tested, and their mathematics, set out at version
       1.1, is in development. The field locates its identity, as thermodynamics does, in its impossibilities:
       if the laws hold, self-improvement cannot stably outrun its own correction, the growth exponent is
       capped by the reciprocal of the correction shortfall, and no ladder of same-class oversight climbs
       past its ceiling by adding rungs. The paper states what would make the field wrong, tries to house
       its questions inside twelve neighbouring fields, gives each of fifty-one objections a disposition, and
       prints the four results that would dissolve the field. Its honest status is the Carnot stage: one memoir,
       instruments built, decisive measurements preregistered by timestamp and not run, staked where failure
       will be as visible as success.

Version 3.5 sets the paper in this form, states Laws IV and V as registered on 15 October 2026 and makes the
corrections recorded for it, among them Law II’s one-line statement, the premise that fixes the ceiling’s value and the
bounds that prior work states, now conceded exactly; Appendix A lists every version and correction, with its date.

1    Introduction
In 1824 the steam engine was the most consequential machine on earth, and nobody could say what
limited it. Engineers improved engines the way engineers do, by building the next one; the question of
what any engine could do, independent of its construction, was not merely unanswered but unasked,
because it belonged to no existing subject. Sadi Carnot asked it in a memoir (Carnot 1824) that founded a
science, and the pattern has repeated ever since: cybernetics was proposed as a field in 1948, in the book
that named it (Wiener 1948), before most of its results existed; exobiology was named in 1960 (Lederberg
1960) before a single specimen. When working machines precede their theory, the theory that forms is not
a branch of an old field. It is a new one, because the old fields’ native objects were never the machine’s
behaviour as such. The present moment has exactly this shape. Systems now improve their own outputs
using their own outputs: models that critique and revise their reasoning, pipelines that generate their own
training signal, laboratories automating parts of their own research loop (Chen et al. 2026). They work.
Whether they stay under control as they grow is the control problem of AI safety (Bostrom 2014), and
for systems that improve themselves it is a question of rates: whether their correction keeps pace with
their growth. Ask what bounds them and the literature answers with scenarios, forecasts and intuitions,
and with formal models, bounds and fitted scaling laws, each stated in its own variables (Section 2); it
has not yet answered with one set of state variables in which a limit can be stated once and tested across
substrates. That is not a criticism of the literature; it is the diagnostic signature of a missing field.
    This paper founds that field. Its one derived result is a ceiling: inside a stated minimal model,
correction out-scales the burden that growth creates only while the growth exponent stays below a value
set by how correction capacity scales with capability, and for a corrector of the system’s own class that
value is at most two, on a premise the programme names as the conjecture’s most likely point of failure
(Section 6). It is written to be attacked. Section 7 makes the positive case as a scored list of conditions;
Section 12 collects every objection its author and his adversarial readers could find and prints what
happened to each; Section 14 states what would dissolve the field; Section 15.1 states what would have to
happen before anyone should call it a science.
    Our contributions are as follows.
    1. The field’s object and its measurement principle: the self-improvement loop as the native object,
       which none of nine neighbouring fields takes as primitive (Section 3), and capability measured on
       a ratio scale with a true zero and no ceiling, with five state variables written as the programme’s
       notation register defines them (Section 4).
    2. The five laws of the ARC Theory stated as named conjectures under test, each with its status
       (Section 5), and one bound worked in full: inside a stated minimal model the burden-to-capacity
       ratio scales as 𝑅 𝛼(1−𝛾) −1 , so the crossover is 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾), and at the same-class premise
       𝛾 = 1/2 it is 2 (Section 6).
    3. The claim stated as what it forbids, four outcomes under the preregistered analyses (Section 1.1),
       and the four results that would dissolve the field, with the commitment to publish each under this
       title (Section 14).
    4. An attempt to house the field’s questions in twelve candidate host fields in their own variables,
       conceding what each already holds, with a dissolution map by host and the one coupling no host
       can state (Section 3.1).
    5. The case for the field scored against six licence conditions, with the precedent class of fields founded
       on a limit theorem and its disanalogy (Section 7); the rival’s best case, with the programme’s own
       contrary pilot printed (Section 8); the grade of substrate independence (Section 9); the instruments
       (Section 10); and the decisive experiments for Laws I to III, preregistered by timestamp and not run,
       with what would confirm a new area rather than a new chapter (Section 11).

1.1    What is claimed, stated as what is forbidden
A claim that forbids nothing is not a claim, and a paper that concedes as much as this one does owes the
reader its prohibitions first. Under the preregistered analyses, the field forbids four outcomes:
    1. A stable system holding its growth exponent above the ceiling, 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾), for a sustained run.
    2. A same-class corrector whose correction leverage 𝛾 measures wholly above one half.
    3. A corrector-class contrast that runs the other way, or that falls wholly below the preregistered
       support threshold wherever the cap should bind: the decisive trial’s own anti-directional and
       equivalence verdicts.
    4. Monotone returns in the growth exponent, with no interior maximum, as reinvestment rises.
    The first, the third and the fourth would each make the field wrong. The second is a prohibition
of a different weight: it kills the one-half cap and the value two that follows from it, while the ceiling
relation stands, because the relation returns whatever leverage exponent is measured. A true null on the
corrector-class trial’s six-family roster returns an inconclusive verdict at the preregistered precision rather
than an equivalence verdict, which is reported as a failed attempt to confirm and never as a refutation.
Everything conceded in Section 12 is conceded in the light of these four; none of the concessions softens
them.


2     Related work: debts conceded by name
This field is assembled from debts, each conceded in full: the cybernetic intuition that a regulator must
match the variety of what it regulates, so that control capacity must scale with the controlled system
(Ashby 1956; Conant and Ashby 1970), to which this field adds the exponent form and the class lever; the
impossibility line of AI-control research (Yampolskiy 2020), to which it adds the design requirement
the impossibility implies; the transport-network limits of biological scaling (West et al. 1997), conceded
as premise, to which it adds the internal replacement limit that operates when fixing the pipe no longer
fixes the growth; the intelligence-explosion question as posed by its named owners from 1965 onward
(Good 1966; Vinge 1993; Chalmers 2010; Hutter 2012; Bostrom 2014), to which it adds the missing state
variables; the self-modifying-program line of the 2000s, whose Gödel machines rewrite any part of their
own code once they have found a proof that the rewrite is useful (Schmidhuber 2003), the theoretical
ancestry of a program that rewrites itself, to which it adds measured rates, an exponent and a class cap; the
functional-equation mathematics, the machinery, never claimed; and the scalable-oversight programme
(Irving et al. 2018; Christiano et al. 2018; Leike et al. 2018; Burns et al. 2023), whose ladders are the
object the third law is about. What is claimed as original is the assembly: the loop as native object, scale

     Formal treatments of recursive self-improvement predate this field, and this paper claims no priority for
a formal treatment as such. Wang (2018) formally defines one class of recursively self-improving systems,
proves that, in a restricted setting, such systems can be computable, and reports simulations suggesting that
they can be efficient; Reddy (2020) sets out Markov-chain, history-embedding and program-generating
models of recursive self-improvement. Chen et al. (2026) survey recursive self-improvement across 1,250
papers and list, among their open problems, stability engineering as a discipline and measurement fit for
governance; that is context for the need this field answers, not evidence for any of its laws. On recursive
error, models collapse when trained on recursively generated data (Shumailov et al. 2024), and in an
analytically tractable linear setting the test error has a finite bound, independent of the number of iterations,
when real and generated data accumulate rather than replace each other (Gerstgrasser et al. 2024). On
correctors, Engels et al. (2025) fit scaling laws for the success of oversight under a capability gap between
overseer and overseen and analyse nested scalable oversight, a quantitative baseline that any claim about
how correction scales must beat; Rudoler et al. (2026) prove that, in a supervised setting, monitoring
with vanishing error and query rates is possible exactly when the class of possible monitor functions has
finite Littlestone dimension, and report results suggesting that monitors co-trained with adapting workers
keep pace better than fixed monitors, in code-security settings; and Wang et al. (2026) report that, in the
setting they study, self-evolution without execution-based verification fails to improve. Jafari et al. (2025)
develop an analytic framework for recursive self-improvement in which limits from power, bandwidth
and memory define a service envelope that caps instantaneous improvement, and an endogenous growth
model coupling capital to compute, data and energy defines a critical boundary between superlinear and
subcritical regimes; under the paper’s reading rule, its comparison with this field waits until it has been
read in full.
     The neighbouring fields that could hold the field’s questions are tried in their own variables in
Section 3.1, with what each already holds conceded; the precedent class of fields founded on a limit
theorem is set out in Section 7.1; and the objections that the field already exists under another name are
answered in Section 12, group A.


3     The object, and why it belongs to no one
Definition 1 (Recursive system). Define a recursive system as any system whose outputs re-enter its own
improvement process.

The field’s native object is that loop. Its neighbours each own a tool and none owns the object, and each is
named here with what it does own, because the discipline is assembled from debts to them, conceded in
Section 2.
    A field is licensed when the questions that matter are about an object none of the existing fields takes
as primitive. The questions that matter here, how fast can self-improvement go, what bounds it, and what
kind of overseer can keep up, are questions about the loop.

1. Adaptive control and self-tuning regulation. Stated there: a plant with a controller whose gains
adapt online (Åström and Wittenmark 1973); stability by a Lyapunov argument; the adaptation gain
against the disturbance rate. What fits: Law II is, in form, an adaptive-control stability condition, one
rate against another, and the loop that rewrites its own controller is half present, because a self-tuning
regulator does rewrite its gains. What breaks: adaptive control keeps the controller’s structure fixed and
adapts parameters; it has no variable for the controller’s own capability growing with depth, and no notion
that a controller built from the plant’s own class is capped in what it can correct; its conservation law
(feedback cannot reduce sensitivity everywhere) is a law of fixed loops. Verdict: could host Law II as a
chapter, “stability of self-modifying regulators”, if the class variable proves empty. Moves there if: the
corrector-class ratio holds at 1.00.

2. Queueing theory and the stability of service systems. Stated there: correctable burden arrives at a
rate proportional to capability gained; correction is a server whose capacity scales with capability; the
system is stable when the load ratio stays below one (Lindley 1952). What fits: this is the ARC Ceiling’s
derivation, and the paper concedes it. Section 6 is a queueing stability condition with an unusual coupling:
the arrival rate grows with the work already served. What breaks: queueing theory has no notion that the
server is built from the same class as the arrivals and therefore capped in how its capacity scales; it carries
no depth exponent and assumes stationarity. Verdict: the strongest host for the form of Law III; if the
class cap is empty, the ARC Ceiling belongs here as a growth-coupled queue and the field’s name attaches
to nothing in it. Moves there if: the eclipse measurement retires the class distinction.

3. Endogenous growth theory in economics. Stated there: a knowledge production function in which
the rate of new knowledge depends on the existing stock raised to an exponent; the share of output
reinvested in research is the policy dial; the field’s long argument over whether that exponent is below
one (growth needs ever more inputs) or at one and above (growth can be explosive) (Romer 1990; Jones
1995) is exactly the question of whether recursion is sustainable. What fits: Law I, taken alone, is a
knowledge-production exponent under another name, and the reinvestment dial the preregistered studies
turn is the savings rate of a growth model; the paper concedes both. What breaks: growth theory has no
drift ledger, because ideas in its models do not corrupt the stock that produced them, and no corrector; it
therefore cannot state Laws II or III, and it does not ask what the improver is made of. Verdict: could host
Law I entirely. The field’s claim to Law I is only through its coupling to Laws II and III; that coupling
is the whole of what is claimed. Moves there if: drift and correction prove inseparable from capability
under measurement (objection G3), leaving a growth exponent and nothing else.

4. Software reliability engineering and defect dynamics. Stated there: faults injected per change
against faults repaired per unit effort; reliability growth when repair outpaces injection (Musa 1975); the
accumulation of unrepaired debt. What fits: drift and the correction rate are, operationally, injection and
repair rates, and the field’s estimators for them owe this discipline their shape; conceded. What breaks: a
reliability model does not let the system’s repair capacity grow as a function of its own capability, has no
class variable and no depth exponent. Verdict: could host the measurement methodology for the two
rates, not the laws. Moves there if: the rates turn out to be measurable only as engineering metrics with
no lawful relation between them.

5. Machine learning: self-play, self-training, learned optimisers and model collapse. Stated there:
outputs re-entering training is the self-play loop; degradation of models trained on their own outputs
is drift measured in special cases (Shumailov et al. 2024); learned optimisers are learners that rewrite
the learner. What fits: every preregistered experiment could be published here as a machine-learning
result, and this is the most likely institutional home if the field fails to earn its name; conceded without
reservation. What breaks: the literature carries no ratio-scaled capability law across depth with a stated
exponent, no bound on what a learner-rewriting learner can do, and no class cap. Its model-collapse line
does pair drift with a correction, real data kept or accumulated beside generated data, and shows the
bounded-versus-divergent dichotomy for training dynamics (Section 2); it does not vary what the corrector
is made of. Verdict: hosts the experiments; cannot state the laws. Moves there if: the substrate crossing
fails, leaving the laws true of language-model loops only, which is machine learning (objection H5).

6. Scaling-law empirics. Stated there: capability against parameters, data and compute; exponents
fitted (Kaplan et al. 2020), architecture-blind by construction. What fits: the exponent-fitting craft, the
model families, the discipline of reporting intervals; conceded. What breaks: the resource here is the
system’s own re-entering output; resource scaling predicts monotone returns where the field predicts an
interior maximum. Verdict: hosts the method, not the object. Moves there if: recursive depth reduces,
under measurement, to compute spent, or the titration shows monotone returns.

7. Scalable oversight and alignment research. Stated there: a weaker or same-class corrector
supervising a stronger system (Burns et al. 2023); ladders of correctors (Engels et al. 2025); the premise
that they scale. What fits: the corrector-class question is institutionally theirs, and the eclipse result,
whichever way it falls, is an oversight result; conceded. What breaks: the literature bounds what a
corrector can do in its own variables, the capability gap between overseer and overseen (Engels et al.
2025) and the complexity of the class of monitor functions (Rudoler et al. 2026), but none of its bounds

depends on whether the corrector shares the corrected system’s architecture, training distribution and
blind spots, so it cannot state the third law or the cap that its own ladders assume away. Verdict: the
strongest institutional host for the class-cap result. If the field dissolves, the eclipse measurement is

8. Evolutionary computation and error-threshold theory. Stated there: strategy parameters that
evolve alongside the solution (the improver improving itself, which self-adaptive evolution strategies
have done for decades, Beyer and Schwefel 2002); and, in the theory of replicating populations, the error
threshold, above which mutation outruns selection and information is lost (Eigen 1971). What fits: the
error threshold is the closest existing law to Law II, a rate of corruption against a rate of correction with a
boundary between persistence and collapse, and the paper concedes it as an antecedent of the second law’s
shape; self-adaptation is a working instance of the loop rewriting its improver. What breaks: selection is
external to the replicator, the corrector has no class, and capability is not a ratio ladder. Verdict: could
host Law II as a generalised error threshold. Moves there if: the correction-versus-drift race finds the
boundary but the class lever does nothing.

9. Statistical physics: scaling, universality and renormalisation. Stated there: the same exponent
families across unrelated substrates as a universality class (Wilson 1971); scaling collapse as the test.
What fits: the field’s substrate-independence hope is this tradition’s hope, and its fourth-cell test is a
universality test; conceded. What breaks: no correction variable, no drift variable, no corrector whose
composition is the lever. Verdict: hosts the cross-domain families if they survive and belong to no loop.
Moves there if: the families hold and the loop-specific laws do not.

10. Computability, algorithmic information and the self-referential improver line. Stated there:
programs that rewrite any part of their own code once they have found a proof that the rewrite is useful,
such a rewrite being globally optimal in the sense the proof fixes (Schmidhuber 2003); the limits of
self-reference (Turing 1936). What fits: this is the theoretical ancestry of “a program that rewrites itself”,
conceded. What breaks: those results concern provability and optimality, not measured rates; they carry
no exponent, no drift and no class cap. Verdict: ancestry, not a host. Moves there if: nothing empirical
survives and only the self-reference question remains.

11. Second-order cybernetics, autopoiesis and general systems theory. Stated there: systems that
produce and maintain themselves (Maturana and Varela 1980); the observer inside the loop. What fits:

4       Scale discipline, and the five state variables
Before any law, a measurement principle, learned the hard way and adopted as founding method: capability
must be measured on a ratio scale with a true zero and no ceiling. Thermodynamics could not state
its laws in their quantitative form until temperature was absolute; a bounded percentage score does to
Recursive Dynamics what a Celsius-only thermometer does to the third law of thermodynamics: it makes
the laws unstatable, because a score capped at 100 per cent cannot follow a power law in anything. The
field’s instrument is therefore a calibrated difficulty ladder: a latent, ratio-scaled capability measure with a
true zero and no ceiling, on which doubling means the same thing at every height; and every quantitative
surface names which of three model families it is reading (unbounded or latent capability, error decay, or
bounded performance), because the exponent means something different in each. The programme adopted
this after catching its own worked example running a bounded score through unbounded arithmetic; the
correction is recorded, and the lesson is promoted here from a bug fix to a founding principle: scale
discipline precedes law.

7.1   The precedent class: fields founded on a limit theorem in their own variables
The comparison that supports the case for the field most is not thermodynamics alone but the class of
fields whose identity is a bound stated in variables the older fields did not carry, holding across substrates.
Thermodynamics: Carnot (1824), no engine beats the reversible one; the bound came first and the formal
laws followed decades later. Information theory: Shannon (1948), channel capacity as a limit theorem,
with entropy and mutual information as state variables no prior field carried, and substrate independence
(wires, radio, later genomes) as the proof of generality. Computability: Turing (1936), the halting
impossibility, a field defined by what cannot be computed. Control theory’s own limit: Bode (1945),
feedback cannot reduce sensitivity everywhere, a conservation law of the loop, so the nearest neighbour
to this field earned its identity from a bound on what feedback can do. Statistics: the Cramér-Rao bound,
1945 to 1946 (Rao 1945; Cramér 1946), a floor on estimator variance.
     The pattern is one thing: a field arrives when a limit, stated in variables the old fields did not carry,
holds across substrates. Recursive Dynamics puts a limit of exactly that shape to the test: 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾),
with the same-class cap on 𝛾 at one half. The disanalogy is printed in the same breath: every field
above earned its name by measurement and by many hands; this one was founded by one person before
its decisive measurements, with no replication by other hands yet, and the parallel is a scaffold, not a
certificate.


8     The rival’s best case, steelmanned
The strongest objection is not that the laws are unproven. It is that the mechanism may be empty:
correction may just be correction, in which case what a corrector is made of does not matter and the class
distinction dissolves. This rival is not a straw man. The scalable-oversight programme in all its forms,
weak-to-strong generalisation (Burns et al. 2023), debate (Irving et al. 2018), amplification (Christiano
et al. 2018), recursive reward modelling (Leike et al. 2018), builds same-class ladders on the implicit
premise that they scale, and the strongest published framework adjacent to this question, the scaling
laws for scalable oversight of Engels et al. (2025), is architecture-blind: it gives overseer and overseen
oversight-specific Elo scores that depend on general capability alone, so it implies the class-blind null
without needing to state it.
     Worse for this programme, its own pilot leans the rival’s way. The parallel-channel measurement in
the programme’s published corpus found correction channels can be correlated, adding almost nothing
in four of the five models measured, and one model contradicted the pattern outright. That model’s
row is printed whole here, as the theory registration requires: on Gemini 3 Flash, Paper II reports an
endpoint sequential exponent of 0.59 with a bootstrap 95 per cent interval of minus 1.3 to 2.9, a regression
sequential exponent of 0.49 with a standard error of 0.20 and a coefficient of determination of 0.86, and a
compute-matched parallel exponent of 0.31. It is one model’s fit and inconclusive. Correlated channels
are exactly the mechanism that would weaken same-class correction in a way that also weakens cross-class
correction, dragging the decisive ratio toward one. The eclipse design is therefore preregistered with the
programme’s own contrary evidence on its face, and it commits to publishing a null result under the same
title as a discovery about the world rather than an embarrassment to be buried. A field that writes its
rival’s best evidence into its own preregistered design is doing the one thing that cannot be faked.

10     Instruments
A field is its instruments before it is its results. Recursive Dynamics ships with an instrument canon
built before any confirmatory data existed: the calibrated capability ladder (Section 4); a blinded scoring
stack in which every output is laundered so no scorer can tell what produced it, with label-randomised
nulls computed through the identical pipeline, so the decisive experiment’s null is imposed structurally
rather than fitted; estimator code that is hash-pinned, fixture-gated (every preregistered verdict branch has
a planted-truth test the code must pass before anything ships), and governed by a preregistered defect
clause under which the specification, not the code, is the analysis, so a post-registration coding error is a
disclosed repair on preregistered terms rather than a dead study. Scoring is by blinded multi-model panels,
never by human raters; where an external human criterion is methodologically unavoidable, a single
author-rated fallback is preregistered, blind, washout-retested (he re-scores a registered thirty per cent of
items after a washout of at least fourteen days, blind to his first-pass scores, as the theory registration
records), and disclosed as author-rated in every headline.

scorer can share the blind spots of the scored. The laundering and the forced null remove the scorer’s
knowledge of provenance, not its class. The programme’s answer is cross-family panels and a preregistered
human-criterion validation instrument, and the honest statement is that the circularity is reduced, disclosed
and measured, not abolished. It returns as objection C4.

11.1    What would confirm a new area rather than a new chapter
A result that any old field could state in its own variables confirms a chapter of that field. A field is
confirmed by a conjunction that none of its neighbours can express. Stated once, with what is preregistered
today separated from what is only set out for registration:
     Preregistered today: a regime in which the correction rate out-scales drift (the correction-versus-drift
race); a corrector-class ratio durably above 1.00 where the cap binds, with the hard kill that any same-class
pair whose 𝛾 interval sits wholly above one half ends the cap (the eclipse measurement, with the class
audit and the correlated-failure probe beside it); an interior maximum of the growth exponent at or below
two across the reinvestment levels, under the replication-gated rule (the titration); and the fourth-cell
substrate test.
     Set out here, and to be registered before any data are seen: that the ratio rises with load (dose-response,
which separates a mechanism from a static difference); that a cross-class corrector moves the titration’s
maximum upward, linking Laws II and III through the class lever; and that a single system crosses the
stability boundary as its reinvestment dial turns. None of these three is a prediction of the field until its
registration carries it, and this paper does not count them as such.
     The consistency check no single field can fake, with invented numbers for illustration only: suppose
the eclipse measurement returned a same-class 𝛾 of 0.45 and a cross-class 𝛾 of 0.70. The ARC Ceiling
would then predict a same-class maximum near 1.8 and a cross-class maximum near 3.3 in the titration.
If the titration returned its maxima in those places, two preregistered studies with different instruments
would agree through the one bound. That agreement, in the manner of Joule’s measurements agreeing
with Carnot’s bound, is the founding result: it is inexpressible in the variables of learning theory, control,
scaling empirics or oversight.
     The founding conjunction, then: a Law II regime; a corrector-class ratio above 1.00; an interior
maximum in the reinvestment titration; the same exponent structure in one non-artificial substrate; and at
least one of these replicated by strangers. The values travel with the legs rather than inside them. The
one-half cap on same-class correctors is reported with the ratio, and the maximum’s location is reported
with the maximum, so a leg survives a value that lands elsewhere and the record still says where it landed.
Each alone is a finding in an old field’s vocabulary. The conjunction is not, and that is the exact sense in
which results would confirm a new area rather than a new chapter.
     What a partial result means is stated here rather than argued afterwards, because the trial carrying
the second leg forecasts support at 0.82 only where the author’s own belief puts the effect and returns
less across the rest of the band it predicts, so the full conjunction is likelier missed than cleared. Two
legs, a Law II regime with either the class ordering or the interior maximum, establish that the loop has
a limit of its own and that the limit is measurable. Three add the ceiling as a bound with a measured
value in it. Four add substrate independence, which is what makes the variables the field’s rather than one
domain’s. Five is the field. The stranger’s replication is listed last because it is the field’s condition and
not the theory’s: the theory can be right and still wait for it.

A.    Redundancy: the field already exists under another name
A1. Cybernetics already studies self-correcting loops; Wiener’s field is this field. Rejected, with

16     Limitations
The paper prints its limitations where they arise; they are gathered here plainly, each with the place it is
argued.
     • The correction leverage 𝛾 has never been measured, by anyone, so the value two rests on a premise
       the programme names as the conjecture’s most likely point of failure (Section 6; objections B4 and
       G2).
     • The bound of Section 6 is a conditional crossover inside a minimal model: it does not establish
       bounded harm, finite-horizon safety or a law of nature, and its treatment of depth has been criticised
       in review (objection B5).
     • When models score models, the scorer can share the blind spots of the scored; the circularity is
       reduced, disclosed and measured, not abolished (Section 10; objection C4).
     • Whether five state variables suffice, and whether the quantities the studies measure are the objects
       the laws name, are open questions (objections C1 and C2); the capability ladder is calibrated but
       not yet validated by other hands (objection C5).
     • The minimal model assumes stationarity and separability of drift and correction, and reads capability
       on one scalar axis (objections G3 and G4).
     • Substrate independence stands at exploratory grade, and the fourth-cell test that would catch flexible
       fitting has not run (Section 9; objection C3).
     • The decisive experiments are preregistered by timestamp and not run, and the field has one author,
       no replication by other hands and no use by strangers (Sections 11 and 15.1; objections B1, D2 and
       D3).


17     Conclusion
Until the entry criteria are met, one sentence carries the field’s whole status: Recursive Dynamics is a new
field at the Carnot stage: one memoir in, instruments built, decisive measurements preregistered by
timestamp and not yet run, staked in public where failure will be as visible as success.
     The invitation that follows from everything above is the only one the field can honestly extend: run
the test that could kill it. Every instrument, seed, schedule and threshold is written down for that purpose.
The designs of the decisive trials are lodged privately on OSF today, and each becomes public with its
registration. An independent run outranks the programme’s own.

The theory registration this paper refers to, the paper’s place among the laws of the ARC Theory, and the
registers its claims are tied to.

D.1    The theory registration
Theory-level predictions registered on OSF: DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/P8CKQ, registered 8 September
2026 (version 1.100), updated 13 September 2026 (version 1.102) and 15 October 2026 (version 1.103),
twenty-two propositions. Companion: Register · The ARC Theory · Operational Definitions, version
1.9.1, 5 October 2026.

D.2    Placement
This is the founding paper of Recursive Dynamics, the field as a whole. It states the field’s object, its
measurement principle and its five state variables (Sections 3 and 4), and it states the five laws of the
ARC Theory, the field’s founding theory, as named conjectures under test (Section 5): Law I, the ARC
Principle, Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, and Law III, the ARC Ceiling, registered on 8 September
2026; Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, added on 24 September
2026 and registered as laws on 15 October 2026 in version 1.103, neither yet tested, their mathematics
in development. It derives Law III inside a stated model (Section 6). It decides no law and makes no
measurement of its own; its decisive experiments are preregistered by timestamp and not run (Section 11).
It does not treat the Eden Protocol or the HARI Treaty.

antecedents, which carry per-claim statuses; where a register and this text differ, the text is corrected at
the paper’s next version and the correction listed in Appendix A.
