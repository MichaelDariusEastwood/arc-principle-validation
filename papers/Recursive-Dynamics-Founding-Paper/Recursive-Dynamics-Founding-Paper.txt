                                                         Abstract
           What limits a system that improves itself using its own outputs, whatever it is made of? This
       paper founds Recursive Dynamics, the science of that question. Its object is the self-improvement
       loop, described by five state variables: capability, recursive depth, correction rate, drift and correction
       leverage. The field carries the five laws of the ARC Theory, each a law under test. Law I, the ARC
       Principle, 𝑈 = 𝐼 × 𝑅 𝛼 , is the foundation; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, reads 𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘; Law III,
       the ARC Ceiling, reads 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾). Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC
       Embedding Law, complete the five. The paper derives one bound. In a model of two assumptions,
       correction keeps pace with the burden that growth creates only while the growth exponent stays below
       1/(1 − 𝛾), where 𝛾 is how correction capacity scales with capability. For a corrector of the system’s
       own class, a premise the programme names as the conjecture’s most likely point of failure caps 𝛾
       at one half. The ceiling is then at most two on the relative rung, the ratio of burden to correction
       capacity, and two thirds once burden is counted over an exposure that grows with capability. The paper
       states four outcomes that would make the field wrong and four results that would dissolve it, tries to
       house its questions in twelve neighbouring fields, and answers fifty-five objections. No law is decided
       yet: the instruments through which Laws I to III take their word are design drafts, and three related
       studies are preregistered by timestamp and not yet run. One registered test has returned a verdict: the
       placement limb of Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, P21, is supported on the performance axis only,
       under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not
       evaluable and one disclosed recovery amendment.

revise their reasoning, pipelines that generate their own training signal, laboratories automating parts
of their own research loop (Chen et al. 2026). Some already work, in the settings their builders report.
Whether they stay under control as they grow is the control problem of AI safety (Bostrom 2014), and
for systems that improve themselves it is a question of rates: whether their correction keeps pace with
their growth. Ask what bounds them and the works this paper reads answer with scenarios, forecasts
and intuitions, and with formal models, bounds and fitted scaling laws, each stated in its own variables
(Section 2). None of them answers with one set of state variables in which a limit can be stated once and
tested across substrates. That is not a criticism of those works; it is what this paper reads as the signature
of a missing field.

correction out-scales the burden that growth creates only while the growth exponent stays below a value

value is at most two, on the relative rung, the ratio of burden to correction capacity, on a premise the
programme names as the conjecture’s most likely point of failure (Section 6). It is written to be attacked.

1.1   What is claimed, stated as what is forbidden
A claim that forbids nothing is not a claim, and a paper that concedes as much as this one does owes the
reader its prohibitions first. Under the preregistered analyses, the field forbids four outcomes:
   1. A stable system holding its growth exponent above the ceiling, 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾), for a sustained run.
   2. A same-class corrector whose correction leverage 𝛾 measures wholly above one half.
   3. A corrector-class contrast that runs the other way, or that falls wholly below the preregistered
      support threshold wherever the cap should bind: the corrector-class trial’s own anti-directional
      verdict, its equivalence verdict and its below-threshold outcome.
   4. Monotone returns in the growth exponent, with no interior maximum, as reinvestment rises.
     The first is P16 of the theory registration, the prohibition itself, whose instrument waits on its identity
and on decisions the author has not yet taken. The second is P6’s refuter, whose estimator waits until its
corrector taxonomy is frozen; the third and the fourth have studies preregistered by timestamp (Section 11).
All four are this paper’s commitments; the registration’s rules for the laws’ words are narrower, as
Section 11 sets out.
     The first, the third and the fourth would each make the field wrong. The second is a prohibition of
a different weight: it kills the one-half cap and the value two, on the relative rung, that follows from it
(the programme claims the cap and conjectures that 𝛾 sits exactly at it), while the ceiling relation stands,
because the relation returns whatever leverage exponent is measured. A true null on the corrector-class
trial’s six-family roster returns an inconclusive verdict at the preregistered precision rather than an
equivalence verdict, which is reported as a failed attempt to confirm and never as a refutation. Everything
conceded in Section 12 is conceded in the light of these four; none of the concessions softens them.

2    Related work: debts conceded by name
This field is assembled from debts, each conceded in full: the cybernetic law that only variety in a
regulator can force down the variety that disturbances cause in what it regulates (Ashby 1956), and the
theorem that a regulator that is maximally both successful and simple must be a model of the system it
regulates (Conant and Ashby 1970), from which this field draws the intuition that control capacity must
scale with the controlled system; an exponent condition of that kind, regulatory strength growing faster
than drift as powers of throughput, is earlier (Engidaw 2026a), and this field adds the criterion stated in
capability and the class lever; the impossibility line of AI-control research (Yampolskiy 2020), which
already holds that motivational control likely needs to be added at the design or implementation phase,
not after deployment, to which it adds a rate that such design must meet; the transport-network limits of
biological scaling (West et al. 1997), conceded as premise, to which it adds the internal replacement limit
that operates when fixing the pipe no longer fixes the growth; the intelligence-explosion question as posed
by its named owners from 1965 onward (Good 1965; Vinge 1993; Chalmers 2010; Hutter 2012; Bostrom
2014), to which it adds the missing state variables; the self-modifying-program line of the 2000s, whose
Gödel machines rewrite any part of their own code once they have found a proof that the rewrite is useful
(Schmidhuber 2003), the theoretical ancestry of a program that rewrites itself, to which it adds rates to
be measured, an exponent and a class cap; the functional-equation mathematics, the machinery, never
claimed; and the scalable-oversight programme (Irving et al. 2018; Christiano et al. 2018; Leike et al.
2018; Burns et al. 2023), whose ladders are the object of Law III, the ARC Ceiling. What this field claims
as its own is the assembly: the loop as native object, scale discipline as founding method, the bounds as
laws, the corrector’s class as the lever, and the instruments and experiments, preregistered by timestamp,
that bear on Laws I to III.
     Formal treatments of recursive self-improvement predate this field, and this paper claims no priority for
a formal treatment as such. Wang (2018) formally defines one class of recursively self-improving systems,
proves that, in a restricted setting, such systems can be computable, and reports simulations suggesting that
they can be efficient; Reddy (2020) sets out Markov-chain, history-embedding and program-generating
models of recursive self-improvement. Chen et al. (2026) survey recursive self-improvement across 1,250
papers; they rank verification signals from formal verifiers, the strongest, to intrinsic self-assessment,
the weakest, hold that a loop’s ceiling is set by its verification signal, and observe that demonstrated
self-improvement strength tracks that ranking, a qualitative pattern and, in their words, “not a measured
law”; among their open problems they list stability engineering as a discipline and measurement fit for
governance. That ranking states no exponent: it is context for the need this field answers, not a test of any
of its laws. On recursive error, models collapse when trained on recursively generated data (Shumailov
et al. 2024), and in an analytically tractable linear setting the test error has a finite bound, independent
of the number of iterations, when real and generated data accumulate rather than replace each other
(Gerstgrasser et al. 2024). Recursive depth as a scaling dimension is not this field’s: Alabdulmohsin
and Zhai (2025) introduced recursive inference scaling, comparing its variants in a compute-matched
regime and deriving data scaling laws, and the theory registration concedes to that paper the term, the
compute-matched comparison and the first quantitative scaling treatment of recursive depth; of the two
works it names as nearest the mathematical object, one fits an exponent on recurrence count in looped
language models (Schwethelm et al. 2026) and the other reports test-time looping following a saturating
exponential decay (Prairie et al. 2026). Across more than 350 models, errors are substantially correlated
(Kim et al. 2025). Model mistakes become more similar as capability rises (Goel et al. 2025). On
correctors, Engels et al. (2025) fit scaling laws for the success of oversight under a capability gap between
overseer and overseen and analyse nested scalable oversight, a quantitative baseline that any claim about
how correction scales must beat; Rudoler et al. (2026) prove that, in a supervised setting, monitoring
with vanishing error and query rates is possible exactly when the class of possible monitor functions has

     A search on 14 November 2026 of arXiv and the web found earlier thresholds of two kinds. Two
are thresholds stated as values and not as exponents: a critical ratio of regulation to throughput, below
which escape becomes almost certain (Engidaw 2026a), and, where capability scales faster with training
compute than alignment overhead does, a finite compute threshold beyond which keeping safety margins
becomes increasingly difficult without architectural innovation (Engidaw 2026b). One is a condition on
growth itself, with no corrector in it: a software singularity exactly when 𝑟 exceeds one, where 𝑟 is the
number of times the output metric doubles for each doubling of cumulative research inputs (Davidson
2023). The search found no earlier statement of a critical growth exponent, equal to one divided by one
minus the correction-leverage exponent, above which a self-improving system does not stay correctable.
That expression follows from its burden and capacity assumptions by elementary algebra, by the same kind
of comparison of two powers as the threshold of Engidaw (2026b); what is particular is those assumptions,
which can be tested. A search of 3 September 2026, recorded in the theory registration, located no earlier
publication stating and testing any single one of its propositions as registered; it could not reach Google
Scholar, Semantic Scholar or OpenAlex, and the registration commits to repeating it before any priority
claim on the conjunction. The same audit found close formal predecessors for the phenomena beneath
seven of the propositions.


3     The object, and the twelve fields that do not state it
Definition 1 (Recursive system). Define a recursive system as any system whose outputs re-enter its own
improvement process.

The field’s native object is that loop. Its neighbours each own a tool and none owns the object, and each is
named here with what it does own, because the discipline is assembled from debts to them, conceded in
Section 2.
    A field is licensed when the questions that matter are about an object none of the existing fields takes
as primitive. The questions that matter here, how fast can self-improvement go, what bounds it, and what
kind of overseer can keep up, are questions about the loop.

1. Adaptive control and self-tuning regulation. Stated there: a plant with a controller whose gains
adapt online (Åström and Wittenmark 1973); in the wider field, stability by a Lyapunov argument and the
adaptation gain against the disturbance rate. What fits: Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, is, in form, an
adaptive-control stability condition, one rate against another, and the loop that rewrites its own controller
is half present, because a self-tuning regulator does rewrite its gains. What breaks: adaptive control keeps
the controller’s structure fixed and adapts parameters; it has no variable for the controller’s own capability
growing with depth, and no notion that a controller built from the plant’s own class is capped in what it
can correct; its conservation law (feedback cannot reduce sensitivity everywhere) is a law of fixed loops.
Verdict: could host that law as a chapter, “stability of self-modifying regulators”, if the class variable
proves empty. Moves there if: the corrector-class ratio holds at 1.00.

2. Queueing theory and the stability of service systems. Stated there: correctable burden arrives at a
rate proportional to capability gained; correction is a server whose capacity scales with capability; the
system is stable when the load ratio stays below one, the condition of a single queue with a single server,
whose general theory Lindley (1952) develops. What fits: this is the ARC Ceiling’s derivation, and the
paper concedes it. Section 6 is a queueing stability condition with an unusual coupling: the arrival rate
grows with the work already served. What breaks: queueing theory has no notion that the server is built
from the same class as the arrivals and therefore capped in how its capacity scales; it carries no depth
exponent and assumes stationarity. Verdict: the strongest host for the form of Law III, the ARC Ceiling; if
the class cap is empty, that law belongs here as a growth-coupled queue and the field’s name attaches to

3. Endogenous growth theory in economics. Stated there: a knowledge production function in which
new knowledge depends on the existing stock raised to an exponent; the share of human capital or labour
devoted to research is the policy dial; the argument over whether that exponent is one, so that a constant
research effort sustains growth (Romer 1990), or below one, so that growth needs ever more research
inputs (Jones 1995), is exactly the question of whether recursion is sustainable. What fits: Law I, the ARC
Principle, taken alone, is a knowledge-production exponent under another name, and the reinvestment
dial the preregistered studies turn is the research share of a growth model; the paper concedes both.
What breaks: growth theory has no drift ledger, because ideas in its models do not corrupt the stock
that produced them, and no corrector. It therefore cannot state Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, or
Law III, the ARC Ceiling, and it does not ask what the improver is made of. Verdict: could host Law I,
the ARC Principle, entirely. The field claims it through its coupling to those other two; that coupling
is the whole of what is claimed. Moves there if: drift and correction prove inseparable from capability
under measurement (objection G3), leaving a growth exponent and nothing else.

4. Software reliability engineering and defect dynamics. Stated there: faults injected per change
against faults repaired per unit effort; reliability growth when repair outpaces injection; the accumulation
of unrepaired debt; and, in Musa (1975), the execution time of testing needed to reach a reliability goal,
with the errors remaining, estimated from the intervals between failures. What fits: drift and the correction
rate are, operationally, injection and repair rates, and the field’s estimators for them owe this discipline
their shape; conceded. What breaks: a reliability model does not let the system’s repair capacity grow
as a function of its own capability, has no class variable and no depth exponent. Verdict: could host
the measurement methodology for the two rates, not the laws. Moves there if: the rates turn out to be
measurable only as engineering metrics with no lawful relation between them.

4    Scale discipline, and the five state variables
Before any law, a measurement principle, learned the hard way and adopted as founding method: capability
must be measured on a ratio scale with a true zero and no ceiling. Thermodynamics could not state
its laws in their quantitative form until temperature was absolute; a bounded percentage score does to
Recursive Dynamics what a Celsius-only thermometer does to the third law of thermodynamics: it makes
the laws unstatable, because a score capped at 100 per cent cannot follow a power law in anything. The
field’s instrument is therefore a calibrated difficulty ladder: a latent, ratio-scaled capability measure with a
true zero and no ceiling, on which doubling means the same thing at every height; and every quantitative
surface names which of three model families it is reading (unbounded or latent capability, error decay, or
bounded performance), because the exponent means something different in each. The programme adopted
this after catching its own worked example running a bounded score through unbounded arithmetic; the
correction is recorded, and the lesson is promoted here from a bug fix to a founding principle: scale
discipline precedes law. A ratio scale is necessary and not sufficient. Any power of a ratio scale also has
a true zero, no ceiling and a doubling that means the same at every height, and a power multiplies every

8    The rival’s best case, steelmanned
The strongest objection is not that the laws are unproven. It is that the mechanism may be empty:
correction may just be correction, in which case what a corrector is made of does not matter and the class
distinction dissolves. This rival is not a straw man. The scalable-oversight programme in its several forms,
weak-to-strong generalisation (Burns et al. 2023), debate (Irving et al. 2018), amplification (Christiano
et al. 2018), recursive reward modelling (Leike et al. 2018), draws its correctors, in the designs these
papers set out, from the overseen system’s own kind: small pretrained models of the same family through
most of the first, two copies of one model before a human judge, copies of the model assisting a human,
and agents trained by the same method on simpler tasks. One exception is on the record: in an appendix,
as a proof of concept in vision, the first pairs an AlexNet supervisor with students built on self-supervised
models of other architectures. Whether such correction keeps pace as capability grows, its authors state as
an open question or test, and the strongest published framework adjacent to this question, the scaling
laws for scalable oversight of Engels et al. (2025), is architecture-blind: it gives overseer and overseen
oversight-specific Elo scores that depend on general capability alone, so it carries no variable for what the
corrector is made of.
     Worse for this programme, its own pilot leans the rival’s way. In the programme’s published
measurement of parallel sampling, more samples left the parallel exponent near zero in two of the models
measured, gave 0.22 in a third and none in a fourth, which scored 100 per cent at every sample count,
and one model, the only one whose sequential and parallel exponents could both be read, gave 0.31:
draws from one model added little, which this paper reads as the mark of correlated correction channels.
That model’s row is printed whole here, as the theory registration requires: on Gemini 3 Flash, Paper II
reports an endpoint sequential exponent of 0.59 with a bootstrap 95 per cent interval of minus 1.3 to 2.9, a
regression sequential exponent of 0.49 with a standard error of 0.20 and a coefficient of determination of
0.86, and a parallel exponent of 0.31. It is one model’s fit and inconclusive. Correlated channels are
exactly the mechanism that would weaken same-class correction in a way that also weakens cross-class
correction, dragging the class ratio toward one. The published measurements of correlated errors across
models, which Section 2 sets out, point the same way (Kim et al. 2025; Goel et al. 2025). Parallel
sampling has also been found to outperform sequential sampling in several comparisons (Zeng et al. 2025;
Gu et al. 2026), so the programme’s reading of its own parallel exponent is one reading among others.
The eclipse design is therefore preregistered with the programme’s own contrary evidence on its face, and
it commits to publishing a null result under the same title as a discovery about the world rather than an
embarrassment to be buried.

The loop studied here rewrites the controller’s own capability, and the questions it raises (does correction
out-scale drift; is there a ceiling and whose property is it) are not cybernetics’ questions. What would
change it: if those questions turn out to be answerable inside cybernetics with nothing added, the field

A2. Dynamical systems theory already has stability conditions; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law,

       B5, G3 and G4); its Assumption 1, burden in proportion to capability gained, is unmeasured, and
       on one power trajectory it cannot be told apart from burden following a power of capability itself.

     • Substrate independence stands at exploratory grade, and the fourth-cell test that would catch flexible
       fitting has not run (Section 9; objection C3).
     • The field has one author, no replication by other hands and no use by strangers (Section 15.1;

17     Conclusion
Until the entry criteria are met, one sentence carries the field’s whole status: Recursive Dynamics is a
new field at the Carnot stage: one memoir in, instruments built, the instruments through which
Laws I to III take their word still design drafts, three related studies preregistered by timestamp
and not yet run, one registered counted test run to a verdict by the programme’s own run, and the
whole staked in public where failure will be as visible as success.
    The invitation that follows from everything above is the only one the field can extend: run the test
that could kill it. Every instrument, seed, schedule and threshold is written down for that purpose. The
designs of those studies are prepared as draft registrations awaiting human submission, and each becomes
public with its registration. An independent run outranks the programme’s own.
