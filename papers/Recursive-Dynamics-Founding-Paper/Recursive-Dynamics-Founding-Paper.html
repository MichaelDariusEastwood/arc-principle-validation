Abstract

What limits a system that improves itself using its own outputs, whatever it is made of? This paper founds Recursive Dynamics, the science of that question. Its object is the self-improvement loop, described by five state variables: capability, recursive depth, correction rate, drift and correction leverage. The field carries the five laws of the ARC Theory, each a law under test. Law I, the ARC Principle, U=I×RαU = I \times R^{\alpha}, is the foundation; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, reads βC>k\beta_C > k; Law III, the ARC Ceiling, reads αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma). Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, complete the five. The paper derives one bound. In a model of two assumptions, correction keeps pace with the burden that growth creates only while the growth exponent stays below 1/(1−γ)1/(1-\gamma), where γ\gamma is how correction capacity scales with capability. For a corrector of the system’s own class, a premise the programme names as the conjecture’s most likely point of failure caps γ\gamma at one half. The ceiling is then at most two on the relative rung, the ratio of burden to correction capacity, and two thirds once burden is counted over an exposure that grows with capability. The paper states four outcomes that would make the field wrong and four results that would dissolve it, tries to house its questions in twelve neighbouring fields, and answers fifty-five objections. No law is decided yet: the instruments through which Laws I to III take their word are design drafts, and three related studies are preregistered by timestamp and not yet run. One registered test has returned a verdict: the placement limb of Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, P21, is supported on the performance axis only, under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not evaluable and one disclosed recovery amendment.

1 Introduction

In 1824 the steam engine was the most consequential machine on earth, and nobody could say what limited it. Engineers improved engines the way engineers do, by building the next one; whether that improvement had a limit had often been asked, but never treated in general, independent of any mechanism or working substance, because the question belonged to no existing subject. Sadi Carnot treated it in that general form in a memoir (Carnot 1824) that founded a science, and the pattern has repeated ever since: cybernetics was proposed as a field in 1948, in the book that named it (Wiener 1948), before most of its results existed; exobiology was named in 1960 (Lederberg 1960) before a single specimen. When working machines precede their theory, the theory that forms is not a branch of an old field. It is a new one, because the old fields’ native objects were never the machine’s behaviour as such. The present moment has exactly this shape. Systems now improve their own outputs using their own outputs: models that critique and revise their reasoning, pipelines that generate their own training signal, laboratories automating parts of their own research loop (Chen et al. 2026). Some already work, in the settings their builders report. Whether they stay under control as they grow is the control problem of AI safety (Bostrom 2014), and for systems that improve themselves it is a question of rates: whether their correction keeps pace with their growth. Ask what bounds them and the works this paper reads answer with scenarios, forecasts and intuitions, and with formal models, bounds and fitted scaling laws, each stated in its own variables (Section 2). None of them answers with one set of state variables in which a limit can be stated once and tested across substrates. That is not a criticism of those works; it is what this paper reads as the signature of a missing field.

This paper founds that field. Its one derived result is a ceiling: inside a stated minimal model, correction out-scales the burden that growth creates only while the growth exponent stays below a value set by how correction capacity scales with capability, and for a corrector of the system’s own class that value is at most two, on the relative rung, the ratio of burden to correction capacity, on a premise the programme names as the conjecture’s most likely point of failure (Section 6). It is written to be attacked. Section 7 makes the positive case as a scored list of conditions; Section 12 collects every objection its author and his adversarial readers could find and prints what happened to each; Section 14 states what would dissolve the field; Section 15.1 states what would have to happen before anyone should call it a science.

1.1 What is claimed, stated as what is forbidden

A claim that forbids nothing is not a claim, and a paper that concedes as much as this one does owes the reader its prohibitions first. Under the preregistered analyses, the field forbids four outcomes:

  1. A stable system holding its growth exponent above the ceiling, αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma), for a sustained run.

  2. A same-class corrector whose correction leverage γ\gamma measures wholly above one half.

  3. A corrector-class contrast that runs the other way, or that falls wholly below the preregistered support threshold wherever the cap should bind: the corrector-class trial’s own anti-directional verdict, its equivalence verdict and its below-threshold outcome.

  4. Monotone returns in the growth exponent, with no interior maximum, as reinvestment rises.

The first is P16 of the theory registration, the prohibition itself, whose instrument waits on its identity and on decisions the author has not yet taken. The second is P6’s refuter, whose estimator waits until its corrector taxonomy is frozen; the third and the fourth have studies preregistered by timestamp (Section 11). All four are this paper’s commitments; the registration’s rules for the laws’ words are narrower, as Section 11 sets out.

The first, the third and the fourth would each make the field wrong. The second is a prohibition of a different weight: it kills the one-half cap and the value two, on the relative rung, that follows from it (the programme claims the cap and conjectures that γ\gamma sits exactly at it), while the ceiling relation stands, because the relation returns whatever leverage exponent is measured. A true null on the corrector-class trial’s six-family roster returns an inconclusive verdict at the preregistered precision rather than an equivalence verdict, which is reported as a failed attempt to confirm and never as a refutation. Everything conceded in Section 12 is conceded in the light of these four; none of the concessions softens them.

2 Related work: debts conceded by name

This field is assembled from debts, each conceded in full: the cybernetic law that only variety in a regulator can force down the variety that disturbances cause in what it regulates (Ashby 1956), and the theorem that a regulator that is maximally both successful and simple must be a model of the system it regulates (Conant and Ashby 1970), from which this field draws the intuition that control capacity must scale with the controlled system; an exponent condition of that kind, regulatory strength growing faster than drift as powers of throughput, is earlier (Engidaw 2026a), and this field adds the criterion stated in capability and the class lever; the impossibility line of AI-control research (Yampolskiy 2020), which already holds that motivational control likely needs to be added at the design or implementation phase, not after deployment, to which it adds a rate that such design must meet; the transport-network limits of biological scaling (West et al. 1997), conceded as premise, to which it adds the internal replacement limit that operates when fixing the pipe no longer fixes the growth; the intelligence-explosion question as posed by its named owners from 1965 onward (Good 1965; Vinge 1993; Chalmers 2010; Hutter 2012; Bostrom 2014), to which it adds the missing state variables; the self-modifying-program line of the 2000s, whose Gödel machines rewrite any part of their own code once they have found a proof that the rewrite is useful (Schmidhuber 2003), the theoretical ancestry of a program that rewrites itself, to which it adds rates to be measured, an exponent and a class cap; the functional-equation mathematics, the machinery, never claimed; and the scalable-oversight programme (Irving et al. 2018; Christiano et al. 2018; Leike et al. 2018; Burns et al. 2023), whose ladders are the object of Law III, the ARC Ceiling. What this field claims as its own is the assembly: the loop as native object, scale discipline as founding method, the bounds as laws, the corrector’s class as the lever, and the instruments and experiments, preregistered by timestamp, that bear on Laws I to III.

Formal treatments of recursive self-improvement predate this field, and this paper claims no priority for a formal treatment as such. Wang (2018) formally defines one class of recursively self-improving systems, proves that, in a restricted setting, such systems can be computable, and reports simulations suggesting that they can be efficient; Reddy (2020) sets out Markov-chain, history-embedding and program-generating models of recursive self-improvement. Chen et al. (2026) survey recursive self-improvement across 1,250 papers; they rank verification signals from formal verifiers, the strongest, to intrinsic self-assessment, the weakest, hold that a loop’s ceiling is set by its verification signal, and observe that demonstrated self-improvement strength tracks that ranking, a qualitative pattern and, in their words, “not a measured law”; among their open problems they list stability engineering as a discipline and measurement fit for governance. That ranking states no exponent: it is context for the need this field answers, not a test of any of its laws. On recursive error, models collapse when trained on recursively generated data (Shumailov et al. 2024), and in an analytically tractable linear setting the test error has a finite bound, independent of the number of iterations, when real and generated data accumulate rather than replace each other (Gerstgrasser et al. 2024). Recursive depth as a scaling dimension is not this field’s: Alabdulmohsin and Zhai (2025) introduced recursive inference scaling, comparing its variants in a compute-matched regime and deriving data scaling laws, and the theory registration concedes to that paper the term, the compute-matched comparison and the first quantitative scaling treatment of recursive depth; of the two works it names as nearest the mathematical object, one fits an exponent on recurrence count in looped language models (Schwethelm et al. 2026) and the other reports test-time looping following a saturating exponential decay (Prairie et al. 2026). Across more than 350 models, errors are substantially correlated (Kim et al. 2025). Model mistakes become more similar as capability rises (Goel et al. 2025). On correctors, Engels et al. (2025) fit scaling laws for the success of oversight under a capability gap between overseer and overseen and analyse nested scalable oversight, a quantitative baseline that any claim about how correction scales must beat; Rudoler et al. (2026) prove that, in a supervised setting, monitoring with vanishing error and query rates is possible exactly when the class of possible monitor functions has finite Littlestone dimension, and report results suggesting that monitors co-trained with adapting workers keep pace better than fixed monitors, in code-security settings; and Wang et al. (2026) report that, in the setting they study, self-evolution without execution-based verification fails to improve. Jafari et al. (2025) develop an analytic framework for recursive self-improvement in which limits from power, bandwidth and memory define a service envelope that caps instantaneous improvement, and an endogenous growth model coupling capital to compute, data and energy defines a critical boundary between superlinear and subcritical regimes; under the paper’s reading rule, its comparison with this field waits until it has been read in full.

The neighbouring fields that could hold the field’s questions are tried in their own variables in Section 3.1, with what each already holds conceded; the precedent class of fields founded on a limit theorem is set out in Section 7.1; and the objections that the field already exists under another name are answered in Section 12, group A.

A search on 14 November 2026 of arXiv and the web found earlier thresholds of two kinds. Two are thresholds stated as values and not as exponents: a critical ratio of regulation to throughput, below which escape becomes almost certain (Engidaw 2026a), and, where capability scales faster with training compute than alignment overhead does, a finite compute threshold beyond which keeping safety margins becomes increasingly difficult without architectural innovation (Engidaw 2026b). One is a condition on growth itself, with no corrector in it: a software singularity exactly when rr exceeds one, where rr is the number of times the output metric doubles for each doubling of cumulative research inputs (Davidson 2023). The search found no earlier statement of a critical growth exponent, equal to one divided by one minus the correction-leverage exponent, above which a self-improving system does not stay correctable. That expression follows from its burden and capacity assumptions by elementary algebra, by the same kind of comparison of two powers as the threshold of Engidaw (2026b); what is particular is those assumptions, which can be tested. A search of 3 September 2026, recorded in the theory registration, located no earlier publication stating and testing any single one of its propositions as registered; it could not reach Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar or OpenAlex, and the registration commits to repeating it before any priority claim on the conjunction. The same audit found close formal predecessors for the phenomena beneath seven of the propositions.

3 The object, and the twelve fields that do not state it

Definition 1 (Recursive system). Define a recursive system as any system whose outputs re-enter its own improvement process.

The field’s native object is that loop. Its neighbours each own a tool and none owns the object, and each is named here with what it does own, because the discipline is assembled from debts to them, conceded in Section 2.

Table 1. Neighbouring fields: what each owns, and why, in the works this paper reads, it does not take the loop as its object.
Neighbouring field What it owns Why, in the works read, it does not take the loop
Dynamical systems theory Change in general, fixed points, stability; it lends this field its mathematics It does not take self-rewriting update rules as its object
Cybernetics Regulation: a controller holding a plant to a reference; the requisite-variety law that only variety in the controller can force down the variety that disturbances cause (Ashby 1956) It centres designed, external loops; here the loop rewrites the controller’s own capability
Control theory Stabilising a plant with a controller outside it Here the controller is the plant, made of the same stuff, sharing its failure modes
Complexity science Emergence and self-organisation It carries no correction budget
Learning theory The learner fixed, the data varied Here the learner varies itself
Evolutionary dynamics Variation and selection No designer directs reinvestment into the improvement process itself
Scaling-law empirics Capability against external resources (parameters, data, compute) Here the resource of interest is the system’s own re-entering output
Scalable oversight Correctors, and ladders of them; bounds on oversight in its own variables None of its bounds depends on what the corrector is made of, which is what Law III, the ARC Ceiling, is about
Functional-equation mathematics The machinery of the scaling forms Claimed here as nothing more than machinery

A field is licensed when the questions that matter are about an object none of the existing fields takes as primitive. The questions that matter here, how fast can self-improvement go, what bounds it, and what kind of overseer can keep up, are questions about the loop.

3.1 The attempt to house it elsewhere

Table 1 is the kind of demarcation that reads as marketing to exactly the readers who matter, because it never tried the fit. This section tries. For each candidate host the three questions (how fast can self-improvement go; what bounds it; what overseer keeps up) are stated in the host’s own variables, everything the host already covers is conceded to it, what breaks is stated precisely, a verdict is given, and the result that would move the field into that host is named. Twelve hosts, in rough order of how much of the field each could hold. Where a host’s statement rests on a canonical work, that work is cited for what is stated there; finer literatures not yet read are left uncited, under the paper’s rule that nothing is cited before it is read.

1. Adaptive control and self-tuning regulation.

Stated there: a plant with a controller whose gains adapt online (Åström and Wittenmark 1973); in the wider field, stability by a Lyapunov argument and the adaptation gain against the disturbance rate. What fits: Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, is, in form, an adaptive-control stability condition, one rate against another, and the loop that rewrites its own controller is half present, because a self-tuning regulator does rewrite its gains. What breaks: adaptive control keeps the controller’s structure fixed and adapts parameters; it has no variable for the controller’s own capability growing with depth, and no notion that a controller built from the plant’s own class is capped in what it can correct; its conservation law (feedback cannot reduce sensitivity everywhere) is a law of fixed loops. Verdict: could host that law as a chapter, “stability of self-modifying regulators”, if the class variable proves empty. Moves there if: the corrector-class ratio holds at 1.00.

2. Queueing theory and the stability of service systems.

Stated there: correctable burden arrives at a rate proportional to capability gained; correction is a server whose capacity scales with capability; the system is stable when the load ratio stays below one, the condition of a single queue with a single server, whose general theory Lindley (1952) develops. What fits: this is the ARC Ceiling’s derivation, and the paper concedes it. Section 6 is a queueing stability condition with an unusual coupling: the arrival rate grows with the work already served. What breaks: queueing theory has no notion that the server is built from the same class as the arrivals and therefore capped in how its capacity scales; it carries no depth exponent and assumes stationarity. Verdict: the strongest host for the form of Law III, the ARC Ceiling; if the class cap is empty, that law belongs here as a growth-coupled queue and the field’s name attaches to nothing in it. Moves there if: the eclipse measurement retires the class distinction.

3. Endogenous growth theory in economics.

Stated there: a knowledge production function in which new knowledge depends on the existing stock raised to an exponent; the share of human capital or labour devoted to research is the policy dial; the argument over whether that exponent is one, so that a constant research effort sustains growth (Romer 1990), or below one, so that growth needs ever more research inputs (Jones 1995), is exactly the question of whether recursion is sustainable. What fits: Law I, the ARC Principle, taken alone, is a knowledge-production exponent under another name, and the reinvestment dial the preregistered studies turn is the research share of a growth model; the paper concedes both. What breaks: growth theory has no drift ledger, because ideas in its models do not corrupt the stock that produced them, and no corrector. It therefore cannot state Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, or Law III, the ARC Ceiling, and it does not ask what the improver is made of. Verdict: could host Law I, the ARC Principle, entirely. The field claims it through its coupling to those other two; that coupling is the whole of what is claimed. Moves there if: drift and correction prove inseparable from capability under measurement (objection G3), leaving a growth exponent and nothing else.

4. Software reliability engineering and defect dynamics.

Stated there: faults injected per change against faults repaired per unit effort; reliability growth when repair outpaces injection; the accumulation of unrepaired debt; and, in Musa (1975), the execution time of testing needed to reach a reliability goal, with the errors remaining, estimated from the intervals between failures. What fits: drift and the correction rate are, operationally, injection and repair rates, and the field’s estimators for them owe this discipline their shape; conceded. What breaks: a reliability model does not let the system’s repair capacity grow as a function of its own capability, has no class variable and no depth exponent. Verdict: could host the measurement methodology for the two rates, not the laws. Moves there if: the rates turn out to be measurable only as engineering metrics with no lawful relation between them.

5. Machine learning: self-play, self-training, learned optimisers and model collapse.

Stated there: outputs re-entering training is the self-play loop; degradation of models trained on their own outputs is drift measured in special cases (Shumailov et al. 2024); learned optimisers are learners that rewrite the learner. What fits: every preregistered experiment could be published here as a machine-learning result, and this is the most likely institutional home if the field fails to earn its name; conceded without reservation. What breaks: in the works this paper reads, the literature carries no ratio-scaled capability law across depth with a stated exponent, no bound on what a learner-rewriting learner can do, and no class cap. Its model-collapse line does pair drift with a correction, real data kept or accumulated beside generated data, and shows the bounded-versus-divergent dichotomy for training dynamics (Section 2); it has begun to vary what the corrector is made of (Feng et al. 2024), though none of these works, in the parts this paper reads, states a cap set by the corrector’s class (objection A5). Verdict: hosts the experiments; cannot state the laws. Moves there if: the substrate crossing fails, leaving the laws true of language-model loops only, which is machine learning (objection H5).

6. Scaling-law empirics.

Stated there: language-model loss against parameters, data and compute, with power-law exponents fitted for one architecture, the Transformer, within which model shape matters very little (Kaplan et al. 2020). What fits: the exponent-fitting craft, the model families, the discipline of reporting intervals; conceded. What breaks: the resource here is the system’s own re-entering output; an interior maximum alone does not separate the two, since dividing a fixed budget between the task and the improver produces one; the registration separates them by what moves the maximum, the correction exponent on the field’s account and the cost ratio on the allocation account, and the titration as drafted holds the cost ratio fixed. Verdict: hosts the method, not the object. Moves there if: recursive depth reduces, under measurement, to compute spent, or the titration shows monotone returns.

7. Scalable oversight and alignment research.

Stated there: a weaker or same-class corrector supervising a stronger system (Burns et al. 2023); ladders of correctors (Engels et al. 2025); the question whether they scale. What fits: the corrector-class question is institutionally theirs, and the eclipse result, whichever way it falls, is an oversight result; conceded. What breaks: the literature bounds what a corrector can do in its own variables, the capability gap between overseer and overseen (Engels et al. 2025) and the complexity of the class of monitor functions (Rudoler et al. 2026), but neither bound depends on whether the corrector shares the corrected system’s architecture, training distribution and blind spots, so neither can state Law III, the ARC Ceiling, or a cap set by what the corrector is made of. Verdict: the strongest institutional host for the corrector-class result. If the field dissolves, the eclipse measurement is published here and belongs here. Moves there if: the ratio and the cap prove real but nothing couples them to a growth exponent in depth, which would make the finding an oversight theorem rather than a field.

8. Evolutionary computation and error-threshold theory.

Stated there: strategy parameters that adapt alongside the solution, the improver improving itself, which evolution strategies, a family dating from the 1960s, do by adapting their own operators (Beyer and Schwefel 2002); and, in the theory of replicating populations, the error threshold, above which mutation outruns selection and information is lost, in a theory of selection that treats the quality factor and the error distribution (Eigen 1971). What fits: the error threshold is the closest existing law to Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law: a rate of corruption against a rate of correction with a boundary between persistence and collapse. The paper concedes it as an antecedent of that law’s shape; self-adaptation is a working instance of the loop rewriting its improver. What breaks: selection is external to the replicator, and capability is not a ratio ladder. The corrector there is of the replicator’s own class: a replication enzyme that copied with greater fidelity would itself require a longer gene, and longer genes breach the error threshold, the dilemma called Eigen’s paradox (Holmes 2005); what it constrains is copying fidelity against sequence length, not a correction leverage that scales with capability across depth. Verdict: could host Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, as a generalised error threshold. Moves there if: the correction-versus-drift race finds the boundary but the class lever does nothing.

9. Statistical physics: scaling, universality and renormalisation.

Stated there: in the Ising model of a ferromagnet, critical exponents fixed by the renormalisation-group equations, whose solution goes to a fixed point, and not by the details of the starting Hamiltonian (Wilson 1971); in the wider field, the same exponent families across unrelated substrates as a universality class, with scaling collapse as the test. What fits: the field’s substrate-independence hope is this tradition’s hope, and its fourth-cell test is a universality test; conceded. What breaks: no correction variable, no drift variable, no corrector whose composition is the lever. Verdict: hosts the cross-domain families if they survive and belong to no loop. Moves there if: the families hold and the loop-specific laws do not.

10. Computability, algorithmic information and the self-referential improver line.

Stated there: programs that rewrite any part of their own code once they have found a proof that the rewrite is useful, such a rewrite being globally optimal in the sense the proof fixes (Schmidhuber 2003); the limits of self-reference (Turing 1936). What fits: this is the theoretical ancestry of “a program that rewrites itself”, conceded. What breaks: those results concern provability and optimality, not measured rates; they carry no exponent, no drift and no class cap. Verdict: ancestry, not a host. Moves there if: nothing empirical survives and only the self-reference question remains.

11. Second-order cybernetics, autopoiesis and general systems theory.

Stated there: systems that produce and maintain themselves (Maturana and Varela 1980); the observer inside the loop. What fits: the conceptual picture of a self-producing system is theirs and is conceded. What breaks: the tradition is qualitative; it has no ratio ladder, no exponents, no preregistered decider. Verdict: a philosophical host, not a measuring one. Moves there if: the field’s quantities prove unmeasurable, at which point it was never a science and belongs with the qualitative traditions of self-organisation.

12. Metascience and organisational learning.

Stated there: science itself as the original self-correcting recursive improver; institutions that learn. What fits: an application domain and a candidate non-artificial substrate for the crossing. What breaks: no capability ladder, and the corrector is a community, not a class. Verdict: a domain the field would be tested in, not a home.

The dissolution map by host.

If the founding conjunction fails, the parts do not vanish; they go home, and the paper says where. Law I, the ARC Principle, returns to endogenous growth theory as a knowledge-production exponent. Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, returns to adaptive control and error-threshold theory as a rate condition. The ARC Ceiling returns to queueing theory as a growth-coupled stability condition. The corrector-class result, whichever way it falls, is published in scalable oversight. The instruments and the experiments are machine learning. The cross-domain families, if they survive, are statistical physics. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, registered as laws on 15 October 2026 (Section 5), are not yet placed on this map. Nothing placed on it is orphaned by the field’s death, which is what makes the death publishable.

The residue no host holds.

After the twelve attempts, one thing remains that none of them can state in its own variables: a cap on correction leverage that depends on what the corrector is made of, coupled to a growth exponent in recursive depth, so that the sustainable rate of self-improvement is a property of the corrector’s class. In the works this paper reads, growth theory has the exponent and no corrector; control and queueing have the stability condition and no class; oversight has the corrector and bounds in its own variables, and no cap set by the corrector’s class; machine learning has the loop, conditions under which retraining on a model’s own outputs stays bounded and a qualitative ranking of verifiers, and no cap on correction leverage. Recursive Dynamics is the name for that coupling. If the coupling is empty, the parts go home and the name dies, and the field will have cost its neighbours nothing but a careful table.

4 Scale discipline, and the five state variables

Before any law, a measurement principle, learned the hard way and adopted as founding method: capability must be measured on a ratio scale with a true zero and no ceiling. Thermodynamics could not state its laws in their quantitative form until temperature was absolute; a bounded percentage score does to Recursive Dynamics what a Celsius-only thermometer does to the third law of thermodynamics: it makes the laws unstatable, because a score capped at 100 per cent cannot follow a power law in anything. The field’s instrument is therefore a calibrated difficulty ladder: a latent, ratio-scaled capability measure with a true zero and no ceiling, on which doubling means the same thing at every height; and every quantitative surface names which of three model families it is reading (unbounded or latent capability, error decay, or bounded performance), because the exponent means something different in each. The programme adopted this after catching its own worked example running a bounded score through unbounded arithmetic; the correction is recorded, and the lesson is promoted here from a bug fix to a founding principle: scale discipline precedes law. A ratio scale is necessary and not sufficient. Any power of a ratio scale also has a true zero, no ceiling and a doubling that means the same at every height, and a power multiplies every exponent the laws are written in, so the theory registration fixes the scale on which its exponents are read and treats a benchmark ladder as the capability of the burden model only where a measurement model joining the two is registered.

6 One bound, worked in full

Carnot did not describe engines; he computed what no engine could beat. Here is this field’s equivalent, in two assumptions and four lines, exactly as it stands in the programme’s public record.

Let capability grow as C(R)=C0(R/R0)αC(R) = C_0 (R/R_0)^{\alpha}. Assume two things about the loop:

Assumption 1 (Burden). Self-modification generates correctable burden in proportion to capability gained: B(R)=bdC/dRB(R) = b\,\mathrm{d}C/\mathrm{d}R per unit depth.

Assumption 2 (Correction capacity). Correction service capacity scales with capability to a power: A(R)=aC(R)γA(R) = a\,C(R)^{\gamma}, with 0≤γ<10 \le \gamma < 1.

Proposition 1 (The burden-to-capacity ratio). With capability growing as above, under Assumptions 1 and 2, with α\alpha, bb and γ\gamma constant over the range read and correction capacity depending on depth only through capability, the burden-to-capacity ratio scales as B(R)/A(R)∝Rα(1−γ)−1.\begin{equation*} B(R)/A(R) \propto R^{\alpha(1-\gamma)-1}. \end{equation*}(1)

Proof. Since C(R)=C0(R/R0)αC(R) = C_0 (R/R_0)^{\alpha}, dC/dR=αC/R\mathrm{d}C/\mathrm{d}R = \alpha C/R, so B(R)/A(R)=(bα/a)C(R)1−γ/R=(bα/a)C01−γR0−α(1−γ)Rα(1−γ)−1.B(R)/A(R) = (b\alpha/a)\,C(R)^{1-\gamma}/R = (b\alpha/a)\,C_0^{1-\gamma} R_0^{-\alpha(1-\gamma)}\,R^{\alpha(1-\gamma)-1}. ◻

Everything is in the exponent. If α(1−γ)<1\alpha(1-\gamma) < 1, correction asymptotically out-scales the burden its own growth creates: the loop can run. If α(1−γ)>1\alpha(1-\gamma) > 1, burden out-scales correction however well the loop starts, and the ratio grows without limit; what that does to the errors themselves depends on how they are removed, which these four lines do not fix. The crossover is αcrit=1/(1−γ),\begin{equation*} \alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma), \end{equation*}(2) and equality is not automatically safe: at the tie, the outcome hangs on coefficients, delays and saturation, which is the regime where every interesting failure lives. Equivalently, a loop growing as RαR^{\alpha} needs a correction leverage of at least 1−1/α1 - 1/\alpha: burden per unit depth grows as C1−1/αC^{1-1/\alpha}, capacity as CγC^{\gamma}, and the ceiling is where the two elasticities meet.

Two of those conditions are choices. With correction service depending on depth directly, A(R)=aC(R)γRηA(R) = a\,C(R)^{\gamma} R^{\eta}, the crossover is (1+η)/(1−γ)(1+\eta)/(1-\gamma). The relation registered is the case η=0\eta = 0, and the one-parameter generalisation stands as a named rival in P20’s comparison (theory registration). Where α\alpha varies with depth, as the elasticities of Section 4 may, the ratio’s local exponent gains dln⁡α/dln⁡R\mathrm{d}\ln\alpha/\mathrm{d}\ln R, so equation (2) holds where the exponents are steady over the range read. And γ<1\gamma < 1 is assumed: at γ≥1\gamma \geq 1 the ratio falls for every growth exponent and there is no ceiling.

Now the premise that gives the ceiling its number. The value one half follows from four conditions, none yet measured (theory registration): that correction capacity is read as inverse residual uncertainty; that a corrector’s channels are uncorrelated; that their number grows in proportion to the capability being corrected; and that they are comparable in variance and quality. Under them capacity grows as the square root of the channel count, so γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2. Positive correlation lowers the exponent, the more so as channels multiply: at a common correlation ρ\rho among NN channels it is (1−ρ)/(2(1+(N−1)ρ))(1-\rho)/\bigl(2(1+(N-1)\rho)\bigr), about 0.06 at ρ=0.1\rho = 0.1 and N=64N = 64. A corrector of the system’s own class, sharing its architecture, training distribution and blind spots, is the case in which negative correlation is not expected, so on these conditions one half is a ceiling for that class, not a typical value. The conditions are where the premise can fail: channels whose quality rises with the capability they correct, or that differ in variance, can carry γ\gamma above one half, and an error law better than the square root moves the ceiling upward without limit. Substituting γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2: αcrit=2.\begin{equation*} \alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 2. \end{equation*}(3)

The value two belongs to the relative rung: it bounds whether correction capacity keeps pace with the burden arriving per unit depth. A stricter question asks whether the burden the system carries stops growing, and it needs the exposure that burden is counted over to be named. Where that exposure grows with capability, the same assumptions subtract γ\gamma from two rather than from one, so the boundary is 1/(2−γ)1/(2-\gamma), two thirds at γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2. Cumulative burden is stricter again and depends on the clock, which this model does not carry (theory registration).

Class here is composition: how far a corrector’s blind spots overlap the system’s, the axis the theory registration calls failure-mode independence. The registration keeps the word class, in P7 and the corrector-class audit, for a different axis, whether a corrector’s strength is fixed by construction or scales with what it corrects, and the two can run in opposite directions: a formal verifier is fixed by construction and close to maximally independent of the system it checks.

Inside this model, growth up to quadratic in depth is then the most that a same-class corrector can sustain, and it can reach that much only at the ceiling itself, where the tie leaves the outcome to coefficients, delays and saturation; beyond it, correction keeps pace only if the corrector escapes the independence ceiling, and the way of escape the theory names is to change what the corrector is made of. A ladder of correctors of the system’s own class is itself a corrector of that class, so on the premise no ladder of them passes the ceiling by adding levels. That is the engineering content of this bound in one sentence: beyond quadratic growth, the corrector’s composition is the lever. These four lines do not establish bounded harm, finite-horizon safety, or a law of nature; they establish a conditional crossover whose premises are measurable, which is exactly what a young field’s first bound should be.

7.1 The precedent class: fields founded on a limit theorem in their own variables

The comparison that supports the case for the field most is not thermodynamics alone but the class of fields whose identity is a bound stated in variables the older fields did not carry, holding across substrates. Thermodynamics: Carnot (1824), no engine beats the reversible one; the bound came first and the formal laws followed decades later. Information theory: Shannon (1948), channel capacity as a limit theorem, with entropy and mutual information as state variables no prior field carried, and substrate independence (wires, radio, later genomes) as the proof of generality. Computability: Turing (1936), the proof that no general process can decide whether an arbitrary machine is circle-free or ever prints a given symbol, the root of what is now called the halting problem, a field defined by what cannot be computed. Control theory’s own limit: Bode (1945), feedback cannot reduce sensitivity everywhere, a conservation law of the loop. Statistics: the Cramér-Rao bound, 1945 to 1946, a floor on estimator variance, given by Rao (1945) and named also for Cramér, whose treatise of 1946 sets out statistical estimation (Cramér 1946).

The pattern is one thing: a limit stated in variables the old fields did not carry, and holding across substrates, can found a field, as Carnot’s, Shannon’s and Turing’s did, or come to define one that already exists, as Bode’s and the Cramér-Rao bound did. Recursive Dynamics puts a limit of exactly that shape to the test: αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma), with the same-class cap on γ\gamma at one half. The disanalogy is printed in the same breath: every field above earned its name by measurement and by many hands; this one was founded by one person before its decisive measurements, with no replication by other hands yet, and the parallel is a scaffold, not a certificate.

8 The rival’s best case, steelmanned

The strongest objection is not that the laws are unproven. It is that the mechanism may be empty: correction may just be correction, in which case what a corrector is made of does not matter and the class distinction dissolves. This rival is not a straw man. The scalable-oversight programme in its several forms, weak-to-strong generalisation (Burns et al. 2023), debate (Irving et al. 2018), amplification (Christiano et al. 2018), recursive reward modelling (Leike et al. 2018), draws its correctors, in the designs these papers set out, from the overseen system’s own kind: small pretrained models of the same family through most of the first, two copies of one model before a human judge, copies of the model assisting a human, and agents trained by the same method on simpler tasks. One exception is on the record: in an appendix, as a proof of concept in vision, the first pairs an AlexNet supervisor with students built on self-supervised models of other architectures. Whether such correction keeps pace as capability grows, its authors state as an open question or test, and the strongest published framework adjacent to this question, the scaling laws for scalable oversight of Engels et al. (2025), is architecture-blind: it gives overseer and overseen oversight-specific Elo scores that depend on general capability alone, so it carries no variable for what the corrector is made of.

Worse for this programme, its own pilot leans the rival’s way. In the programme’s published measurement of parallel sampling, more samples left the parallel exponent near zero in two of the models measured, gave 0.22 in a third and none in a fourth, which scored 100 per cent at every sample count, and one model, the only one whose sequential and parallel exponents could both be read, gave 0.31: draws from one model added little, which this paper reads as the mark of correlated correction channels. That model’s row is printed whole here, as the theory registration requires: on Gemini 3 Flash, Paper II reports an endpoint sequential exponent of 0.59 with a bootstrap 95 per cent interval of minus 1.3 to 2.9, a regression sequential exponent of 0.49 with a standard error of 0.20 and a coefficient of determination of 0.86, and a parallel exponent of 0.31. It is one model’s fit and inconclusive. Correlated channels are exactly the mechanism that would weaken same-class correction in a way that also weakens cross-class correction, dragging the class ratio toward one. The published measurements of correlated errors across models, which Section 2 sets out, point the same way (Kim et al. 2025; Goel et al. 2025). Parallel sampling has also been found to outperform sequential sampling in several comparisons (Zeng et al. 2025; Gu et al. 2026), so the programme’s reading of its own parallel exponent is one reading among others. The eclipse design is therefore preregistered with the programme’s own contrary evidence on its face, and it commits to publishing a null result under the same title as a discovery about the world rather than an embarrassment to be buried.

10 Instruments

A field is its instruments before it is its results. Recursive Dynamics ships with an instrument canon built before any confirmatory data existed: the calibrated capability ladder (Section 4); a blinded scoring stack in which every output is laundered so no scorer can tell what produced it, with label-randomised nulls computed through the identical pipeline, so the corrector-class contrast’s null is imposed structurally rather than fitted; estimator code that is hash-pinned, fixture-gated (every preregistered verdict branch has a planted-truth test the code must pass before anything ships), and governed by a preregistered defect clause under which the specification, not the code, is the analysis, so a post-registration coding error is a disclosed repair on preregistered terms rather than a dead study. Scoring is by blinded multi-model panels, never by human raters; where an external human criterion is methodologically unavoidable, a single author-rated fallback is preregistered, blind, washout-retested (he re-scores a registered thirty per cent of items after a washout of at least fourteen days, blind to his first-pass scores, as the theory registration records), and disclosed as author-rated in every headline. Of these, what is public, and where: reference code for the ladder (a mock ladder and a checkable ladder over a reference set of tasks with exact answers), a blinding-integrity simulator, and executable decision rules with planted-truth tests and pinned source hashes stand on the programme’s repository on GitHub (available under the Replication Licence, version 1.0), which calls them reference code and not a validated deciding instrument; and a defect-repair rule, under which an implementation defect found after registration and before the run’s freeze may be repaired only where the repair changes no registered choice, is deposited with the registration of Unit 83 (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/MN73W), the counted test of P21, the placement limb of Law V, the ARC Embedding Law: whether correction inside each revision round pulls away, as depth rises, from a monitor that sees only the finished register (Section 11). Each drafted study’s own ladder, scoring stack, estimators and defect clause are written and dated in its design and become public with its registration.

One limitation is printed here rather than discovered by a reviewer: when models score models, the scorer can share the blind spots of the scored. The laundering and the forced null remove the scorer’s knowledge of provenance, not its class. The programme’s answer is cross-family panels and a preregistered human-criterion validation instrument, and the circularity is reduced, disclosed and measured, not abolished. It returns as objection C4.

11 What each experiment can decide

Three related studies are drafted for Laws I to III, and the theory registration fixes what each can decide: a proposition’s status there “is the status of the instrument that would decide it, never the status of a neighbouring unit that would inform it”. Section 5 gives the propositions through which each law takes its word; the registration’s instrument for each of them is a design draft, P16’s blocked and P3’s not evaluable until the correction exponent is measured, and none of the three studies is one of those instruments. Each is set out below with what it decides or informs. Their nulls belong to the rivals, their numbers are computed and printed in their designs, and their outcomes are publishable on identical terms whichever way they fall. Each of the three is a design preregistered by timestamp and prepared as a draft registration awaiting human submission; none is registered on OSF and none has run. Submission is the author’s own act, and no tool or agent submits anything. In this paper registered is said of what OSF has accepted: the theory registration of 8 September 2026 with its twenty-two propositions, updated on 13 September 2026, on 15 October 2026, when version 1.103 registered Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, as laws, and on 29 October 2026; and four study units. A study, an analysis, a threshold or a verdict fixed in one of the dated designs is called preregistered, short for preregistered by timestamp.

Beside the three, four study units are registered on OSF. Unit 83, registered on 22 October 2026 (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/MN73W) and run on 27 and 28 October 2026 (results: DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/98HKR), is the first registered test of a proposition a law carries, P21; its verdict is given below, with Law V, the ARC Embedding Law. Unit 82, registered on 27 October 2026 (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/JUFG4), is a counted test of P17, whether a named corrector panel’s failures are conditionally independent on a frozen fault set, which the registration places beneath the value two of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, on the relative rung; it measures overlap between correctors, not the channel correlation the value is derived in, and a supported P17 leaves the value conjectured, and has not yet run. Unit 83b, registered on 7 November 2026 (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/4HK32), tests P21 on three providers and has not yet run. Unit 06, the corrector-class audit, which surveys the class of corrector that deployed AI alignment uses and is none of the three, was registered on 1 October 2026 (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/RV2B5) and became public on 4 October 2026, at the end of OSF’s approval period; its registered run of 6 October 2026 was administratively incomplete, with no inferential verdict, since its Run B stopped at its first call, and it is reported in full with its five deviations (results: DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/4BPHG).

Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, remains a law under test: its placement limb, P21, is supported on the performance axis only, under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not evaluable, which is not support for the information-asymmetry mechanism (the registration decides P21 on the access axis, a comparison of placements on the outcome, and prints a limb so supported while its mechanism axis is not evaluable as supported on the performance axis only), and one disclosed recovery amendment; its build-order limb, read on P12, is undecided, since P12 is untested. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, has no deciding study yet, and P15 and P12 have no instrument.

The correction-versus-drift race (the critical-correction-ratio study) informs P4, the proposition through which Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, takes its word, and decides none: does self-correction out-scale drift at all, anywhere? A durable yes is the field’s first existence result; a universal no leaves that law without a subject.

The corrector-class contrast (the eclipse measurement) asks whether what a corrector is made of matters, the class lever on which this paper’s account of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, rests. It measures a matched difference, between cross-class and same-class correctors, in residual-error decay under a fixed protocol, with the ratio reported second; it estimates neither a correction-capacity exponent nor a ceiling (Paper XI, Section 7.2). Pool same-class and cross-class corrector pairs, launder everything, and force the null: if what a corrector is made of does not matter, the difference is exactly zero and the ratio exactly 1.00, by construction. That null is the rival’s answer, not this programme’s. The programme’s mechanism predicts a durable ratio above 1.00 where the cap binds, and that is the discovery in its favour; durably below 1.00 inverts the mechanism and ends the claim as derived; 1.00 holding tight everywhere, even where the cap should bind, retires the class distinction itself. On the present design that verdict does not fire under realistic differences between targets: a true null returns inconclusive, which is a failed attempt to confirm and not a demonstration of equality. The outcomes this instrument can return against the framework are two: the reverse ordering, and a class difference whose interval lies wholly below the registered support threshold. It is preregistered by timestamp on a roster of six target models with five matched blocks each, and it registers its whole power curve: the design returns a supported verdict with probability 0.82 where the author’s stated belief puts the effect, 0.65 at the midpoint of the band he predicts and 0.07 a quarter of the way into it. What it can decide is therefore this paper’s own claim about the class lever, by the kill conditions of Sections 1.1 and 14: a contrast the wrong way ends the mechanism as derived, and a contrast in the predicted direction shows, under its protocol, an association between the corrector’s class and correction at fixed capability, not architectural causation. In the theory registration it decides nothing: it informs P13, whose entry says that no law falls with it. The cap’s own refuter, a same-class corrector whose measured correction-exponent interval lies entirely above one half (P6), needs the correction-exponent estimator the registration names for P6, a design draft blocked until its corrector taxonomy is frozen.

The capability-ladder titration (the ARC Bound study; the ARC Bound is the theory registration’s name for the value two) decides P2: whether the growth exponent has an interior maximum, at or below two, across eight reinvestment levels, under a two-stage replication-gated rule in which one counterexample cell, replicated, rejects the Bound outright in the design’s own terms; in the registration the ARC Bound is refuted only where P6 is refuted, and loses its derivation where P20 is. The theory registration places P2 in ARC-1, the register that tests Law I, the ARC Principle, but not among the propositions through which that law takes its word, and keeps it apart from the boundary claim that P3, P16 and P20 carry: until a unit exists that can tell the two kinds of maximum apart, an interior maximum it returns is reported as an allocation maximum and never as support for P3.

A drafted follow-up grid extends the ratio to every ordered pair of model families, locating where the class effect lives; and the mechanism probe (correlated failure on shared substrate) measures directly the error correlation the derivation posits, so the ratio and its mechanism must agree or their tension is itself a preregistered finding.

11.1 What would confirm a new area rather than a new chapter

A result that any old field could state in its own variables confirms a chapter of that field. A field is confirmed by a conjunction that none of its neighbours can express. Stated once, with what is preregistered today separated from what is only set out for registration:

Preregistered today: a regime in which the correction rate out-scales drift (the correction-versus-drift race); a corrector-class contrast with its ratio durably above 1.00 where the cap binds (the eclipse measurement, with the correlated-failure probe beside it; the corrector-class audit reads the other axis, P7’s); an interior maximum of the growth exponent at or below two, on the relative rung, across the reinvestment levels, under the replication-gated rule (the titration); and the fourth-cell substrate test.

Set out here, and to be registered before any data are seen: that the ratio rises with load (dose-response, which separates a mechanism from a static difference); that a cross-class corrector moves the titration’s maximum upward, linking Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, and Law III, the ARC Ceiling, through the class lever; and that a single system crosses the stability boundary as its reinvestment dial turns. None of these three is a prediction of the field until its registration carries it, and this paper does not count them as such.

The consistency check no single field can fake, with invented numbers for illustration only: suppose a measurement of correction leverage returned a same-class γ\gamma of 0.45 and a cross-class γ\gamma of 0.70. The eclipse measurement cannot supply these. The ARC Ceiling would then predict a same-class maximum near 1.8 and a cross-class maximum near 3.3 in the titration. The theory registration requires the two to be read on one system, in one composition class, at one capability level: a maximum from one unit’s titration and an exponent from another unit’s roster “compares two quantities that were never joined”, and such a pairing is never scored as support or refutation. Read on one system, agreement of that kind would be inexpressible in the variables of learning theory, control, scaling empirics or oversight; the registration’s instrument for it is the paired frontier-and-exponent unit it names for P3, a design draft not evaluable until the correction exponent is measured.

The founding conjunction, then: a regime of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; a corrector-class ratio above 1.00; an interior maximum in the reinvestment titration; the same exponent structure in one non-artificial substrate; and at least one of these replicated by strangers. The values travel with the legs rather than inside them. The one-half cap on same-class correctors waits for a measurement of γ\gamma, and the maximum’s location is reported with the maximum, so a leg survives a value that lands elsewhere and the record still says where it landed. Each alone is a finding in an old field’s vocabulary. The conjunction is not, and that is the exact sense in which results would confirm a new area rather than a new chapter.

What a partial result means is stated here rather than argued afterwards, because the trial carrying the second leg forecasts support at 0.82 only where the author’s own belief puts the effect and returns less across the rest of the band it predicts, so the full conjunction is likelier missed than cleared. Under the theory registration none of the four legs decides a law: the race informs P4, the class contrast informs P13, which stands in no register, and the titration decides P2, which is never evidence for the ceiling. Together they would show that the loop’s questions are answerable in the field’s variables; the ceiling’s own verdict waits for P16, P20 and P3. Substrate independence is what would make the variables the field’s rather than one domain’s, and the stranger’s replication is what would make the field. The stranger’s replication is listed last because it is the field’s condition and not the theory’s: the theory can be right and still wait for it.

12 Every objection found, with its disposition

This section is the paper’s reason for existing; it entered at version 2.0 (Appendix A). The objections below were collected from the programme’s own adversarial readings, from outside reviewers, from a criticism of the derivation’s treatment of depth, and from the author’s attempt to argue the field out of existence. Each is stated in its strongest form. Each carries one of these dispositions: conceded (it is true and it is printed); rejected (with the reason); partly conceded; reading debt (a literature that must be read before the point can be argued either way, under the rule that nothing is cited before it is read); or decided by experiment (a preregistered decider settles it). Fifty-five objections (group D runs D1, D2, D3 and D5; Appendix A.3, entry 14), counted by the opening words of each disposition: forty-three conceded or partly conceded, eleven rejected, one handed to experiment. Four of the forty-three (B3, D3, F2 and H1) concede in one respect and reject in another. Thirty-nine of the fifty-five carry a sentence on what would change their disposition.

A. Redundancy: the field already exists under another name

A1. Cybernetics already studies self-correcting loops; Wiener’s field is this field.

Rejected, with a condition. The co-scaling intuition is cybernetics’ and is conceded to it, in the requisite-variety law and the regulator theorem that Section 2 states (Ashby 1956; Conant and Ashby 1970). Cybernetics owns regulation: a designed controller holding a plant to a reference, with the loop’s purpose set from outside. The loop studied here rewrites the controller’s own capability, and the questions it raises (does correction out-scale drift; is there a ceiling and whose property is it) are not cybernetics’ questions. What would change it: if those questions turn out to be answerable inside cybernetics with nothing added, the field dissolves into it, which would end the field by adoption rather than by refutation, as objection A11 says of any host.

A2. Dynamical systems theory already has stability conditions; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, is a standard stability statement.

Conceded, with a boundary. The mathematics is borrowed and is claimed as nothing more. What is claimed is which measurable quantities carry the condition in built systems, and that they can be measured. What would change it: if the variables reduce to standard ones with nothing added under measurement, that law is a relabelling, and that is a dissolution condition.

A3. Scaling laws already describe how capability grows with resources.

Conceded, with a kill condition. Scaling empirics owns capability against external resources; the variable here is the system’s own re-entering output, and host 6 of Section 3.1 sets out what separates the field’s account of the maximum from an allocation account. What would change it: either result that moves the field into scaling empirics (the same host), the titration’s monotone returns showing no ceiling behaviour; Recursive Dynamics would then be scaling empirics under a new name, and this paper says so.

A4. Scalable oversight is already the field of overseers; this is a corner of it.

Partly conceded. Scalable oversight builds correctors and ladders of them, and its antecedent standing is conceded in Section 2. It has bounds of its own: scaling laws for the probability of successful oversight against the capability gap, with the conditions under which nested oversight succeeds (Engels et al. 2025), and a proof that, in a supervised setting, monitoring with vanishing error and query rates is possible exactly when the class of monitor functions has finite Littlestone dimension (Rudoler et al. 2026). In the works this paper reads, it carries no cap on correction leverage set by what the corrector is made of: their designs draw correctors from the overseen system’s own kind, with one exception on the record (Section 8). Its literature has begun to measure how alike different models’ mistakes are and what that does when one model judges another (Kim et al. 2025; Goel et al. 2025). Law III, the ARC Ceiling, is precisely a claim about that variable; the eclipse measurement tests its mechanism, whether the corrector’s class matters, and not the value of its cap (Section 11). What would change it: a bound on corrector capacity derived inside the oversight literature that this field merely renames.

A5. The literatures on self-training degradation and on models trained on their own outputs already measure drift.

Partly conceded, and the paper narrows as it said it would. That naive recursion can amplify error without bound, while retaining the original data keeps it bounded, is established for training dynamics by the two works Section 2 cites for it (Shumailov et al. 2024; Gerstgrasser et al. 2024). That literature carries both rates, owns the drift variable’s measurement in these settings, and has begun to vary what the corrector is made of: Feng et al. (2024) show that verifiers, even imperfect ones, can prevent model collapse and, comparing ground truth, a stronger model and the generator itself as selectors of generated data, that “a stronger model is not automatically a better selector”. None of these works, in the parts this paper reads, states how correction scales with capability, or a cap set by the corrector’s class, so the field’s contribution here narrows to that cap and its coupling to a growth exponent in depth. What would change it: a result in that literature that states how correction scales with capability, or a cap set by what the corrector is made of.

A6. The intelligence-explosion literature has asked these questions since 1965.

Conceded, and recorded as a debt in Section 2. The question is inherited from its named owners; the field sets out the missing state variables and the instruments, not the question. What would change it: nothing; the concession is permanent.

A7. Evolution is the original recursive improver and evolutionary dynamics already has its theory.

Partly conceded. Evolutionary dynamics has variation and selection; it has no designer directing reinvestment into the improvement process itself and no correction budget in the sense used here. The field’s claim to generality includes evolved systems only where a corrector exists, and Section 15.2 says what falls outside. What would change it: a treatment of designed reinvestment and correction leverage already standard in evolutionary theory.

A8. Statistical physics already has a theory of scaling and universality; this is renormalisation with new names.

Partly conceded. Universality classes and scaling collapse are the right ancestors for the field’s substrate-independence hope, and the functional-equation machinery is conceded as machinery. What that tradition does not carry is a correction variable, a drift variable, or a corrector whose composition is the lever. What would change it: a universality argument that derives the corrector-class distinction, or its absence, from first principles, which would be a result this field would adopt gratefully.

A9. AI safety as a field already covers this.

Rejected. In the safety literature this paper reads, AI safety is a problem area, not a science of a loop: it draws on the neighbours above and takes none of their objects as its own. This field is a candidate for one of the sciences safety would draw on, in the way thermodynamics is a science engineering draws on. What would change it: a published set of state variables and laws for self-improvement, with deciders, already standing under the safety banner.

A10. This is systems theory, rebranded.

Rejected. Systems theory carries no correction budget, no class cap on the corrector, and no preregistered decider; a rebranding does not carry kill conditions. What would change it: a systems-theoretic treatment of a corrector’s composition class that this field merely renames.

A11. The demarcation never tried the fit; it dismissed each neighbour with a sentence.

Conceded for every version of this paper before 2.3, and answered by Section 3.1, which attempts to house the questions in twelve candidate fields in their own variables, concedes Law I, the ARC Principle, to endogenous growth theory, the form of the ARC Ceiling to queueing theory and the shape of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, to error-threshold theory, prints the dissolution map by host, and isolates the one coupling no host can state. What would change it: a host that can state the class-dependent cap coupled to the depth exponent in its own variables, which would end the field by adoption rather than by refutation.

A12. Recursive Dynamics is the ARC Theory renamed: the same laws, the same author, a larger word.

Rejected, with a test. Conceded that the field and its founding theory share an author, a date and a record. Rejected that they are the same object. The theory is the answers; the field is the questions, the five state variables and the instruments that would refute those answers, and it is defined so that a reader who rejects all five laws and keeps the measurement is inside it, by the minimal commitment of Section 15.2. What would change it: the test is separability, and the dissolution map of Section 3.1 is where to run it: if no question, variable or instrument survives the deletion of the laws, the objection is right, the field is a rebadging, and this paper will say so.

B. Standing: the field has no results

B1. Thermodynamics had many hands and a century; this has one person and zero replications.

Conceded in full, before any reader could raise it. The parallel is in shape only; standing is not claimed, and Section 15.1 says what would earn it.

B2. The laws are not laws; they are conjectures from a minimal model.

Partly conceded. Each is a law under test: law is the field’s name for a relation it puts to the test, the status travels with the name on every surface, and no law is called supported until a registered verdict says so. That Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, and Law III, the ARC Ceiling, are proved only inside minimal models is conceded (Sections 5 and 6).

B3. The programme’s own headline number was retracted; why trust the rest?

Conceded as fact, rejected as inference. The retraction is printed wherever the number once stood, and the discipline that produced it (blinding, registered kill conditions, public correction within the hour of finding) is the reason the remaining claims carry statuses rather than assertions. A programme that never retracts has either never been wrong or never checked. What would change it: a retraction concealed rather than printed, anywhere in the record.

B4. No measurement of the correction leverage γ\gamma, as this paper defines it, is known; the ARC Ceiling’s number is therefore a guess.

Conceded as far as the record reaches: the programme has not measured γ\gamma, the exponent of correction capacity in capability (Assumption 2), and knows of no measurement of it. The nearest it knows is of another quantity. Song et al. (2024) define the generation-verification gap as the gain in performance when a generator’s outputs are reweighted by a verifier’s score; in base models of several families on two mathematical benchmarks, they find that a relative form of a model’s gap on its own outputs rises monotonically with pre-training compute under some verification methods, while the absolute gap shows no such trend, and that, with generator and verifier varied separately, the gap grows with the verifier’s compute and shrinks with the generator’s. That relates a gain from correction to a resource; it is not an exponent of correction capacity in capability read on a ratio scale, so it does not measure γ\gamma, which is the field’s single most consequential open measurement (Section 11). The number two is therefore no stronger than its premise (Section 5). What would change it: a measurement of γ\gamma, which the eclipse measurement does not make (Section 11).

B5. The derivation’s treatment of depth has been criticised in review. One form of that criticism: correction service may depend on depth directly, and not only through capability.

Conceded and printed as a limitation (Section 6): with that dependence the crossover becomes (1+η)/(1−γ)(1+\eta)/(1-\gamma), a rival the registration names. What would change it: a treatment that survives the criticism, which is wanted and invited.

B6. Carnot demonstrated his bound inside the memoir (Carnot 1824); the ARC Ceiling is a conditional model result whose key premise is unproved.

Conceded, and the difference is exactly why the parallel is confined to shape. The ARC Ceiling’s premise is measurable and registered. What would change it: nothing about the history; only the measurement.

C. Method: the instruments may not measure what the laws name

C1. The five state variables are arbitrary; why not four, or nine?

Conceded as open, as Section 4 says; the notation register’s collision entries already distinguish the letters the programme has reused. What would change it: a preregistered study in which one variable reduces to another under measurement, or a relation that cannot be stated without a sixth.

C2. The construct bridge is open: the quantities the studies measure may not be the objects the laws’ symbols name.

Conceded and held open in the register by name. What would change it: the bridge closing in either direction, which is itself a preregistered finding.

C3. Substrate independence is just the flexibility of the fitted families.

Conceded as the live risk. The fourth-cell test exists to catch exactly this. What would change it: the fourth cell, run.

C4. Models scoring models is circular: the scorer shares the blind spots of the scored.

Partly conceded, for the reasons Section 10 gives: the blinding hides provenance and not the scorer’s class, and the circularity is reduced and measured, not abolished. What would change it: a validation result showing model panels and the human criterion diverge systematically, which would demote every panel-scored result to exploratory.

C5. The capability ladder is itself unvalidated; a wrong ruler makes every law wrong.

Conceded as a dependency. The ladder is calibrated and its scale discipline is a founding principle precisely because the programme once got it wrong; but a calibrated instrument is not a validated one until independent hands use it. What would change it: replication of ladder measurements by other hands.

C6. The author-rated fallback for the human criterion is an author grading his own theory.

Conceded, with the safeguards printed: blind, laundered, washout-retested, disclosed as author-rated in every headline, and preregistered as a fallback rather than a design. What would change it: external raters, which the design names as the preferred path the moment they exist.

C7. The preregistered experiments are underpowered or too small to decide anything.

Decided by experiment, for what the studies can decide: under the theory registration two of the three decide no registered proposition whatever their power, and their power bears on this paper’s own kill conditions (Sections 1.1 and 14). Power is computed in the designs from pilot dispersion, and an inconclusive verdict is a preregistered outcome with its own branch, not a failure to be hidden. What would change it: an inconclusive verdict, which would narrow the claim to “not yet decidable at this scale” and say so.

C8. Frontier models change faster than the experiments can run; results will be obsolete on arrival.

Partly conceded. A law of the loop is meant to hold across models; if a measured exponent moves with every model generation, that is itself evidence against substrate independence and would be reported as such. What would change it: the follow-up grid across families, which locates whether the effect is a property of the loop or of a generation.

C9. Behaviour-scored instruments cannot certify a system after it rewrites itself.

Conceded as a limit of every instrument that reads outputs: a formal result holds that static black-box probing cannot distinguish a model that stays robust after an update from one that conceals behaviour a single update can release (Bakman et al. 2026). The field’s verdicts are about measured behaviour under their protocols, never certificates of alignment after an update. What would change it: an instrument that reads more than behaviour, validated against planted concealment.

D. Naming and standing of the author

D1. Naming a discipline before the evidence is a marketing move.

Rejected, with the precedent printed: cybernetics was named in the 1948 book that proposed it (Wiener 1948) and exobiology in 1960 (Lederberg 1960) before a single specimen. A name makes a new field citable and attackable as one unit. The safeguard is the name’s own kill condition, printed in Section 14: the name dies with its object, not with any one law’s stated form, and the record says which death it was, in public.

D2. One person cannot found a paradigm; paradigms are conferred by communities.

Conceded. What is claimed is a candidate: a way of organising the questions. Whether it becomes anyone’s paradigm is decided by adoption and replication, which are two of the entry criteria, and neither is the author’s to declare. This paper withholds the word paradigm as a description of the present.

D3. The author has no institution, no doctorate and no co-authors.

Conceded as fact; rejected as an argument about the field. Every claim is tied to its dated record, every number to its source and every instrument to the test that could kill it, so that nothing here requires trusting the author. Independence has one advantage that is also printed: there is no institution to protect, so corrections publish the day they are found. What would change it: nothing; the work is judged by its record.

D5. The phrase recursive dynamics is already used in robotics.

Conceded as a fact about another sense of the words: the Featherstone tradition’s recursive dynamics algorithms (Featherstone 2008) are a family of computational methods, a different referent, and that use is no prior claim on the name of this field. The author’s own use of the phrase stands in his sealed record of 8 December 2024, and he made it the name of the field on 27 August 2026. Nor is this recursion theory, the mathematics of computability.

E. Scope: too broad, or too narrow

E1. Too broad: any self-improving system includes science itself, economies, evolution, so the field is either everything or nothing.

Partly conceded. The field’s laws switch on where one narrower thing exists: a corrector, something detecting and repairing its own errors while the system grows. A star has dynamics and no drift ledger; nothing there corrects its own growth. Section 15.2 draws the boundary. What would change it: the laws holding only for one substrate, which would make the field narrow rather than everything, and honest about it.

E2. Too narrow: this is really about language-model loops, dressed as a general science.

Partly conceded. The experiments run on language-model loops because those are the loops that exist to be measured; the substrate-independence claim is at exploratory grade and its fourth-cell test is preregistered by timestamp and awaits submission. What would change it: the cross-domain families failing the fourth cell, after which the field would say it is a science of one substrate until shown otherwise.

E3. The field conflates correction with alignment, and capability with intelligence.

Conceded as a risk and answered with definitions: correction is the detection and repair of the system’s own errors against a stated criterion, which may or may not be an alignment criterion; capability is whatever the calibrated ladder measures. The field makes no claim about intelligence as such. What would change it: a demonstration that the ladder measures something other than what the laws need it to measure, which is C5 again.

F. Timing and community

F1. It is premature; wait for the data, then name the field.

Rejected. The studies drafted for the laws are preregistered by timestamp; the instruments exist; the name lets the field be attacked as one unit before the data arrive, which is when attack is cheapest and most useful. The name carries its own kill condition for exactly this reason.

F2. A field needs a community first; a field of one is a hobby.

Conceded as to the definition of a science; rejected as to the definition of a field at its founding. Section 7 scores condition 6 as not met. The founding paper exists so that a community can form around a testable object, or decline to.

F3. Naming it invites priority disputes rather than science.

Rejected. Every antecedent this paper has found is conceded by name in Section 2; what the field claims as its own is the assembly, and the assembly is dated. A dispute about priority is for the dated record, which is there to be checked.

G. The concept itself

G1. The crossover αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma) is a triviality of power-law algebra, not a law.

Partly conceded. The algebra is elementary, here and in the comparison that Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, states, and is printed so that nobody need take it on trust. The content is not the algebra; it is the claim that the two premises describe real loops and that γ\gamma is capped for same-class correctors. What would change it: nothing about the algebra; the premises are what the experiments test.

G2. The same-class cap at one half is an assumption dressed as a result.

Conceded, and named by the programme as the single most likely point of failure. It carries a hard kill, P6’s refuter (Section 1.1), which needs a measurement of γ\gamma; the eclipse measurement tests the class lever, not the cap’s value (Section 11). What would change it: a measurement of γ\gamma.

G3. The model assumes stationarity and separability of drift and correction; real loops have neither.

Partly conceded. The minimal model is minimal on purpose; the preregistered analyses fit drift and correction jointly and report the interval, and non-separability would show as an unfit rather than being averaged away. What would change it: a result of a preregistered study in which drift and correction cannot be fitted jointly, which would be reported as a limit of the model.

G4. Capability is multidimensional; a scalar ladder throws the structure away.

Conceded as a simplification. The ladder is one axis chosen for measurability; the field’s claim is that the laws hold along it, not that it exhausts capability. What would change it: the laws holding on one axis and failing on another, which would be a finding about the field’s scope.

G5. The ceiling ignores economics: a lab can buy correction with money, not just with a better corrector class.

Partly conceded. Buying correction at a fixed spend raises the coefficient aa in the derivation, which moves the depth at which burden first exceeds capacity but not the crossover exponent. Spending that grows with capability buys channels, and the premise of Section 6 needs their number to grow in proportion to capability: correlated channels bought faster add little, but independent channels bought faster, or channels whose quality rises, would raise γ\gamma, and that is part of what the premise stakes. What would change it: a demonstration that purchased correction changes the exponent rather than the coefficient, which would refute the premise’s conditions for one half.

G6. The dissolution clause is cheap talk; nobody publishes their own field’s ending.

Rejected on the record. The programme has already retracted its headline number, withdrawn a formulation of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, and demoted a favourable result to a pilot within thirteen minutes, all in public; that demotion was reversed on 7 September 2026 under the attestation-class rule, as Section 9 records. The commitment to publish the field’s ending is made by a record that has already published smaller endings.

G7. Because every claim is conditional, nothing is actually claimed.

Rejected, and answered up front in Section 1.1 so that the concessions below cannot be mistaken for silence. Conditionals with measurable antecedents are claims: they forbid outcomes. The field forbids a stable system holding its growth exponent above the ceiling for a sustained run, a same-class correction exponent measured wholly above one half, a corrector-class contrast that runs the other way or falls wholly below the preregistered support threshold, and monotone returns with no interior maximum as reinvestment rises. Any of those would be a real result against it, and Section 1.1 states which of them make the field wrong and which removes the one-half cap and the value two, on the relative rung, while the ceiling relation stands.

G8. Depth is not time, and the laws confuse them.

Conceded as a hazard and answered by the register: every quantitative surface names its model family, and the depth studies treat depth as the count of re-entries at a fixed wall-clock policy. What would change it: a result of a preregistered study in which the exponent depends on the clock rather than the count, which would be reported as a limit of the variable.

G9. The tie regime is undecided, so the bound decides nothing near the boundary.

Conceded, and printed where the tie arises, at α(1−γ)\alpha(1-\gamma) equal to one (Section 6). The bound is a statement about exponents, and near equality it hands over to the coefficients by design.

G10. Correction capacity tracks effort, not capability.

Partly conceded: an official report finds that more capable models do not necessarily have better safeguards and that their strength appears to be set mostly by the effort and resource invested in defences (UK AI Security Institute 2025). Assumption 2 is a claim about how capacity scales with capability at a given investment; investment that grows with capability enters through the premise’s channel count (Section 6). What would change it: capacity measured to vary with investment and not at all with capability at matched investment.

G11. Capability does not always grow with depth.

Conceded as a scope condition: extending reasoning length has been found to lower accuracy on constructed tasks (Gema et al. 2025). Section 6 assumes growth over the range read, and Law I, the ARC Principle, is registered against named rival forms. What would change it: decline with depth in the settings the studies scope, which the functional-form comparison would report.

G12. The correlation that caps γ\gamma may itself rise with capability.

Conceded, and it points the premise’s way: model mistakes have been found to grow more similar as capability rises (Goel et al. 2025), and on Section 6’s account rising correlation lowers γ\gamma as the system grows. What would change it: correlation among a same-class corrector’s channels measured flat or falling with capability.

H. Practice, policy and the height of the bar

H1. Cross-class correction is already practice: ensembles, verifiers, humans in the loop.

Conceded as practice, rejected as theory. No measurement known to the programme shows whether same-class ladders cap at a correction leverage of one half, or whether a different class escapes; the field turns a habit into a specification with a number. What would change it: a published measurement of the class cap from inside the practice.

H2. If it is true it is trivial.

Rejected. The predictions are risky and specific: a class-dependent ceiling on correction leverage at one half; an interior maximum of the growth exponent as reinvestment rises; a ratio pinned at one by construction that the field says will move. None is trivially true and each can fail.

H3. It cannot be shown wrong.

Rejected. Four dissolution results are printed in Section 14, each tied to a preregistered study, with hard kills and rival-favouring nulls, and the field’s name carries its own death clause.

H4. Policy relevance is premature; talking about regulators is safety-washing.

Conceded on timing. Policy weight follows results, never formulation; Section 13 is written in the conditional for that reason.

H5. Even if all five laws hold in language models, that is AI engineering, not a field.

Conceded as the demanding bar, and it is the right bar. A field needs the substrate crossing: the same exponent structure in at least one non-artificial system. That is why the fourth-cell test is part of the founding conjunction in Section 11.1 and not an optional extra.

I. What is not answered here

I1. There may be objections this list has missed.

Conceded, by construction. The list is published so that it can be extended, and every objection added from outside will be printed with its disposition in the next version, under the standing invitation that ends this paper.

13 What this buys, if it holds

Fields earn adoption when strangers can compute with them. If the laws survive their registered tests, the following become calculations rather than debates: given a measured γ\gamma for a corrector class, the maximum sustainable growth exponent of any system it oversees follows from the ARC Ceiling; given measured drift, the required correction rate follows from Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; oversight architectures acquire a specification sheet (the corrector’s composition class, its measured γ\gamma, its implied ceiling) the way engines acquired efficiency ratings; and the safety question “can oversight keep up” becomes a comparison of γ\gamma measured for each corrector class rather than a scenario argument. Everything in this paragraph is conditional on results that do not yet exist, and is written in the conditional for that reason.

13.1 And if it fails

The negative result is also a contribution, and it is one the oversight programme needs. A ratio of 1.00 holding tight everywhere retires the class distinction on which the case against same-class ladders rests, though it is not a finding about the cap. A universal absence of any regime where correction out-scales drift says self-correction never wins, which is a different and worse world that policy should know about. The designs commit to publishing either outcome under the same title. A field whose failure is informative is not a vanity project.

14 What would dissolve the field

If the correction-versus-drift race finds no regime where correction out-scales drift in the systems and settings it covers, Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, has no subject matter there, and the record says how far that reaches. If the corrector-class contrast runs the other way, or falls wholly below the preregistered support threshold wherever the cap should bind, the field’s distinctive mechanism is dead and its remains return to the hosts named in the dissolution map of Section 3.1. The trial’s own operating characteristics decide which observations are available: on a roster whose targets genuinely differ it returns an inconclusive verdict rather than an affirmative equivalence, so a null is a failed attempt to confirm and the dissolution reading rests on the directional and below-threshold outcomes named here. If the titration finds no interior maximum and no ceiling behaviour, the central impossibility dissolves. Under the theory registration a monotone profile refutes P2 and leaves P3, P16 and P20, through which Law III, the ARC Ceiling, takes its word, standing and testable; this paper’s kill is its own, and it is kept. If the cross-domain families fail the fourth-cell test, substrate independence was an artefact of family flexibility. Any one death wounds the field, and this paper commits to publishing each under this title.

A name given before its tests must be able to die with them, and the record must be able to say which death it was. A law failing in its stated form kills that law and the theory that stated it; the field carries the corrected form, or none, as the record allows, and a reader who rejects all five laws and keeps the measurement is still inside it, by the minimal commitment of Section 15.2. The name itself dies when the object goes: if the corrector-class contrast fails in either way named above, the residue no host could state has proved empty, every piece returns to its host, and this paper will say so under this title rather than quietly disappear. The trial samples six model families; the theory registration treats family as a proxy for how far correctors fail together. If the laws survive their registered tests and hold outside the domains that raised them, the name has earned its generality. Either way the record decides, not the naming.

15 What this paper does and does not show

The paper founds the field: it sets out the object, the variables, the laws as named conjectures under test, one bound derived inside a stated model, and the instruments, those through which Laws I to III take their word still design drafts, with three related studies preregistered by timestamp. It confirms no law. None of the three studies has run, because none runs before its registration is public. Of the four study units registered on OSF, two have run, and one, Unit 83, has returned a verdict on a proposition a law carries, by the programme’s own run (Section 11). What would have to happen before the field could be called a science is set out first, and what the field is not, with the minimal commitment, second.

15.2 What the field is not, and the minimal commitment

The field is not a forecast of when machines surpass people, not a theory of intelligence, not an ethics, not a theory of everything, and not a claim about consciousness. Its laws switch on where a corrector exists inside a growing system and switch off where none does; the composed hierarchies that its scaling mathematics bears on (stars included) are graded as correspondence of form and no further. The programme’s speculative coda on recursion at cosmological scale is separable and sits outside the field; a reader can reject it entirely and lose nothing here.

The minimal commitment required to work in the field is small and stated so that it can be refused: that the self-improvement loop is a legitimate object of measurement, with quantities that can be defined, instruments that can measure them, and relations that can be shown wrong. A reader who accepts that and rejects all five laws is inside the field; a reader who accepts the laws and refuses measurement is not.

16 Limitations

The paper prints its limitations where they arise; they are gathered here plainly, each with the place it is argued.

  • The programme has not measured the correction leverage γ\gamma and knows of no measurement of it as this paper defines it, so the value two rests on a premise the programme names as the conjecture’s most likely point of failure (Section 6; objections B4 and G2). The value two is the relative rung; counted over an exposure that grows with capability, the same assumptions give two thirds (Section 6).

  • The bound of Section 6 is a conditional crossover inside a minimal model: it does not establish bounded harm, finite-horizon safety or a law of nature, and its treatment of depth has been criticised in review (objection B5).

  • When models score models, the scorer can share the blind spots of the scored; the circularity is reduced, disclosed and measured, not abolished (Section 10; objection C4).

  • Whether five state variables suffice, and whether the quantities the studies measure are the objects the laws name, are open questions (objections C1 and C2); the capability ladder is calibrated but not yet validated by other hands (objection C5).

  • The minimal model assumes stationarity and separability of drift and correction, constant exponents, no direct dependence of correction on depth, and capability on one scalar axis (Section 6; objections B5, G3 and G4); its Assumption 1, burden in proportion to capability gained, is unmeasured, and on one power trajectory it cannot be told apart from burden following a power of capability itself.

  • Substrate independence stands at exploratory grade, and the fourth-cell test that would catch flexible fitting has not run (Section 9; objection C3).

  • The field has one author, no replication by other hands and no use by strangers (Section 15.1; objections B1, D2 and D3); Section 15 gives where its tests stand.

17 Conclusion

Until the entry criteria are met, one sentence carries the field’s whole status: Recursive Dynamics is a new field at the Carnot stage: one memoir in, instruments built, the instruments through which Laws I to III take their word still design drafts, three related studies preregistered by timestamp and not yet run, one registered counted test run to a verdict by the programme’s own run, and the whole staked in public where failure will be as visible as success.

The invitation that follows from everything above is the only one the field can extend: run the test that could kill it. Every instrument, seed, schedule and threshold is written down for that purpose. The designs of those studies are prepared as draft registrations awaiting human submission, and each becomes public with its registration. An independent run outranks the programme’s own.
