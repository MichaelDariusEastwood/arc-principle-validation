# Recursive Dynamics: what limits any system that improves itself, whatever it is made of?

**Version:** 3.6
**Revised:** 17 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-08-31
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 3.6):

> What limits a system that improves itself using its own outputs, whatever it is made of? This paper founds Recursive Dynamics, the science of that question. Its object is the self-improvement loop, described by five state variables: capability, recursive depth, correction rate, drift and correction leverage. The field carries the five laws of the ARC Theory, each a law under test. Law I, the ARC Principle, $U = I \times R^{\alpha}$, is the foundation; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, reads $\beta_C > k$; Law III, the ARC Ceiling, reads $\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma)$. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, complete the five. The paper derives one bound. In a model of two assumptions, correction keeps pace with the burden that growth creates only while the growth exponent stays below $1/(1-\gamma)$, where $\gamma$ is how correction capacity scales with capability. For a corrector of the system’s own class, a premise the programme names as the conjecture’s most likely point of failure caps $\gamma$ at one half. The ceiling is then at most two on the relative rung, the ratio of burden to correction capacity, and two thirds once burden is counted over an exposure that grows with capability. The paper states four outcomes that would make the field wrong and four results that would dissolve it, tries to house its questions in twelve neighbouring fields, and answers fifty-five objections. No law is decided yet: the instruments through which Laws I to III take their word are design drafts, and three related studies are preregistered by timestamp and not yet run. One registered test has returned a verdict: the placement limb of Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, P21, is supported on the performance axis only, under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not evaluable and one disclosed recovery amendment.

From The ARC Theory (version 7.10), on the status of Laws IV and V:

> Laws IV and V (the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law) were added on 24 September 2026 and registered as laws on 15 October 2026 in version 1.103, each a law under test; their mathematics, set out at version 1.2 of 29 October 2026, is in development.
