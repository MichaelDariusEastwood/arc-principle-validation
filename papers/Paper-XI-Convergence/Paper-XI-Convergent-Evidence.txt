                                                         Abstract
           I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it
       reasons; only proof will move the world. This paper asks how later work in separate fields bears on
       the ARC Theory’s conjectured links between recursive capability, the limits of external control and
       correction embedded in a system. It presents a selected retrospective register beside the author’s dated
       records over a 21-month window from 8 December 2024 to 1 September 2026. The contribution is
       a source trail, a record of revised comparisons and a separation of questions that require different
       measurements. The retained historical table and a frozen graded-register snapshot identify available
       sources and unresolved cells. They supply no aggregate evidential verdict: no fixed search population,
       disconfirming denominator or chance model is available. One dependent lineage connects a paper
       on diversity among machine agents (Hernández-Espinosa et al. 2026) with version 1 of a formal-
       verification preprint (Gumbau Mezquita 2026a); the text distinguishes that historical version from its
       revised version 4. The human parallel is argued separately in Paper C. The oversight comparison
       is equally specific: Unit 01, a design preregistered by timestamp, measures a matched difference in
       residual-error decay at fixed capability; it measures neither correction-capacity scaling nor a ceiling.
       P6’s same-class bound remains a conjecture subject to its registered domain and conditions. Section 6
       states the requirements for a later audit. The register bears on Law I of the ARC Theory and, as
       context only, on Law V; it tests neither.

1    Introduction
I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons;
only proof will move the world. If control of a capable AI system were lost and could not be won back,
what the system itself carries would decide what it does. The programme’s position is that people keep
oversight, containment and shutdown for as long as they physically can, and never give them up by choice;
correction built into a system is the contingency for control lost against our will, not a reason to let go of it.
The programme states the principle behind that contingency in three parts: above a threshold, recursive
self-correction produces super-linear capability gains; external alignment constraints are structurally
insufficient to contain it; and the corrective must be embedded, not imposed. This paper asks whether
later work in separate fields bears on those parts. In plain words, its answer is a record, not a verdict. It
sets later work by others against the author’s dated records, the earliest a manuscript of 8 December 2024
(Eastwood 2024), row by row, with dates, source availability and the part of the question each comparison
bears on. The rows bear on different parts; no row, and not the set, establishes the three together; no total
is claimed; and no aggregate evidential verdict follows. A researcher choosing an oversight design can
use the comparisons to distinguish residual-error reduction, corrective capacity and formal verification
guarantees.
     This paper belongs to the ARC Theory (the Theory of Artificial Recursive Creation), the founding
theory of Recursive Dynamics, the science of systems that improve themselves, which the author founded
(the founding paper). The theory is carried by five laws, each a law under test: Law I, the ARC Principle,
that usable capability is a power of recursive depth, with the exponent measured rather than assumed;
Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; Law III, the ARC Ceiling; and Laws IV and V, the ARC Persistence
Law and the ARC Embedding Law, added on 24 September 2026 and registered as laws on 15 October
2026, their mathematics in development. The laws and twenty-two propositions are registered on OSF (the
theory registration), and the statement paper states the theory whole. This paper is a register bearing on
Law I and, as context only, on Law V; it tests neither, and Section 9 states what it does and does not show.
     The register is a selected retrospective record. It preserves a table of earlier comparisons beside a
graded-register snapshot and its disclosed dependence groups (Section 4). The record can be inspected
source by source; it does not provide a search denominator or establish causal independence. Section 6
states what a later audit would need. A further comparison appears in Section II.A.8 of the Eden Protocol’s
philosophical statement.
     Our contributions are:
    1. A dated register of later work by others in separate fields, with dates, source availability and
       the graded register’s evidence classes and disclosed relationships, with unresolved source cells
       identified (Section 4).
    2. The retained snapshot of 2 July 2026, kept whole as the paper’s dated record with its superseded
       classes, and the rule that credits priority outward wherever the dates demand it (Section 4.2).
    3. A plain statement of one dependent chain, from the manuscript of 8 December 2024 to a peer-
       reviewed paper and a preprint that credits it, graded as context, not confirmation, with the human
       parallel argued separately in Paper C (Section 5).
    4. Row-level checks and the requirements for a future audit, including a fixed population, dependence
       assessment, a disconfirming search and a comparison model (Section 6).
    5. The distinction between P6’s conditional, conjectural bound and Unit 01’s current residual-decay
       contrast, with a separately specified hypothesis about human input (Section 7).

2     Related work
2.1   The rival instrument
The work that sits closest to this programme’s question, and so the paper this one should be weighed
against, is Engels et al. (2025), “Scaling Laws For Scalable Oversight”, arXiv:2504.18530, first posted
25 April 2025 at 17:54:27 UTC (a time taken from arXiv’s record alone, arXiv Atom; a NeurIPS 2025
Spotlight Poster, confirmed on 15 August 2026 against the NeurIPS virtual page for poster 115536, as
recorded in the statement paper’s reference dossier). It asks how oversight itself scales and answers
quantitatively: oversight success is modelled as a game between capability-mismatched players whose
oversight-specific Elo is a piecewise-linear function of general intelligence with two plateaus, and optimal
numbers of oversight levels are derived numerically and in some cases analytically for Nested Scalable
Oversight, in which trusted models oversee stronger untrusted models that then become the trusted models
at the next step.
     The measurable distinction is between the questions and their instruments. Engels et al. (2025)
already states a slope condition: oversight improves when the overseer’s game-specific Elo rises faster
with general Elo than the overseen system’s (𝑚 𝐺 > 𝑚 𝐻 ). It recommends iterating a design until that
condition holds. A composition label is not a variable in that model. The ARC programme asks
whether a frozen corrector-class relationship is associated with a different residual-decay exponent under a
matched protocol, and separately whether corrective capacity keeps pace with capability. Neither question
establishes a universal cap on Nested Scalable Oversight. P6 of the theory registration is a conjecture
with an admitted population and scale; its correction-capacity exponent is distinct both from Law II’s 𝛽𝐶
and from Unit 01’s residual-decay exponent (Section 7.2).

2.2   Antecedents and near-misses
On the question. Hutter asked directly whether intelligence can explode (“Can Intelligence Explode?”,
Hutter 2012, arXiv, 28 February 2012, read at its source), separating “speed from intelligence explosion”
and undertaking to “consider possible bounds on intelligence”, augmenting the analysis of Chalmers
(2010). The question itself goes back to Good (1966), and the separation of speed from intelligence
explosion is at least fourteen years old. These antecedents establish that the question is older; this selected
comparison is not a complete novelty search. Growth exponents of the form the programme uses are
older (Jones 1995).
     On impossibility. Three 2025 arXiv papers argue for limits on alignment or its control: Yao (2025b),
“The Alignment Trap: Complexity Barriers” (arXiv:2506.10304, v1 12 June 2025 02:30:30 UTC, a
time taken from arXiv’s record alone, independently observed by the Internet Archive on 13 June
2025; cited by Spizzirri (2025), arXiv:2512.03048); Yao (2025a), “On the Mathematical Impossibility
of Safe Universal Approximators” (arXiv:2507.03031, 3 July 2025, whose abstract uses the phrase
“irreducible uncontrollability”); and Ball et al. (2025), “On the Impossibility of Separating Intelligence
from Judgment” (arXiv:2507.07341, 9 July 2025). All three are worst-case and qualitative: measure
zero, coNP-completeness, cryptographic hardness. None reports an average-case scaling exponent or a
rate. The first argues that the set of safe policies has measure zero and that verifying a policy’s safety is

coNP-complete; the second, that perfect, reliable control of a universal approximator is a mathematical
impossibility; the third, under cryptographic hardness assumptions, that some large language models
admit no efficient prompt filter and that output filtering is intractable in a natural setting. The third
concludes that “safety cannot be achieved by designing filters external to the LLM internals (architecture
and weights)” and argues that “an aligned AI system’s intelligence cannot be separated from its judgment”;
that argument from filtering intractability is consistent with this programme’s architecture dependence,
though it does not test it. Two of the three papers are by one sole author; to call them a wave would
overstate the literature’s breadth, though not the seriousness of the six-author paper.
    On model similarity and oversight. Goel et al. (2025) is a direct predecessor for the error-
complementarity question. Its Chance Adjusted Probabilistic Agreement measures overlap in model
mistakes beyond a chance baseline. It reports judge preference for similar models, a role for complement-
ary knowledge in weak-to-strong generalisation, and increasing similarity of mistakes with capability.
Unit 01’s draft instead targets a matched residual-decay contrast over repeated revisions. That difference in
estimand supplies a comparison to test; it does not make the general connection between model similarity
and oversight new.
    The nearest structural analogue. The quantum error-correction threshold result (Aharonov and Ben-Or
2008) concerns reliable computation under a specified local noise model, including exponentially decaying
correlations. Its critical physical noise rate is not a correction-capacity elasticity. The comparison is
methodological, with the axes kept separate (Section 7.3).
    On feedback and stability (classical). The proposition that insufficient corrective gain can cause
instability has a long control-theory lineage, including the small-gain analysis of Zames (1966). An
exponent comparison across capability is a distinct operationalisation under test, not a claim to have
originated the general stability question. Its measurement and validity remain to be established for the
systems it is intended to describe.
    The nearest quantitative neighbour. Liu and Meng (2026), “Self-Correction as Feedback Control:
Error Dynamics, Stability Thresholds, and Prompt Interventions in LLMs”, arXiv:2604.22273 (a single
source, the abstract read via the paper’s own html page, 12 August 2026), recasts self-correction as a
closed-loop control problem via a two-state Markov model parameterised by an Error Introduction Rate
and an Error Correction Rate, and derives a directly measurable stability threshold: iterate only when
ECR/EIR exceeds Acc/(1 − Acc). The differential, stated wherever it is cited: theirs is a per-step rate
threshold at a fixed capability level, deciding whether another iteration helps now; this programme’s
criterion is a scaling relation across capability, deciding whether corrective strength keeps pace as the
system improves. Theirs has no scaling exponent on the corrector and no capability-growth exponent, the
two quantities the programme’s co-scaling criterion compares, and no architecture or composition-class
term; the two models meet at zero growth, as Paper X shows. Two adjacent empirical results from the
same search, each read at its source: Li (2025) (arXiv:2601.00828, first posted on 24 December 2025)
reports an accuracy-correction paradox, weaker models achieving materially higher intrinsic correction
rates than stronger ones, per-step evidence directionally adjacent to the same-class cap and never to be
quoted as an exponent measurement; and Tsui (2025) (arXiv:2507.02778, Self-Correction Bench, first
posted on 3 July 2025) reports a systematic self-correction blind spot across open models.

3     Method
This is a selected retrospective register of works encountered across the domains named in Table 1. The
edition has no reproducible search log, fixed search population, complete inclusion and exclusion record,
or disconfirming denominator. Its rows therefore support comparisons between named documents; they
do not estimate how often the pattern being considered occurs in the literature.
     For a comparison to be checkable, the record needs the author’s dated passage, the outside passage,
the exact source version, the relevant dates and their timestamp classes, and the relation claimed between
the passages. A related theme, a shared mathematical form and a test of the same proposition are different
relations. Section 4 retains the earlier table and identifies source cells that remain unresolved. The
machine-readable snapshot that accompanies this edition records the graded register exactly as it stood on
1 November 2026; it does not certify the completeness or accuracy of every row.
     The manuscript of 8 December 2024 and its accompanying records are the author’s dated source
bundle (Eastwood 2024). A document date, a message header, a repository timestamp and a Bitcoin
anchor carry different information (Appendix B). The absence of a citation to the author is not evidence of
causal independence. Shared citations, organisations or intellectual lineages can reveal dependence; their
absence from the available record cannot exclude it. The four disclosed groups are recorded relationships,
not the result of a completed independence audit. No edition-wide verification of all message hashes,
affiliations, funding relationships or earliest public disclosures is claimed here.

5     The dependent chain and the closed loop
5.1   The chain, stated plainly
The manuscript dated 8 December 2024 records the author’s control-failure question and embedded-
correction direction (Eastwood 2024). A later paper discusses diversity among machine agents as a
response to limits on controllability (Hernández-Espinosa et al. 2026). Version 1 of a subsequent preprint

documents form a stated intellectual lineage. Their absence of a citation to the author does not establish
causal independence, and their formal claims are not identical to the manuscript’s directional claim.

5.2   Three degrees of neurodivergence
Degree 1: the researcher (Paper C, PNP). Michael Darius Eastwood. ADHD, diagnosed in adulthood;
autism, formally diagnosed in October 2026. Brain that connects domains uniform thinkers don’t see as
adjacent. Builds a framework across domains while representing himself in complex legal proceedings.
Paper C sets out the PNP (Polymathic Neurodivergent Profile) as a descriptive, non-diagnostic framework.
Clinical documentation exists and is held privately. The method is the hypothesis.

Degree 2: the protection argument (Hernández-Espinosa et al. 2026). The paper argues from
undecidability and irreducibility for limits on controllability and argues for diversity of reasoning among
machine agents as a contingent response. Its publisher record gives affiliations including Oxford Immune
Algorithmics, a University of Oxford spinout, King’s College London, the Alan Turing Institute and
the University of Tokyo. Its argument concerns machine agents. The parallel with people belongs to
Paper C; this is not a clinical finding about the author. This comparison uses the publisher’s abstract and
the accessible preprint, not the full text of the published article.

Degree 3: the historical formal argument (Gumbau Mezquita 2026a). Gumbau Mezquita’s version 1,

5.3    The closed loop
The expression “closed loop” describes how the author relates these records:
    1. Intellectual history. Paper C’s account of the author’s reasoning can explain why he asked the
       question. It does not measure the hypothesised recursive mechanism.
    2. Literature context. The register places named documents beside that question. Selection and
       dependence remain properties to investigate, not settled credentials of the pattern.
    3. Formal scope. The claims of Gumbau Mezquita (2026b) stand or fall under their mathematical
       assumptions. A thematic connection to the author’s manuscript neither proves those results nor
       transfers them to the ARC Theory.
    4. Traceability. Version-pinned links and dated records let a reader inspect the comparison. A file hash
       identifies a file’s exact contents; it does not establish the truth of a claim, the time of composition,
       or independent discovery.


6     Falsifiability
The present claims are comparisons between specified documents. A row fails if its source does not
say what is attributed to it, if its date or version is wrong, or if the stated relationship conflates different
quantities. Such a finding changes that row and any passage that relies on it. The register offers no
aggregate evidential verdict, so it has no aggregate rejection threshold to report.
    A later audit would need the following provisions before its results could be read as evidence about a
broader pattern:
    1. Source and date checks. Pin both passages and the source versions, distinguish their timestamp
       classes and resolve missing primary sources. An unlocated source stays unresolved.
    2. Dependence assessment. Fix criteria for shared intellectual lineage and documented exposure.
       Classify a traceable response to the author’s dissemination as engagement. Do not infer independence
       from a missing citation.
    3. A fixed audit population. Declare which records are eligible, how duplicate events are grouped, and
       how disagreements are adjudicated before scoring. Unit 58’s draft re-audit, first anchored on 12
       August 2026 at block 962066, is preregistered by timestamp and awaits submission; it does not
       supply a chance model for this paper’s selected register.
    4. Disconfirming cases and a comparison model. Define a search population, inclusion rules, a
       disconfirming search and the baseline against which any excess of matches would be measured.
       Those elements have not been supplied here. No count, rate or chance-adjusted significance follows
       from the present rows.
    5. Separate theory tests. Apply the theory registration’s own operational definitions and refuters to
       data collected under the relevant registered design. A literature resemblance does not decide a
       prediction about recursive capability or corrective capacity.
   The practical use of this list is to turn a promising comparison into a question that another researcher
could check. Until a suitable audit is specified and run, the register remains a selected historical record.

7     Framework predictions the convergence pattern points to
7.1   The eclipse
The oversight question is whether corrective capacity can keep pace with the system being corrected.
Proposition P6 of the theory registration, version 1.104, conjectures a correction exponent at or below
one half for same-class correctors. It is a conjecture, not a consequence of a shared substrate or of
statistical independence alone. The registration requires a prospectively frozen, versioned taxonomy and
an independently justified capability ratio scale. It separates scaling class, lineage, substrate, training
provenance, access to external ground truth and failure-mode dependence; no one axis substitutes for
another. The population remains blocked until the required taxonomy is fixed.
     The route to one half additionally assumes that corrective capacity is inverse residual uncertainty,
that correction channels are uncorrelated, that their number grows in proportion to capability, and that
their variance and quality are comparable. These assumptions require separate measurement. Neither
the register nor the material of a corrector establishes them. The registered P6 refuter requires a
valid exceedance of one half replicated on a second, disjoint fault set under the same settings and the
registration’s precision rules. Its outcomes are REFUTED, NO COUNTEREXAMPLE FOUND AMONG
N and INCONCLUSIVE; a finite survey cannot return support for this universal bound. Unit 82 tests
P17’s proxy for error dependence, not P6 or a ceiling value (Section 9.4).

mapping between those axes is established here, so this analogy supplies neither a proof of P6 nor a
blanket exemption from a properly specified counterexample.

7.4   A programme hypothesis from the same mechanism
The programme also asks whether adding human judgement can improve correction under a matched
budget and information set. Debate (Irving et al. 2018), amplification (Christiano et al. 2018), recursive
reward modelling (Leike et al. 2018) and reinforcement learning from human preferences (Christiano
et al. 2017) put human input in different places. Training-time preference labels are not an active human
controller at each correction step. A human participant’s different substrate alone establishes neither
complementary errors nor a higher exponent. The testable hypothesis is that measured complementary
error structure predicts better correction under a specified protocol; a human-versus-model contrast would
need its own frozen taxonomy, outcomes and matched arms. No such comparison is measured by this
register.


8     What this means
For the programme. The register locates questions worth separating: how capability grows, how
correction scales, and what verification can guarantee under a particular formal model. Its contribution is
a dated comparison of named sources and a record of revisions to those comparisons. Each row must
stand on its own source. Neither a shared vocabulary nor the number of rows establishes the ARC Theory.

For the researcher. The PNP framework in Paper C describes the author’s account of how he connects
domains. The argument of Hernández-Espinosa et al. (2026) concerns diversity among machine agents;
the human parallel remains Paper C’s hypothesis. Personal history can explain why a question was asked.
It cannot substitute for measuring the mechanism. The source bundle and version history let a reader
inspect the dated record without treating biography or a matching file hash as scientific confirmation.

For AI safety. A researcher choosing an oversight design can use this comparison to ask a concrete
question: does changing the corrector improve residual-error decay under matched resources, and does
that improvement persist as target capability grows? Those are separate measurements. Engels et al.
(2025) already studies oversight scaling; Goel et al. (2025) measures overlap in model mistakes; Ball et al.
(2025) studies computational limits on filtering. Their different targets help specify what an ARC test
would have to measure. A useful evaluation must report legitimate task completion alongside correction,
rather than achieve apparent safety by preventing the task. That usefulness measure is an evaluation
requirement here, not a new registered result.

10     Limitations
The register’s limitations are stated where each arises; they are gathered here.
     • The former summed register was assembled retrospectively, without a pre-outcome scoring rule, a
       fixed horizon, a base-rate model or a complete failure register; the graded register replaces its sum
       with a grade for each row, and its rows still cannot be summed (Section 4.1).
     • No disconfirming search with its denominator has been reported, and no population, inclusion
       rules or base-rate model have been declared, so the register cannot yet be shown to be more than a
       selected sample of the domains read (Section 6).
     • The rows are graded within the programme, by its own record, not by an outside reviewer; Unit 58’s
       re-audit is a draft preregistered by timestamp, awaiting submission; its draft does not provide a
       chance model for this register (Section 6).
     • Margins stated against arXiv submission dates (rows 1, 8, 9, 18 and 19 of Table 1) rest on arXiv’s
       record alone, and first public disclosure has not been separately verified for those rows (Section 4.2).
     • Several press-sourced rows lack an identified article or official address; the corresponding source
       cells are explicitly unresolved (Section 4.2).
     • The author’s non-awareness of the Willow preprint when he wrote the December 2024 manuscript
       is biographical attestation, and the grade does not move on it (Section 4.2).
     • The closed loop’s first two properties are intellectual history and motivation, not measurements of
       the framework’s dynamics (Section 5.3).
     • The Unit 01 draft reports pilot evidence pointing towards a null residual-decay class contrast, which
       is not a measurement of the capacity ceiling (Section 7.2).


11     Conclusion
This selected retrospective register places named later works beside the author’s dated records and
preserves how those comparisons changed. It supplies a source trail and a set of questions for further
work, not an aggregate evidential verdict. The dependent chain is version-pinned, unresolved source cells
remain visible, and the human parallel belongs to Paper C. The oversight question is concrete: whether
a change in correction improves performance under a fixed protocol, and whether correction capacity
then keeps pace with capability. The current Unit 01 draft addresses the first through a residual-decay
contrast; it cannot decide the second or a universal ceiling. Law I remains a law under test, and the theory
registration’s refuters govern any verdict on the theory.

Version 1.14: the quantum-error-correction objection. The objection to pre-empt, because a
physicist reaches it in one step. Fault-tolerant quantum error correction suppresses logical error
exponentially in code distance below threshold, which looks like a counterexample to any square-root cap.
Two scope facts answer it: the exponential suppression runs on the redundancy axis at fixed capability,
not on the coupling of corrective strength to the capability of the corrected system, which is the exponent
this programme measures; and the threshold theorem itself requires sufficiently uncorrelated physical
noise, which is the independence premise again, wearing hardware. Likewise, concentration bounds give
exponentially falling error probability while estimator width falls as the square root: different quantities,
and conflating them manufactures a refutation.
