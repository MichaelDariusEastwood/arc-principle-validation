Abstract

I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons; only proof will move the world. This paper asks how later work in separate fields bears on the ARC Theory’s conjectured links between recursive capability, the limits of external control and correction embedded in a system. It presents a selected retrospective register beside the author’s dated records over a 21-month window from 8 December 2024 to 1 September 2026. The register bears on Law I of the ARC Theory and, as context only, on Law V; it tests neither.

1 Introduction

I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons; only proof will move the world. If control of a capable AI system were lost and could not be won back, what the system itself carries would decide what it does. The programme’s position is that people keep oversight, containment and shutdown for as long as they physically can, and never give them up by choice; correction built into a system is the contingency for control lost against our will, not a reason to let go of it. The programme states the principle behind that contingency in three parts: above a threshold, recursive self-correction produces super-linear capability gains; external alignment constraints are structurally insufficient to contain it; and the corrective must be embedded, not imposed. This paper asks whether later work in separate fields bears on those parts. In plain words, its answer is a record, not a verdict. It sets later work by others against the author’s dated records, the earliest a manuscript of 8 December 2024 (Eastwood 2024), row by row, with dates, source availability and the part of the question each comparison bears on. The rows bear on different parts; no row, and not the set, establishes the three together; no total is claimed; and no aggregate evidential verdict follows. A researcher choosing an oversight design can use the comparisons to distinguish residual-error reduction, corrective capacity and formal verification guarantees.

2 Related work

2.1 The rival instrument

The work that sits closest to this programme’s question, and so the paper this one should be weighed against, is Engels et al. (2025), “Scaling Laws For Scalable Oversight”, arXiv:2504.18530, first posted 25 April 2025 at 17:54:27 UTC (a time taken from arXiv’s record alone, arXiv Atom; a NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight Poster, confirmed on 15 August 2026 against the NeurIPS virtual page for poster 115536, as recorded in the statement paper’s reference dossier). It asks how oversight itself scales and answers quantitatively: oversight success is modelled as a game between capability-mismatched players whose oversight-specific Elo is a piecewise-linear function of general intelligence with two plateaus, and optimal numbers of oversight levels are derived numerically and in some cases analytically for Nested Scalable Oversight, in which trusted models oversee stronger untrusted models that then become the trusted models at the next step.

The measurable distinction is between the questions and their instruments. Engels et al. (2025) already states a slope condition: oversight improves when the overseer’s game-specific Elo rises faster with general Elo than the overseen system’s (mG>mHm_G > m_H). It recommends iterating a design until that condition holds. A composition label is not a variable in that model. The ARC programme asks whether a frozen corrector-class relationship is associated with a different residual-decay exponent under a matched protocol, and separately whether corrective capacity keeps pace with capability. Neither question establishes a universal cap on Nested Scalable Oversight. P6 of the theory registration is a conjecture with an admitted population and scale; its correction-capacity exponent is distinct both from Law II’s βC\beta_C and from Unit 01’s residual-decay exponent (Section 7.2).

2.2 Antecedents and near-misses

On the question. Hutter asked directly whether intelligence can explode (“Can Intelligence Explode?”, Hutter 2012, arXiv, 28 February 2012, read at its source), separating “speed from intelligence explosion” and undertaking to “consider possible bounds on intelligence”, augmenting the analysis of Chalmers (2010). The question itself goes back to Good (1966), and the separation of speed from intelligence explosion is at least fourteen years old. These antecedents establish that the question is older; this selected comparison is not a complete novelty search. Growth exponents of the form the programme uses are older (Jones 1995).

On impossibility. Three 2025 arXiv papers argue for limits on alignment or its control: Yao (2025b), “The Alignment Trap: Complexity Barriers” (arXiv:2506.10304, v1 12 June 2025 02:30:30 UTC, a time taken from arXiv’s record alone, independently observed by the Internet Archive on 13 June 2025; cited by Spizzirri (2025), arXiv:2512.03048); Yao (2025a), “On the Mathematical Impossibility of Safe Universal Approximators” (arXiv:2507.03031, 3 July 2025, whose abstract uses the phrase “irreducible uncontrollability”); and Ball et al. (2025), “On the Impossibility of Separating Intelligence from Judgment” (arXiv:2507.07341, 9 July 2025). All three are worst-case and qualitative: measure zero, coNP-completeness, cryptographic hardness. None reports an average-case scaling exponent or a rate. The first argues that the set of safe policies has measure zero and that verifying a policy’s safety is coNP-complete; the second, that perfect, reliable control of a universal approximator is a mathematical impossibility; the third, under cryptographic hardness assumptions, that some large language models admit no efficient prompt filter and that output filtering is intractable in a natural setting. The third concludes that “safety cannot be achieved by designing filters external to the LLM internals (architecture and weights)” and argues that “an aligned AI system’s intelligence cannot be separated from its judgment”; that argument from filtering intractability is consistent with this programme’s architecture dependence, though it does not test it. Two of the three papers are by one sole author; to call them a wave would overstate the literature’s breadth, though not the seriousness of the six-author paper.

On model similarity and oversight. Goel et al. (2025) is a direct predecessor for the error-complementarity question. Its Chance Adjusted Probabilistic Agreement measures overlap in model mistakes beyond a chance baseline. It reports judge preference for similar models, a role for complementary knowledge in weak-to-strong generalisation, and increasing similarity of mistakes with capability. Unit 01’s draft instead targets a matched residual-decay contrast over repeated revisions. That difference in estimand supplies a comparison to test; it does not make the general connection between model similarity and oversight new.

The nearest structural analogue. The quantum error-correction threshold result (Aharonov and Ben-Or 2008) concerns reliable computation under a specified local noise model, including exponentially decaying correlations. Its critical physical noise rate is not a correction-capacity elasticity. The comparison is methodological, with the axes kept separate (Section 7.3).

On feedback and stability (classical). The proposition that insufficient corrective gain can cause instability has a long control-theory lineage, including the small-gain analysis of Zames (1966). An exponent comparison across capability is a distinct operationalisation under test, not a claim to have originated the general stability question. Its measurement and validity remain to be established for the systems it is intended to describe.

The nearest quantitative neighbour. Liu and Meng (2026), “Self-Correction as Feedback Control: Error Dynamics, Stability Thresholds, and Prompt Interventions in LLMs”, arXiv:2604.22273 (a single source, the abstract read via the paper’s own html page, 12 August 2026), recasts self-correction as a closed-loop control problem via a two-state Markov model parameterised by an Error Introduction Rate and an Error Correction Rate, and derives a directly measurable stability threshold: iterate only when ECR/EIR\mathrm{ECR}/\mathrm{EIR} exceeds Acc/(1−Acc)\mathrm{Acc}/(1-\mathrm{Acc}). The differential, stated wherever it is cited: theirs is a per-step rate threshold at a fixed capability level, deciding whether another iteration helps now; this programme’s criterion is a scaling relation across capability, deciding whether corrective strength keeps pace as the system improves. Theirs has no scaling exponent on the corrector and no capability-growth exponent, the two quantities the programme’s co-scaling criterion compares, and no architecture or composition-class term; the two models meet at zero growth, as Paper X shows. Two adjacent empirical results from the same search, each read at its source: Li (2025) (arXiv:2601.00828, first posted on 24 December 2025) reports an accuracy-correction paradox, weaker models achieving materially higher intrinsic correction rates than stronger ones, per-step evidence directionally adjacent to the same-class cap and never to be quoted as an exponent measurement; and Tsui (2025) (arXiv:2507.02778, Self-Correction Bench, first posted on 3 July 2025) reports a systematic self-correction blind spot across open models.

3 Method

The edition has no reproducible search log, fixed search population, complete inclusion and exclusion record, or disconfirming denominator. Its rows therefore support comparisons between named documents; they do not estimate how often the pattern being considered occurs in the literature.

For a comparison to be checkable, the record needs the author’s dated passage, the outside passage, the exact source version, the relevant dates and their timestamp classes, and the relation claimed between the passages. A related theme, a shared mathematical form and a test of the same proposition are different relations. Section 4 retains the earlier table and identifies source cells that remain unresolved. The machine-readable snapshot that accompanies this edition records the graded register exactly as it stood on 1 November 2026; it does not certify the completeness or accuracy of every row.

The manuscript of 8 December 2024 and its accompanying records are the author’s dated source bundle (Eastwood 2024). A document date, a message header, a repository timestamp and a Bitcoin anchor carry different information (Appendix B). The absence of a citation to the author is not evidence of causal independence. 5.3 The closed loop

The expression “closed loop” describes how the author relates these records:

  1. Intellectual history. Paper C’s account of the author’s reasoning can explain why he asked the question. It does not measure the hypothesised recursive mechanism.

  2. Literature context. The register places named documents beside that question. Selection and dependence remain properties to investigate, not settled credentials of the pattern.

  4. Traceability. Version-pinned links and dated records let a reader inspect the comparison. A file hash identifies a file’s exact contents; it does not establish the truth of a claim, the time of composition, or independent discovery.

6 Falsifiability

The present claims are comparisons between specified documents. A row fails if its source does not say what is attributed to it, if its date or version is wrong, or if the stated relationship conflates different quantities. Such a finding changes that row and any passage that relies on it. The register offers no aggregate evidential verdict, so it has no aggregate rejection threshold to report.

A later audit would need the following provisions before its results could be read as evidence about a broader pattern:

  1. Source and date checks. Pin both passages and the source versions, distinguish their timestamp classes and resolve missing primary sources. An unlocated source stays unresolved.

  2. Dependence assessment. Fix criteria for shared intellectual lineage and documented exposure. Classify a traceable response to the author’s dissemination as engagement. Do not infer independence from a missing citation.

  3. A fixed audit population. Declare which records are eligible, how duplicate events are grouped, and how disagreements are adjudicated before scoring. Unit 58’s draft re-audit, first anchored on 12 August 2026 at block 962066, is preregistered by timestamp and awaits submission; it does not supply a chance model for this paper’s selected register.

  4. Disconfirming cases and a comparison model. Define a search population, inclusion rules, a disconfirming search and the baseline against which any excess of matches would be measured. Those elements have not been supplied here. No count, rate or chance-adjusted significance follows from the present rows.

  5. Separate theory tests. Apply the theory registration’s own operational definitions and refuters to data collected under the relevant registered design. A literature resemblance does not decide a prediction about recursive capability or corrective capacity.

The practical use of this list is to turn a promising comparison into a question that another researcher could check. Until a suitable audit is specified and run, the register remains a selected historical record.

7 Framework predictions the convergence pattern points to

7.1 The eclipse

The oversight question is whether corrective capacity can keep pace with the system being corrected. Proposition P6 of the theory registration, version 1.104, conjectures a correction exponent at or below one half for same-class correctors. It is a conjecture, not a consequence of a shared substrate or of statistical independence alone. The registration requires a prospectively frozen, versioned taxonomy and an independently justified capability ratio scale. It separates scaling class, lineage, substrate, training provenance, access to external ground truth and failure-mode dependence; no one axis substitutes for another. The population remains blocked until the required taxonomy is fixed.

The route to one half additionally assumes that corrective capacity is inverse residual uncertainty, that correction channels are uncorrelated, that their number grows in proportion to capability, and that their variance and quality are comparable. These assumptions require separate measurement. Neither the register nor the material of a corrector establishes them. The registered P6 refuter requires a valid exceedance of one half replicated on a second, disjoint fault set under the same settings and the registration’s precision rules. Its outcomes are REFUTED, NO COUNTEREXAMPLE FOUND AMONG N and INCONCLUSIVE; a finite survey cannot return support for this universal bound. Unit 82 tests P17’s proxy for error dependence, not P6 or a ceiling value (Section 9.4).

The conditional crossover illustrates why the distinction matters. In the model, capability follows C(R)=C0(R/R0)αC(R)=C_0(R/R_0)^\alpha, marginal correctable burden is B(R)=bdC/dRB(R)=b\,dC/dR, and correction service capacity is A(R)=aC(R)γA(R)=aC(R)^\gamma, with positive coefficients and 0≤γ<10\leq\gamma<1. The crossover is αcrit=11−γ.\begin{equation*} \alpha_{\text{crit}} = \frac{1}{1-\gamma}. \end{equation*}(1) Here γ\gamma is the correction-capacity elasticity, denoted γC\gamma_C in the Unit 01 draft; it is not a residual-decay exponent. The value two follows conditionally when γ=1/2\gamma=1/2. At equality the exponents tie and coefficients, delay, saturation and feedback dynamics still matter. This crossover does not by itself establish finite-horizon safety or bounded harm. The model and its empirical adequacy are separate from the literature comparisons in this paper.

7.2 What Unit 01 would measure

Unit 01, the corrector-class contrast, is a draft preregistered by timestamp: its design was first anchored on 28 August 2026 at block 964465, the draft has been revised since, and it awaits the author’s submission. This paper reports no new run.

As drafted, it compares the decay of residual error over repeated revisions under same-class and cross-class correctors at fixed capability; γN\gamma_N denotes that residual-decay exponent. It estimates neither γC\gamma_C, nor the capability-growth exponent α\alpha, nor the crossover in equation (1), and it does not assume γN=γC\gamma_N=\gamma_C. Its decision rules will be published with its registration.

Corrector class is classified, not randomly assigned: a contrast found on its roster would establish an association under its protocol, not architectural causation, a universal half-exponent bound or a ceiling verdict. The disclosed pilot points towards a null class contrast, which is not a finding about the capacity ceiling.

7.3 The objection to pre-empt

Quantum error correction makes the importance of the measurement axis concrete. The threshold result of Aharonov and Ben-Or (2008) concerns reliable quantum computation under a specified local noise model; its stated model permits exponentially decaying noise correlations. It does not require independence in the unrestricted sense asserted in this paper’s earlier wording. Error suppression as redundancy or code distance grows is not the same estimand as correction-capacity elasticity against capability. No mapping between those axes is established here, so this analogy supplies neither a proof of P6 nor a blanket exemption from a properly specified counterexample.

7.4 A programme hypothesis from the same mechanism

The programme also asks whether adding human judgement can improve correction under a matched budget and information set. Debate (Irving et al. 2018), amplification (Christiano et al. 2018), recursive reward modelling (Leike et al. 2018) and reinforcement learning from human preferences (Christiano et al. 2017) put human input in different places. Training-time preference labels are not an active human controller at each correction step. A human participant’s different substrate alone establishes neither complementary errors nor a higher exponent. The testable hypothesis is that measured complementary error structure predicts better correction under a specified protocol; a human-versus-model contrast would need its own frozen taxonomy, outcomes and matched arms. No such comparison is measured by this register.

8 What this means

For the programme.

The register locates questions worth separating: how capability grows, how correction scales, and what verification can guarantee under a particular formal model. Its contribution is a dated comparison of named sources and a record of revisions to those comparisons. Each row must stand on its own source. Neither a shared vocabulary nor the number of rows establishes the ARC Theory.

For the researcher.

The PNP framework in Paper C describes the author’s account of how he connects domains. The argument of Hernández-Espinosa et al. (2026) concerns diversity among machine agents; the human parallel remains Paper C’s hypothesis. Personal history can explain why a question was asked. It cannot substitute for measuring the mechanism. The source bundle and version history let a reader inspect the dated record without treating biography or a matching file hash as scientific confirmation.

For AI safety.

A researcher choosing an oversight design can use this comparison to ask a concrete question: does changing the corrector improve residual-error decay under matched resources, and does that improvement persist as target capability grows? Those are separate measurements. Engels et al. (2025) already studies oversight scaling; Goel et al. (2025) measures overlap in model mistakes; Ball et al. (2025) studies computational limits on filtering. Their different targets help specify what an ARC test would have to measure. A useful evaluation must report legitimate task completion alongside correction, rather than achieve apparent safety by preventing the task. That usefulness measure is an evaluation requirement here, not a new registered result.

9.2 What it does not show

It does not show the structural principle, or any of its three parts. The rows bear on different parts of it: a mathematics result on unverifiability addresses whether an external constraint can be verified, and says nothing about the rate at which capability grows with depth or about where a corrective must sit; a policy development or a cultural echo addresses neither the growth rate nor the sufficiency of external constraint. Neither any row nor the set is offered as establishing the three parts together, and no total is claimed. The register is not a test statistic and yields no aggregate evidential verdict. Section 6 identifies what a later audit would require. The closed loop of Section 5.3 is intellectual history and motivation, not a measurement. The paper tests none of Laws I to V.

9.3 This paper and Laws IV and V

Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, is carried by proposition P15, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, by propositions P12 and P21. The abstract identifies the programme’s account of correction embedded in a system, and Section 8 records later works that argue for limits on external control or verification, a pattern it calls context, not confirmation. In the theory registration, embedded correction is carried by P21, one of the two propositions that carry Law V, and so this paper bears on Law V as context only. It compares no correction that takes part in each revision round with correction that sees only the finished output, varies no build order, and measures no gain installed by external post-training under later capability-directed training. 10 Limitations

The register’s limitations are stated where each arises; they are gathered here.

  • The former summed register was assembled retrospectively, without a pre-outcome scoring rule, a fixed horizon, a base-rate model or a complete failure register; the graded register replaces its sum with a grade for each row, and its rows still cannot be summed (Section 4.1).

  • No disconfirming search with its denominator has been reported, and no population, inclusion rules or base-rate model have been declared, so the register cannot yet be shown to be more than a selected sample of the domains read (Section 6).

  • The rows are graded within the programme, by its own record, not by an outside reviewer; Unit 58’s re-audit is a draft preregistered by timestamp, awaiting submission; its draft does not provide a chance model for this register (Section 6).

  • Margins stated against arXiv submission dates (rows 1, 8, 9, 18 and 19 of Table 1) rest on arXiv’s record alone, and first public disclosure has not been separately verified for those rows (Section 4.2).

  • Several press-sourced rows lack an identified article or official address; the corresponding source cells are explicitly unresolved (Section 4.2).

  • The author’s non-awareness of the Willow preprint when he wrote the December 2024 manuscript is biographical attestation, and the grade does not move on it (Section 4.2).

  • The closed loop’s first two properties are intellectual history and motivation, not measurements of the framework’s dynamics (Section 5.3).

  • The Unit 01 draft reports pilot evidence pointing towards a null residual-decay class contrast, which is not a measurement of the capacity ceiling (Section 7.2).

11 Conclusion

This selected retrospective register places named later works beside the author’s dated records and preserves how those comparisons changed. It supplies a source trail and a set of questions for further work, not an aggregate evidential verdict. The dependent chain is version-pinned, unresolved source cells remain visible, and the human parallel belongs to Paper C. The oversight question is concrete: whether a change in correction improves performance under a fixed protocol, and whether correction capacity then keeps pace with capability. The current Unit 01 draft addresses the first through a residual-decay contrast; it cannot decide the second or a universal ceiling. Law I remains a law under test, and the theory registration’s refuters govern any verdict on the theory.
