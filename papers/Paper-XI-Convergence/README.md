# Are separate fields converging on the same principle of recursive amplification?

**Revised:** 1 November 2026

## Summary

From the abstract (version 1.16):

> I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons; only proof will move the world. This paper asks how later work in separate fields bears on the ARC Theory’s conjectured links between recursive capability, the limits of external control and correction embedded in a system. It presents a selected retrospective register beside the author’s dated records over a 21-month window from 8 December 2024 to 1 September 2026. The contribution is a source trail, a record of revised comparisons and a separation of questions that require different measurements. The retained historical table and a frozen graded-register snapshot identify available sources and unresolved cells. They supply no aggregate evidential verdict: no fixed search population, disconfirming denominator or chance model is available. One dependent lineage connects a paper on diversity among machine agents (Hernández-Espinosa et al. 2026) with version 1 of a formal-verification preprint (J. P. Gumbau Mezquita 2026); the text distinguishes that historical version from its revised version 4. The human parallel is argued separately in Paper C. The oversight comparison is equally specific: Unit 01, a design preregistered by timestamp, measures a matched difference in residual-error decay at fixed capability; it measures neither correction-capacity scaling nor a ceiling. P6’s same-class bound remains a conjecture subject to its registered domain and conditions. Section 6 states the requirements for a later audit. The register bears on Law I of the ARC Theory and, as context only, on Law V; it tests neither.
