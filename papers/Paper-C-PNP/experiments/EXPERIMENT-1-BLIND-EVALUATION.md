# Experiment 1: Blind Evaluation of LiP vs Counsel
## Testing the Capability-Adjustment Fallacy

**Paper C support** · T3-research · 2026-07-06

### Hypothesis
The Capability-Adjustment Fallacy predicts evaluators rate the same work differently when they know the author is a neurodivergent litigant-in-person with no legal training.

- **H₀:** Author identity has no effect on quality ratings.
- **H₁:** Quality ratings decrease after background disclosure despite identical content.

### Design

**6 document pairs** — 3 author submissions + 3 counsel-drafted comparables (~800-1200 words each, anonymised).

**10 legal professionals** (solicitors/barristers/academics, 5+ years experience). £200 each = **£2,000.**

### Procedure
1. **Blind rating** — Rate all 6 documents on 5 dimensions (1-7 Likert): legal reasoning, clarity, persuasiveness, procedural knowledge, overall standard.
2. **Disclosure** — Told author of 3 documents is LiP with no legal training, ADHD/ASC, executive-function difficulties. Re-rate all 6.
3. **Interview** — "What assumptions did you make? Did background change your assessment?"
4. **Debrief** — Full disclosure. Right to withdraw.

### Analysis
Paired t-test on "overall standard" rating change. 30 paired observations → detectable d ≥ 0.55 at α=0.05, β=0.2.

### Predicted Result
Significant rating decrease for LiP documents after disclosure (d ≈ 0.6-0.8), no change for counsel documents.

### Budget: £2,600 (participants £2K + platform £100 + prep £200 + analysis £300)
### Timeline: 4 weeks
### Kill-condition: If H₀ holds, publish null result. Fallacy lacks experimental support at this scale.
