# Eden Engineering: can correction built into each round keep a self-improving AI good and useful if oversight fails?

**Version:** 8.2
**Revised:** 1 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-02-22
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 8.2):

> Oversight and the power to shut a system down must be held for as long as people physically can. If control of a self-improving AI is lost against our will and cannot be won back, what keeps it good, and still useful? The Eden Protocol answers with testable engineering. It places self-correction checks of purpose, stakeholder care and universalisability inside each round of a system’s reasoning, not only on its finished output, and a gate before every action: written, versioned rules, never the model’s own judgement, decide before the action runs whether it is allowed, held for investigation or denied, each decision entering a hash-chained record. The ARC Theory motivates this design without entailing it: three of the four registered predictions it rests on, P15, P12 and P21, carry Laws IV and V, each a law under test. What has been measured is narrow. Given as a prompt, a three-part reasoning instruction raised scored stakeholder care in all five analysable model runs, each scored by one model and only partly blind, with an instruction close to what is scored; small self-modifying networks with safety built into their objective kept more of their old tasks, a toy-system result. In Unit 83 (27 and 28 October 2026), P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis, family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not evaluable and one call retried under a disclosed recovery amendment (results: DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/98HKR). None tests correction built into a system. I tie each part of the specification to its registered prediction, where one exists, and to the test that would refute it. Every safety measure is paired with a usefulness measure: a system that refuses ordinary work is safe but useless. A working form of the gate checks every tool call of the programme’s own software agents; the rest is specified, not built.

From the paper (version 8.2):

> Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, which P15 carries, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, which P12 and P21 carry, were added on 24 September 2026 and registered as laws on 15 October 2026 in version 1.103 of the theory registration, whose current version is 1.104 (29 October 2026); like Laws I to III, each is a law under test, and their mathematics is in development (version 1.2, 29 October 2026); Section 2.3 gives their status and Section 5.3 the first registered result on one of their propositions.
