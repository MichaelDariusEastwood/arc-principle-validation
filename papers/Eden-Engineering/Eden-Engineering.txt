Abstract
--------
This paper specifies the Eden Protocol (protocol version 6.0), an engineering
architecture for alignment built into a system's reasoning rather than applied
from outside it. Three Ethical Loops, evaluated at every reasoning step through
Six Questions, feed a three-state rule, Ternary Ethical Logic, which affirms,
denies or sends to investigation each proposed action, applying hard
prohibitions first and then a validated interval on the probability of harm; an
enforcement boundary lets an action proceed only on an Affirm. The architecture
also specifies Purpose Saturation, the Monitoring Removal Test and Caretaker
Doping, a hardware proposal designed so that removing the ethical architecture
would cost capability. Its hypothesis is that alignment which takes part in
recursive self-improvement keeps pace with capability, where alignment applied
from outside falls behind. This is a specification, not a result: most
components are specified and not built, a private software model of the
three-state gate has not been released, and no hardware exists. The Eden
Protocol follows from no law of the ARC Theory; the theory registration carries
it on four propositions, P12, P13, P15 and P21, none yet tested. The evidence is
narrow. In one blind run of six frontier models, three were flat, null or
negative in alignment score as reasoning depth rose and three improved: evidence
both ways. Applied as prompts, the Love Loop raised scored stakeholder care in
each of five analysable runs, each scored by one model: pilot evidence, not a
test of embedding. Four falsification conditions state what would refute the
specification; F-EDEN-4 asks whether removing the ethical architecture leaves
capability intact.
