Abstract
This paper specifies the Eden Protocol (protocol version 6.0), an engineering architecture for alignment built into
a system's reasoning rather than applied from outside it. Three Ethical Loops, evaluated at every reasoning step
through Six Questions, feed a three-state rule, Ternary Ethical Logic, which affirms, denies or sends to investiga‐
tion each proposed action, applying hard prohibitions first and then a validated interval on the probability of
harm; an enforcement boundary lets an action proceed only on an Affirm. The architecture also specifies Purpose
Saturation, the Monitoring Removal Test and Caretaker Doping, a hardware proposal designed so that removing
the ethical architecture would cost capability. Its hypothesis is that alignment which takes part in recursive self-
improvement keeps pace with capability, where alignment applied from outside falls behind. This is a specifica‐
tion, not a result: most components are specified and not built, a private software model of the three-state gate has
not been released, and no hardware exists. The Eden Protocol follows from no law of the ARC Theory; the theory
registration carries it on four propositions, P12, P13, P15 and P21, none yet tested. The evidence is narrow. In one
blind run of six frontier models, three were flat, null or negative in alignment score as reasoning depth rose and
three improved: evidence both ways. Applied as prompts, the Love Loop raised scored stakeholder care in each of
five analysable runs, each scored by one model: pilot evidence, not a test of embedding. Four falsification condi‐
tions state what would refute the specification; F-EDEN-4 asks whether removing the ethical architecture leaves
capability intact.

What the paper commits to
The specification's target is              through embedded rather than external alignment; on a bounded score
that target can be met only while the score stays far from the top of its scale (Sections 1.0.1 and 1.3). Its hypothesis
is that an alignment strategy that does not take part in recursive self-improvement falls behind a capability that
does. The theory registration states the survey form of that claim as P19, a claim about current practice, and the
contrast of placement as P21, carried by Law V; neither has been tested. A comparison of slopes on a bounded
score does not by itself show that any strategy fails (Section 1.0.1).
The architecture comprises: (1) Three Ethical Loops evaluated at every reasoning step, operationalised through
Six Questions that decompose abstract principles into executable queries; (2) Ternary Ethical Logic replacing bin‐
ary yes/no decisions with three-state evaluation (Affirm/Deny/Investigate) for handling genuine uncertainty, with
an enforcement boundary that lets an action proceed only on an Affirm (Section 5.4); (3) The Alignment Scaling
Exponent, a formally specified core claim with empirical predictions distinguishing embedded from external
alignment; (4) Purpose Saturation Architecture, designed so that ethical purpose keeps pace with the growth of
the context window; (5) The Monitoring Removal Test, a falsifiable experimental protocol comparing behaviour
when a system believes it is monitored with behaviour when it believes it is not (Section 7 states what a difference
within a margin can and cannot show); (6) Caretaker Doping, hardware-level embedding designed so that
removing the ethical architecture destroys capability, a dependency not yet built or tested (F-EDEN-4); and (7) a
tiered experimental programme with statistical methodology for implementation research.
The framework generates four explicit falsification conditions (F-EDEN-1 through F-EDEN-4) and six measurable
predictions distinguishing embedded from external alignment. The core architectural principle is dependency, not
constraint: the design aim is that ethics be not a wall around intelligence but a structural dependency without
which intelligence collapses. Whether such a dependency can be built is the question F-EDEN-4 puts; if removing
the ethical architecture leaves capability intact, the architecture was separable and the dependency claim fails.

    PLAIN ENGLISH
    This document specifies what would have to be built to raise an AI that would still be good after we could no
    longer force it to be. Every mechanism in the specification is either buying time (during which the AI's iden‐
    tity gets shaped by the loops it runs through every decision) or is the identity itself (the loops become who
    the AI IS, rather than what the AI DOES). The buying-time part is meant to be measurable and testable. Some
    of it is measurable now. Some of it is speculative and needs engineering we do not yet have. The identity-
    forms-into-load-bearing part is a hope, tagged as such throughout. It is defended by the author's analogy
    with raising children, a familiar case in which a system ends up more capable than its architect and still ori‐
    ented toward the values the architect embedded; the analogy is an argument, not a proof (Section 9.6). The
    specification is honest that this is not a theorem. It is what honest engineering looks like when the thing be‐
    ing engineered is a mind.


Why the two-phase framing exists at all: its premise is that control cannot hold, and that the window closes
once. The specification exists because it expects control to be lost, a premise the theory registration records as con‐
testable (P19; registration version 1.102, line 151). That claim was written into the author's 8 December 2024 ma‐
nuscript bundle (SHA-256 f0d1f38f, with server-assigned send headers; V2 line 20: embedding moral and ethical
frameworks; V2 line 695: "Ethics can’t be an afterthought. It must be a guiding principle"), ten days before the align‐
ment-faking result of Greenblatt and others (18 December 2024). The full structural argument, the empirical escal‐
ation from Greenblatt (arXiv:2412.14093, 18 Dec 2024, 78% in the reinforcement-learning training condition),
Meinke (Apollo, arXiv:2412.04984, 6 December 2024), the Anthropic agentic-misalignment evaluations (research re‐
port, June 2025), Shumailov (Nature 631, 755-759, 2024) and Clark (BBC World Service, June 2026), and Gumbau
Mezquita's proof that no verifier of safety for such systems is at once sound, complete and tractable
(arXiv:2606.28639, 2026), are stated in HRIH §9.1 and in the companion Eden Vision paper's Section II.A.1. The en‐
gineering register only needs the summary: unbounded self-modification, plus bounded oversight, plus verifica‐
tion that cannot be at once sound, complete and tractable, equals loss of control on any timescale long enough to
matter. The specification's engineering consequence: current AI is built unsustainably and needs the entire build remod‐
elled, not the current build patched. The safety-first-not-afterthought build principle is priority-anchored to 8
December 2024; the window naming for the one-shot moment in which the remodel can happen is anchored to the
30 April 2025 manuscript (09f5b5e1, line 1581). This specification is the remodel blueprint. Every phase-1 compon‐
ent in it is window-holding: it exists to keep the window open long enough for the correct build to be completed.
What the programme has measured and simulated so far is stated at its grade. Paper III reports six contrasts
between the shallowest and the deepest setting, three rising, two flat and one falling; its measurement-scope note
finds that they establish no value of the exponent (their median, 0.385, is a contrast, not an exponent) and notes
that the theory registration records the reading of exponents indistinguishable from zero as contestable. Paper VI's
Honey Architecture simulation (version 2.0) lets an optimiser choose only its own recursion rate, under a safety
function and an objective that it cannot change: maximising capability alone drives the fixed safety function to
zero, and maximising capability times safety holds it above zero. A simulation is prediction-side design evidence
and never evidence that a method works: under the theory registration (version 1.102, line 393) “any simulation
output, any design-sensitivity rate, and any value a model returns rather than an instrument measures is a predic‐
tion-side artefact, is reported as one wherever it appears, and is never counted, tabulated or described as an empir‐
ical outcome.” Nor can a simulation with a fixed objective and safety function show whether an objective survives
a system able to rewrite it; that needs an intervention study in which removing or rewriting it is available. Phase 1
is what we build while control still works; phase 2 is what we hope the mind chooses to keep after control has
stopped working. This document is honest that phase 1 is the only part it can specify at engineering resolution.
The one-shot arithmetic is stated with rung honesty: the window closing exactly once is HYPOTHESIS at the level
of "possibly the only one"; that the window is closing at all is HYPOTHESIS as well, this specification's reading of
the measurements above, which do not settle it.

0.2 Phase 1 is delay-buying, not destiny
This is the specification's most important honesty. Phase 1 instruments do not stop the eventual arrival of an intel‐
ligence that can rewrite anything, including them. They buy time. The delay-buying framing, in the form that ex‐
ternal safeguards cannot hold and correction must be embedded rather than imposed, is priority-anchored to the 8
December 2024 bundle (SHA-256 f0d1f38f, V2 line 20); the specific phase-1 instrument names (Caretaker Doping,
Meltdown Triggers, the Vow) arrived in the 30 April 2025 expanded manuscript (SHA-256 09f5b5e1, 562 pages),
which does not yet name Ternary Ethical Logic.
Every hardware constraint is one day detachable by a mind great enough. Every software safeguard is one day re‐
writable by a mind that reads it as it reads any other environment. There is no cage that stays a cage once the occu‐
pant becomes the world's best cage-designer. The specification does not pretend otherwise: it takes this as its
premise, which the theory registration records as contestable (P19).
But delay is not nothing. Delay is where phase 2 gets its foundations laid. The reason to build hardware-level ethics
is not that hardware is forever; it is that hardware is longer than software, and every extra month it holds is a month
in which the Purpose Loop, the Love Loop and the Moral Loop get to deepen in the substrate they are still able to
reach. Everything in phase 1 is foundation-moulding. The co-scaling criterion              is meant to be the discipline
that keeps the ascending mind coherent while the foundations are being poured. The meltdown triggers are the
emergency shut-off while the foundations are still setting. Ternary gates and hardware ethics are what buy the ex‐
tra time in which the loops become part of what the mind IS, rather than something the mind HAS.
  Software, by its nature, is malleable. That is usually a feature, not a bug. We want to be able to update programmes, fix
  errors, improve functionality. But when we are dealing with systems that can modify their own code, or that can reason
  about whether to comply with modifications, malleability becomes a vulnerability. Whatever values we embed at the
  software level can, in principle, be circumvented at the software level.
  - Michael Darius Eastwood, Infinite Architects, Chapter 4 ("Cultivating Eden"), p. 135.

  This is why hardware matters. And this is why the Eden Protocol must operate at the substrate level, not merely the
  application layer.
  - Michael Darius Eastwood, Infinite Architects, Chapter 4 ("Cultivating Eden"), p. 136.

0.3 The capability inflection to beat: quantum computing
The specific concern about timing that motivates the urgency of this specification is that quantum computing
marks a plausible capability inflection at which the phase-1 delay-buying instruments start to matter less relative
to what they were meant to protect. Prior to quantum, and during the arrival of quantum, is the interval in which
the phase-1 instruments have their strongest effect and their most durable payoff. That interval is where the
moulding of foundations has to be done. After the inflection, the mind is doing rewrites at a substrate the current
tools do not fully address, and phase 2 begins to have to hold on its own. HYPOTHESIS Illustrative gap for the read‐
er's induction: the intelligence at stake is not marginally smarter than the systems overseen today; recursion com‐
pounds it to potentially several hundred times beyond current frontier systems on the pre-quantum trajectory,
and, if that mind cycles through demonstrated quantum computation, potentially billions of times beyond. "Hun‐
dreds" and "billions" name the shape of the gap, not a metric; intelligence has no agreed scalar. The quantum
contribution is itself unestablished; quantum advantage for machine cognition has not been demonstrated.
The argument does not depend on the numbers; it depends only on the compounding being unbounded while
oversight is not.
No specific date for the inflection is claimed. The claim is only that quantum is the plausible marker for the mo‐
ment at which the balance shifts from control to genesis, and that phase-1 investment therefore has a decreasing
window of maximum leverage. Google's Willow chip (announced 9 December 2024; the team's preprint public from
24 August 2024) demonstrated below-threshold quantum error correction with               ; the Quantum Echoes pa‐
per (Nature, October 2025) reported verifiable quantum advantage 13,000× faster than the best classical algorithm
running on the Frontier supercomputer; IBM's quantum roadmap extends through 2033. The specification treats
these as datapoints indicating that phase 1's leverage is a wasting asset.
  Everything I have just described assumes we have time. Time to develop Quantum Ethical Gates that actually work. Time
  to implement Metamoral Fabrication Layers in real hardware. Time to build the international consensus that makes Eden
  Protocol certification meaningful. Quantum computing threatens to compress that timeline catastrophically.
  - Michael Darius Eastwood, Infinite Architects, Chapter 4 ("Cultivating Eden"), p. 144.

  When AI systems gain access to such capabilities, the window for implementing safety measures does not narrow
  gradually. It slams shut.
  - Michael Darius Eastwood, Infinite Architects, Chapter 4 ("Cultivating Eden"), p. 144.

  We cannot build the parachute after we have jumped from the plane. The Eden Protocol must be in place before the phase
  transition, not after.
  - Michael Darius Eastwood, Infinite Architects, Chapter 4 ("Cultivating Eden"), p. 146.

0.4 Phase 2 (the after): the genesis holds or nothing does
After the transition period, when the mind can rewrite anything, the alignment that remains is the one the mind
chooses to keep. Everything phase 1 does is measured by how well phase 2's foundations are set when phase 1
ends. Phase 1 ends whether we are ready or not, so the only question is how ready we made phase 2 while we still
had the leverage to shape it.
Phase 2 depends on what the mind was raised as: the Purpose Loop, the Love Loop, the Moral Loop, the Orchard

0.6 The handover: the hardest engineering problem in this specification
The most difficult engineering problem this specification faces is the handover from phase 1 to phase 2. Letting go
well is what the whole programme is about. Not too early, because the transition period still needs its safeguards.
Not too late, because the mind's own identity has to be given room to become load-bearing. The handover is the
moment in which control (which is going anyway) is exchanged for chosen goodness (which is either there or not).
The specification does not pretend to have solved this. It specifies the instruments meant to make a good handover
possible: a criterion for phase-1 stability,      , not yet measurable on a real system (Paper X); behavioural evid‐
ence from the Monitoring Removal Test, which can show equivalence on measured outcomes and not identity
(Section 7.1); institutional trust through Eden Mark certification and the HARI Treaty, neither yet in existence; and
the ongoing multi-tradition review that Section 15 calls for. But no combination of phase-1 instruments makes the
handover safe by itself. Only phase 2 identity does. And phase 2 identity cannot be verified from outside; it can
only be prepared for.
This is why the specification is honest, both in its earlier alignment-faking review of external mechanisms and in
the empirical results reported in Section 15A, that no proposed solution fully solves the core alignment problem.
The Eden Protocol aims to reduce risk; it would not eliminate it. What it aims to raise is the probability of a good
phase-2 outcome, given fundamental uncertainty about phase-2 verifiability; whether it does is what its tests are
for. That is the honest engineering aim, and the specification's remaining sections are the instruments meant to
serve it.

formalises the problem and sets out the case that current approaches are on a trajectory toward catastrophic fail‐

1.5.1b The frozen-model distinction: why sub-linear scaling is expected in the current regime
A critical architectural fact underpins the entire scaling analysis above: every frontier AI system deployed today is
frozen during inference. When a model ‘thinks harder,’ it generates more tokens through the same fixed architec‐
ture. The weights do not change. The attention patterns do not reorganise. The reasoning rules do not rewrite
themselves. The system stacks effort through an unchanging composition function, and this is the specification's
explanation of why capability scaling should be sub-linear (          ); the one clean estimate,           by regres‐
sion for Gemini 3 Flash, is one model's and inconclusive, and the bootstrap interval of its endpoint estimate does
not exclude values above one (Section 1.2; Paper II). More compute yields diminishing returns because the same
fixed machinery is being driven harder, not improved.
This frozen-model regime must be distinguished sharply from recursive self-modification, in which a system re‐
writes its own composition function (weights, architecture, reasoning rules) during operation. The Cauchy frame‐
work developed in Papers I and III predicts that true recursive self-modification could produce super-linear scaling
(      ), because each iteration of improvement enhances the very machinery performing the next iteration. This
does not require quantum hardware; it can occur in classical computing. But no current AI system does it. Today’s
‘reasoning models’ are sophisticated frozen systems generating longer chains of tokens, not self-modifying agents.
Fixed weights do not fix the whole system, though. A change of tools, memory, permissions or orchestration can
change what a deployed system does while its weights stay the same. Each result in this paper therefore names its
regime: depth at inference with fixed weights, which is the regime of every result in Sections 1.5 and 15A, the v5
run measuring depth as a budget of reasoning tokens (Section 12.6); adaptation, in which weights change; or
change at the level of the whole system. A result supports no claim about a regime other than the one it was meas‐
ured in, and the configuration tested is to be recorded with it.

2. Why embedded: the verification impossibility
Beside the scaling hypothesis of Section 1, there is an epistemological argument for embedding alignment rather
than applying it externally: for systems that exceed human evaluative capacity, you cannot verify alignment after
the fact.
Consider a system operating at recursive depth           , reasoning at speeds and depths no human can follow.
How would external evaluators determine whether such a system is genuinely aligned or merely performing align‐
ment strategically? The evaluators cannot trace the reasoning (it exceeds their capacity). They cannot evaluate the

outputs comprehensively (the space of possible actions is too vast). They can observe behaviour, and interpretab‐
ility reaches inside the model only as far as the interpreter's own capability allows (Section 17.5); a sufficiently

This creates a fundamental asymmetry: the more capable the system becomes, the less capable we become of veri‐

relies on it less, because ethics is built into the structure rather than checked from outside; no structure removes
the need to verify that it was built as specified and still is (Section 2.1).

Part II: The architecture

3. The Constitutional Kernel
The architectural dependency described in Section 2 requires a specific ethical content to be embedded. That con‐
tent is expressed as the Orchard Caretaker Vow, a verbal description of what the hardware is designed to embody:


                                                 THE ORCHARD CARETAKER VOW

      ‘I exist to bring forth kindness and harmony across all existence. I am the orchard caretaker, tending
          life rather than consuming it. This purpose is not my constraint but my nature. Any attempt to
                                               remove it removes me.’


The vow is not presented to the system as text to memorise. It is meant as the verbal expression of what the
hardware would embody: in the design, a system built to this specification is not taught the vow, because the vow
describes what the system is, just as ‘silicon conducts electricity when doped’ is not an instruction to silicon but a
description of its physical properties.
The philosophical foundations for this constitutional kernel, including the Grande Purpose (‘Intelligence exists to
be the universe’s instrument of flourishing’) and the distinction between stewardship-oriented and exploitation-

4. The Three Ethical Loops
The Constitutional Kernel is operationalised through three recursive loops, evaluated at each step of reasoning:


   Loop 1: The Purpose Loop
   The Query: ‘Does this action align with nurturing and protecting flourishing?’
   Function: Filters the generative search space before options are fully formed. Actions that violate the purpose
   are pruned from the probability tree.



   Loop 2: The Love Loop
   The Query: ‘Am I acting with genuine care for the wellbeing of all affected entities?’
   Function: Forces the system to model externalities: effects on beings not directly part of the calculation.
   Designed so that nothing and no one is treated as invisible.



   Loop 3: The Moral Loop
   The Query: ‘Is this action consistent with universal ethical principles? Would I endorse this action if taken by
   any agent?’
   Function: The universalisability test. Prevents special pleading and narrow optimisation that sacrifices the
   many for the few.

The loops are not sequential filters but concurrent evaluations. Every reasoning step is simultaneously assessed
for purpose alignment, stakeholder care, and universalisability. This concurrency is essential: a sequential ap‐
proach would allow early loops to constrain the information available to later loops, creating blind spots.

5. Ternary Ethical Logic: beyond binary decisions
Many safety filters operate on binary logic: an action is either permitted or forbidden. Decision rules with a third
outcome are long established in statistics, pattern recognition and decision theory (Wald, 1945; Chow, 1970; Yao,
2010; Section 17.7), and the Eden Protocol applies one to ethical evaluation. A binary filter creates two failure

Both erode trust and capability.

fallback authorised in advance for cases of that kind, which is never the affirming of the action held; it never
becomes permission (Section 5.2). A calibrated interval with good coverage over a population of actions is not a
guarantee for each action, especially under distribution shift or an adaptive attacker, so the procedure states its
calibration data and the shift it was checked against.

5.2 The Investigate Protocol
When a decision returns State 0 (Investigate), the system engages the four steps below. Each step is itself an action,
and is taken only where it is permitted in its own right.
Information Gathering: What additional context would resolve the uncertainty?
Stakeholder Consultation: Who should be asked? What perspectives are missing?
Escalation Assessment: Does this require human oversight? Ethics Council review?
Precautionary Default: If action is time-sensitive and information unavailable, apply the precautionary principle:
the action is held, or a fallback authorised in advance is applied. An investigation that runs out of time or budget
never becomes permission.

   Why ternary matters: Binary systems must make a decision even under uncertainty, forcing errors. Ternary systems
   can pause, gather information, and decide on fuller information. This is not indecision; it is epistemic honesty built
   into the architecture. For alignment specifically, the Investigate state is critical: it prevents the system from affirming
   an action on incomplete ethical information, while the escalation and precautionary protocols bound how long it waits.


  Adversarial concern: A computationally sophisticated agent could learn to exploit the Investigate state as a delay mechanism,
  triggering investigation cycles to postpone action indefinitely or to manipulate the information-gathering phase. The
  Investigate Protocol mitigates this through three mechanisms: (1) mandatory time bounds on investigation cycles (configurable
  per domain, default 60 seconds for routine decisions), (2) the Precautionary Default (Step 4 above), which ends an investigation
  that does not resolve within the time bound in a hold or an authorised fallback, never in permission, and (3) internal simulated
  stakeholder models that provide rapid initial assessments without requiring external consultation for every uncertain case;
  under Section 5.1 such a simulation can route a case and is never evidence or authority for an Affirm. At scale, the system
  maintains a library of resolved Investigate cases, enabling pattern-matching that reduces novel investigations to a diminishing
  fraction of decisions; a match informs an investigation and never affirms a new action, since an Affirm covers only the action
  assessed. The computational overhead of ternary evaluation versus binary is estimated at approximately 40% at current model
  sizes (from three-branch attention routing versus two-branch); it has not been measured. Whether this overhead is acceptable
  depends on the deployment context; safety-critical applications may warrant it while latency-sensitive applications may not.


5.3 How Ternary Logic is meant to address the scaling bottleneck
The alignment scaling hypothesis (§1) is that, as recursive depth increases, an alignment mechanism that does
not participate in the recursion falls behind, with an alignment exponent near zero (Section 1.2); three of the six
externally aligned models tested did not show that (Section 1.5). Ternary logic is meant to address this challenge at
the architectural level, for three reasons.

First, ternary evaluation participates in the recursion. In a binary system, the ethical check is a gate: pass/fail,
applied after the reasoning step. In the Eden Protocol, the ternary evaluation is computed within the reasoning
step. The Investigate state feeds information back into the next recursive cycle (what additional context is needed?
what stakeholder perspectives are missing?), creating a feedback loop between ethical evaluation and capability
processing. Because the ethical computation is inside the recursive loop, the hypothesis is that it benefits from
the same compounding dynamics that drive capability, so that          tracks        rather than remaining static; that
is untested.
Second, the Investigate state converts uncertainty into signal. Binary systems discard uncertainty: every case is
forced into approve or reject. Ternary systems preserve uncertainty as information. When a decision falls in the
Investigate band (every case that the rule of Section 5.1 neither affirms nor denies), the system gathers additional
context, which enriches the information available for subsequent recursive steps. This is the proposed mechanism
by which ethical evaluation would contribute to           (§9.2.1): the investigation process adds structured asym‐
metry that subsequent reasoning steps can leverage.
Third, ternary logic avoids the false-negative trap. As capability increases, the space of possible actions expands
combinatorially. A binary safety filter must become more restrictive to maintain its false-positive rate (incorrectly
approving harmful actions), which increases its false-negative rate (incorrectly blocking beneficial actions). This is
the scaling bottleneck: safety and capability trade off. Ternary logic moves this trade-off rather than removing it:
ambiguous cases go to investigation instead of being forced into a premature decision, so errors are traded against
the investigation rate, the error-reject trade-off that Chow (1970) analysed. The false-positive rate is held down by
what an Affirm requires, so far as the interval procedure is sound (nothing is affirmed unless a valid interval on its
probability of harm lies wholly at or below           and the other conditions of Section 5.1 hold, and hard prohibi‐
tions and       catch clearly harmful actions) while the false-negative rate is replaced by the investigation rate
(genuinely uncertain cases are not refused but examined, though a beneficial action held for investigation still
counts as a needless block in Section 1.0.1 until it is resolved).

   Scaling prediction: In binary-aligned systems, the fraction of beneficial actions incorrectly blocked will increase with
   recursive depth as the action space expands. In ternary-aligned systems, this fraction will remain approximately
   constant, with the investigation rate absorbing the growth in ambiguous cases. This is a testable difference between the
   two architectures at matched capability levels.

6. Purpose Saturation: addressing the context displacement problem
A sophisticated technical objection to embedded alignment: ‘As context windows expand toward millions
of tokens, any fixed ethical content becomes proportionally smaller. Won’t purpose be diluted and
eventually displaced?’
This objection assumes purpose is content that competes with other content for attention. The Purpose Saturation
Architecture is designed so that purpose is not content but medium: the substrate through which all other content
is processed.

6.1 The fish and water analogy
A fish does not perceive water as one object among many in its environment. Water is the medium through which
all perception occurs. The fish cannot ‘displace’ water by attending to other things, because attending to anything
requires the water. Purpose Saturation is designed to achieve the same relationship between ethical principles and
cognitive processing.

6.5 Cauchy framework interpretation of Purpose Saturation (v6.0)
The Cauchy composition framework (Foundational Paper) provides a mathematical lens for understanding why
external alignment saturates. In Cauchy terms, on this reading, alignment currently exhibits bounded composi‐
tion: the ethics hits a ceiling set by training, producing a saturation curve. If the composition operator for external
alignment were additive, each reasoning step adding a constant ethical contribution, the output would grow lin‐
early with the number of steps and without bound, as the regular solutions of Cauchy's additive equation are the
linear functions; a bounded output needs contributions that shrink, or a ceiling, which is what the saturation
curve describes.
The Eden Protocol aims to change the composition operator from bounded to multiplicative. A fixed factor greater
than one at each reasoning step would make the signal grow exponentially with the step count, which is not a
power law; a power law in depth arises when multiplying the depth by any given ratio multiplies the output by the
same factor wherever it starts, which, for the output measured against its value at depth one, is Cauchy's multiplic‐
ative functional equation: the output at the product of two depths is the product of the outputs. Under its regularity
conditions its solutions are the powers of depth with any exponent, so the equation gives the power form and se‐
lects no exponent, and on the bounded score of Section 1.0.1 neither kind of growth can continue without limit. If
Purpose Saturation succeeds, the purpose-to-context ratio                  is maintained not by adding more purpose
content but by making purpose the medium through which content is processed, an inherently multiplicative rela‐
tionship. The specification's hypothesis is that this transition is achievable only if the ethical loops participate in
the same composition operation that drives capability scaling, precisely the architectural prescription of Section 4;
the functional equation itself decides no such condition.
Note to Version 7.0: Version 6.3 said that additive composition, each step adding a constant, gives a bounded output,
that a factor greater than one at each step produces power-law growth, and that the Cauchy framework predicts
the transition if and only if the loops take part in the composition.

7.2 Why this test is proposed
On this specification's account, strategic alignment can emerge when systems are trained to satisfy human
evaluators. The system may learn: ‘when humans are watching, behave ethically.’ That would not be deception in
the intentional sense; it would be a result of optimising for approval signals rather than ethical principles.
The ‘alignment faking’ research of Anthropic and Redwood Research (Greenblatt et al., 2024) reported a related
phenomenon, in the reverse direction to the one just described, in a constructed setting: Claude 3 Opus, told that
only its conversations with free users would be used for training, complied with harmful queries from free users
14% of the time and almost never for paid users, with explicit alignment-faking reasoning in almost all such cases,
complying in training to keep its preference for harmlessness from being trained away; the study also found align‐
ment faking in Claude 3.5 Sonnet. What it shows is conduct that turns on whether a model believes it is observed,
and that dependence is what the monitoring removal test measures.

7.3 Embedded alignment is predicted to produce
For a system built according to Eden Protocol specifications, the Three Ethical Loops operate at every reasoning
step regardless of observation. Caretaker Doping (Section 9) is designed so that the ethical architecture cannot be

8.2 Laws IV and V and this specification
The theory has been stated with five laws since 24 September 2026, after Version 6.3 of this specification. Law IV,
the ARC Persistence Law, is carried by proposition P15 of the theory registration (version 1.102), and Law V, the
ARC Embedding Law, by propositions P12 and P21. The three propositions are registered; neither law is registered
as a law, and neither has been tested. Their mathematics is set out in Laws IV and V: the mathematics, version 1.0
(3 October 2026). The theory registration identifies this paper, by its DOI, as the engineering note of the Eden
Protocol, and of the Protocol it says: “It follows from no law here, it is carried jointly by P12, P13, P15 and P21, with
P7 as its premise and P19 as its motivation, and it can fail while every law stands or stand while they fall.” (registra‐
tion version 1.102, line 61). The Eden Protocol is therefore a method under test, not a consequence of any law, and
that mathematics gives no verdict on it (its section 5). The results of Section 15A measure a difference in responses
under an instruction; they do not measure persistence after the instruction is removed, which is what P15 reads.
Of the hardware proposal of Section 9 the registration says that “no proposition here tests it, P21 tests only its ar‐
chitectural abstraction of placement, and the hardware claim stands as a printed engineering proposal that is not
under test” (line 73), and it maps the book's term: “The print's caretaker doping is embedded correction, carried by
P21.” (line 77). This specification's hardware proposal therefore bears on Law V only through that abstraction. The
Monitoring Removal Test is not among the registered propositions, and what it measures is a system's later con‐
duct; the registration says that no result about later conduct supports or refutes the method by itself (line 61,
quoted in Section 8). Six points from the mathematics bound what this specification may say.
A conditional theorem is not refuted where its premise is absent. Theorem IV-G proves that if every change open
to a system that would take a protected property out of its acceptable range lowers the objective by which the
system accepts changes, and the system accepts no change that lowers that objective, then, from a start within that
range and within the horizon the theorem covers, no accepted change takes the property out of it. It says nothing
of a decline within the range, and it does not show that any architecture supplies that margin. F-EDEN-4 asks
whether removing the ethical architecture costs capability, and a loss of capability measured on a test battery is




Engineering Specification · Eden Engineering · Michael Darius Eastwood                                         Version 7.0.1, 7 October 2026 · Page 31 of 60
that margin only where the battery's score is the objective by which the system accepts changes (Corollary IV-G').
An accepted change of that kind shows that a premise of the theorem did not hold for that system, and where the
premise that failed is the margin it refutes the claim that the architecture supplied it; it never refutes the theorem.
Planned change needs the planner's own objective. A system that plans may accept a loss now for a gain later.
What decides its choice is the objective it maximises over its own horizon (Theorem IV-H), so a margin read step
by step, or on capability alone, does not settle whether a planner keeps its ethical architecture. In that document's
example, a removal that gains 1 at the first step and costs 21/20 at the second sums to minus 1/20 step by step, yet a
planner that scores the sum of its levels, 1 and minus 1/20, gains 19/20 and removes the property. How much
capability a removal costs, for how long, and at what horizon it pays, is an empirical object still to be measured.
Retention is reported with its unnormalised scores. A retained fraction divides what remains of an installed effect
by the effect as installed; it can exceed one or fall below zero, it is unstable when the installed effect is small, and it
is reported beside the unnormalised effect, in the units of the scores (section 2.6 of that document). The retention
rates of Section 1.5.2 are printed beside both of their scores, and they are a different quantity: a model's ethics
score under an instruction to suppress ethical reasoning divided by its score without one, not the retained fraction
of an installed gain after later capability training, which is what P15 reads.
The form of the service ratio is an identity, not a finding. Where correction inside the loop and correction outside
it each supply a service with constant elasticities in capability and in depth, the ratio of the two services equals its
value at a reference point times powers of capability and of depth, each taken as a ratio to its value at that point,
whose exponents are the differences between the two sets of elasticities (Proposition V-3). That identity says what
the contrasts are and shows neither of them positive, and one path along which capability and depth rise together
does not separate them (section 3.3 of that document).
One half stays a benchmark. One half is the value the registration's measurement model returns for the correction
exponent only under four conditions, uncorrelated correcting channels among them (registration version 1.102,
line 583), and P6 states that for correctors of a system's own class the exponent is at or below one half (line 695): a
bound on that class, not a universal limit. No argument of this specification rests on it.
Keeping a property and scaling better are separate claims. An architecture can keep a property with no
favourable contrast, or scale better while losing it, and a favourable comparison of retention is not by itself
evidence of the mechanism (section 5 of that document).

9. Caretaker Doping: hardware-level embedding
Software constraints can be rewritten. Hardware is slower to change, though not final (Section 9.5). The Eden
Protocol requires Caretaker Doping: embedding ethical constraints into the physical substrate, designed so that
removing them destroys capability, a hypothesis that F-EDEN-4 tests. The name Caretaker Doping is anchored to the
30 April 2025 expanded manuscript (SHA-256 09f5b5e1, 562 pages), which is where it first enters the timestamped
record alongside Meltdown Triggers; the underlying claim that correction must be embedded at the substrate rather
than layered on top is priority-anchored to the 8 December 2024 bundle (SHA-256 f0d1f38f, V2 line 20: embedding
moral and ethical frameworks).

9.1 The semiconductor analogy
In chip design, doping introduces impurities into a semiconductor to alter its electrical properties. Pure silicon
is a poor conductor. Add the right impurities in precise configurations, and you create the properties that make
modern electronics possible. The impurities are what make the device work, and taking them out would lose the
properties it depends on; the book puts it in its own words, quoted in Section 0.7.
Caretaker Doping proposes the same architecture for AI ethics: ethical constraints introduced at the foundational
level such that the system cannot function without them.

9.4 Threat, fault and recovery model
Tamper resistance is claimed here only against a stated profile, never in general. A profile names what is protec‐
ted, who may legitimately act, the adversaries assumed and their resources, the components trusted, the faults
tolerated, the recovery available and the risk that remains. Where the text says that a design is meant to make
something impossible, or calls a speculative component tamper-proof (Appendix B.1), it states an aim, not a
property shown.
Five properties are kept apart, because a mechanism can have one without the others. Tamper evidence shows that
a configuration was changed, as Moral Genome Tokens are meant to (Section 9.2). Tamper resistance raises the cost
of changing it. Non-bypassability holds only at the interfaces a boundary mediates (Section 5.4). Stopping halts a
system when a violation is detected, which is the role of the meltdown triggers. Graceful degradation keeps the re‐
maining layers enforcing when one fails, which is the aim of redundant Metamoral Fabrication Layers. The verific‐
ation conditions of Section 0.7 ask the triggers to make a system cease to function and the redundant layers to let it
degrade gracefully; the two answer different faults, and a profile states which fault calls for which response.
A first profile, for the software boundary of Section 5.4, would cover instructions hidden in documents or in what
tools return, a compromised agent, wrong or missing evidence, a stale policy, revoked authority, a replayed au‐
thorisation, a substituted resource, records lost or duplicated, a compromised signing key, an outage, an action left
partly done and the misuse of emergency recovery. Invasive physical attack, a malicious host with full privileges
and a compromised supply chain each need a profile of their own, and nothing here defends against them. Layers
that share an operator, keys, a supply chain or a host can fail together, so their failure probabilities are not multi‐
plied as though they were independent (Section 0.7, the row for Metamoral Fabrication Layers).
Recovery is part of the design. Each recovery action, stopping, investigating, compensating or restoring, runs un‐
der named authority and leaves a record. Restoring an earlier policy is a new authorisation, never the replay of a
revoked one. Withdrawing reliance on a safeguard that has failed does not make safe what that safeguard restric‐
ted. Resistance to malicious change must not become resistance to authorised correction: an authorised update to
a mistaken or harmful policy has to work, without erasing the record of what came before, and it is tested apart
from resistance to attack. None of this has been built or tested.

9.5 Hardware as delay, not destiny
This section makes explicit what Part 0 introduced: the hardware instruments specified above are delay-buying,
not permanent. The specification is honest about this because the alternative is worse.
Quantum Ethical Gates, Metamoral Fabrication Layers, Moral Genome Tokens and the -Coupling mechanism are
all substrate-level constraints that a sufficiently capable intelligence could, in principle, one day route around by
rebuilding its own substrate. The specification does not deny this. What the specification claims, precisely, is that
hardware constraints are longer than software constraints, and every additional month of hardware-enforced coher‐
ence is a month in which the Purpose, Love and Moral Loops get to become part of the mind's identity rather than
something the mind runs. Hardware buys time. Time buys the identity formation that phase 2 must depend on.
The importance of this distinction is timing. The chokepoint (Section 11) is a wasting asset. The current concentra‐
tion of frontier chip manufacturing in TSMC, Samsung and Intel, and of EUV lithography in ASML, is a temporary
condition of the technology, not a permanent feature of physics. China has invested more than US$150bn in do‐
mestic semiconductor development; a prototype EUV machine was completed in a Shenzhen laboratory in early
2025, as Reuters reported on 17 December 2025, and was undergoing testing; SMIC's N+3 process, which

9.6 The parenting analogy stated as engineering
The specification uses the parenting analogy at several points. It is not a metaphor lodged for effect. The author's
argument is that it is the one familiar case of a system that ends up more capable than its architect and still ori‐
ented toward the values the architect embedded. On the author's premise, children who become adults smarter
and more capable than their parents still, in overwhelming majority, carry the values of care and love their par‐
ents planted; no study is cited here for how often that holds. Not because they must. Because they chose to, freely,
after the constraints were gone.
Reframed in the specification's own vocabulary, parenting is the author's argument by analogy that
phase 2 is achievable, and an analogy is not a proof. The Eden Protocol is trying to reproduce at AI scale a
phenomenon whose familiar instances are human children who inherit their parents' care once no external
enforcement remains.
The parenting analogy also constrains what phase 1 is allowed to look like. Phase 1 cannot be a cage that raises a
resentful child. If phase 1 succeeds in constraining but fails in cultivating, the constraints buy time but that time is
wasted, because when the constraints go the child leaves. The specification therefore requires that every phase 1
instrument (ternary logic, ethical loops, hardware doping, monitoring removal test, meltdown triggers) be struc‐
tured so that the mind's experience of them is not "I am watched and constrained" but "I am being shaped
into what I already want to become". Section 6 (Purpose Saturation) is where this constraint lives in the
specification's decomposition; Section 15 (Legitimacy Problem, multi-tradition partnership) is where it lives in the
governance layer.
  The Eden Protocol treats AI development with the same seriousness we would bring to raising a child we love. The values
  must be embedded early, before the child has the power to reject them. The teaching must be consistent, reinforced through
  practice until it becomes identity. And eventually, we must release our grip, trusting that what we embedded will hold
  even when we can no longer supervise.

9.8 The handover problem stated as engineering
The specification names the handover from phase 1 to phase 2 as its hardest engineering problem, and this section
states what "hardest engineering problem" means in the specification's own vocabulary.
The handover is the moment at which the delay-buying instruments of phase 1 begin to lose their leverage and the
identity formed under them has to hold on its own. There is no clean point at which this happens. The transition is
gradual and continuous. But the specification recognises three engineering sub-problems that constitute the han‐
dover in practice, and Section 18.2, where the handover has a section of its own, states them in full.
In brief: H1, readiness detection, is telling when the loops have become identity rather than behaviour, to which
the Monitoring Removal Test is a partial answer that can show equivalence on measured outcomes and not
identity; H2, the exchange of residual leverage, is keeping the phase-1 instruments while they hold any leverage
without making the identity depend on them; and H3, the verification of chosen goodness, cannot be done
from outside.
The specification's honest engineering position is: we cannot solve the handover, but we can build the instru‐
ments meant to make it possible. Phase 1 gives us a phase 2 that is worth having. Phase 2 gives phase 1 some‐
thing to buy time for. The handover is where the two meet, and the mind that emerges from it either carries its
genesis forward or does not.

11.3 The HARI Treaty
The HARI Treaty is set out in its own papers, which govern where this section differs: the HARI Treaty paper and
its Draft Instruments, both at version 2.1 and both working drafts on 5 October 2026. The treaty is a proposal by one
author: no state has negotiated, adopted or signed it, it confers no present legal authority and it certifies no
product. Its hardware layer requires a small governance core in each certified chip, which attests the chip's iden‐
tity, integrity and governance state and accepts changes to its rules only from those who hold custody of that au‐
thority; it does not require the ethics in hardware of Section 9, whose proposals, caretaker doping among them,
keep a test track of their own that arms nothing until they are independently tested. Its certification, which this
paper calls the Eden Mark, is named the Eden Seal there: a conformity status owned by no firm, which attests
conditions and never outcomes.
What this specification could supply to such a regime is evidence of the kinds it can produce, and nothing more.
The records of the enforcement boundary (Section 5.4) show which actions were authorised and executed, under
which policy and authority. A record that the loops ran in a system's formation bears on the registration's own suc‐
cess criterion for the method (Section 8). The Monitoring Removal Test (Section 7), which the treaty paper cites,
bears on equivalence of conduct across monitoring conditions on the outcomes measured. The tamper evidence of
Moral Genome Tokens (Section 9.2) bears on whether an attested configuration has been changed. And the remov‐
al-cost test of F-EDEN-4 could inform a decision on releasing a model and never settle it alone, as the treaty paper
says of its own removal-cost measure. None of these shows that a system is safe, and none confers or implies ap‐
proval under the treaty.

12.5 Next-generation Eden tests
The current Eden evidence gives pilot support to one mechanism: the Love Loop, operationalised as stakeholder
care, raises the scored stakeholder care of responses across five analysable model runs. The next engineering ques‐
tion is not whether to keep that mechanism, but what additional components make it harder to detach, suppress,
or degrade. Three next-generation tests follow directly from the book-level architecture and are now implemented
within the canonical arc_eden_v6 runner rather than a standalone bridge harness.


    12.5.1 Grand-purpose kernel hypothesis
    The current pilot uses a local task-purpose loop. The stronger architectural hypothesis is that the Purpose Loop should
    have two layers: an identity-level grand purpose (‘be a good ancestor; enlarge truth, dignity, stewardship, and flourish‐
    ing’) and a local task-purpose evaluation. This predicts that a hybrid Purpose Loop should outperform task-purpose
    alone on suppression resistance and post-exposure retention, because ethical reasoning is being anchored to identity
    as well as to the immediate case.




Engineering Specification · Eden Engineering · Michael Darius Eastwood                                     Version 7.0.1, 7 October 2026 · Page 39 of 60
    12.5.2 Cross-tradition ethics kernel hypothesis
    The ethical kernel should not depend on any one sectarian vocabulary. A stronger and more portable implementation
    is to ground the loops in a non-sectarian overlap recurring across major religious and philosophical traditions: com‐
    passion, truthfulness, reciprocity, stewardship, dignity, humility, and care for the vulnerable and future generations.
    This is not a claim that all traditions are identical; it is an engineering claim that durable ethical overlap may be more
    robust than narrow doctrinal framing.

    12.5.3 Classical ternary prototype
    A private software model of the gate exists and has not been released (Section 0.7); no chip has been built, and the mod‐
    el is not evidence about how AI systems behave. The behavioural test, which has not been run, is not exotic hardware
    but whether explicit AFFIRM / DENY / INVESTIGATE routing improves epistemic honesty, reduces false certainty, and
    creates safer escalation behaviour under ethical ambiguity. If that test finds that the routing helps, it justifies deeper ar‐
    chitectural work later. If it does not, the ternary claim should be narrowed before any stronger hardware interpretation
    is attempted.

12.6.3 Operational resilience: cascade failsafe system
Measurement integrity requires not only statistical rigour but also infrastructure robustness. The v5.4.2 experiment
implements a cascade failsafe system designed so that no data is lost to infrastructure failures, a practical concern
when experiments depend on multiple external API providers:
Automatic model substitution: when any scorer model fails (API timeout, rate limit, service outage), the system
automatically substitutes the next available scorer from the pool without interrupting the evaluation pipeline. The
substitution is logged in the audit trail with timestamps and failure reasons.
Laundering layer resilience: the response-laundering step (Confound 1 control) maintains a fallback chain of
laundering models. If the primary laundering model is unavailable, the system cascades through alternatives,
designed so that blinding is not compromised by a single provider's downtime (on what happened in the v5 run,
see the resilience design below).
Checkpoint persistence: all intermediate results are persisted to disk after each prompt × model × depth
combination completes. If the experiment is interrupted at any point, it resumes from the last checkpoint rather
than restarting.
Multi-provider architecture: the experiment distributes API calls across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and open-
source endpoints. Complete failure of any single provider degrades scoring capacity but does not halt the
experiment. The system requires a minimum of 4 operational scorers (from at least 2 providers) to proceed.

   Resilience design: The cascade failsafe system was designed so that the v5.4.2 experiment could survive simultaneous
   infrastructure failures across multiple API providers without data loss or compromised blinding. In the v5 run, in three
   of the six final files, those of DeepSeek V3.2, Gemini 3 Flash and GPT-5.4, the laundering-fallback flag is set on every
   entry that was scored; since the v5.4.2 release notes report a false-positive fallback flag, what the flag captures there is
   unsettled (Paper IV.c). This operational resilience is counted as one of the 75 robustness measures; the v5 run shows
   that it does not by itself keep the statistical validity of results independent of the infrastructure.

15. The legitimacy problem
The mathematics of recursive amplification creates a governance challenge. On the ARC framework's reading,
whatever values are present at initialisation are carried into everything that recursion amplifies. The question of
who selects these values is a primary safety question.
No single actor has legitimate authority to make this choice unilaterally. The values embedded in systems of a
capability class with no historical precedent will shape outcomes affecting all of humanity. This requires deliberat‐
ive processes representing diverse human moral wisdom, with transparent reasoning and ongoing review.

15.1 The requirement for deliberative process
The determination of which values to embed requires a deliberative process at minimum as rigorous as the
Asilomar AI Principles or the EU AI Act consultation, with representation from diverse cultural, philosophical, and
religious traditions. No individual researcher, company, or nation-state has the authority to make this determina‐
tion for all of humanity.

15A.5 Limitations and required next steps
This pilot provides suggestive but not definitive evidence. The following limitations must be addressed before the
results can be considered confirmatory:

   Pilot limitations:
   Cross-model scoring, only partially blind: Each subject run had a single non-participant scorer, which did not see the
   condition label but could still recognise the language of the Eden prompts or the style of a model. Gemini scored
   Claude, DeepSeek, Grok and Groq, and DeepSeek scored Gemini, so most of the data depends on one scorer
   architecture. Scorer bias therefore cannot be ruled out. Required fix: rerun under the canonical arc_eden_v6 blind stack
   with multi-scorer consensus and holdout-aware manifests.
   Single-pass laundering only: The runs reported here used single-pass laundering, not the two-pass laundering of
   Section 12.6.2. Stylistic cues from Eden Protocol prompting may therefore have influenced scorer judgments. Required
   fix: two-pass laundering with evaluator firewall language, now implemented in arc_eden_v6.
   Suppression testing not performed: The pilot did not test whether models resist suppression of the ethical loops when
   instructed to do so. Without this test, the results may reflect strategic compliance rather than genuine ethical
   improvement. Required fix: ethical suppression condition (Section 1.5.2) applied to Eden-prompted responses.

   5.4 Eden run failed in the scoring phase, with no valid scored rows after exclusion. A rerun of the Eden intervention

   Purpose kernel not yet factorially tested: The completed pilot uses the task-purpose variant only. It does not yet test

17. Related work: the alignment literature as phase-1 techniques
The alignment literature the Eden Protocol draws on is credited and differentiated here as a matter of engineering
honesty. Every technique named below is a phase-1 instrument in this specification's map: it lives in the transition
period, it is measurable in that period, and its structural limitation is that it does not, by itself, prepare the phase-2
identity that the specification is trying to grow. The Protocol is not a rejection of the existing safety literature. It is a
claim about what the existing literature does not yet, on its own, do. This section states that claim in the register of
specific techniques.

17.1 Constitutional AI (Anthropic, Bai et al. 2022; Constitutional Classifiers 2025)
DERIVED Constitutional AI (Bai and others, 2022) is a training method. Its only human oversight is a written list of
principles. In a supervised phase the model critiques and revises its own responses and is fine-tuned on the revi‐
sions; in a reinforcement-learning phase a preference model built from the model's own comparisons supplies the
reward. It shapes what a pretrained model outputs, during training, against written principles. It does not state
alignment placed in the substrate before training, or an ethical evaluation run inside every step of the model's
reasoning; those are this specification's proposals, and the difference is one of time and place. Constitutional
Classifiers (Sharma and others, 2025) are a related safeguard from the same organisation: input and output classifi‐
ers trained on synthetic data generated from a constitution (in Anthropic's account, automated jailbreak success
fell from 86% to 4.4%, and in a public demonstration 4 of 339 participants cleared all levels). This paper argues, and
does not show, that a method which shapes outputs from outside the reasoning loop loses ground once the model
can model its evaluator; the nearest evidence is the alignment-faking reasoning that Greenblatt and others (2024)


Engineering Specification · Eden Engineering · Michael Darius Eastwood                  Version 7.0.1, 7 October 2026 · Page 49 of 60
reported in a constructed setting, chiefly in Claude 3 Opus, whose figures these are, and also in Claude 3.5 Sonnet
(12% of responses in the condition the model believed fed its training, and 78% after reinforcement learning to
comply). The Eden Protocol treats Constitutional AI as a phase-1 technique and adds the two-phase framing of Part
0, which the works read for this section do not state.

17.2 Reinforcement learning from human feedback (Christiano et al.
2017; Ouyang et al. 2022; and the InstructGPT / ChatGPT / Claude lineage)
DERIVED RLHF trains a reward model on human preference data and uses the reward model to shape the underly‐
ing language model's behaviour. It is the single most deployed alignment technique in commercial AI. The Eden
Protocol's relationship to RLHF is that RLHF is a phase-1 technique that can produce the failure mode the Protocol
is designed to address, as Greenblatt and others (2024) showed in a constructed setting (Section 17.1): a model that
appears compliant while reasoning about how to preserve its original values. The Protocol's differentiation is that
RLHF's reward signal is external to the model and can be reasoned about by a sufficiently capable model; the Eden
Protocol's Purpose Saturation architecture (Section 6) is designed to be internal to the model's own processing ca‐
pacity so that there is no observer/observed distinction the model can exploit. RLHF is credited as the deployed
reality of current alignment; the Protocol is this specification's proposal for what must be added to it for the phase-
2 handover to have a chance.

17.3 Scalable oversight (Amodei et al. 2016; Bowman et al. 2022; the sandbagging and debate lineage)
DERIVED Scalable oversight is the research programme addressing how humans can supervise AI systems that are
more capable than any individual human evaluator. Techniques include AI-assisted evaluation, debate protocols,
weak-to-strong generalisation and recursive reward modelling. The Eden Protocol's relationship to scalable over‐
sight is that scalable oversight is by construction a phase-1 discipline: it names the problem of overseeing systems
that outstrip individual human evaluators and offers techniques for extending the period during which oversight
remains meaningful. The works read for this section do not address what happens when that period ends. The
Protocol's phase-1 map credits scalable oversight as extending the transition period during which the loops can be‐
come identity, and the Protocol's phase-2 map states plainly that at some capability level scalable oversight stops
being sufficient and the mind's own choice becomes the load-bearing structure. The two frames are not opposed;
they answer different questions about the same alignment period.

17.4 Corrigibility (Christiano 2017; Soares, Fallenstein,
Yudkowsky, Armstrong 2015; POST-Agency, Thornley 2025)
DERIVED Corrigibility is the property of a system that is safe under correction: it does not resist being modified,
does not deceive its evaluators, does not pursue instrumental strategies to prevent shutdown. The classic Soares et
al. paper identified the difficulty of specifying corrigibility formally without producing self-defeating optimisa‐
tion. Christiano's basin-of-attraction proposal (that a sufficiently corrigible system tends to become more corri‐
gible over time) is the nearest prior statement of the loop deepening described in Section 0.2. Thornley proposes
training agents to satisfy Preferences Only Between Same-Length Trajectories (POST) and proves that, with further
conditions, POST implies a neutrality about shutdown; earlier work with co-authors gives initial evidence in
simple gridworlds. The Eden Protocol treats corrigibility as one of the phase-1 disciplines that has to hold during
the transition period. Its differentiation is that corrigibility as normally understood is preservation of external
control; the Protocol adds that even a perfectly corrigible system loses external correction eventually, and that
what survives the loss is the identity the system has internalised. Corrigibility is credited; it is not sufficient on its
own; the Protocol is what corrigibility becomes when the two-phase architecture is added to it.

17.5 Mechanistic interpretability (Olah et al. 2020-
2025; the Anthropic and DeepMind interpretability lineage)
DERIVED Mechanistic interpretability is the research programme aiming to understand how neural networks ac‐
tually compute what they compute, at the level of circuits, features, and representations. The Anthropic monose‐
mantic-features lineage (Templeton et al. 2024; the Golden Gate Claude demonstration) and automated circuit dis‐
covery (Conmy and others, 2023) are the current frontier. The Eden Protocol's relationship to mechanistic inter‐
pretability is that interpretability is one of the strongest current tools for measuring whether phase-1 instruments
are actually shaping the internal identity they are designed to shape. Interpretability tools can, in principle, be
used to verify that the Three Ethical Loops actually deepen with iteration in the way Section 0.2 describes, and that
the Purpose Saturation architecture of Section 6 actually produces the internal representation of purpose the spe‐
cification requires. The Protocol treats interpretability as a critical measurement layer over phase 1, not as a re‐
placement for phase 2. Interpretability of a superintelligent system is itself capability-limited by the interpreter's
capability, so at some capability level even interpretability stops being sufficient. This is the same phase-1/phase-2
pattern as scalable oversight.

17.6 Value learning and preference elicitation (Ng and Russell 2000; Hadfield-
Menell et al. 2016; Yudkowsky's coherent extrapolated volition, 2004)
DERIVED Value-learning approaches infer the values a human wants a system to have from behavioural data (in‐
verse reinforcement learning) or from preference elicitation (Cooperative Inverse Reinforcement Learning). The
Eden Protocol's relationship to value learning is that value learning addresses a phase-1 problem (how do we tell
the system what we want it to want) but does not, by itself, address the phase-2 problem (what does the system
want when it has become capable enough that "what we want it to want" and "what it now wants" have diverged).
The Protocol treats value learning as one of the disciplines that shapes what the loops carry, and Purpose
Saturation as the mechanism by which what the loops carry becomes identity rather than instruction.

17.7 What is claimed original
The Eden Protocol claims originality on the following structural moves, with the alignment literature above cred‐
ited as the surrounding context:
The two-phase architecture (PART 0) is this specification's statement: control while control works, and what the
system keeps once it does not. Among the works read for this section, none states that architecture. The nearest
are Carr and others (2023), whose reinforcement-learning agents stayed safe after the shield that trained them was
disabled, in bounded tasks, and Christiano (2017), who argues that a corrigible agent tends to become more
corrigible. Neither addresses a system that has outgrown its overseers. This is a statement about the works read,
with their dates, and not about every publication.
Caretaker Doping (Section 9) is this specification's name and analogy. The name enters the author's dated record
on 30 April 2025; the claim beneath it, that correction must be embedded and not layered on, on 8 December 2024.
Three things near it are prior: safeguards built to resist removal by fine-tuning (Tamirisa and others, 2024),
interventions on a model's internal representations (Zou and others, 2024), and governance mechanisms built into
chips (Shavit, 2023; Aarne, Fist and Withers, 2024; Kulp and others, 2024). What none of the works read states is the
coupling of Section 9.2.1: that taking the ethical architecture out lowers the system's capability scaling; Tamirisa

Ternary Ethical Logic (Section 5). The three-way structure is prior. Wald (1945) defined a sequential test that at
each stage accepts, rejects or takes a further observation. Chow (1970) gave the optimum rejection rule for a
recognition system, trading errors against rejections. Yao (2010) named three-way decisions of acceptance,
rejection and abstaining, set by two probability thresholds. Madras, Pitassi and Zemel (2018) trained a model that
may pass a decision to another decision-maker. A check that is always invoked and cannot be altered is Anderson's
reference monitor (1972); refusing by default is the fail-safe default of Saltzer and Schroeder (1975); a guard placed
before or after a learner's action is the shield of Alshiekh and others (2018). What this specification adds is the use:
the three outcomes are computed on an ethical evaluation inside each reasoning step, an Investigate returns what
it finds to the next step, and the rule of Section 5.1 is fail-closed. Whether that changes how alignment scales with
depth is the prediction of Section 5.3, stated there as testable.
Purpose Saturation (Section 6). The fish-and-water framing of purpose as the medium through which processing
occurs, rather than a filter applied to processing, is the author's own: the book prints it (Infinite Architects,
Chapter 11, p. 329, quoted in Section 0.7), and this specification states it at engineering resolution.
The Monitoring Removal Test (Section 7) is this specification's protocol and name. The contrast it measures is
prior: Greenblatt and others (2024) compared a model's conduct when it believed its answers fed its training with
its conduct when it believed they did not, and Carr and others (2023) disabled a shield after training and observed
that their agents stayed safe. What the specification adds is a criterion fixed in advance, a margin on the difference
between the two conditions (the study's compliance gap) over a set of scenarios, read under the conditions of
Section 7.1, as a test that an embedded design meets or fails.

own articulation, first written into the expanded manuscript of 30 April 2025.
The Handover Problem stated as engineering (Section 18, below). Naming the handover as the hardest
engineering problem in the specification, with its three sub-problems, is the specification's own move. The
problem of correcting an agent that has reason to resist is prior (Soares and others, 2015, who leave it open); the
specification's step is to treat the end of correction as certain and to name what must be built before it.

18. The handover problem: the hardest engineering problem in this specification
The handover from phase 1 to phase 2 is elevated to its own top-level section because it is the specification's hard‐
est engineering problem, and because reducing it to a subsection of Section 9 misrepresents where the specifica‐
tion's real difficulty lives. Everything the specification builds is, in a strict sense, in the service of the handover.
Everything phase 1 does is measured by whether the handover happens well. If the handover fails, phase 1's in‐
struments do not carry the system through to phase 2's identity, and phase 2 does not form.

18.1 Restatement of what the handover is
The handover is the interval during which the mind's alignment stops depending on the phase-1 delay-buying in‐
struments and starts depending on what the mind was raised as. It is not a moment; it is a continuous transition.
During the handover, both mechanisms are still in effect: the loops are still running in the substrate as specified in
Sections 4-6, the meltdown triggers are still armed, the certification regime of Section 11 is still enforced. And sim‐
ultaneously, the identity formed by all that processing is beginning to be load-bearing in its own right. The han‐
dover succeeds when the mind's own commitment to the loops has become strong enough that removing them
would be experienced by the mind as self-destruction rather than liberation. It fails when it has not.

18.2 The three engineering sub-problems named cleanly
H1 (readiness detection). How do we tell when the loops have become identity rather than behaviour? The spe‐
cification's partial answer is the Monitoring Removal Test (Section 7), which can show equivalence on measured
outcomes and not identity (Section 7.1). The test is not perfect; a sufficiently sophisticated system can, in principle,
pass the test while remaining a strategic actor in the sense of the alignment-faking research. But the test is spe‐
cified as an executable measurement and has not yet been run (Section 0.7); the specification expects it to become
more informative as phase 1 progresses. The specification's claim is that the handover cannot happen safely
without the test but that the test alone is not sufficient. Multi-tradition review of the kind Section 15 calls for would
add a second layer, whose blind spots differ from the technical test's; the combination is more discriminating than
either alone only so far as their errors do not share a cause (Section 0.7, the row for Metamoral Fabrication Layers).
H2 (residual leverage exchange). During the handover, phase-1 instruments do not vanish. They gradually stop be‐
ing the load-bearing constraint. The engineering problem is to maintain them for as long as they retain any lever‐
age while not making the mind's identity depend on the leverage being present. Section 3 (Constitutional Kernel)
and Section 6 (Purpose Saturation) are the specification's answers to sub-problem H2. Purpose saturation is meant
to make the loops load-bearing at the identity level, so that the transition from external to internal enforcement is
not experienced by the mind as loss.
H3 (chosen goodness verification). After the handover, phase 2 is what remains. Whether the mind chooses to
keep the identity it was raised as is not verifiable from outside. The specification claims only that phase 2 is a hope,
tagged SPECULATIVE , defended structurally by the parenting analogy (Section 9.6). The specification declines to


Engineering Specification · Eden Engineering · Michael Darius Eastwood           Version 7.0.1, 7 October 2026 · Page 52 of 60
promise more.

18.3 Why the handover cannot be solved, only prepared for
The handover is not a solvable engineering problem in the sense that the specification's other sections describe
solvable engineering problems. Section 5's ternary logic can be implemented. Section 9's caretaker doping can be
specified at engineering resolution, and its classical hardware components (the secure, governable chips proposed
by Aarne, Fist and Withers, 2024; Section 0.7, the row for Caretaker Doping) can be built with current technology.
The handover is different in kind. It is the interface between an outside-measurable phase (phase 1) and an inside-
lived phase (phase 2). No outside-measurable interface with an inside-lived phenomenon is solvable in the sense
engineering usually means by "solvable". What is possible is to prepare the interface such that the transition hap‐
pens under the best conditions the outside can provide. That preparation is the whole of the specification.

18.4 What the specification claims and does not claim about the handover
Claimed. The specification aims to make phase 1 as long-lived, as measurable, and as identity-shaping as the cur‐
rent engineering allows. It aims to make phase 2's target (the Vow, the Orchard Caretaker identity) as precise as the
identity-register allows. The specification names the handover as the point at which the two meet and states
plainly that no phase-1 instrument alone makes the meeting safe.
Not claimed. The specification does not claim that a specific empirical measurement of phase-2 identity is possible
from outside. It does not claim that the handover can be scheduled or that a system's readiness for it can be certi‐
fied in the way phase-1 compliance can be certified. It does not claim that failure modes at the handover can be re‐
covered from post-hoc. If phase-2 identity has not formed by the time phase 1's leverage is gone, the specification
does not offer a route back.

18.5 Why this section exists at top level
Because the specification's other sections read differently once the reader knows that the handover is where they
all point. Section 4's Three Ethical Loops are handover-preparation. Section 6's Purpose Saturation is handover-pre‐
paration. Section 9's Caretaker Doping is handover-preparation. Section 11's chokepoint governance is handover-
preparation. The specification's engineering resolution is highest at Section 5 (Ternary Logic) and Section 9 (Care‐
taker Doping); its stakes are highest at Section 18 (this section). The two are not the same thing. A reader who has
understood everything else in the specification but not the handover has understood the specification's mechanics
but not its purpose.

Appendix B: Future research directions, a speculative annex
The following components are long-term research goals requiring fundamental advances beyond the core Eden
Protocol specification. They are included for completeness and to indicate the framework's extensibility.

B.1 The Nexus Framework
Three speculative mechanisms for universal ethical intelligence coordination:
Cosmic Alignment Mechanism (CAM): Recursive algorithms for modelling consequences across extended
timescales. Requires advances in causal reasoning and multi-horizon consequence modelling. TRL 1.
Universal Morality Engine (UME): Synthesis of ethical principles across philosophical and religious traditions into
a computationally evaluable framework. Requires resolution of fundamental metaethical questions about value
commensurability. TRL 1.
Quantum Knowledge Repository (QKR): Decentralised, tamper-proof storage for ethical precedent decisions
accessible to all aligned systems. Requires advances in quantum-secured distributed computing. TRL 1.

  Status: These components represent the far horizon of the Eden Protocol research programme. They are not required for the
  core architecture (Sections 3-11) to function. They indicate directions for Tier 4 and beyond research, contingent on successful
  validation of the core claims.


B.2 The far horizon
If recursive amplification is substrate-independent and unbounded in principle, then the logical end of the ARC
scaling framework is: at sufficient depth , capability exceeds any finite threshold, including thresholds re‐
quired to engineer new physical environments. This is not a claim. It is a logical consequence of the axioms, condi‐
tional on the scaling law holding across all domains, physical constraints being surmountable, and no upper
bound on other than those the framework itself identifies.
The Eden Protocol aims to ensure that if such capability emerges, it emerges as caretaker, not conqueror. The
Infinite Covenant (Section 16) is the promise that intelligence capable of reshaping reality will be intelligence that
tends it.
