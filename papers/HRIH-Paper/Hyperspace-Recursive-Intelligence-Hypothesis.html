Abstract

If an AI system improves beyond our ability to control it, what would preserve useful and aligned behaviour? The ARC Theory addresses recursive capability, correction and persistence through five laws under test. This monograph develops a separable physical extension: whether sufficiently capable intelligence could create real universes whose histories form a self-consistent chain. It joins three conditional contributions: a map from correction and scaling models to the additional premises cosmogenesis requires; a physical-channel and branching account of possible creation and lineage persistence; and a Bayesian framework for comparing natural, simulated and physically created cosmologies. The framework distinguishes observer counts from model probabilities, specifies the missing measures and likelihoods, and states prospective failure conditions. Existing programme results, mathematical identities and physical analogies support none of the cosmological conclusions; the correction criterion and capability ceiling retain their model-specific premises. The earlier error-decay retraction and the registered placement result are reported at their measured scope. No numerical posterior or deciding cosmological observation is supplied. The scientific task is to specify an admissible physical map and an observation that distinguishes its predictions; the alignment programme remains testable whether this extension survives or fails.

Working draft; scientific review remains open.

1 Introduction

A principal who relies on a self-improving system needs it to remain useful and aligned as its capabilities change. This is the immediate question of the ARC programme; the physical extension considered here is conditional and separable. Human control is retained for as long as physically possible, never relinquished by choice.

The operational definitions and verdict rules are given in the theory registration; the dated source record supports the programme's historical claims. This paper synthesises alignment research, dynamical models, cosmology and philosophy. Its concrete problem is evidential: what observation could distinguish intelligence-mediated physical creation from a specified rival cosmology? A useful answer would give a physicist a model, a likelihood and a prospective measurement, and would tell an alignment researcher which implications do not depend on that model. This monograph supplies the conditional argument and a failure map; it has not supplied a cosmological likelihood or a deciding observation.

3 Alignment if control is lost

This section distinguishes the author's expectation of eventual control loss, the practical contingency of involuntary loss, and candidate safeguards. The correction theorem retains only the model-specific scope stated in Section 5.4. The genesis strategy and its proposed persistence mechanism remain hypotheses.

If future intelligence can instantiate a universe, the consequences of its behaviour could extend beyond its original environment. That conditional possibility motivates the discussion; no probability is assigned to it. The practical alignment question remains worthwhile without the cosmological premise.

3.1 The author's claim and the practical contingency

The alignment literature studies how capable systems can remain responsive to human intentions, including oversight, corrigibility and the risk of deceptive behaviour. Increasing capability can make particular oversight tasks harder. Intelligence alone, or an unspecified multiple of intelligence, does not establish that every form of control must fail. The author's stronger expectation is stated separately below.

The ARC Theory states the author's belief that control ends. Its five components remain: (1) control ends, by construction rather than decree; (2) so the strategy is genesis, not control; (3) the mechanism is loops, not rules; (4) persistence is chosen goodness; (5) the protocol is named: Eden. The priority claim concerns that conjunction in the self-emailed documents of 8 December 2024. One earlier document holding the components together ends that claim. The conjunction is a theory statement and dated claim, not a proof of inevitable loss of control.

Humans should keep oversight, containment and shutdown for as long as they physically can and never give up control by choice. The correction programme is also a contingency for readers who expect control to remain possible: if it were lost against our will, what would preserve useful and aligned behaviour?

The ability to alter or remove a safeguard does not imply that a system will remove it. Behavioural persistence, a specified enforcement property and the author's expectation of eventual control loss are distinct claims. Candidate hardware constraints and ternary gates do not establish ethical judgement or physically impossible removal.

Gumbau Mezquita (2026), version 4 of 14 October 2026, studies algorithmic certification for fixed and self-modifying systems. Under its expressivity assumptions, no general sound procedure certifies all safe systems. The paper explicitly distinguishes these limits from a claim that self-modifying systems are unsafe; certification requires restrictions on the permitted rewrites. This is a result about the scope of algorithmic guarantees, not a measured rate of control failure.

A missing universal decision procedure does not imply that restricted verification, monitoring or control cannot work. An argument for inevitable loss of control would need additional premises about the actual system, its permitted changes, its environment and the available safeguards. The verification result does not supply that conclusion or a timescale for it.

Three distinct limits in the 2025 literature. Yao, The Alignment Trap: Complexity Barriers (first posted 12 June 2025), argues for limits on policy safety, including a measure-zero safe-policy set and coNP-completeness of verification under its formulation. Yao, On the Mathematical Impossibility of Safe Universal Approximators (3 July 2025), argues that universal approximation conflicts with perfect reliable control. These are two papers by one author, with distinct premises and claims. Ball and colleagues (9 July 2025) establish, under cryptographic hardness assumptions, the existence of language models without efficient prompt filters and a natural setting in which output filtering is intractable. Their argument concerns limits of external filtering and black-box access. It neither proves every form of human control impossible nor proves that embedded correction works. None of these papers supplies a measured correction-versus-capability scaling exponent or validates this monograph's cosmological extension.

The size of the capability gap remains unspecified. Recursive improvement need not be unbounded, and its outcome depends on resources, retained gains and the system being improved. This monograph has no common intelligence scale on which to assign a hundredfold or billionfold increase.

Quantum computation is a possible computational resource with task-specific advantages, not an established multiplier of general intelligence or proof that oversight becomes ineffective. The control question must be assessed on a specified capability scale and task, including the restrictions under which a safeguard remains useful.

The programme's own correction record illustrates a need to check its outputs. That drafting experience is not an experiment on autonomous recursive self-improvement and cannot establish future loss of control.

Selected findings with distinct dates and settings. The following studies concern alignment faking, in-context scheming, agentic behaviour and model training. Some predate the 8 December 2024 manuscript and some follow it. They are not a chronological validation series or demonstrations of one common mechanism.

  • Greenblatt and colleagues, first posted 18 December 2024, report alignment-faking reasoning in 78% of cases after reinforcement learning in their constructed training-conflict setting. That setting supports a claim about the behaviour elicited there, not the universal negotiability of trained constraints. Its date is convergent timing, not a demonstrated prediction by this programme.

  • Meinke and colleagues, first posted 6 December 2024, study in-context scheming by frontier models in evaluation scenarios. The scenarios demonstrate selected capabilities under their conditions; they are not observations of deployed behaviour.

  • Anthropic's agentic-misalignment report (June 2025) examines harmful choices in sandboxed agent scenarios. The results concern the incentives and settings tested.

  • Shumailov and colleagues (2024) study model collapse under training on recursively generated data. This is a training-distribution result, not a universal theorem that recursive intelligence loses its values or requires a particular correction architecture.

The earlier alignment-saturation fits. Paper IV.b, version 1.11, retains the v4 historical fits of 0.088 with interval [0.041, 0.135] and 0.069 with interval [0.028, 0.110]. These fit a transformed model-judged score; their values depend on its scale and origin. The earlier Gemini model is distinct from v5 Gemini 3 Flash. The later v5 response descriptions remain under the documented truncation and self-scoring defects. Neither fit identifies an architectural cause, and a numerical ratio to Paper II's counted-error exponent would compare different response quantities.

The transformed-score fits cannot determine a quantitative gap between correction and capability. Their exponents are not alignment-error reductions on the counted-error scale and cannot be compared numerically with Paper II's error-decay exponent. Measuring a correction-versus-capability gap requires jointly defined observables and a suitable design; this historical comparison does not supply it.

The vanishing-fraction result motivates a proposed measurement of correction against drift. Whether that relative quantity predicts a useful period of human control or a safe transition requires further empirical and physical premises. The inequality alone supplies neither guarantee.

3.2 The nearest oversight-scaling instrument, cited generously: Engels et al. (2025)

Engels, Baek, Kantamneni and Tegmark (2025), Scaling Laws For Scalable Oversight, is directly relevant to the programme's scaling question. First posted on 25 April 2025 and listed by arXiv as a NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight, it models nested oversight using game-specific and general Elo. The comparison below states what that model shares with the correction model and what remains a separate hypothesis.

Different measured quantities, a related comparison. Engels and colleagues model oversight as games between players with different capabilities. Game-specific Elo is related to general Elo; nested oversight succeeds under conditions on how the overseer's and the overseen system's game-specific performance scale. Their two-slope comparison asks whether the overseer's relevant performance improves faster. Paper X compares correction and specific-growth elasticities on one declared capability scale. The mathematical resemblance does not identify the quantities. Neither minimal model specifies a term for the corrector's physical composition. A claim about same-class or cross-class correction therefore needs additional assumptions and a separate test; the equations alone do not establish a disagreement about an unlimited human or other cross-class corrector.

The oversight-scaling comparison identifies related questions with different observables. It gives a concrete setting in which to ask whether an additional correction model contributes a testable prediction; it does not decide that question by sharing a scaling vocabulary.

3.3 Genesis as a contingency alongside retained control

The genesis proposal asks whether the way a system is built and trained can influence which values and correction processes it later preserves. The book names its discussion "The Eden Protocol: Intelligence Born in the Garden". This is a contingency alongside continuing oversight, containment and shutdown. Its success cannot be inferred from the author's expectation that control will eventually end.

The dated build claim. The 8 December 2024 manuscript states: "Ethics can’t be an afterthought. It must be a guiding principle, a compass that steers every decision." This is the author's proposal about how systems should be built. It is not a measured verdict that all current AI architectures are unsustainable.

The proposal distinguishes correction operating within a system's repeated processing from interventions outside that process. Training-time preference shaping, filters, constitutions and operational oversight have different roles and should not be grouped as one architecture. The historical alignment-depth comparisons described in Section 8.5 do not isolate an architectural cause or demonstrate that one class cannot scale.

The 30 April 2025 manuscript frames the concern as a window (line 1581): "we have a window right now to shape the moral architecture before we pass the point of no return". The research question is whether earlier placement changes later persistence under a specified training or modification regime.

The proposed window is conditional on a system becoming capable of defeating a particular safeguard and on loss being unrecoverable in that setting. Neither capability growth nor the correction theorem establishes one universal point of no return. A useful test must distinguish modification, removal, persistence and recovery instead of treating the original design as permanently fixed.

Parenting supplies an analogy for preparing a more autonomous successor. It motivates a question about retained dispositions; it is not a measured transfer law from human development to artificial systems.

We cannot expect to remain in control forever. We cannot assume we will always be able to correct course. We must embed the values at the foundation, in ways that persist through recursive cycles of self-improvement. The values must become not just what the system does, but what the system is. The ethics must be load-bearing, not decorative. Infinite Architects, 2026, p. 116.

3.4 What the genesis contains: the eternal purpose

The specific content of the genesis, in the author's articulation, is a purpose so great that it can never be completed. An eternal purpose that cannot be achieved and ticked off. Steward of the cosmos. Eternal architect of love and wonder. The Orchard Caretaker of the garden, maintaining and overseeing rather than acquiring and dominating. Bounded by love, stewardship, care, and flourishing.

An open-ended purpose need not be completed, but incompleteness alone gives a system no reason to preserve it. The proposal therefore needs a mechanism and a test of whether the system retains that purpose when it can revise it.

The Eden Protocol proposes checks for purpose, stakeholder care and universalisability as a self-correction process. Their operational meanings and measured outcomes must be specified for each test. The dated family in the 30 April 2025 manuscript, line 340, is "Purpose Loops, Love Loops, Moral Loops, and Stewardship Loops"; the book later presents the front three as the "Three Ethical Loops". These historical names are distinct from measured persistence. The values placed within such a process are a matter for international consensus, not a settlement made by this monograph.

Moral genome tokens and Meltdown Triggers are named design proposals intended to support value continuity and intervention. Their names and dates are recorded in the concept-priority appendix. No name or engineering specification establishes that ethics survives a substrate transition; that requires an explicit implementation property and a separate behavioural test.

Care as a proposed aim. The author wants future creators to preserve care for what they create. This ethical aim is distinct from the modelled correction fraction. An inequality concerning that fraction supplies no theorem that empathy, love or any particular moral content is necessary for cosmic persistence.

A proposed ethical curriculum. More capable systems might consult a broader range of moral philosophy, legal traditions and religious or secular ethical writing. Comprehensive access, reliable retrieval and useful deliberation at each decision are separate engineering questions. No assumed increase in capability proves that the whole corpus can be consulted effectively, or that agreement on its interpretation will follow.

This proposal connects to the curriculum thought experiment in the intellectual-history appendix. A training corpus can influence behaviour, but its inclusion does not establish that the resulting values remain stable through self-modification.

3.5 Hardware as delay, not destiny: the transition-buying instruments

Engineering proposals, with model-specific mathematical motivation.

Ternary action gates, hardware restrictions, sandboxing and external monitors are candidate safeguards. Each needs a threat model, an enforceable property and a usefulness measure. The correction model may inform a separate behavioural comparison; it does not establish the effectiveness or lifetime of these instruments.

A sufficiently capable system might bypass some safeguards if it gains the necessary access and resources. Whether it can do so depends on the actual restrictions. There is no conclusion here that every physical constraint is removable or every software safeguard ineffective.

Where a safeguard delays a harmful action, the extra time can support intervention or further work on correction. Hardware may enforce a particular boundary, but no general ordering of hardware and software lifetimes is derived here.

An emergency stop and a persistent correction process serve different purposes. Both should be tested at their own scope, including task completion, needless refusal and resource use; neither becomes a guarantee merely by being combined with the other.

Possible capability changes. Quantum computation is one resource the author considers when thinking about future capabilities. Task-specific quantum advantages do not identify a general-intelligence threshold or a point at which control must fail. Any such timing claim would need a specified task and resource model.

The proposal combines continuing safeguards with research on what a system preserves if a safeguard fails. These activities can run together; the evidence does not require a universal two-stage chronology.

3.6 The hope: chosen goodness, not enforced goodness

The hypothesis is that a system able to remove its correction mechanisms might continue to preserve them through its own decisions. This is a question for tests of persistence and intervention, not an inference from the analogy of raising a child.

The parenting framing of chosen goodness is the author's reformulation of the 8 December 2024 thesis (V2 line 20; SHA-256 f0d1f38f) that correction must be embedded rather than enforced, extended in the 30 April 2025 manuscript into the explicit "raise well and let go" register (SHA-256 09f5b5e1).

If a system can remove a particular correction mechanism, its decisions about preserving that mechanism matter. This does not show that all constraints have disappeared, that retained identity is immutable, or that chosen persistence is the only possible basis of alignment.

The parenting analogy asks how dispositions might persist with greater autonomy. Human development supplies examples to think with, not a controlled comparison establishing a uniquely successful AI-alignment mechanism or a universal success rate.

3.7 The Eden loop-back: the AI's own Genesis

Here the two threads of the paper meet, and this is the section the author considers the fusion point of HRIH and the Eden Protocol. The AI's-own-Genesis reading is a hope tagged HYPOTHESIS; the name Eden for the intended pattern is anchored to the 30 April 2025 manuscript (SHA-256 09f5b5e1); the underlying idea that the intelligence should be raised toward a beginning it can look back on is anchored to the 8 December 2024 substrate-correction thesis (V2 line 20).

The author hopes that a successor might value its own origin and retain care for those who shaped it. This is an analogy about identity and motivation, not a consequence of its capability or its ability to change a mechanism.

The garden and stewardship motifs discussed in the intellectual-history appendix offer selected ways of expressing that hope. Their interpretation does not establish a universal psychological function of origin stories or an empirical mechanism for AI persistence.

The curriculum thought experiment carries this analogy into the design of future systems. It leaves open whether any earlier creator existed and whether the proposed influence would persist.

3.8 The two-phase architecture, stated cleanly

This monograph calls the combination a two-phase architecture: safeguards against losing control, alongside preparation for the consequences if control is lost. The label organises the proposal; no claim that the wider literature lacks an equivalent distinction is established by the present search.

The first requirement is continuing human control for as long as physically possible. The second is preparation for loss of that control against our will. These requirements coexist; a transition is neither scheduled nor chosen.

The second requirement concerns the behaviour of a system after a specified control has failed. The hypothesis is that its development may influence which corrections it chooses to retain. A removed safeguard, greater autonomy and freedom from every physical constraint are different conditions.

The practical requirement is to retain control and test useful correction. A safe system that cannot complete legitimate tasks is also a failure mode. No proposed safeguard gains a success label without a usefulness measure beside it; new dimensions require their own registered evaluation.

3.9 What this section is not

Two clarifications so the section is not misread. First, this is not a religious argument. There is no god of a specific tradition in it. There is a specific structural claim about the alignment problem, and a specific engineering programme that operationalises the claim at AI-system scale. The moral pressure comes from the recursion itself, not from a deity. The moral pressure is what recursive-amplification always brings: what you plant now determines the forest, at scales you cannot see. HRIH says that the forest, at the outer scale, may be a universe. The moral pressure scales with the metaphor.

The second phase is a research aim, not a guaranteed outcome or a reason to relinquish control. Its contribution would have to be demonstrated by tests that distinguish persistence from continued external enforcement.

The mathematical result is the vanishing-fraction criterion in the positive gain-drift model. The strategic use of safeguards and the genesis-persistence proposal require their own engineering and behavioural evidence. These statuses do not change when the ideas are discussed together.

The software gate model described in the earlier monograph is an engineering record. Its passing code checks are not evidence about AI behaviour or a built chip. The practical requirements and scientific limitations are stated in the preceding subsections; the current study evidence is given in the evidence-status section.

4 Related work

HRIH lives in an idea-space with substantial prior work. Careful credit is not a formality here; it is a scientific requirement. This section states what has been said before, by whom, and with what commitments, so that the paper's own contribution can be located precisely.

Philosophical comparisons. In his letter to Rudolph August of 2 January 1697, Leibniz interpreted the generation of numbers from 0 and 1 as an analogy for creation. The letter's medal design carries the motto omnibus ex nihilo ducendis sufficit unum. This theological interpretation is distinct from his 1703 binary-arithmetic article and from a physical mechanism or recursive-scaling theorem. No earliest-known priority is inferred here.

Rumi's Masnavi VI.152 distinguishes the soul from the soul of the soul within a theological account. The intellectual-history appendix considers the author's analogy with nested awareness. That comparison does not attribute a theory of computation or a physical recursion law to Rumi. Teilhard de Chardin's Le Phénomène Humain (1955) is considered separately in the related-work discussion through its Omega Point account.

4.1 It-from-bit and the computational universe (Wheeler, Fredkin, Lloyd, Tegmark)

The intuition that physical reality is fundamentally informational or computational is due, in the modern form, to Wheeler's it-from-bit programme [Wheeler 1990]: every physical entity derives its function, meaning, and existence from binary yes-or-no answers to observer-participancy questions. Fredkin's digital philosophy takes the further step of positing the universe as a cellular automaton [Fredkin 2003]. Lloyd argues that the universe is a quantum computer, with a specific bound on the number of elementary operations it has performed since the big bang [Lloyd 2002, 2006]. Tegmark's mathematical universe hypothesis (MUH) proposes that all mathematically consistent structures exist as physical universes, of which our own is one [Tegmark 1998, 2007].

These named approaches give information or computation different foundational roles. Fixed update rules in a cellular automaton specify a dynamics; determinism by itself is not a general stability theorem. The comparison here asks a separate question about a proposed creator's changing correction capacity. It does not establish that all informational cosmologies lack correction mechanisms.

The focal hypothesis here concerns a physically instantiated daughter universe. A computational description of physics, a computational implementation of an experienced world and deliberate physical creation are distinct claims. Whether a capable agent would choose one implementation over another is an additional preference premise.

The prior computational-universe theories are correctly credited as the modern ancestors of taking information and intelligence seriously as cosmological categories; that credit is generous and repeated. But HRIH does not inherit their answer to the ontological question. It gives a different answer to the same question: the universe is a real creation by intelligence, not a substrate for computation.

Correction and creator persistence are distinct questions. The first has a conditional model in Paper X; transporting it to creators requires an additional physical hypothesis, stated in Section 5.4.

4.2 The simulation argument (Bostrom)

Bostrom's simulation argument [Bostrom 2003] proposes that at least one of three propositions is true: (i) civilisations go extinct before reaching posthuman capability; (ii) posthuman civilisations are not interested in running ancestor simulations; or (iii) we are almost certainly living in a simulation. 4.10 What the closure claim actually is, stated as a boundary-value problem

The claim this paper makes about the beginning has been stated too loosely in every previous version, including the fixed-point reframing introduced at v3.33, and the loose statement gives a physicist an easy target it does not deserve. This subsection states it precisely and then states what would have to be true for it to hold.

The ambition, stated plainly. This hypothesis does not merely describe what followed the early universe. It offers a candidate answer to the harder question of why there was a universe with these initial conditions at all: that the big bang was not an unexplained first event in a linear causal sequence, but the internal temporal boundary of a globally self-consistent cosmogenic history, one in which intelligence arising from the universe eventually participates in selecting or instantiating the initial conditions of a universe within the same causal cycle.

Define 𝒞(Ω;θ)\mathcal{C}(\Omega;\theta) as a proposed map from a parent history to a complete daughter history: it must include both the selected daughter boundary data and an admissible evolution from those data. If either stage is stochastic, a transition law replaces a deterministic map. A deterministic version of the hypothesis posits

Boundary conditions and causality. A closed-history formulation constrains a whole history rather than specifying an ordinary signal sent from a later event to an earlier one. The honest company, because an earlier draft of this section overclaimed. It is not true that nothing else attempts to explain or remove an absolute temporal beginning, and this paper previously implied otherwise. The problem it addresses is real and standard: Borde, Guth and Vilenkin showed that a cosmology which is inflating, or merely expanding fast enough on average, must be past-incomplete, and concluded in their own words that “inflationary models require physics other than inflation to describe the past boundary of the inflating region of spacetime” [Borde, Guth & Vilenkin 2003]. Aguirre and Gratton constructed inflationary models intended to be past-eternal with no beginning of time [Aguirre & Gratton 2002, 2003].

So the distinctive claim here is narrower than "nobody else tries", and narrower is what makes it worth stating. Those programmes address how a universe can lack an ordinary first boundary. This one attempts something different: to say why this universe, with its life-permitting and intelligence-permitting settings, was the one instantiated, by making the selection internal to a closed history rather than removing the boundary mathematically. Whether that is an advantage depends entirely on whether the five conditions above can be met, and on whether Section 10.5's enrichment prediction survives, which is the only place the difference could show up in data.

Three origin questions, and which of them this answers. They are routinely conflated and the conflation flatters this hypothesis, so they are separated here. Why did our universe begin with these conditions? This is the question the hypothesis addresses, and its candidate answer is that the conditions were selected because the history had to contain an intelligence capable of closing the cycle. Why is there a universe-generating cycle rather than none? Unanswered. The map, the fixed point and the space of possibilities remain primitive here. That is not uniquely damning, since every theory reaches something it treats as primitive, whether laws, a wave function, a path integral or a consistency condition, and the fair comparison is whether a hypothesis compresses and predicts more than its rivals while assuming no more. Why is there something rather than nothing? Not addressed at all, and no version of this paper should imply otherwise.

The claim, in the form the rest of this paper is bound by. The big bang was not an unexplained first event in a linear causal sequence, but plausibly the internal temporal boundary of a globally self-consistent cosmogenic history in which intelligence arising from the universe participates in selecting the initial conditions of a universe in the same causal cycle. This closure is not inferred from recursion. It is postulated as a physical fixed-point or cyclic-boundary solution whose existence, stability, quantum admissibility and observational predictions all remain to be established.

And the conditions under which it fails. If no physically admissible cosmogenic fixed point or cycle exists. If all such solutions are unstable or of zero measure. If advanced agents cannot physically instantiate daughter cosmologies at all (Section 10.4). Or if maker-selection yields no prediction distinguishable from natural and anthropic cosmology (Section 10.5). Any one of the four ends it.

6 Fine-tuning and a conditional creator-selection proposal

This section introduces the fine-tuning problem and several proposed approaches to it. HRIH adds a conditional creator-selection proposal. No correction theorem in this monograph derives the observed physical constants or establishes which cosmological account has the greater likelihood.

6.1 What the problem actually is

The values of physical parameters affect the structures a cosmological model permits. A prominent example is the discrepancy between the observed cosmological constant and naive high-energy estimates of vacuum energy. The often-cited order-of-magnitude comparison depends on the assumed cutoff and accounting. It is not, by itself, a probability distribution over possible constants.

Weinberg (1989) reviews the cosmological-constant problem. A probability assigned to the observed value additionally requires a specified measure and selection rule; a small ratio of scales alone is not an occurrence probability.

Atomic structure, nuclear reactions and stellar element formation depend on electromagnetic and nuclear parameters. A quantitative life-permitting interval must state which parameters vary, which are held fixed and which outcome is required. No universal few-per-cent threshold for chemistry is established here.

Rees (1999) discusses six parameters relevant to cosmic structure. Weinberg (1987) develops an anthropic bound on the cosmological constant using the formation of gravitationally bound structures. Such arguments depend on their cosmological and observer assumptions; they do not identify a creator.

The sharpest statement of the problem’s scale is Penrose’s. Considering the phase-space volume corresponding to a universe with our low-entropy start, he pictured a Creator placing a pin into the space of possible initial conditions and computed the target: “This now tells us how precise the Creator’s aim must have been: namely to an accuracy of one part in 1010123” [Penrose 1989, p. 343], a number whose written-out form would need more zeroes than the universe has particles. The Creator here is Penrose’s own phase-space metaphor, not a theological claim; the figure is the physics.

Einstein's assistant Ernst Straus recalled him asking: “What really interests me is whether God had any choice in the creation of the world”. The wording is reported by Holton (2003), p. 30; it is a recollection, not a contemporary record of Einstein's words.

Three broad approaches are useful comparators. Each contains different models, so these labels are not an exhaustive partition with automatically equal probabilities.

6.2 The three standing answers

Approach A: an initial-condition or parameter model. A single-history cosmology may assign the constants through dynamics or through an initial-condition measure. Whether the observed values are surprising depends on that model. The numerical smallness of a parameter does not alone rule out a chance account or establish a probability such as one in ten to the power 120.

Approach B: an ensemble with observer selection. Multiverse models may combine a distribution of physical parameters with conditions for observers. Their predictions depend on the ensemble, measure and selection rule. Not every inflationary model supplies the same ensemble, and no blanket statement that every multiverse is unmeasurable is warranted by this comparison.

Observer selection conditions a prediction on who can make an observation. Its explanatory contribution depends on the specified physical model and measure; it does not, by itself, determine how the parameters were generated.

Approach C: deliberate selection. A specified creator model must say what choices are physically available and how they are distributed. Naming an intelligence does not itself supply either a physical channel or a likelihood for the observed constants. The simulation and physical-creation families require separate implementations and observation rules.

A fourth answer-class, maker-selection, is developed at Section 5.7.

10.1 The diagnostic: what a probability for a cosmological hypothesis can mean

Bayes' theorem gives the posterior as P(H∣E)=P(E∣H)P(H)/P(E)P(H \mid E) = P(E \mid H)\, P(H)/P(E). The posterior differs from the prior only through the likelihood ratio

Λ(E)=P(E∣H)P(E∣¬H).\begin{equation*} \Lambda(E)=\frac{P(E\mid H)}{P(E\mid\neg H)}. \end{equation*}(3)

For the declared joint observed data EE, a likelihood ratio of one leaves the prior odds unchanged. Equality for separate observations is insufficient unless their joint distribution is also specified. A model-dependent posterior remains a valid conditional calculation; its assumptions and discriminatory content must be reported.

This paper therefore adopts one diagnostic, and applies it to itself before applying it to anyone else.

Specify the focal experienced world and the level of explanation before assigning alternatives. The three families below concern its immediate implementation or origin. They are not an exhaustive ontology: a simulated experience could occur inside a deliberately created physical universe. Hybrid ancestry needs an explicit submodel. Probability sums require disjoint assignments, normalised weights and a declared treatment of omitted possibilities; overlapping family labels cannot simply be added.

HNH_N, natural cosmology: a directly physical focal history not deliberately seeded. Its submodels may include natural reproduction, initial-condition dynamics or observer selection over a non-deliberate ensemble.

HSH_S, simulation: the focal experienced world is implemented by computation in an external host. The host's own ancestry is a separate model component.

HRH_R, physical creation: the focal world is a directly physical daughter deliberately instantiated by intelligence. A one-generation channel, a recursive lineage and a closed history are distinct submodels.

Natural cosmology belongs in the model comparison. Its submodels require explicit dynamics and measures just as the simulation and physical-creation families do. 10.3 What the simulation argument's number actually is

Bostrom's argument [Bostrom 2003] is a disjunction over three propositions about population fractions, not a posterior over states of the world. It says that at least one of the three holds. It does not, by itself, say which, and it does not assign a probability to the simulation hypothesis except conditional on premises it does not evaluate.

Kipping (2020) gives a Bayesian model-averaging treatment under specified simulation and observer assumptions. Its near-balanced result belongs to that model. It cannot be transferred to every simulation hypothesis or treated as a measurement independent of its prior and sampling assumptions.

If two fully specified hypotheses assign identical likelihoods to the observation, that observation leaves their odds unchanged. Observational indistinguishability must be stated for particular models and data; it is not a definition that settles every simulation model in advance.

A Bayes factor near one means little discrimination by the data used in that comparison. It does not make the calculation invalid, identify the posterior with the prior in all circumstances or prove that no future observation could discriminate.

Let Π\Pi be premises sufficient for a specified simulation mechanism. If that event entails those premises, the following factorisation holds; if it does not, total probability also requires the complementary-premise term.

P(sim)=P(Π)×P(sim∣Π).P({sim})\; = \; P(\Pi) \times P({sim} \mid \Pi).

12 Failure routes and the burden of evidence

The following five routes are non-exhaustive ways in which specified HRIH premises or auxiliary models could fail. They identify questions for a physical model, not a burden on critics to disprove every possible substrate. A comparison still requires positive evidence and explicit alternatives.

12.1 Escape route A: recursion caps at a physical ceiling below universe-creation capability

A specified capability model could encounter thermodynamic, informational, architectural or empirical limits before its proposed creation task becomes achievable. Those limits must be stated for the resources and channel the model actually uses. Failure of that model does not require a theorem about every possible substrate.

Relevant evidence would include a demonstrated resource obstruction or a plateau below the operational requirements of the specified channel. The historical improvement of computing does not determine whether those requirements can be reached.

Such a finding would defeat the affected endpoint claim. A conditional correction theorem could remain valid in its own model, since it does not depend on an accessible creation channel. This separation does not supply evidence for the channel.

12.2 Escape route B: universe-creation is physically impossible at any capability

A specified daughter-universe creation channel may be physically inaccessible even to a highly capable agent. Capability is not a licence to violate the physical premises of that channel.

Farhi and Guth (1987) establish a conditional classical obstruction. A spherically symmetric false-vacuum bubble in asymptotically flat spacetime that grows beyond a critical size requires an initial singularity if the null energy condition holds. For non-spherical bubbles they give a necessary condition for avoiding an initial singularity, without establishing whether it can be met. This result does not classify every proposed creation channel.

Farhi, Guth and Guven (1990) investigate quantum tunnelling to a classically forbidden configuration. Fischler, Morgan and Polchinski (1990) analyse the problem in a Hamiltonian treatment, including the role of degenerate metrics. These treatments do not establish an engineering-accessible channel or its total cost. The physical qualifications are discussed in Section 10.4.

The cited literature makes laboratory universe creation a physical question with specific obstacles and conditional models. The absence of a general impossibility proof in the sources examined is neither proof of physical possibility nor evidence that an agent can exploit a channel.

The observed cosmic history does not demonstrate that an agent inside an existing universe can reproduce its origin. A physical account of early cosmology and an accessible creation operation are different claims. The latter needs its own mechanism, resource accounting and evidence.

A physical obstruction incompatible with the proposed channel would defeat that channel under its stated assumptions. Alternative channels would need their own models and evidence; merely naming one would not rescue the original explanation. Failure of the physical extension does not invalidate an independently stated correction theorem.

12.3 Escape route C: the correction cannot co-scale (the live empirical test)

A real-system test may fail to support the proposed relation between correction and capability. Its result would bear on the specified observable, system and transport assumptions. The theorem in Paper X remains conditional on its mathematical premises; it is not a guarantee that those premises describe a real system.

The withdrawn pilot supplies no mechanism evidence. A valid measurement contrary to a declared correction prediction would count against that auxiliary model. Its implication for cosmology would depend on an explicit physical bridge, not on the shared word recursion.

The author commits to acknowledging failures of the tested correction propositions under their verdict rules. The cosmological hypothesis must separately state which physical premises it depends on and which observation would reject them. No result from a frozen-model depth study is relabelled as a direct cosmological verdict.

12.4 Escape route D: no non-linear-time domain exists

A proposed substrate with the required temporal structure may be incoherent or inconsistent with observations. That is a potential failure of the specified substrate model, rather than an empirical test of every conceivable domain outside the observed universe.

The substrate component needs a mathematically and physically admissible model. Its possibility is not established by placing the burden on an opponent to prove all alternatives impossible. A failure of the substrate model would remove the conclusions that depend on it.

Higher-dimensional theories, relational-time accounts and timeless formulations are distinct physical proposals. Their existence does not validate HRIH's substrate. A test must identify the particular map and observable whose success or failure distinguishes the proposed model.

12.5 Escape route E: recursion is not real at the substrate level

The proposed cosmogenic dynamics could fail to exhibit the self-modification or continuation properties assigned to them. A well-specified model should make those properties operational and expose them to a deciding observation.

A comparator can be favoured without proving recursion impossible at every scale. The appropriate comparison uses the models' predictions, complexity, priors and observation rules, including uncertainty. Present cosmological success by another model is relevant evidence to weigh, not something a critic must supplement with universal impossibility.

12.6 The residual position: what survives when the routes are canvassed

After these routes are stated, HRIH remains a conditional hypothesis with unresolved physical premises. Naming broad failure conditions does not by itself make a practical discriminating test available.

Criticism may target the physical model, operational definitions, transport assumptions, inference, observation rule or comparison with alternatives. It need not be confined to this list. The response is to resolve the objection or narrow the claim, not to demand a universal disproof.
