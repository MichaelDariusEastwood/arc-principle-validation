# The Hyperspace Recursive Intelligence Hypothesis: can recursive self-improvement end in physical creation?

**Version:** 3.53
**Revised:** 2 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-07-03
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 3.53):

> If an AI system improves beyond our ability to control it, what would preserve useful and aligned behaviour? The ARC Theory addresses recursive capability, correction and persistence through five laws under test. This monograph develops a separable physical extension: whether sufficiently capable intelligence could create real universes whose histories form a self-consistent chain. It joins three conditional contributions: a map from correction and scaling models to the additional premises cosmogenesis requires; a physical-channel and branching account of possible creation and lineage persistence; and a Bayesian framework for comparing natural, simulated and physically created cosmologies. The framework distinguishes observer counts from model probabilities, specifies the missing measures and likelihoods, and states prospective failure conditions. Existing programme results, mathematical identities and physical analogies support none of the cosmological conclusions; the correction criterion and capability ceiling retain their model-specific premises. The earlier error-decay retraction and the registered placement result are reported at their measured scope. No numerical posterior or deciding cosmological observation is supplied. The scientific task is to specify an admissible physical map and an observation that distinguishes its predictions; the alignment programme remains testable whether this extension survives or fails.
