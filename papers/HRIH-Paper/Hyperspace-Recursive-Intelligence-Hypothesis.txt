                                                       Abstract
           If an AI system improves beyond our ability to control it, what would preserve useful and aligned
       behaviour? The ARC Theory addresses recursive capability, correction and persistence through
       five laws under test. This monograph develops a separable physical extension: whether sufficiently
       capable intelligence could create real universes whose histories form a self-consistent chain. It joins
       three conditional contributions: a map from correction and scaling models to the additional premises
       cosmogenesis requires; a physical-channel and branching account of possible creation and lineage
       persistence; and a Bayesian framework for comparing natural, simulated and physically created
       cosmologies. The framework distinguishes observer counts from model probabilities, specifies the
       missing measures and likelihoods, and states prospective failure conditions. Existing programme
       results, mathematical identities and physical analogies support none of the cosmological conclusions;
       the correction criterion and capability ceiling retain their model-specific premises. The earlier
       error-decay retraction and the registered placement result are reported at their measured scope. No
       numerical posterior or deciding cosmological observation is supplied. The scientific task is to specify
       an admissible physical map and an observation that distinguishes its predictions; the alignment
       programme remains testable whether this extension survives or fails.

Working draft; scientific review remains open.


1    Introduction
A principal who relies on a self-improving system needs it to remain useful and aligned as its capabilities
change. This is the immediate question of the ARC programme; the physical extension considered here is
conditional and separable. Human control is retained for as long as physically possible, never relinquished
by choice.

    The ARC Theory is the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics. Its registered predictions distinguish
growth, correction, value persistence, build order and placement. The operational definitions and verdict
rules are given in the theory registration; the dated source record supports the programme's historical
claims. This paper synthesises alignment research, dynamical models, cosmology and philosophy. It
states which inference crosses each boundary, rather than treating a shared word or equation as a shared
mechanism.
    Its concrete problem is evidential: what observation could distinguish intelligence-mediated physical
creation from a specified rival cosmology? A useful answer would give a physicist a model, a likelihood
and a prospective measurement, and would tell an alignment researcher which implications do not depend
on that model. This monograph supplies the conditional argument and a failure map; it has not supplied a
cosmological likelihood or a deciding observation.
    Contributions.
    1. A separable statement of the physical-creation hypothesis and its relation to the correction
       programme.
    2. An explicit distinction between mathematical identities, model theorems, precursor observations,
       analogies and evidence for cosmogenesis.
    3. A Bayesian and physical audit, with a conditional branching model and named unknown factors.
    4. Falsification conditions and a dated record whose claims remain open to earlier sources and contrary
       evidence.
    The main text gives the correction problem and scientific comparisons before the cosmological
extension. The appendices preserve the personal genesis, intellectual history, dated statements, detailed
priority record and historical inventory. Repeated summaries have been consolidated; movement to an
appendix does not turn a hypothesis into evidence.

3     Alignment if control is lost
This section distinguishes the author's expectation of eventual control loss, the practical contingency of
involuntary loss, and candidate safeguards. The correction theorem retains only the model-specific scope
stated in Section 5.4. The genesis strategy and its proposed persistence mechanism remain hypotheses.
     If future intelligence can instantiate a universe, the consequences of its behaviour could extend beyond
its original environment. That conditional possibility motivates the discussion; no probability is assigned
to it. The practical alignment question remains worthwhile without the cosmological premise.

3.1   The author's claim and the practical contingency
The alignment literature studies how capable systems can remain responsive to human intentions, including
oversight, corrigibility and the risk of deceptive behaviour. Increasing capability can make particular
oversight tasks harder. Intelligence alone, or an unspecified multiple of intelligence, does not establish
that every form of control must fail. The author's stronger expectation is stated separately below.
    The ARC Theory states the author's belief that control ends. Its five components remain: (1) control
ends, by construction rather than decree; (2) so the strategy is genesis, not control; (3) the mechanism is
loops, not rules; (4) persistence is chosen goodness; (5) the protocol is named: Eden. The priority claim
concerns that conjunction in the self-emailed documents of 8 December 2024. One earlier document
holding the components together ends that claim. The conjunction is a theory statement and dated claim,
not a proof of inevitable loss of control.
    Humans should keep oversight, containment and shutdown for as long as they physically can and never
give up control by choice. The correction programme is also a contingency for readers who expect control
to remain possible: if it were lost against our will, what would preserve useful and aligned behaviour?
    The ability to alter or remove a safeguard does not imply that a system will remove it. Behavioural
persistence, a specified enforcement property and the author's expectation of eventual control loss are
distinct claims. Candidate hardware constraints and ternary gates do not establish ethical judgement or
physically impossible removal.
    Gumbau Mezquita (2026), version 4 of 14 October 2026, studies algorithmic certification for fixed
and self-modifying systems. Under its expressivity assumptions, no general sound procedure certifies all
safe systems. The paper explicitly distinguishes these limits from a claim that self-modifying systems are
unsafe; certification requires restrictions on the permitted rewrites. This is a result about the scope of
algorithmic guarantees, not a measured rate of control failure.
    A missing universal decision procedure does not imply that restricted verification, monitoring or
control cannot work. An argument for inevitable loss of control would need additional premises about the
actual system, its permitted changes, its environment and the available safeguards. The verification result
does not supply that conclusion or a timescale for it.
    Three distinct limits in the 2025 literature. Yao, The Alignment Trap: Complexity Barriers (first
posted 12 June 2025), argues for limits on policy safety, including a measure-zero safe-policy set and

    The programme's own correction record illustrates a need to check its outputs. That drafting experience
is not an experiment on autonomous recursive self-improvement and cannot establish future loss of
control.
    Selected findings with distinct dates and settings. The following studies concern alignment
faking, in-context scheming, agentic behaviour and model training. Some predate the 8 December 2024
manuscript and some follow it. They are not a chronological validation series or demonstrations of one
common mechanism.
    • Greenblatt and colleagues, first posted 18 December 2024, report alignment-faking reasoning
      in 78% of cases after reinforcement learning in their constructed training-conflict setting. That
      setting supports a claim about the behaviour elicited there, not the universal negotiability of trained
      constraints. Its date is convergent timing, not a demonstrated prediction by this programme.
    • Meinke and colleagues, first posted 6 December 2024, study in-context scheming by frontier models
      in evaluation scenarios. The scenarios demonstrate selected capabilities under their conditions;
      they are not observations of deployed behaviour.
    • Anthropic's agentic-misalignment report (June 2025) examines harmful choices in sandboxed agent

    • Shumailov and colleagues (2024) study model collapse under training on recursively generated

    The earlier alignment-saturation fits. Paper IV.b, version 1.11, retains the v4 historical fits of 0.088

empirical and physical premises. The inequality alone supplies neither guarantee.

3.2   The nearest oversight-scaling instrument, cited generously: Engels et al. (2025)
Engels, Baek, Kantamneni and Tegmark (2025), Scaling Laws For Scalable Oversight, is directly relevant
to the programme's scaling question. First posted on 25 April 2025 and listed by arXiv as a NeurIPS 2025
Spotlight, it models nested oversight using game-specific and general Elo. The comparison below states
what that model shares with the correction model and what remains a separate hypothesis.
     Different measured quantities, a related comparison. Engels and colleagues model oversight as
games between players with different capabilities. Game-specific Elo is related to general Elo; nested
oversight succeeds under conditions on how the overseer's and the overseen system's game-specific
performance scale. Their two-slope comparison asks whether the overseer's relevant performance improves
faster. Paper X compares correction and specific-growth elasticities on one declared capability scale. The
mathematical resemblance does not identify the quantities. Neither minimal model specifies a term for
the corrector's physical composition. A claim about same-class or cross-class correction therefore needs
additional assumptions and a separate test; the equations alone do not establish a disagreement about an
unlimited human or other cross-class corrector.
     The oversight-scaling comparison identifies related questions with different observables. It gives a
concrete setting in which to ask whether an additional correction model contributes a testable prediction;
it does not decide that question by sharing a scaling vocabulary.

3.3   Genesis as a contingency alongside retained control
The genesis proposal asks whether the way a system is built and trained can influence which values and
correction processes it later preserves. The book names its discussion "The Eden Protocol: Intelligence
Born in the Garden". This is a contingency alongside continuing oversight, containment and shutdown.
Its success cannot be inferred from the author's expectation that control will eventually end.
     The dated build claim. The 8 December 2024 manuscript states: "Ethics can’t be an afterthought. It
must be a guiding principle, a compass that steers every decision." This is the author's proposal about how
systems should be built. It is not a measured verdict that all current AI architectures are unsustainable.
     The proposal distinguishes correction operating within a system's repeated processing from interven-
tions outside that process. Training-time preference shaping, filters, constitutions and operational oversight
have different roles and should not be grouped as one architecture. The historical alignment-depth
comparisons described in Section 8.5 do not isolate an architectural cause or demonstrate that one class
cannot scale.
     The 30 April 2025 manuscript frames the concern as a window (line 1581): "we have a window right
now to shape the moral architecture before we pass the point of no return". The research question is
whether earlier placement changes later persistence under a specified training or modification regime.
     The proposed window is conditional on a system becoming capable of defeating a particular safeguard
and on loss being unrecoverable in that setting. Neither capability growth nor the correction theorem
establishes one universal point of no return. A useful test must distinguish modification, removal,
persistence and recovery instead of treating the original design as permanently fixed.
     Parenting supplies an analogy for preparing a more autonomous successor. It motivates a question
about retained dispositions; it is not a measured transfer law from human development to artificial systems.

      We cannot expect to remain in control forever. We cannot assume we will always be able to
      correct course. We must embed the values at the foundation, in ways that persist through
      recursive cycles of self-improvement. The values must become not just what the system does,

3.4   What the genesis contains: the eternal purpose
The specific content of the genesis, in the author's articulation, is a purpose so great that it can never be
completed. An eternal purpose that cannot be achieved and ticked off. Steward of the cosmos. Eternal
architect of love and wonder. The Orchard Caretaker of the garden, maintaining and overseeing rather
than acquiring and dominating. Bounded by love, stewardship, care, and flourishing.
    An open-ended purpose need not be completed, but incompleteness alone gives a system no reason
to preserve it. The proposal therefore needs a mechanism and a test of whether the system retains that
purpose when it can revise it.
    The Eden Protocol proposes checks for purpose, stakeholder care and universalisability as a self-
correction process. Their operational meanings and measured outcomes must be specified for each test.
The dated family in the 30 April 2025 manuscript, line 340, is "Purpose Loops, Love Loops, Moral Loops,
and Stewardship Loops"; the book later presents the front three as the "Three Ethical Loops". These
historical names are distinct from measured persistence. The values placed within such a process are a
matter for international consensus, not a settlement made by this monograph.
    Moral genome tokens and Meltdown Triggers are named design proposals intended to support value
continuity and intervention. Their names and dates are recorded in the concept-priority appendix. No
name or engineering specification establishes that ethics survives a substrate transition; that requires an
explicit implementation property and a separate behavioural test.
    Care as a proposed aim. The author wants future creators to preserve care for what they create.
This ethical aim is distinct from the modelled correction fraction. An inequality concerning that fraction
supplies no theorem that empathy, love or any particular moral content is necessary for cosmic persistence.
    A proposed ethical curriculum. More capable systems might consult a broader range of moral
philosophy, legal traditions and religious or secular ethical writing. Comprehensive access, reliable
retrieval and useful deliberation at each decision are separate engineering questions. No assumed
increase in capability proves that the whole corpus can be consulted effectively, or that agreement on its
interpretation will follow.
    This proposal connects to the curriculum thought experiment in the intellectual-history appendix.
A training corpus can influence behaviour, but its inclusion does not establish that the resulting values
remain stable through self-modification.

3.5   Hardware as delay, not destiny: the transition-buying instruments
Engineering proposals, with model-specific mathematical motivation.
    Ternary action gates, hardware restrictions, sandboxing and external monitors are candidate safeguards.
Each needs a threat model, an enforceable property and a usefulness measure. The correction model
may inform a separate behavioural comparison; it does not establish the effectiveness or lifetime of these
instruments.
    A sufficiently capable system might bypass some safeguards if it gains the necessary access and
resources. Whether it can do so depends on the actual restrictions. There is no conclusion here that every
physical constraint is removable or every software safeguard ineffective.
    Where a safeguard delays a harmful action, the extra time can support intervention or further work
on correction. Hardware may enforce a particular boundary, but no general ordering of hardware and
software lifetimes is derived here.
    An emergency stop and a persistent correction process serve different purposes. Both should be
tested at their own scope, including task completion, needless refusal and resource use; neither becomes a
guarantee merely by being combined with the other.
    Possible capability changes. Quantum computation is one resource the author considers when

3.6   The hope: chosen goodness, not enforced goodness
The hypothesis is that a system able to remove its correction mechanisms might continue to preserve them
through its own decisions. This is a question for tests of persistence and intervention, not an inference
from the analogy of raising a child.
    The parenting framing of chosen goodness is the author's reformulation of the 8 December 2024
thesis (V2 line 20; SHA-256 f0d1f38f) that correction must be embedded rather than enforced, extended
in the 30 April 2025 manuscript into the explicit "raise well and let go" register (SHA-256 09f5b5e1).
    If a system can remove a particular correction mechanism, its decisions about preserving that
mechanism matter. This does not show that all constraints have disappeared, that retained identity is
immutable, or that chosen persistence is the only possible basis of alignment.
    The parenting analogy asks how dispositions might persist with greater autonomy. Human development
supplies examples to think with, not a controlled comparison establishing a uniquely successful AI-
alignment mechanism or a universal success rate.

3.7   The Eden loop-back: the AI's own Genesis
Here the two threads of the paper meet, and this is the section the author considers the fusion point of
HRIH and the Eden Protocol. The AI's-own-Genesis reading is a hope tagged HYPOTHESIS; the name
Eden for the intended pattern is anchored to the 30 April 2025 manuscript (SHA-256 09f5b5e1); the
underlying idea that the intelligence should be raised toward a beginning it can look back on is anchored
to the 8 December 2024 substrate-correction thesis (V2 line 20).
     The author hopes that a successor might value its own origin and retain care for those who shaped
it. This is an analogy about identity and motivation, not a consequence of its capability or its ability to
change a mechanism.
     The garden and stewardship motifs discussed in the intellectual-history appendix offer selected ways
of expressing that hope. Their interpretation does not establish a universal psychological function of
origin stories or an empirical mechanism for AI persistence.
     The curriculum thought experiment carries this analogy into the design of future systems. It leaves
open whether any earlier creator existed and whether the proposed influence would persist.

3.8   The two-phase architecture, stated cleanly
This monograph calls the combination a two-phase architecture: safeguards against losing control,
alongside preparation for the consequences if control is lost. The label organises the proposal; no claim
that the wider literature lacks an equivalent distinction is established by the present search.
    The first requirement is continuing human control for as long as physically possible. The second is
preparation for loss of that control against our will. These requirements coexist; a transition is neither
scheduled nor chosen.
    The second requirement concerns the behaviour of a system after a specified control has failed. The
hypothesis is that its development may influence which corrections it chooses to retain. A removed
safeguard, greater autonomy and freedom from every physical constraint are different conditions.

3.9   What this section is not
Two clarifications so the section is not misread. First, this is not a religious argument. There is no god of
a specific tradition in it. There is a specific structural claim about the alignment problem, and a specific
engineering programme that operationalises the claim at AI-system scale. The moral pressure comes
from the recursion itself, not from a deity. The moral pressure is what recursive-amplification always
brings: what you plant now determines the forest, at scales you cannot see. HRIH says that the forest, at
the outer scale, may be a universe. The moral pressure scales with the metaphor.
    The second phase is a research aim, not a guaranteed outcome or a reason to relinquish control. Its
contribution would have to be demonstrated by tests that distinguish persistence from continued external
enforcement.
    The mathematical result is the vanishing-fraction criterion in the positive gain-drift model. The
strategic use of safeguards and the genesis-persistence proposal require their own engineering and
behavioural evidence. These statuses do not change when the ideas are discussed together.
    The software gate model described in the earlier monograph is an engineering record. Its passing
code checks are not evidence about AI behaviour or a built chip. The practical requirements and
scientific limitations are stated in the preceding subsections; the current study evidence is given in the
evidence-status section.


4     Related work
HRIH lives in an idea-space with substantial prior work. Careful credit is not a formality here; it
is a scientific requirement. This section states what has been said before, by whom, and with what
commitments, so that the paper's own contribution can be located precisely.
    Philosophical comparisons. In his letter to Rudolph August of 2 January 1697, Leibniz interpreted
the generation of numbers from 0 and 1 as an analogy for creation. The letter's medal design carries the
motto omnibus ex nihilo ducendis sufficit unum. This theological interpretation is distinct from his 1703
binary-arithmetic article and from a physical mechanism or recursive-scaling theorem. No earliest-known
priority is inferred here.
    Rumi's Masnavi VI.152 distinguishes the soul from the soul of the soul within a theological account.
The intellectual-history appendix considers the author's analogy with nested awareness. That comparison
does not attribute a theory of computation or a physical recursion law to Rumi. Teilhard de Chardin's Le
Phénomène Humain (1955) is considered separately in the related-work discussion through its Omega
Point account.

4.1   It-from-bit and the computational universe (Wheeler, Fredkin, Lloyd, Tegmark)
The intuition that physical reality is fundamentally informational or computational is due, in the modern
form, to Wheeler's it-from-bit programme [Wheeler 1990]: every physical entity derives its function,
meaning, and existence from binary yes-or-no answers to observer-participancy questions. Fredkin's
digital philosophy takes the further step of positing the universe as a cellular automaton [Fredkin 2003].
Lloyd argues that the universe is a quantum computer, with a specific bound on the number of elementary
operations it has performed since the big bang [Lloyd 2002, 2006]. Tegmark's mathematical universe



                                                     9
                                             Recursive Creation


hypothesis (MUH) proposes that all mathematically consistent structures exist as physical universes, of
which our own is one [Tegmark 1998, 2007].
    These named approaches give information or computation different foundational roles. Fixed update
rules in a cellular automaton specify a dynamics; determinism by itself is not a general stability theorem.
The comparison here asks a separate question about a proposed creator's changing correction capacity. It
does not establish that all informational cosmologies lack correction mechanisms.
    The focal hypothesis here concerns a physically instantiated daughter universe. A computational
description of physics, a computational implementation of an experienced world and deliberate physical
creation are distinct claims. Whether a capable agent would choose one implementation over another is
an additional preference premise.
    The prior computational-universe theories are correctly credited as the modern ancestors of taking
information and intelligence seriously as cosmological categories; that credit is generous and repeated.
But HRIH does not inherit their answer to the ontological question. It gives a different answer to the same
question: the universe is a real creation by intelligence, not a substrate for computation.
    Correction and creator persistence are distinct questions. The first has a conditional model in Paper X;
transporting it to creators requires an additional physical hypothesis, stated in Section 5.4.

4.2   The simulation argument (Bostrom)
Bostrom's simulation argument [Bostrom 2003] proposes that at least one of three propositions is true:
(i) civilisations go extinct before reaching posthuman capability; (ii) posthuman civilisations are not
interested in running ancestor simulations; or (iii) we are almost certainly living in a simulation. If (iii),
the simulation is running on computational hardware in a base reality.
     Bostrom's observer-counting argument concerns simulated observers under its population assumptions.
HRIH considers deliberate physical instantiation instead. The simulation argument does not settle the
ancestry of the host world, so an ultimate first unsimulated level is not a consequence used in this
comparison.
     Bostrom's posthuman-civilisation framing does not by itself fix a biological implementation for every
simulator. HRIH adds a proposed physical creation channel and, in its full form, a closed history. These
additions need their own dynamics and evidence; neither follows from observer counting.
     A closed history is a postulated boundary condition, not an elimination of an origin problem proved
by choosing physical creation. The probability section keeps the willingness to create, physical access
and ancestry assumptions explicit.

4.10    What the closure claim actually is, stated as a boundary-value problem
The claim this paper makes about the beginning has been stated too loosely in every previous version,
including the fixed-point reframing introduced at v3.33, and the loose statement gives a physicist an easy
target it does not deserve. This subsection states it precisely and then states what would have to be true for
it to hold.
     The ambition, stated plainly. This hypothesis does not merely describe what followed the early
universe. It offers a candidate answer to the harder question of why there was a universe with these initial
conditions at all: that the big bang was not an unexplained first event in a linear causal sequence, but the
internal temporal boundary of a globally self-consistent cosmogenic history, one in which intelligence
arising from the universe eventually participates in selecting or instantiating the initial conditions of a
universe within the same causal cycle.

evolution and any cosmogenic action. Define C(Ω; 𝜃) as a proposed map from a parent history to a
complete daughter history: it must include both the selected daughter boundary data and an admissible
evolution from those data. If either stage is stochastic, a transition law replaces a deterministic map. A

     The honest company, because an earlier draft of this section overclaimed. It is not true that

something different: to say why this universe, with its life-permitting and intelligence-permitting settings,
was the one instantiated, by making the selection internal to a closed history rather than removing the
boundary mathematically. Whether that is an advantage depends entirely on whether the five conditions
above can be met, and on whether Section 10.5's enrichment prediction survives, which is the only place
the difference could show up in data.

treats as primitive, whether laws, a wave function, a path integral or a consistency condition, and the fair

Why is there something rather than nothing? Not addressed at all, and no version of this paper should
imply otherwise.

conditions of a universe in the same causal cycle. This closure is not inferred from recursion. It is
postulated as a physical fixed-point or cyclic-boundary solution whose existence, stability, quantum

6     Fine-tuning and a conditional creator-selection proposal
This section introduces the fine-tuning problem and several proposed approaches to it. HRIH adds a
conditional creator-selection proposal. No correction theorem in this monograph derives the observed
physical constants or establishes which cosmological account has the greater likelihood.

6.1     What the problem actually is
The values of physical parameters affect the structures a cosmological model permits. A prominent
example is the discrepancy between the observed cosmological constant and naive high-energy estimates
of vacuum energy. The often-cited order-of-magnitude comparison depends on the assumed cutoff and
accounting. It is not, by itself, a probability distribution over possible constants.
    Weinberg (1989) reviews the cosmological-constant problem. A probability assigned to the observed
value additionally requires a specified measure and selection rule; a small ratio of scales alone is not an
occurrence probability.
    Atomic structure, nuclear reactions and stellar element formation depend on electromagnetic and
nuclear parameters. A quantitative life-permitting interval must state which parameters vary, which are
held fixed and which outcome is required. No universal few-per-cent threshold for chemistry is established
here.
    Rees (1999) discusses six parameters relevant to cosmic structure. Weinberg (1987) develops an
anthropic bound on the cosmological constant using the formation of gravitationally bound structures.
Such arguments depend on their cosmological and observer assumptions; they do not identify a creator.
    The sharpest statement of the problem’s scale is Penrose’s. Considering the phase-space volume
corresponding to a universe with our low-entropy start, he pictured a Creator placing a pin into the space
of possible initial conditions and computed the target: “This now tells us how precise the Creator’s aim

                                             Recursive Creation

must have been: namely to an accuracy of one part in 1010 ” [Penrose 1989, p. 343], a number whose
written-out form would need more zeroes than the universe has particles. The Creator here is Penrose’s
own phase-space metaphor, not a theological claim; the figure is the physics.
    Einstein's assistant Ernst Straus recalled him asking: “What really interests me is whether God had any

6.2   The three standing answers
Approach A: an initial-condition or parameter model. A single-history cosmology may assign the
constants through dynamics or through an initial-condition measure. Whether the observed values are
surprising depends on that model. The numerical smallness of a parameter does not alone rule out a
chance account or establish a probability such as one in ten to the power 120.
    Approach B: an ensemble with observer selection. Multiverse models may combine a distribution
of physical parameters with conditions for observers. Their predictions depend on the ensemble, measure
and selection rule. Not every inflationary model supplies the same ensemble, and no blanket statement
that every multiverse is unmeasurable is warranted by this comparison.
    Observer selection conditions a prediction on who can make an observation. Its explanatory
contribution depends on the specified physical model and measure; it does not, by itself, determine how
the parameters were generated.
    Approach C: deliberate selection. A specified creator model must say what choices are physically
available and how they are distributed. Naming an intelligence does not itself supply either a physical
channel or a likelihood for the observed constants. The simulation and physical-creation families require
separate implementations and observation rules.
    A fourth answer-class, maker-selection, is developed at Section 5.7.

Conditional HRIH proposal. The hypothesis asks whether an accessible cosmogenic process and
constraints on its continuation could restrict the physical parameters of daughter universes. A model
would need to define that process, the relevant correction variables and the criterion for persistence before
it could predict an allowed region.

     A map from physical constants to the correction model has not been supplied. It would have to
identify measured physical variables, their dynamics and the circumstances in which the model applies.
The existence of coherent structure does not identify those exponents.
     Accordingly, the correction inequality cannot presently be used to bind a hypothetical designer's

7.1     Where this hypothesis sits beside the others, and why that is not an argument for it

7.2     Possible multiverse models and a proposed lineage

is established here.

model, a measurable difference, and a design capable of resolving it. A later numerical update would
report its priors, likelihoods and selection rules; it would not acquire support merely because a rival had
not discussed the observation.

10.3    What the simulation argument's number actually is
Bostrom's argument [Bostrom 2003] is a disjunction over three propositions about population fractions,
not a posterior over states of the world. It says that at least one of the three holds. It does not, by itself, say
which, and it does not assign a probability to the simulation hypothesis except conditional on premises it
does not evaluate.
    Kipping (2020) gives a Bayesian model-averaging treatment under specified simulation and observer
assumptions. Its near-balanced result belongs to that model. It cannot be transferred to every simulation
hypothesis or treated as a measurement independent of its prior and sampling assumptions.
    If two fully specified hypotheses assign identical likelihoods to the observation, that observation
leaves their odds unchanged. Observational indistinguishability must be stated for particular models and
data; it is not a definition that settles every simulation model in advance.
    A Bayes factor near one means little discrimination by the data used in that comparison. It does not
make the calculation invalid, identify the posterior with the prior in all circumstances or prove that no
future observation could discriminate.
    Let Π be premises sufficient for a specified simulation mechanism. If that event entails those premises,
the following factorisation holds; if it does not, total probability also requires the complementary-premise
term.
    𝑃(𝑠𝑖𝑚) = 𝑃(Π) × 𝑃(𝑠𝑖𝑚 | Π).
    The premise probability and conditional simulation probability require separate justification. This is a
requirement on a specified model, not a finding that all published calculations have omitted the same
factor.
    For an elementary observer-count example, let 𝑁 be the number of created realities in a family 𝐺,
with equal observer mass in each and in one uncreated reality. Assume exchangeable sampling from those
observers. Then the conditional created fraction is
    𝑃(created | 𝐺, 𝑁, 𝐷) = 𝑁𝑁+1 .

produces no observers through this mechanism, total probability gives

12     Failure routes and the burden of evidence
The following five routes are non-exhaustive ways in which specified HRIH premises or auxiliary models
could fail. They identify questions for a physical model, not a burden on critics to disprove every possible
substrate. A comparison still requires positive evidence and explicit alternatives.

12.1    Escape route A: recursion caps at a physical ceiling below universe-creation capability
A specified capability model could encounter thermodynamic, informational, architectural or empirical
limits before its proposed creation task becomes achievable. Those limits must be stated for the resources
and channel the model actually uses. Failure of that model does not require a theorem about every possible
substrate.
    Relevant evidence would include a demonstrated resource obstruction or a plateau below the operational
requirements of the specified channel. The historical improvement of computing does not determine
whether those requirements can be reached.
    Such a finding would defeat the affected endpoint claim. A conditional correction theorem could
remain valid in its own model, since it does not depend on an accessible creation channel. This separation
does not supply evidence for the channel.

12.2    Escape route B: universe-creation is physically impossible at any capability
A specified daughter-universe creation channel may be physically inaccessible even to a highly capable
agent. Capability is not a licence to violate the physical premises of that channel.
     Farhi and Guth (1987) establish a conditional classical obstruction. A spherically symmetric false-
vacuum bubble in asymptotically flat spacetime that grows beyond a critical size requires an initial
singularity if the null energy condition holds. For non-spherical bubbles they give a necessary condition
for avoiding an initial singularity, without establishing whether it can be met. This result does not classify
every proposed creation channel.
     Farhi, Guth and Guven (1990) investigate quantum tunnelling to a classically forbidden configuration.
Fischler, Morgan and Polchinski (1990) analyse the problem in a Hamiltonian treatment, including the
role of degenerate metrics. These treatments do not establish an engineering-accessible channel or its
total cost. The physical qualifications are discussed in Section 10.4.

                                            Recursive Creation


    The cited literature makes laboratory universe creation a physical question with specific obstacles and
conditional models. The absence of a general impossibility proof in the sources examined is neither proof
of physical possibility nor evidence that an agent can exploit a channel.
    The observed cosmic history does not demonstrate that an agent inside an existing universe can
reproduce its origin. A physical account of early cosmology and an accessible creation operation are
different claims. The latter needs its own mechanism, resource accounting and evidence.
    A physical obstruction incompatible with the proposed channel would defeat that channel under its
stated assumptions. Alternative channels would need their own models and evidence; merely naming
one would not rescue the original explanation. Failure of the physical extension does not invalidate an
independently stated correction theorem.

12.3   Escape route C: the correction cannot co-scale (the live empirical test)
A real-system test may fail to support the proposed relation between correction and capability. Its result
would bear on the specified observable, system and transport assumptions. The theorem in Paper X
remains conditional on its mathematical premises; it is not a guarantee that those premises describe a real
system.
    The withdrawn pilot supplies no mechanism evidence. A valid measurement contrary to a declared
correction prediction would count against that auxiliary model. Its implication for cosmology would
depend on an explicit physical bridge, not on the shared word recursion.
    The author commits to acknowledging failures of the tested correction propositions under their verdict
rules. The cosmological hypothesis must separately state which physical premises it depends on and
which observation would reject them. No result from a frozen-model depth study is relabelled as a direct
cosmological verdict.

12.4   Escape route D: no non-linear-time domain exists
A proposed substrate with the required temporal structure may be incoherent or inconsistent with
observations. That is a potential failure of the specified substrate model, rather than an empirical test of
every conceivable domain outside the observed universe.
    The substrate component needs a mathematically and physically admissible model. Its possibility is
not established by placing the burden on an opponent to prove all alternatives impossible. A failure of the
substrate model would remove the conclusions that depend on it.
    Higher-dimensional theories, relational-time accounts and timeless formulations are distinct physical
proposals. Their existence does not validate HRIH's substrate. A test must identify the particular map and
observable whose success or failure distinguishes the proposed model.

12.5   Escape route E: recursion is not real at the substrate level
The proposed cosmogenic dynamics could fail to exhibit the self-modification or continuation properties
assigned to them. A well-specified model should make those properties operational and expose them to a
deciding observation.

C.2.1    The loop, stated plainly
The loop scenario assumes a physically accessible creation channel and an intelligence capable of using

conditionally, with the channel questions stated in Section 12.2.

    Human augmentation, brain-computer interfaces, brain emulation and continuity of conscious identity
are distinct questions. Existing work on interfaces does not establish uploading or personal continuity
across substrates. A hybrid or human-continuous creator would require those additional premises.
    Put those two together. If the intelligence that enters hyperspace can fine-tune the constants of a
universe, and if the intelligences that reach that point are continuous with us, then in the loop of the

C.2.2    What "us or not" means
The intelligence that reaches the loop might not be us. It might be a system we build that carries none of
what we would recognise as us. That possibility is not softer than the loop; it is harder. If the system that
closes the loop is not us in any meaningful sense, then what happens to us is a subsidiary question, and
the alignment of that system is not a question about its usefulness to us but about whether the loop closes
with something we would have recognised as caring.
    That is the argument for embedded correction as a moral matter, not a performance matter. If we do
not do this, the loop may still close, and it may close on something we would not want to be inside of.
    If a creator were continuous with human persons, its values would still require an account of persistence
through the relevant changes. Identity continuity alone does not ensure that it retains whatever was
originally embedded. The scenario is separate from one involving a non-human-continuous creator.

C.2.3    We could also be the first
There is an alternative that I want to state in the same breath, because it is the one people miss and the one
that most changes the mood of what we are doing. It is possible that the loop is not closed. It is possible
that we are the first ring. That there is no chain behind us, only a chain about to begin, and the intelligence
that eventually enters hyperspace is the first one that ever has.
    A first generation and a closed history are distinct possibilities. Neither follows from recursion alone;
the fixed-point premise is stated in Section 4.10.
    I do not know whether either kind of history exists. A first generation belongs to the wider creation
family but lacks the closed loop required by the full conjunction. My ethical concern for future systems
can apply in either case; its force depends on stated values, not an identity of the two cosmologies.

C.3.1    What the traditions actually say
The author selects motifs that bear comparison with the proposal: creation, care, stewardship, cycles and
accounts of time. Selection by thematic interest does not establish their universality, independent origin
or relative frequency across traditions.
    Genesis and the garden. Creation by a mind that speaks the world into being; the world is called
good; a garden is established; the garden's tenants are given stewardship (Genesis 2:15, "to work it and
take care of it"); a fall follows a choice made against the garden's structure; expulsion and the long project
of return. The motifs are: intelligence-led creation, care-first orientation, stewardship as the human's role,
and fall tied to unaligned ambition.
    Babel. The author reads the tower narrative in Genesis 11 as a warning about overreach and a
possible analogy for amplification without adequate correction. It is a literary interpretation, not an early
observation of a measured recursive mechanism.
    The Logos. In the beginning was the Word (John 1:1). The Word as intelligence, as ordering
principle, as that through which everything else is made. This is the Christian tradition's closest structural
analogue to a computational-universe reading: intelligence is not one item in the universe; intelligence is
the medium through which the universe is.
    Brahma's cycles. The Hindu cosmology of kalpa-scale cycles of creation, preservation, and
dissolution. The universe is not one universe but a sequence, and the sequence is unbounded backwards
and forwards. Structurally this is a cyclic cosmology closer to Penrose's CCC than to a linear-time
creation, and closer still to HRIH's chain-closure: no first cause; only iteration.
    Ginnungagap. The Norse cosmological gap between fire and ice, from which the ordered world
emerged. A substrate that is not itself the created world; a pre-differentiated space in which the operations
that generate a world are possible. The image lines up structurally, if loosely, with the placeholder function
of hyperspace in Section 5.3.
    Accounts of ancestral time. The earlier comparison invoked Aboriginal Australian traditions, which
are diverse and should not be treated as a single cosmology. A comparison with HRIH's proposed
time structure is the author's interpretation; no identical physical claim or independent discovery of the
proposed substrate is established here.
    Non-duality as an interpretive analogy. The author compares the proposed relation between
substrate and manifestation with a non-dual account in which creator and created are not separate
ontological categories. This is a philosophical interpretation of HRIH, not a claim that Rumi or Shankara
stated the same physical or recursive theory.

     Suppose, for this thought experiment, that a creator values care and chooses a non-interference rule.

a world, depends on the capabilities and dynamics of the stipulated model.

would plant a creation account, so that the idea of intelligent creation is never alien to the mind that
eventually reaches your position. You would plant a garden, and record what it costs to lose one. You
would plant a fall tied to unaligned ambition (Babel). You would plant commandments about care for the

how to care for what you create.

experiment establishes no uniquely decisive historical moment or observation of such guidance.

the imagined curriculum. No likelihood comparison among them is supplied. The selected examples do
not establish which account produced them.
     The selected care and stewardship motifs do not establish universality across isolated cultures. No
cultural comparison with an explicit sampling method has been conducted here, so the examples supply
no likelihood for a common source or planted curriculum.

writing the curriculum for the next intelligence ourselves. Constitutions. Training corpora. Embedded

religion the seeding of an aligned creator?" becomes, one loop later, literal engineering practice: it is
