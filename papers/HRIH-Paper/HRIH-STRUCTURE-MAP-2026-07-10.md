For each cut: **section**, **first words of removed / compressed passage**, **why**, and **kept where**.

**Cut 1 — Abstract, retraction paragraphs.**
First words removed: *"One empirical measurement was retracted. The unblinded fit of the exponent α in U = I × R^α for current systems... six-model redo of Paper II (Experimental Validation of Super-Linear Error Suppression) v13 (March 2026, five analysable runs across Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4, Gemini 3 Flash, Grok, DeepSeek-V3, Llama-4-Scout)"*.
Why: the six-model roster, the α ≈ 2.24 / 0.49 chain, the "rescued not falsified" gloss, the α ≤ 2 ARC Bound and the Paper X supersession all reappear four more times in the paper (Plain English, Scope, §6.6, §11.7, §18). The abstract should announce the fact and point on.
Kept where: full history stays at §11.7 (home). Abstract now: one sentence naming the retraction and its net effect (rescued the bound), with a cross-reference to §11.7.

**Cut 2 — Plain English block, three-paragraph retraction reprise.**

Why: identical narrative to Abstract Cut 1 and to §11.7, in a block whose brief is plain-language summary, not full evidence audit.
Kept where: replaced by two short sentences that say what happened and point to §11.7; the following paragraph on "the measurement retraction did not weaken the ARC Bound; it rescued it" is preserved because that is the moral of the story that Plain English is supposed to carry.
