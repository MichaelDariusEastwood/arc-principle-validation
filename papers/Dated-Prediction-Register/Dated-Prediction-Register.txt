                                                          Abstract
            A prediction supports a theory only if it was made before the evidence that tested it. Five
       cold-model assessments of this programme converged on one criticism: that its confirmations might be
       accommodations. This register answers it with one dated chain and states exactly what the chain does
       and does not establish. It does not make the programme preregistered in the registry sense: registered
       is kept for an accepted registry submission, and a design fixed by a dated timestamp before its run
       is called preregistered by timestamp. It establishes something narrower and checkable: every core
       quantitative claim has at least one dated artefact fixing it before the measurement that tested it, except
       Paper VII’s fifty-domain cohort, whose predictions were not timestamped in advance of their own fits,
       and a stranger can verify each link in minutes from public materials. The claim is the conjunction:
       any single prediction here may have been stated earlier by someone else, and where that is known it
       is listed as convergence; what no earlier record contains is all of these statements together, in one
       record, before the programme’s experiments ran. The chain runs from a sealed manuscript bundle
       of 8 December 2024, through a printed edition of 2 January 2026 with nine numbered predictions
       and their falsification criteria, a public priority ledger of 13 February 2026 and result files of March
       2026 whose headers froze designs and planted answers before the responses, to twelve predictions
       committed in public before their fits on 17 March 2026 and the staged draft registrations. Its sharpest
       artefact is a correction: public commit history shows the author demoting his own successful 10/12
       result from “pre-registered” to “pilot dry run” thirteen minutes after recording it. A census gives
       every dated prediction with its date, the kind of record that dates it and its status.

Version 2.2 brings the register’s record of registration up to date, with the registered verdict on P21, and makes its
corrections; Appendix B lists every version and correction, with its date.

1      The doctrine, stated exactly
Three vocabulary rules govern every sentence of this paper. First, as amended on 3 October 2026,
registered is kept for a registry submission that has been accepted, and a design fixed by a dated timestamp
before its run is preregistered by timestamp, with its date and receipt, which has been the programme’s
usage since 24 September 2026; as read on 9 November 2026, four of the programme’s studies have
accepted registry submissions, the submission click is a human act, and no tool or agent performs it.
Second, the printed book (Eastwood 2026f) is a prior statement, never a preregistration: print fixes a
prediction, but only a registration fixes an analysis plan. Third, forward statements and retrospective
matches are never summed: this register contains only dated forward statements, and retrospective
convergences live separately in the convergence register, typed by evidence class. The state under the
first rule, as read on 9 November 2026, is this. Five registrations have been accepted. One is the theory
registration of 8 September 2026, which registers the theory’s twenty-two propositions and its five laws,
each a law under test: Laws I to III since 8 September 2026, and Laws IV and V since its version 1.103
of 15 October 2026 (Section 8.1); it is not the registration of any study. The other four are registrations
of trials, each submitted by the author and accepted by OSF: Unit 06 (1 October 2026), Unit 83 (22
October 2026), Unit 82 (27 October 2026) and Unit 83b (7 November 2026), each set out in Section 8.2.
Every other trial is a draft awaiting the author’s submission, apart from those Section 8.3 gives as held,
withdrawn, set aside or superseded. Sixty-five of the programme’s designs, Unit 06 among them, are
preregistered by timestamp, the first of them anchored on 12 August 2026 at Bitcoin block 962066.
     The questions this register serves have long literatures: when a prediction made before its evidence
counts for more than an accommodation (Hitchcock and Sober 2004), the cost of hypothesising after the
results are known (Kerr 1998), and the practice of fixing a plan before the data (Nosek et al. 2018); this
register applies them to one programme’s dated record. The claim made here is the conjunction. Each
part may be conceded individually without cost: someone may have predicted any one of these things
earlier, a folder is not a registry, a book is not an analysis plan. The dated co-occurrence of all of them in
one record before the programme’s experiments ran is the claim, and it is refutable: produce an earlier
single record containing this ensemble, or show any link’s date to be wrong.

3      The printed appendices, graded severally
Appendix F (Eastwood 2026f) closes: “These predictions are my wager. If they fail, the framework
is wrong or incomplete. If they succeed, something important has been glimpsed. Time will judge.”
The programme grades the five predictions unequally, because claiming them equally would be false
precision. Prediction 2 (alignment drift above 15 per cent within 18 months without hardware-level
constraint, below 5 per cent with it, difference significant and replicable) is preregistration-grade in
substance: quantitative thresholds for both arms, a time window, an inferential commitment. The prepared
study-v draft registration carries those printed thresholds unchanged into its formal hypothesis, citing the
appendix by name: the book made the prediction, the registration supplies the measurement, and the
numeric commitment predates the study that will test it. The theory registration states the limit of that
draft: Prediction 2 is “the subject of its own drafted unit, with the hardware claim and the eighteen-month
window declared untested inside it”. Predictions 3 and 4 are near-grade (a threshold and deadline without
named benchmarks; a comparison and method without a threshold). Predictions 1 and 5 are predictions
only, and Prediction 5 is graded the weakest, vague enough to be claimed either way, so it is held to the
strictest standard before it may ever be claimed.
     Appendix A (Eastwood 2026f) is graded separately and less kindly, because its predictions and its
reasoning do not stand together. Section A.3 carries four predictions, each with a variable to vary and
an outcome to measure, and the fourth is a controlled design in the ordinary sense: identical training
data, different sequencing of value-relevant examples, persistence of the early values measured against the
later ones. Section A.4 states four falsifiers in advance. But section A.2, which is where the square is
justified, argues from three phenomena it calls exponential to a relation that is quadratic, and its third
argument makes the slip explicit by describing compounding at one per cent a cycle as “the exponential
behaviour predicted by R2 ” (Eastwood 2026f, Appendix A). Exponential growth and quadratic growth
are different classes and the quadratic is the slower of the two, so the section’s supports do not reach its
conclusion. The prediction is dated, public and genuine; the printed derivation beside it is not sound, and
the programme’s own chronology says as much in saying the book printed the square without deriving it
and dating the formal open exponent to 17 January 2026. Nor has the measurement decided it. On Gemini

4    The thirteen minutes
On 17 March 2026 at 00:43 UTC, commit e5c22dd recorded “Pre-registered 12-domain extension:
10/12 confirmed (p = 5.44e-4)”. At 00:47, f5e06a8 adopted a conservative miss posture. At 00:56,
c853277 downgraded the run to a pilot dry run. The result was never retracted; ten of twelve stands
in the result files today. What was corrected, by the author, unprompted, in public, months before any
external review existed, was the claim about the result’s status. The packet even instructs against its own
over-claim: “Do not upload this packet unchanged as a preregistration. Use it only as an audit trail for the
first locked 12-domain dry run.” The demotion is kept here exactly as it was written. It rested on two
grounds. The first, that a registry timestamp was required, was corrected on 6 September 2026 by the
programme’s attestation-class rule, which does not demote a prediction for the absence of a registry form,
and the record was re-read under that rule on 7 September 2026: the twelve predictions were committed
to the public repository at 00:19:19 UTC on 17 March 2026, attestation class public repository commit
date, twenty-three minutes before the fits ran, so they were timestamped in advance of their outcome, and
they are described on those terms and never as registered or preregistered. The second ground stands: the
packet’s own written condition, that it be deposited to a registry before data extraction, was not met, since
the run and the extraction preceded that night’s deposit. One row repeats a domain, operator class and
predicted model already public at 23:54:53 UTC on 16 March 2026, attestation class public repository
commit date, with a matching outcome, so the strictly fresh count reads 9 of 11, 𝑝 = 1.372 × 10−3 ; a
second row shares an operator class and predicted family with an entry public before the predictions
commit, and dropping it too gives 8 of 10, 𝑝 = 3.404 × 10−3 . The result and both counts stand as dated
results under a superseded taxonomy (the pre-correction candidate set, with no logarithmic candidate) and
a superseded statistic (the one-sided binomial against one third, which a later public correction replaced
with a permutation test conditioned on both marginals), and the theory registration records the twelve
domains as “burned for reuse as fresh domains”. This is the programme’s correction discipline operating
at minute resolution, and it is why the honesty record and the prediction record are the same record.


5    What this register does not establish
It does not make the programme preregistered in the registry sense. Four study units have accepted
registrations (Section 8.2), and two of them have run: Unit 06, administratively incomplete, and Unit 83,
which has reported the programme’s first registered verdict. Every earlier run is dated from its own record,
never given a blanket status, and this register claims registration for nothing else. The March 2026 folder
governs only the 12-domain extension; its own registration text states it does not retroactively preregister

deadlines that can simply fail.

7    Verification
Every chain link names its artefact. The repository is public; its commit dates are written by the committer
and are author-settable in principle, not an independent anchor, and an OSF deposit dated by its server
corroborates them where one exists; the print edition is fixed by ISBN; the OpenTimestamps proofs are
Bitcoin-attested; the machine-readable register (https://www.michaeldariuseastwood.com/research/data/
design-embedded-predictions-register.json) carries all thirty-six run-file rows with per-artefact paths and
date bases. A reader who trusts none of the prose can start from the JSON, open the named files, and
check the dates.

8.2     The registered units
Four study units are registered on OSF, each submitted by the author and accepted by the registry. Unit 06,
the corrector-class audit, was registered on OSF on 1 October 2026 and has been public since 4 October
2026, at the end of OSF’s approval period (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/RV2B5). In its own words it is “the
instrument the theory registration (version 1.102, DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/P8CKQ) names as deciding P7”,
a survey of current practice that the registration places in no register and names as the premise of the
Eden Protocol. Its three registered predictions:
      • The class distribution. “The prediction is that at least 90 per cent of the classified, non-indeterminate
        deployed subset falls in class 1, static, or class 2, capability-carried, and that class 3 mechanisms
        are absent from deployed production systems or confined to research prototypes.”
      • Decidability from public documentation. “The prediction is that at least 80 per cent of inventoried
        mechanisms receive an agreed class when two blind assignments are made, the second without
        sight of the first.”
      • Exhaustiveness of the taxonomy. “The prediction is that no inventoried mechanism requires a
        fifth dependency.”
    It was run on 6 October 2026 and is reported in full with its five deviations; one of its two
runs could not start, so it is administratively incomplete, with no inferential verdict (results DOI
10.17605/OSF.IO/4BPHG).
    Unit 83, the counted test of P21 under Law V, was registered on OSF on 22 October 2026 (DOI
10.17605/OSF.IO/MN73W) and ran on 27 and 28 October 2026: P21 is supported under the registered test,
by the programme’s own run, on the access axis, family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not evaluable
and one call retried under a disclosed recovery amendment (results: DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/98HKR). In
the words of Operational Definitions, “The access axis names the varied access and action permissions;
performance names the outcome measured” (Eastwood 2026i). Law V’s placement limb is therefore
printed, as the registration’s verdict rule requires, as supported on the performance axis only, which is
not support for the information-asymmetry mechanism; its build-order limb, read on P12, is undecided,
since P12 is untested. In Unit 83, the in-round corrector saw the proposed item and could repair it; the
monitor reviewed the accumulated register at each round, with nothing marked, and could accept or reject.
Information access and repair rights therefore changed together, so the comparison does not identify their
separate effects.

C     Priority record
This register makes no priority claim for any single prediction, by decision and not by omission. It is a
register of dated forward statements, and its own claim is the ensemble rather than priority over any one
of them: earlier statements by others are recorded as convergence in the separate convergence register,
typed by evidence class. Listing per-row priority claims here would assert the thing the register declines
to assert. Its own dated record: first published on 25 August 2026, the date the register states; its DOI,
10.17605/OSF.IO/C3NTF, registered on 4 October 2026 (a server date); each version since dated in
Appendix B. Each link of the chain in Section 2 carries the kind of record that dates it.


D     Notation and glossary
D.1    Terms
registered. Kept for a registry submission that has been accepted (Section 1).
preregistered by timestamp. Said of a design fixed by a dated timestamp before its run, with its date
      and receipt (Section 1).
printed prior statement. The standing of the book’s printed predictions: a prior statement, never a
      preregistration, since print fixes a prediction but only a registration fixes an analysis plan (Section 1).
convergent timing. The grade of an outside result that was public before the programme’s dated record
      of the same point: a convergence in time, and never a prediction (Section 3; Table 5).
kind of dating record. The four kinds of record that fix a date: a Bitcoin anchor; a deposit dated by its
      server (an OSF upload, a Zenodo deposit, a GitHub merge or release); a commit date, which the
      committer writes; or a date written inside a document. No kind is presented as stronger than it is
      (Sections 2 and 7).
attestation class. The kind of record that dates a prediction, such as a public repository commit date;
      under the attestation-class rule a prediction is dated by that record and is not demoted for the
      absence of a registry form (Section 4).
prediction 1 to prediction 5. The five numbered predictions of the book’s Appendix F, by the print’s
      own numbers (Section 6); they are not the registered propositions P1 to P5.
