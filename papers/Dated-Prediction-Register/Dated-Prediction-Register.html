Abstract

A prediction supports a theory only if it was made before the evidence that tested it. Five cold-model assessments of this programme converged on one criticism: that its confirmations might be accommodations. This register answers it with one dated chain and states exactly what the chain does and does not establish. It does not make the programme preregistered in the registry sense: registered is kept for an accepted registry submission, and a design fixed by a dated timestamp before its run is called preregistered by timestamp. It establishes something narrower and checkable: every core quantitative claim has at least one dated artefact fixing it before the measurement that tested it, except Paper VII’s fifty-domain cohort, whose predictions were not timestamped in advance of their own fits, and a stranger can verify each link in minutes from public materials. The claim is the conjunction: any single prediction here may have been stated earlier by someone else, and where that is known it is listed as convergence; what no earlier record contains is all of these statements together, in one record, before the programme’s experiments ran. The chain runs from a sealed manuscript bundle of 8 December 2024, through a printed edition of 2 January 2026 with nine numbered predictions and their falsification criteria, a public priority ledger of 13 February 2026 and result files of March 2026 whose headers froze designs and planted answers before the responses, to twelve predictions committed in public before their fits on 17 March 2026 and the staged draft registrations. Its sharpest artefact is a correction: public commit history shows the author demoting his own successful 10/12 result from “pre-registered” to “pilot dry run” thirteen minutes after recording it. A census gives every dated prediction with its date, the kind of record that dates it and its status.

Version 2.2 brings the register’s record of registration up to date, with the registered verdict on P21, and makes its corrections; Appendix B lists every version and correction, with its date.

1 The doctrine, stated exactly

Three vocabulary rules govern every sentence of this paper. First, as amended on 3 October 2026, registered is kept for a registry submission that has been accepted, and a design fixed by a dated timestamp before its run is preregistered by timestamp, with its date and receipt, which has been the programme’s usage since 24 September 2026; as read on 9 November 2026, four of the programme’s studies have accepted registry submissions, the submission click is a human act, and no tool or agent performs it. Second, the printed book (Eastwood 2026k) is a prior statement, never a preregistration: print fixes a prediction, but only a registration fixes an analysis plan. Third, forward statements and retrospective matches are never summed: this register contains only dated forward statements, and retrospective convergences live separately in the convergence register, typed by evidence class. The state under the first rule, as read on 9 November 2026, is this. Five registrations have been accepted. One is the theory registration of 8 September 2026, which registers the theory’s twenty-two propositions and its five laws, each a law under test: Laws I to III since 8 September 2026, and Laws IV and V since its version 1.103 of 15 October 2026 (Section 8.1); it is not the registration of any study. The other four are registrations of trials, each submitted by the author and accepted by OSF: Unit 06 (1 October 2026), Unit 83 (22 October 2026), Unit 82 (27 October 2026) and Unit 83b (7 November 2026), each set out in Section 8.2. Every other trial is a draft awaiting the author’s submission, apart from those Section 8.3 gives as held, withdrawn, set aside or superseded. Sixty-five of the programme’s designs, Unit 06 among them, are preregistered by timestamp, the first of them anchored on 12 August 2026 at Bitcoin block 962066.

The questions this register serves have long literatures: when a prediction made before its evidence counts for more than an accommodation (Hitchcock and Sober 2004), the cost of hypothesising after the results are known (Kerr 1998), and the practice of fixing a plan before the data (Nosek et al. 2018); this register applies them to one programme’s dated record. The claim made here is the conjunction. Each part may be conceded individually without cost: someone may have predicted any one of these things earlier, a folder is not a registry, a book is not an analysis plan. The dated co-occurrence of all of them in one record before the programme’s experiments ran is the claim, and it is refutable: produce an earlier single record containing this ensemble, or show any link’s date to be wrong.

3 The printed appendices, graded severally

Appendix F (Eastwood 2026k) closes: “These predictions are my wager. If they fail, the framework is wrong or incomplete. If they succeed, something important has been glimpsed. Time will judge.” The programme grades the five predictions unequally, because claiming them equally would be false precision. Prediction 2 (alignment drift above 15 per cent within 18 months without hardware-level constraint, below 5 per cent with it, difference significant and replicable) is preregistration-grade in substance: quantitative thresholds for both arms, a time window, an inferential commitment. The prepared study-v draft registration carries those printed thresholds unchanged into its formal hypothesis, citing the appendix by name: the book made the prediction, the registration supplies the measurement, and the numeric commitment predates the study that will test it. The theory registration states the limit of that draft: Prediction 2 is “the subject of its own drafted unit, with the hardware claim and the eighteen-month window declared untested inside it”. Predictions 3 and 4 are near-grade (a threshold and deadline without named benchmarks; a comparison and method without a threshold). Predictions 1 and 5 are predictions only, and Prediction 5 is graded the weakest, vague enough to be claimed either way, so it is held to the strictest standard before it may ever be claimed.

Appendix A (Eastwood 2026k) is graded separately and less kindly, because its predictions and its reasoning do not stand together. Section A.3 carries four predictions, each with a variable to vary and an outcome to measure, and the fourth is a controlled design in the ordinary sense: identical training data, different sequencing of value-relevant examples, persistence of the early values measured against the later ones. Section A.4 states four falsifiers in advance. But section A.2, which is where the square is justified, argues from three phenomena it calls exponential to a relation that is quadratic, and its third argument makes the slip explicit by describing compounding at one per cent a cycle as “the exponential behaviour predicted by R2” (Eastwood 2026k, Appendix A). Exponential growth and quadratic growth are different classes and the quadratic is the slower of the two, so the section’s supports do not reach its conclusion. The prediction is dated, public and genuine; the printed derivation beside it is not sound, and the programme’s own chronology says as much in saying the book printed the square without deriving it and dating the formal open exponent to 17 January 2026. Nor has the measurement decided it. On Gemini 3 Flash, Paper II reports an endpoint sequential exponent of 0.59 with a bootstrap 95 per cent interval of minus 1.3 to 2.9, a regression sequential exponent of 0.49 with a standard error of 0.20 and a coefficient of determination of 0.86, and a parallel exponent of 0.31 by endpoint estimate, the two arms not matched for compute. These are exponents of error decay, the rate at which error falls as recursive depth rises, and not measurements of the capability exponent the printed square concerns; they bear on it only through a metric bridge that has not been stated and identified. The fit is one model’s and inconclusive, and the 2.24, an earlier estimate of the same error-decay exponent once read as support for a square, was publicly retracted, which is why the outcomes register grades the quadratic prediction open-undecided rather than fulfilled. Appendix A’s third prediction and third falsifier rest on Google Willow, which carries both dates wherever it appears here: it was announced with its paper in Nature on 9 December 2024, and the team’s preprint was public from 24 August 2024, before the December manuscript record, so it is graded convergent timing and never prediction.

4 The thirteen minutes

On 17 March 2026 at 00:43 UTC, commit e5c22dd recorded “Pre-registered 12-domain extension: 10/12 confirmed (p = 5.44e-4)”. At 00:47, f5e06a8 adopted a conservative miss posture. At 00:56, c853277 downgraded the run to a pilot dry run. The result was never retracted; ten of twelve stands in the result files today. What was corrected, by the author, unprompted, in public, months before any external review existed, was the claim about the result’s status. The packet even instructs against its own over-claim: “Do not upload this packet unchanged as a preregistration. Use it only as an audit trail for the first locked 12-domain dry run.” The demotion is kept here exactly as it was written. It rested on two grounds. The first, that a registry timestamp was required, was corrected on 6 September 2026 by the programme’s attestation-class rule, which does not demote a prediction for the absence of a registry form, and the record was re-read under that rule on 7 September 2026: the twelve predictions were committed to the public repository at 00:19:19 UTC on 17 March 2026, attestation class public repository commit date, twenty-three minutes before the fits ran, so they were timestamped in advance of their outcome, and they are described on those terms and never as registered or preregistered. The second ground stands: the packet’s own written condition, that it be deposited to a registry before data extraction, was not met, since the run and the extraction preceded that night’s deposit. One row repeats a domain, operator class and predicted model already public at 23:54:53 UTC on 16 March 2026, attestation class public repository commit date, with a matching outcome, so the strictly fresh count reads 9 of 11, p=1.372×10−3p = 1.372 \times 10^{-3}; a second row shares an operator class and predicted family with an entry public before the predictions commit, and dropping it too gives 8 of 10, p=3.404×10−3p = 3.404 \times 10^{-3}. The result and both counts stand as dated results under a superseded taxonomy (the pre-correction candidate set, with no logarithmic candidate) and a superseded statistic (the one-sided binomial against one third, which a later public correction replaced with a permutation test conditioned on both marginals), and the theory registration records the twelve domains as “burned for reuse as fresh domains”. This is the programme’s correction discipline operating at minute resolution, and it is why the honesty record and the prediction record are the same record.

5 What this register does not establish

It does not make the programme preregistered in the registry sense. Four study units have accepted registrations (Section 8.2), and two of them have run: Unit 06, administratively incomplete, and Unit 83, which has reported the programme’s first registered verdict. Every earlier run is dated from its own record, never given a blanket status, and this register claims registration for nothing else. The March 2026 folder governs only the 12-domain extension; its own registration text states it does not retroactively preregister the 25-domain or 50-domain cohorts, and that criticism stands. It does not establish priority over other authors for any individual idea; earlier statements by others are convergence and are listed as such. It does not establish that the predictions are true: the standing wagers below are open, and several carry deadlines that can simply fail.

7 Verification

C Priority record

This register makes no priority claim for any single prediction, by decision and not by omission. It is a register of dated forward statements, and its own claim is the ensemble rather than priority over any one of them: earlier statements by others are recorded as convergence in the separate convergence register, typed by evidence class. Listing per-row priority claims here would assert the thing the register declines to assert. D Notation and glossary

D.1 Terms

registered.

Kept for a registry submission that has been accepted (Section 1).

preregistered by timestamp.

Said of a design fixed by a dated timestamp before its run, with its date and receipt (Section 1).

printed prior statement.

The standing of the book’s printed predictions: a prior statement, never a preregistration, since print fixes a prediction but only a registration fixes an analysis plan (Section 1).

convergent timing.

The grade of an outside result that was public before the programme’s dated record of the same point: a convergence in time, and never a prediction (Section 3; Table 5).

kind of dating record.

The four kinds of record that fix a date: a Bitcoin anchor; a deposit dated by its server (an OSF upload, a Zenodo deposit, a GitHub merge or release); a commit date, which the committer writes; or a date written inside a document. No kind is presented as stronger than it is (Sections 2 and 7).

attestation class.

The kind of record that dates a prediction, such as a public repository commit date; under the attestation-class rule a prediction is dated by that record and is not demoted for the absence of a registry form (Section 4).

prediction 1 to prediction 5.

The five numbered predictions of the book’s Appendix F, by the print’s own numbers (Section 6); they are not the registered propositions P1 to P5.

P1 to P22.

The twenty-two propositions of the theory registration, registered on 8 September 2026 (Section 8.1).

ARC-1 to ARC-5; Laws I to V.

The five registers of the theory registration, one for each of the theory’s five laws: ARC-1, Law I, the ARC Principle; ARC-2, Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; ARC-3, Law III, the ARC Ceiling; ARC-4, value persistence, Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law; and ARC-5, the genesis strategy, Law V, the ARC Embedding Law. D.2 Symbols

RR and R2R^{2}.

RR is recursive depth, and R2R^{2} is the square the book prints in its Appendix A, by which AI development scales quadratically rather than linearly with recursive depth: a dated, public and genuine prediction, beside a printed derivation that is not sound (Section 3; Table 5).

α\alpha.

The exponent of recursive depth that Paper II estimates: an exponent of error decay, the rate at which error falls as recursive depth rises, and not the capability exponent the printed square concerns, on which it bears only through a metric bridge not yet stated and identified (Section 3). Its early headline estimate of about 2.24 is retracted (Table 5).

χ\chi and γD\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}.

Section 6’s last wager is P6’s conjecture on χ\chi, the correction-leverage exponent, which the theory registration writes γ\gamma: for a same-class corrector, χ\chi is at most one half, a value Operational Definitions (Eastwood 2026l) calls “a conjecture and not a derivation” (Section 8.1). Versions 1.0 to 2.1 of this register printed the wager as γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2 with the label conversion exponent. It is not γD\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}, “the capability-to-depth conversion exponent”, a different quantity that no registered study has measured. The ceilings the two return agree at exactly one half, so a bare γ\gamma could be read as either, and this register writes the wager with χ\chi.

pp.

The one-sided binomial probability against one third, the chance of a family match under uniform random assignment to three families: the probability of at least the observed number of matches by chance. A later public correction replaced it with a permutation test conditioned on both marginals, so each pp printed here is a dated result under a superseded statistic (Section 4; Paper VII; the theory registration, version 1.104).

d/(d+1)d/(d+1).
