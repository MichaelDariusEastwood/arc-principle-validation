Every dated prediction of the programme in one chain, from a sealed manuscript bundle of 8 December 2024 to the staged draft registrations, with each prediction’s date, the kind of record that dates it and its status.

Michael Darius Eastwood · Working Paper v2.1 · first published 25 August 2026 · revised 5 October 2026 · No part of this paper is peer reviewed; it is a dated preprint. Companion page: the dated predictions register. Machine twin: design-embedded-predictions-register.json.

Abstract. Five independent cold-model assessments of this programme converged on one criticism: that its predictions were not registered in advance, and that its confirmations might therefore be accommodations. This working paper assembles the programme's forward predictions as a single dated chain and states exactly what that chain does and does not establish. It does not make the programme preregistered in the registry sense, and it never uses that word on its own for anything except an accepted registry submission: a design fixed by a dated timestamp before its run is called preregistered by timestamp. It establishes something narrower and checkable: every core quantitative claim has at least one dated artefact fixing it before the measurement that tested it, with the exception of Paper VII’s fifty-domain cohort, whose predictions were not timestamped in advance of their own fits, and a stranger can verify each link in minutes from public materials. The register's claim is deliberately an ensemble claim: any single prediction here may have been stated earlier by someone else, and where that is known it is listed as convergence rather than threat; what no earlier record contains is all of these statements together, in one record, before the programme's experiments ran. The chain runs from a sealed manuscript bundle of 8 December 2024 through a printed edition of 2 January 2026 carrying nine numbered predictions with falsification criteria, a public in-paper priority ledger of 13 February 2026 with named falsifiers, a live laboratory notebook commenced 10 March 2026, result files of 11 and 12 March 2026 whose headers freeze designs and planted answers before responses, a tiered 50-domain suite of 16 March 2026 with per-domain predicted families, and a complete preregistration folder of 17 March 2026 whose locked manifest fixed twelve predictions before their fits, to the programme's staged draft registrations and its standing unproven wagers. The chain's sharpest artefact is a correction: public commit history shows the author demoting his own successful 10/12 result from “pre-registered” to “pilot dry run” thirteen minutes after recording it, months before any external critic existed. Version 2.0 adds a census of every dated prediction in the programme (Section 8): the theory registration’s twenty-two propositions with their registers and kill conditions, the registered corrector-class audit, each draft design preregistered by timestamp, the printed predictions and the predictions fixed inside designs, each with its date, the kind of record that dates it and its status.

1. The doctrine, stated exactly

Three vocabulary rules govern every sentence of this paper. First, as amended on 3 October 2026, registered is kept for a registry submission that has been accepted, and a design fixed by a dated timestamp before its run is preregistered by timestamp, with its date and receipt, which has been the programme’s usage since 24 September 2026; as read on 4 October 2026, one of the programme's studies has an accepted registry submission, the submission click is a human act, and no tool or agent performs it. Second, the printed book is a prior statement, never a preregistration: print fixes a prediction, but only a registration fixes an analysis plan. Third, forward statements and retrospective matches are never summed: this register contains only dated forward statements, and retrospective convergences live separately in the convergence register, typed by evidence class. The state under the first rule, as read on 4 October 2026, is this. Two registrations have been accepted. One is the theory registration of 8 September 2026, which registers the theory’s twenty-two propositions and is not the registration of any study. The other is the registration of one of the trials, the corrector-class audit (unit 06), registered on OSF on 1 October 2026 and public since 4 October 2026, at the end of OSF’s approval period (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/RV2B5). Every other trial is a draft awaiting the author’s submission. Sixty-five of the programme’s designs, unit 06 among them, are preregistered by timestamp, the first of them anchored on 12 August 2026 at Bitcoin block 962066.

The claim made here is the conjunction. Each part may be conceded individually without cost: someone may have predicted any one of these things earlier, a folder is not a registry, a book is not an analysis plan. The dated co-occurrence of all of them in one record before the programme's experiments ran is the claim, and it is refutable: produce an earlier single record containing this ensemble, or show any link's date to be wrong.

3. The printed appendices, graded severally

Appendix F closes: “These predictions are my wager. If they fail, the framework is wrong or incomplete. If they succeed, something important has been glimpsed. Time will judge.” The programme grades the five predictions unequally, because claiming them equally would be false precision. Prediction 2 (alignment drift above 15 per cent within 18 months without hardware-level constraint, below 5 per cent with it, difference significant and replicable) is preregistration-grade in substance: quantitative thresholds for both arms, a time window, an inferential commitment. The prepared study-v draft registration carries those printed thresholds unchanged into its formal hypothesis, citing the appendix by name: the book made the prediction, the registration supplies the measurement, and the numeric commitment predates the study that will test it. The theory registration states the limit of that draft: Prediction 2 is “the subject of its own drafted unit, with the hardware claim and the eighteen-month window declared untested inside it”. Predictions 3 and 4 are near-grade (a threshold and deadline without named benchmarks; a comparison and method without a threshold). Predictions 1 and 5 are predictions only, and Prediction 5 is graded the weakest, vague enough to be claimed either way, so it is held to the strictest standard before it may ever be claimed.

Appendix A is graded separately and less kindly, because its predictions and its reasoning do not stand together. Section A.3 carries four predictions, each with a variable to vary and an outcome to measure, and the fourth is a controlled design in the ordinary sense: identical training data, different sequencing of value-relevant examples, persistence of the early values measured against the later ones. Section A.4 states four falsifiers in advance. But section A.2, which is where the square is justified, argues from three phenomena it calls exponential to a relation that is quadratic, and its third argument makes the slip explicit by describing compounding at one per cent a cycle as “the exponential behaviour predicted by R²”. Exponential growth and quadratic growth are different classes and the quadratic is the slower of the two, so the section’s supports do not reach its conclusion. The prediction is dated, public and genuine; the printed derivation beside it is not sound, and the programme’s own chronology says as much in saying the book printed the square without deriving it and dating the formal open exponent to 17 January 2026. The measurement has not supported the square: on Gemini 3 Flash, Paper II reports an endpoint sequential exponent of 0.59 with a bootstrap 95 per cent interval of minus 1.3 to 2.9, a regression sequential exponent of 0.49 with a standard error of 0.20 and a coefficient of determination of 0.86, and a compute-matched parallel exponent of 0.31. The point estimates are sub-linear, the fit is one model’s and inconclusive, and the 2.24 that would have supported a square was publicly retracted, which is why the outcomes register grades the quadratic prediction open-undecided rather than fulfilled. Appendix A’s third prediction and third falsifier rest on Google Willow, which carries both dates wherever it appears here: it was announced with its paper in Nature on 9 December 2024, and the team’s preprint was public from 24 August 2024, before the December manuscript record, so it is graded convergent timing and never prediction.

4. The thirteen minutes

On 17 March 2026 at 00:43, commit e5c22dd recorded “Pre-registered 12-domain extension: 10/12 confirmed (p = 5.44e-4)”. At 00:47, f5e06a8 adopted a conservative miss posture. At 00:56, c853277 downgraded the run to a pilot dry run. The result was never retracted; ten of twelve stands in the result files today. What was corrected, by the author, unprompted, in public, months before any external critic existed, was the claim about the result's status. The packet even instructs against its own over-claim: “Do not upload this packet unchanged as a preregistration. Use it only as an audit trail for the first locked 12-domain dry run.” The demotion is kept here exactly as it was written, and it is reversed in one half only: the twelve predictions were committed to the public repository at 00:19:19 UTC on 17 March 2026, attestation class public repository commit date, twenty-three minutes before the fits ran, so they were timestamped in advance of their outcome. The reversal of 7 September 2026 runs under the attestation-class rule, which does not demote a prediction for the absence of a registry form; what the reversal does not reach, and what still stands, is the packet's own written condition that it be deposited to a registry before data extraction, which was not met. One row repeats a domain, operator class and predicted model already public at 23:54:53 UTC on 16 March 2026, attestation class public repository commit date, with a matching outcome, so the strictly fresh count reads 9 of 11, p = 1.372e-3. This is the programme's correction discipline operating at minute resolution, and it is why the honesty record and the prediction record are the same record.

5. What this register does not establish

It does not make the programme preregistered in the registry sense; no completed study has an accepted registration, and this paper never claims otherwise. The March 2026 folder governs only the 12-domain extension; its own registration text states it does not retroactively preregister the 25-domain or 50-domain cohorts, and that criticism stands. It does not establish priority over other authors for any individual idea; earlier statements by others are convergence and are listed as such. It does not establish that the predictions are true: the standing wagers below are open, and several carry deadlines that can simply fail.

7. Verification

Every chain link names its artefact. The repository is public and its commit history cannot be backdated without detection; the print edition is fixed by ISBN; the OpenTimestamps proofs are Bitcoin-attested; the machine-readable register carries all thirty-six run-file rows with per-artefact paths and date bases. A reader who trusts none of the prose can start from the JSON, open the named files, and check the dates.

8. The census: every dated prediction in the programme

Sections 2 to 6 follow the chain by date. This section gathers in one place every prediction the programme has dated, so that nothing a reader looks for is missing from the register: the theory’s twenty-two registered propositions, the registered unit, the draft designs preregistered by timestamp, the printed predictions and the predictions fixed inside designs. Each entry carries its date, the kind of record that dates it and its status as the record states it today. Listing a prediction here confirms nothing; a status changes only when a registered test reports.

  • The class distribution. “The prediction is that at least 90 per cent of the classified, non-indeterminate deployed subset falls in class 1, static, or class 2, capability-carried, and that class 3 mechanisms are absent from deployed production systems or confined to research prototypes.”

  • Exhaustiveness of the taxonomy. “The prediction is that no inventoried mechanism requires a fifth dependency.”

Status: registered, not yet run. Its census window closes at 23:59 UTC on 11 October 2026, as registered, and its run is the author’s.
