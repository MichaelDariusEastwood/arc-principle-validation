Abstract
--------
Two frameworks in this programme describe recursive self-improvement and have
never been related. One derives how capability compounds with recursive depth
and yields the scaling exponent α = 1/(1 − β_L). The other derives when a
self-improving system remains correctable and yields the criterion β_X > k. They
use the same two letters for different quantities, which has prevented the
comparison rather than merely obscuring it.

This paper resolves the notation, derives the relation between the frameworks,
and finds that the quantity deciding the outcome is neither capability nor speed
but how fast a system compresses its own iteration time. Writing cycle duration
as Δt ∝ C^{−δ} defines the self-acceleration exponent δ, and the growth exponent
of the corrective framework follows exactly: k = δ − 1/α

Key findings (quotable)
