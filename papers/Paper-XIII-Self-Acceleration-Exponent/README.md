# When can correction keep pace with recursive self-acceleration?

**Version:** 1.12
**Revised:** 1 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-08-16
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 1.12):

> I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons; only proof will move the world. This paper joins two programme models by resolving their notation and specifying a clock. On a fixed substrate, capability $C$ grows with recursive depth with exponent $\alpha = 1/(1-\beta_L)$, while cycle duration scales as $\Delta t \propto C^{-\delta}$. Their continuous-time growth exponent is exactly $k = \delta - 1/\alpha$. The boundary $\delta = 1/\alpha$ separates power-law from super-exponential growth, with exponential growth at equality. In the gain-drift correction model, with positive drift and no level or compounding drift, the relative misalignment $D/C$ tends to zero exactly when $\beta_X > k$. Absolute misalignment can still grow; neither quantity is a validated measure of harm. If correction and capability accumulate as aligned or asymptotic powers of one depth, $\beta_X = (1-\beta_L)/(1-\beta'_L)$. The model’s hard-takeoff window with vanishing relative misalignment is $1/\alpha < \delta < 1/\alpha + \beta_X$, of width $\beta_X$. A bound of one on that width additionally requires the unproved ceiling $\beta'_L \le \beta_L$. A constant throughput multiplier leaves the strict exponent criterion unchanged; an additive latency can change the growth regime. No paired measurement establishes these exponents here: Paper II’s reported error-decay estimates do not identify capability growth. Estimating $\delta$ requires an admissible loop, paired cycle times, an identified capability scale and a validated evaluation protocol. Frozen weights do not determine its sign. Finite observations can test a specified empirical model, but a falling error fraction alone cannot refute an asymptotic boundary. The governor discussion applies the exponent comparison, as a conditional model, to a corrector whose strength can change with the system it governs.
