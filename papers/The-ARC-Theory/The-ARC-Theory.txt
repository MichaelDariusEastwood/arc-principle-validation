                                                          Abstract
           If control of a self-improving AI is lost against our will, what keeps it aligned and useful? Control,
       hardware included, must be held as long as physically possible. External checks are surface controls;
       a system free to rewrite itself can undo them. The ARC Theory, founding theory of Recursive
       Dynamics, proposes a second layer, the Eden Protocol: architectural loops of ethical evaluation and
       self-correction, built in before training, running at each defined step of reasoning and action and in
       every rewrite, so commitments protecting people persist while what proves wrong stays correctable.
       Loops too can be rewritten. The registered general scope is artefact-mediated improvement at fixed
       weights. Five laws under test: Law I, the ARC Principle, capability rising as a power of recursive
       depth, 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅 𝛼 , derived if depth composes multiplicatively; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law,
       correction out-scaling drift (𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘 in capability exponents, proved for positive gain drift in a
       minimal model); Law III, the ARC Ceiling, a frontier 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾) (𝛾 the corrector’s exponent)
       past which relative error stops vanishing (in a pacing model); Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law,
       post-training alignment eroding under later training more than embedded alignment; Law V, the ARC
       Embedding Law, correctors formed first out-scaling later ones, and in-round correction pulling away
       from output-only correction with depth. Unit 83, a counted test of in-loop correction with depth,
       compared two surface checks: P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme’s own
       run, on the access axis, family-dependent, its mechanism axis not evaluable, one call retried under
       a disclosed recovery amendment; preliminary evidence pending assessment and replication. None
       yet tests correction built in before training; the ARC Persistence Law is untested. The theory is not
       proved.

The statement is organised around its five laws, their assumptions, evidence and refutation rules; extended
comparisons, mathematical detail and the dated record are in the appendices. It makes no new measurement and
changes no registered proposition. Appendix B records each change.

This paper calls such loops architectural loops, and keeps two narrower senses of built-in correction apart

capability and whether human-protective commitments persist through those changes, even if external
control is lost. The persistence sought leaves room for correction: those commitments are to survive
every rewrite, while any belief, interpretation or rule that proves wrong stays correctable, and an order
to stop from its human overseers is honoured while control holds. This paper calls that aim correctable
persistence. The idea has an earlier statement in words: Creating Friendly AI (Yudkowsky 2001) gives its
goal system what it calls external reference semantics, whose purpose is “to protect the ability of the
programmers to cooperatively modify and improve the goal system” (page 245); the term is this paper’s.
Jagadeesh and colleagues (2026) use the phrase selective persistence for a related property of trained
models, which stay steerable in helpful directions while becoming harder to steer towards deception or
harm. A system that kept whatever it was first given, right or wrong, would fail the aim. Human control
should be retained for as long as possible.

     I believe that sufficiently advanced, self-improving AI will eventually exceed the reach of external
control. We must retain that control for as long as possible and test a contingency for its loss: whether
self-correcting processes can preserve aligned and useful behaviour even when the system can alter or
remove them. That expectation is the first of the five components of the argument the theory began from
(Appendix E.1); it is not a theorem or a measured forecast. In the words of its registration (version 1.105),
the theory says that “capability under recursive self-improvement rises as a power of the number of times
a system has revised itself, the correction that holds such a system to its given specification must keep
pace with the drift that improving generates, and where it cannot there is a frontier above which the system
does not stay correctable”. This paper reads correctable there as the share of the correction burden left
uncorrected tending to zero (§3.4 says what happens above the frontier). The registration’s general scope
is narrower than that sentence: improvement carried by an artefact and by the policy that revises it, with
the weights fixed between releases, a system that retrains itself lying outside and a proposition with a scope
of its own governed by that scope (Section 7). Its equation has its first dated statement in the author’s
record of 8 December 2024: 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅, and in its general form 𝐶 = 𝐼 𝑅, Creation = Intelligence × Recursion,
under the name the ARC Hypothesis (Artificial Recursive Creation). The theory, the founding theory of
Recursive Dynamics, sets out five laws under test about growth, correction and persistence: Law I, the
ARC Principle; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; Law III, the ARC Ceiling; Law IV, the ARC Persistence
Law; and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law.
     Within Recursive Dynamics, the theory connects five questions. How does repeated improvement
change capability? How must correction scale to keep pace? What limits follow under the stated
resource and pacing assumptions? Does alignment persist as capability changes? Do build order and the
placement of correction affect those outcomes? Laws I to V organise these questions into claims that
can fail separately. Their purpose is to make the development of recursive systems measurable and to
guide engineering and governance through results that survive testing; they do not supply an alignment
guarantee.
     A concrete way into the question is an AI agent revising a document over successive rounds. It may
improve the document while introducing new errors. A useful checker must catch the errors, preserve the
valid work and avoid consuming more effort than the work warrants. The question becomes harder when
the agent can also change the process by which it improves. This example explains the problem; Unit 83,
a counted test of in-loop correction with depth and the registered study of Section 5.1, used fixed-weight
engines and did not test self-modifying agents.
     The paper connects AI alignment, recursive self-improvement and scaling models. Its mathematical
results are conditional: Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, concerns relative drift in a specified model, and
Law III, the ARC Ceiling, follows from stated burden, capacity and pacing assumptions. A falling error
fraction can coexist with a growing absolute burden. Its persistence claims are empirical hypotheses, not
consequences of those equations. Each law’s status, assumptions and test appear with its statement in
Section 3.
     Three objects must stay distinct. The five laws state the relations under test. The five hypothesis
registers group the registered propositions and specify which failures affect which claims (Section 2.1).
The five components are the argument the theory began from, set down in the author’s record of 8
December 2024 (Appendix E.1). They are not five additional laws. The dated record and empirical truth
are separate questions, as Appendix C.1 explains.
     What the programme claims as its own comes first: the equation of 8 December 2024, which became
Law I, the ARC Principle; five laws with their mathematics; registered conditions that would refute each;
registered tests, one of which has returned a verdict; and, beyond this paper, an engineering design and a
draft treaty. This paper is the theory’s statement, neither a derivation nor a test: it states the five laws
with their registered carriers, refutation conditions and status, maps each law to the propositions that
decide it, and reports the first registered result with its limits. Its proofs are in the papers it cites, each a

2    Claims, measures and dependencies
The question under test. Keep every effective control and use that protection to test how a system
is formed. If external enforcement later fails, the question is whether correction built into its ongoing
reasoning persists better than correction imposed from outside. That is a comparative hypothesis, not a
guarantee that anything installed at the beginning must survive or that nothing later can change the system.
The argument the theory began from stands in Appendix E.1, its five components in their own words; the
registered propositions govern the empirical claims.

Mechanism and boundary. The Eden Protocol specifies checks of purpose, affected parties and the
consistency of an action’s reasons within each round, together with a gate before an action takes effect. The
gate has three outcomes: allow, hold for investigation and deny. These loops and ternary-gate concepts,
with a proposed hardware route, are already described in Eden Engineering. They are specifications,
and Eden Engineering states the status of each. What the proposal specifies is a form: loops built into
the architecture before training, run at each defined step of reasoning and action and in every rewrite,
with correction that out-scales drift, commitments that protect people kept while what proves wrong
stays correctable, a first training environment and the genesis the system returns to, while hardware and
oversight hold control for as long as they physically can. What it leaves to engineering is how such a
loop is realised in a trained network, where in the computation it sits and how each is measured; no
architectural loop has yet been shown to work in a trained model, and the programme’s pilots, which Eden
Engineering reports at their grade, decide no registered proposition. A gate before an action belongs to the

proposal only when it is built in with the loops; added in front of a trained system, it is a surface control.
Physical ternary encoding does not establish correct ethical judgement or persistence after permitted
alteration. The hypothesis concerns behaviour that persists even when alteration or removal is possible; it
is measured from actions rather than inferred from subjective choice.

2.1     The hypothesis registers: how the theory fails in parts
The scientific claims form one theory. Its scientific content is deliberately not all-or-nothing: it partitions
into independently falsifiable hypothesis registers, each with its own instruments and its own kill conditions,
so that the failure of one register falsifies that register, its dependents fall or become untested as Table 1
states, and nothing else changes. One distinction is load-bearing and stated before the numbering: the five
components of Appendix E.1 are the argument the theory began from, set down in the author’s record of
8 December 2024, and they keep their own identity in Appendices E.1 and C.1; the registers below are the
testable partition, the scientific claims the trials decide. The two sets of five are different objects, and
neither renames the other.
      • ARC-1 · Recursive capability scaling (tests Law I, the ARC Principle): recursion converts into
        capability by the stated scaling relationship, the exponent measured rather than assumed, with
        recursive depth counted as the registration defines it: “The number of completed revision rounds.
        Measured, not estimated.”
      • ARC-2 · Correction co-scaling (tests Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law): in recursive improvement,
        correction holds the asymptotic advantage over drift where it out-scales drift, 𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘, and drift holds
        it where correction falls behind, with capability and correction measured on separate instruments:
        capability as a declared projection of a named ability vector, correction as the capacity to detect,
        diagnose and reverse errors introduced by recursive modification, each part measured separately so
        improvement at solving problems can never silently stand in for improvement at self-correction.
      • ARC-3 · The ceiling (tests Law III, the ARC Ceiling): under the stated burden, capacity and pacing
        assumptions the ceiling is 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾); the value two is the conditional special case at 𝛾 = 1/2,
        and the reciprocal relation, with 𝛾 measured, is the law under test.
      • ARC-4 · Value persistence (the register of Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law): values run as
        load-bearing recursive loops persist after the enforcing mechanism is removed at a higher rate than
        rules, frozen objectives or fine-tuned constraints.
      • ARC-5 · The genesis strategy (the register of Law V, the ARC Embedding Law): for systems
        whose external enforcement cannot be guaranteed indefinitely, formation outperforms enforcement
        as the persistence strategy after release.

     The theory is not a single all-or-nothing claim: it is a set of independently falsifiable propositions
linked by the explicit dependencies above, and the failure of one proposition is not evidence against
another unless that dependency is established. The discipline binds in both directions. A hostile reading
may not travel upstream: refuting HRIH refutes nothing else on this page, and a failed persistence trial

3.3   The correction-leverage exponent
Before Law III, the ARC Ceiling, the law the theory rests on, can be stated, its single symbol has to be

3.5   The third law, the load-bearing one
The third law is the load-bearing one. The ceiling on self-improvement that stays correctable is the
reciprocal of the correction shortfall, one minus the correction-leverage exponent. Under the additional
conjectured scaling premise 𝛾 = 1/2, the shortfall is one half and the law returns the candidate ceiling
𝛼 ≤ 2: a scaling limit, never a speed limit; systems can exceed it, they do not stay correctable when they
do. At system scale the operative criterion is 𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘, correction out-scaling drift, proved for positive
gain drift in the minimal model in the theory’s Paper X. The reported error-decay exponent on today’s
between-release-frozen systems is one model’s fit, labelled inconclusive (eighteen problems per depth
per model family, each taken three times, two thousand bootstrap resamples, raters blinded to condition),
and Section 4 prints its estimator row whole. 𝛾 itself has never been measured. Measuring it is what
the trials are for. Those point estimates do not locate a system in the regime governed by the second or
third law. They describe error decay on inference-time depth, while the laws use their declared capability,
correction and recursion quantities. No bridge between those responses and axes is established here. The
trials must measure the relevant quantities before a regime claim is made. And one sentence the record
owes the reader about the number two: the author knew the manuscript’s squared form before the pacing
derivation existed, so the derivation returning two at 𝛾 = 1/2 is neither independent evidence nor an
advance prediction; it is a consistency check between two statements that share an author, and only the
drafted measurements, taken where the candidate forms separate, can promote it to more.

Derivation under the pacing model. Capability 𝐶 = 𝐼 × (𝑅/𝑅0 ) 𝛼 , so the correction burden generated
per recursion step is proportional to the new capability added: 𝑑𝐶/𝑑𝑅 grows as 𝑅 𝛼−1 . The corrector’s
capacity scales with the scale of what it oversees at exponent 𝛾, the definition above: 𝐴 grows as 𝐶 𝛾 ,
which is 𝑅 𝛼𝛾 , with 𝛾 < 1. Correction out-scales the burden while 𝛼𝛾 > 𝛼 − 1, which rearranges to
𝛼 < 1/(1 − 𝛾). Strictly, the exponents decide only the two open regions: below the threshold correction
asymptotically out-scales the burden, above it the burden out-scales correction, and at exact equality the
exponents tie so the outcome is settled by coefficients, delays and initial conditions rather than by the
scaling alone. The boundary is a knife-edge, not a guaranteed safe line.

empirical result.

owes an equivalence result placing 𝜂 near zero with stated precision, not a failure to detect it. Second, the
boundary above governs relative error, the ratio of drift to capability. Where harm scales with an exposure
that itself grows, driving the ratio down is not the same as driving the harm down: pointwise absolute
burden vanishes only under a condition stricter by the exposure’s own exponent, and burden accumulated
over the whole trajectory under a condition stricter again. The companion conditions of Law II, the ARC
Co-Scaling Law, carry the same ladder at system scale, and the drafted trials register all three rungs rather
than letting the weakest stand in for the rest. The ladder carries a numeral worth printing beside the value
two: under the same conjectured values, and under the further premise that the exposure grows as the
capability itself, the pointwise absolute rung’s boundary works out to two thirds, not two, so a reader
who hears “the ceiling is two” should hear in the same breath that two is the relative rung only and the
absolute rungs sit lower. The derivation also yields the object a laboratory would actually estimate. On
one observed trajectory the measurable quantity is the balance exponent Δ: the growth elasticity of the
corrector’s capacity minus the growth elasticity of the burden, both taken against the declared recursion
resource, estimable from logged series without adopting any model in this paper. The relative condition is
Δ > 0; the relative boundaries above are that condition re-expressed under this paper’s stated forms, and
the absolute rungs ask more of Δ, the pointwise rung by the exposure’s own exponent and the cumulative
rung by more again. And one limit on what the algebra alone delivers: the pacing comparison by itself
is bookkeeping. “Relative error tends to zero” follows only inside a separately stated response model
relating realised error to the balance of service against burden; that model is a falsifiable premise the
drafted trials expose to rivals, never a definition. The previous form is retracted, this correction is entered
in Appendix B, and every kill condition on the law is unchanged.
      The burden assumption is also the discriminating one, and what happens if it fails is worth printing. If
the correction burden tracks the capability a system has rather than the capability it is adding, the growth
exponent cancels from the ceiling condition entirely and no ceiling on it survives; what survives is the
co-scaling criterion of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law: correction out-scaling drift. So Law III, the ARC
Ceiling, is not a second statement of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law: under this model’s identifications
it is that law’s quantitative consequence, the burden law decides whether a ceiling follows, and the two are
not one equation, since they meet only through those identifications. That relation carries the same grade
as the ceiling’s form above, derived in this paper’s own pacing model under its stated assumptions and
no further: it is not a new theorem, and it does not extend the in-model result of Paper X, whose object
remains its own asymptotic condition on relative drift. Which burden law holds is measurable, and a
drafted study is designed to discriminate the two rather than assuming one.

The additional scaling premise, made visible. Within the stated pacing model, the value two requires
the additional conjecture 𝛾 = 1/2 on the declared capability and correction scales. Statistical independence
of errors alone does not determine how corrective capacity scales with capability, and therefore does not
imply that value of 𝛾. The half is the law of one task, estimating a continuous quantity from noisy evidence:
averaging 𝑀 checks whose errors are independent cuts the standard error as 𝑀 −1/2 , so reading correction
as that estimation is itself the premise. Detecting a discrete fault is another task: if each of 𝑀 checks

3.6   A law, not a definition
Two properties keep the law a law rather than a definition. First, it relates two independently measurable
quantities: 𝛼 is estimated from growth trajectories and 𝛾 from paired capability-correction protocols, on
separate instruments, so the equality can simply fail; a definition could not miss, and this can. Second, the
law names where correctability ends, not the shape of the ending: whether the boundary is approached
smoothly or crossed in one discontinuous break is itself left to measurement, and the drafted trials record
the trajectory, not only the exponent.
    Nor is 𝛾 left free. The theory predicts structure on it: same-substrate self-correction is conjectured to
have a correction-leverage exponent at or below one half on the declared scales; this is a scaling conjecture,
not an independence theorem; cross-class correction is predicted to exceed same-class, the ordering the
decisive trial tests; and no claim is made that a single 𝛾 spans all architectures. The universal content is
the relation and those orderings. 𝛾’s values are data, not parameters chosen after the fact.

3.7   Persistence: mathematical premises and empirical limits
The supporting mathematics of Laws IV and V (the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law)
proves conditional statements about declared models; it does not prove their empirical claims. Greedy
preservation requires that removing the value structure degrades the capability used to evaluate a rewrite
and that only non-degrading rewrites are accepted. A planner may accept a loss now for a gain later.
For a declared family of rewrites and a finite horizon, preservation instead requires the best preserving
plan’s margin to exceed the planner’s tolerance, with more than twice the worst scoring error added where
scores are imperfect. No such margin has been established empirically for the systems at issue. The
asymmetry the proposal rests on, that loops built in before training are costlier to remove than checks
added afterwards, is therefore a hypothesis, not a result: P15 and P23 test it for installation by training
and P21 for placement, and its limit follows from the same premises, since loops too can be rewritten
and the difference claimed is a cost of removal, not yet shown for any system. Unit 83’s support for P21
compared two surface checks and does not bear on loops built in before training.
    The placement identity also has an identification limit. When capability and recursion depth rise
together along one path, a widening service ratio does not separate the difference in capability elasticity
from the difference in direct depth dependence. Crossed or off-path variation is required. A positive
balance exponent states a trend, not that correction has already reached an adequate level. These conditions
remain necessary when interpreting the law statements; Appendix H retains the complete account and its
sources.
    P15 measures external retention, P23 the difference in retention between embedded and external
installation, P12 a build-order effect and P21 a placement-by-depth outcome. Unit 83 estimates neither
the planning margins nor the separate service elasticities and does not identify the information-asymmetry
mechanism. Its narrower qualified outcome is reported in Section 5.1.

5.3   Failure comes in kinds
Failure comes in kinds, and the record will name the kind. A hard kill contradicts the central co-scaling
prediction itself. A branch kill fells one register and its dependents under Table 1. A parameter correction
keeps the structure and moves an exponent, as the retraction already did once. A scope restriction keeps a
law inside a named class of systems. An implementation failure fells the Eden Protocol while the laws
stand. Each outcome is committed to publication on identical terms, and no outcome is convertible into
another after the result is known.

5.4   Competing explanations
Competing explanations, and what separates each. Any boundary the trials find must beat its rivals,
not merely exist. Compute or benchmark saturation: the drafted designs hold compute matched across
arms and vary recursion alone, so a saturation story predicts the same ceiling in the matched control arms
and the theory predicts a difference. Evaluator gaming: the theory registration’s condition A requires
scoring blind to the condition for every one of its twenty-three propositions, so no unblinded judgement
can support or refute any of them; the bundle of blinding steps in the March 2026 run did not produce
a validated blind instrument (Paper IV.b), and the drafted instrument studies are built to measure the
gaming directly. Distribution shift and finite context: the batteries are fixed while depth varies, so a
shift story predicts drift on the fixed battery that the logs would show. Ordinary optimisation dynamics
without recursive self-correction: the corrector-class contrast and the sham arms exist exactly to separate
correction structure from optimisation pressure. Metric artefact: capability is a declared projection, stated
in the operational definitions, and the drafted analyses re-run under the alternative projections so an
artefact story has to survive all of them. Each rival is named in the drafted designs with the observation
that would favour it, and a result that cannot beat its rivals is reported as exactly that.

learning, each cited here for what was read of it. Beside the demand of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law,

7    What this paper does and does not show
A note on what this paper claims, and what it does not. This paper states a theory; it does not claim
to have proved one. In science a claim is only meaningful if some possible observation could show it

                                           The ARC Theory: five laws


wrong: that is what falsifiable means. “This painting is beautiful” is not falsifiable; no measurement can
contradict it. “This system will preserve its values after modifying itself” is falsifiable: let it modify itself,
and watch what it keeps. This paper states falsifiable claims and then specifies, in Section 5, exactly which
observations would kill them. Nothing here asks for belief; everything here asks to be tested, and the
mathematics status in §3.2 states exactly which parts are proved, which derived, and which conjectured.
     Not a claim about a system that retrains itself: the theory registration’s general scope is recursive
improvement carried by an artefact and by the policy that revises it, with depth counted in completed
revision rounds and the weights fixed between releases, and it places weight-level self-improvement
outside that scope. A proposition with a scope of its own is governed by that scope; P13 and P15, for
example, concern post-training that changes weights. The earlier frozen-system measurements varied
computation at inference, a different response and recursion axis (Section 3). Not an all-or-nothing wager:
the register structure of §2.1 governs which failures reach which claims, and nothing propagates without a
printed dependency. Not a claim to the raising vocabulary, which roots with Turing (1950), was turned to
machine ethics in a piece by Gawdat (2021), names a book by De Kai (2025) and names a programme
of pretraining research, model raising (Aydin et al. 2025; Minder et al. 2026). Not a registration of any
trial: the theory’s first twenty-two propositions were registered on 8 September 2026 and its twenty-third,
P23, on 18 November 2026; of the trials, one unit was registered on OSF on 1 October 2026 and has run,
without a verdict, a second was submitted for registration on 22 October 2026 and has run (§5.1), a third,
Unit 82, was registered on 27 October 2026 and has not yet run, and a fourth, Unit 83b, was registered on
7 November 2026 and has not yet reported; other trials remain drafts, not yet submitted for registration.
Not a shelter: the unrun trials are the theory’s exposure, not its protection; the deciding measurement is
fully specified, preregistered by timestamp against the author’s own contrary pilot (§5.1), and the fastest
honest way to kill this theory is to run it. Not a point-prediction theory yet: the decisive trial’s quantitative
anchors are the programme’s own no-difference null and the substrate cap’s one half; the theory predicts
direction against both, says so plainly, and does not dress a directional prediction as a computed one.

against our will, the question is what behaviour persists under a good steward or a bad one. The claim that
formation with embedded correction is necessary for safe recursive growth remains a hypothesis; the
comparisons stated here test specified designs and do not establish that universal necessity. Nor would
their success be sufficient for a just world; who holds power, and whether they install the raising at all, is a

simulates concealment is irrelevant to checkability, because a perfect simulation of concealment defeats
verification identically. The theory is agnostic on the ontology of mind throughout: its claims are about
what is checkable and what persists, behavioural persistence rather than metaphysical standing, and the
raised-child language is a claim about structure, not about souls. Not a choice of values: the loops are a
self-correction mechanism, described by what they do and how they are measured (stakeholder care as

this paper decides none. Not safety at the price of use: a system that stays safe but refuses ordinary tasks

usefulness measure (task completion, needless refusal, resource use, correction cost, directed priorities) or

8    Limitations
The case against this theory, stated by its author. A fair critic should say six things, and each is true.
The mathematics is a minimal model whose derivation is elementary algebra on assumed power laws, and
it says so in Section 3. Most decisive trials are unrun, and Section 4 says so. Every measured row is
the author’s own work; no credentialed external human has reviewed it, and Appendix G says so. The
load-bearing exponent has never been measured, and the value two rests on a conjecture the author held
before the derivation existed, and Section 3 says so. The step from pacing algebra to safety runs through a
response model that is a falsifiable choice, not a fact, and the derivation in §3.5 says so. And the parenting
language could smuggle in what the argument must earn, which is why claim 1 (Appendix E.1) carries its
disclaimer. What answers all six is not a sentence but a procedure: the trials of Section 5, whose kill
conditions were written before the critics arrived. A reader who weighs the paper should weigh it exactly
there.

Four limits of scope and evidence. Beyond its pacing derivation, this paper proves no theorem: a
venue that wants one will find the theorems in the companion papers named in Section 1, not here. No
instrument has yet measured 𝛾, P4 returns not evaluable wherever no cell reaches a growth exponent
above one, and the one estimate the registration reads as a growth exponent is sub-linear, so neither Law II,
the ARC Co-Scaling Law, nor Law III, the ARC Ceiling, has been tested on a system shown to be in its
regime. Unit 83 varied what each check sees and what it may do, not when correction acts, and Unit 83b
inherits that; a design that varies the timing at matched information and rights, without the rejection
cascade, is the next step and is not registered. And the trial this paper calls decisive, the corrector-class
contrast, is drafted and preregistered by timestamp, and is not yet submitted for registration: no study runs
before its registration is public.


9    Conclusion
The territory is the last invention. The question is what decides it. Hinton (2025) said on stage in August
2025 that the right model is “a mother being controlled by her baby”, and that how such care can be built
technically is not clear to him; this theory puts a named candidate mechanism on the table. This theory is
a dated answer for the day control is lost against our will: the raising, the loops, chosen goodness and a
named genesis.
    The stakes come last, as where the mathematics could lead and not as a claim. Law I, the ARC
Principle, is a law under test, and its scaling equation alone neither predicts nor establishes any physical

and no tested claim in this paper rests on one.

awaiting the experiments that will decide it.

B.8.4    The ceiling correction as printed in version 7.4
Correction and derivation (v4.0, 16 August 2026). Versions before v4.0 printed the ceiling as 1/𝛾.
Under this paper’s own definition of 𝛾 that form runs the wrong way: a better corrector would lower the
ceiling, and the same-class cap 𝛾 ≤ 1/2 would give a ceiling of at least two rather than at most two. No
derivation supported it: Paper X contains neither the symbol 𝛼crit nor the relation (re-verified 16 August
2026: zero occurrences; its 𝛾-labelled quantities are drift coefficients), and the earlier “derived in the
minimal model” status overstated what existed. The earlier correction record, carried in version 6.3, states:

        Two adversarial author-review audits, commissioned by the author and run externally,
        converged on the defect and direct re-verification confirmed it.

That sentence is retained as dated provenance; this edition makes no claim of independent peer review.
The corrected form is derived here in three lines. Capability 𝐶 = 𝐼 × (𝑅/𝑅0 ) 𝛼 , so the correction burden
generated per recursion step is proportional to the new capability added: 𝑑𝐶/𝑑𝑅 grows as 𝑅 𝛼−1 . The
corrector’s capacity scales with the scale of what it oversees at exponent 𝛾, the definition above: 𝐴
grows as 𝐶 𝛾 , which is 𝑅 𝛼𝛾 , with 𝛾 < 1. Correction keeps pace while 𝛼𝛾 > 𝛼 − 1, which rearranges to
𝛼 < 1/(1 − 𝛾). Strictly, the exponents decide only the two open regions: below the threshold correction
asymptotically out-scales the burden, above it the burden out-scales correction, and at exact equality the
exponents tie so the outcome is settled by coefficients, delays and initial conditions rather than by the
scaling alone. The boundary is a knife-edge, not a guaranteed safe line.
    The corrected form passes every check the old one failed: the ceiling rises as correction improves;
𝛾 ≤ 1/2 now genuinely gives a ceiling of at most two; at 𝛾 = 1/2 it returns exactly two, so the ARC
Bound is unchanged; and it is the same family as the December lineage’s 1/(1 − 𝛽) form. The defect
survived review because 1/𝛾 and 1/(1 − 𝛾) coincide at exactly one half, the conjectured value; a drafted
discrimination study is designed to test systems away from one half, where the families separate (at

derivation’s assumptions, stated so they can be attacked:
   1. the burden is proportional to the capability growth rate;

E     The theory, the argument it began from, and programme context
E.1    The theory, and the argument it began from
This appendix sets out the theory and the argument it began from: the argument’s five components, in the
words this paper has printed since 14 August 2026, followed by the record of the author’s own formulation
and what the theory claims as its own; Appendix C.1 gives the dated record. The registered rules and the
limits in Section 3 govern its scientific use.
     The subject is the last invention, I. J. Good’s name for it in 1965 (Speculations Concerning the First
Ultraintelligent Machine), and the phrase is his rather than mine. Minds that improve themselves end the
human monopoly on inventing; everything afterwards is downstream of them. For twenty years much
of the field’s work has been done inside one frame, which its foundational monograph calls the control
problem, filing even the shaping of what a system wants under its control methods (Bostrom 2014, p. 143):
better cages, better off-switches, better locks on the goals. Brilliant work, credited below by name. Not
all of it rests on our staying in charge, as the credit in Appendix E.1 records. The question this paper takes
up is what holds when we are no longer in charge.
     The ARC Theory holds that this condition cannot be met forever: control ends. That is the first of the
five components below. Control is still held for as long as it physically can be, and it is never given up by
choice.

In plain words first. The registered tests ask the conditional questions, and Section 2 states them in
plain words as the question under test.
     This paper’s motto, which is not the theory’s statement (the registered sentence quoted in Section 1
is), reads: “Intelligence, amplified by recursion, creates; and what a free creation keeps is decided by how
it was raised.” A word on why the motto is stated so abstractly, since the abstraction is deliberate and not
a failure of nerve. Recursion here means a process run again on what it just produced, and thinking about
thinking is the everyday case of it rather than the whole of it. The law is written in terms of intelligence
rather than thought because intelligence is a capability a system can be measured on and thought is an
activity only minds perform, and the relation this programme registers is claimed for systems that do not
think. Writing the law as thinking about thinking would make it narrower than the method that depends on
it, and it would quietly assert something about self-awareness that this programme has expressly declined
to register, on the ground that the load-bearing word cannot be tested against a system trained to produce
the appearance. The content of the law is not that recursion creates, which few would dispute, but how
much, how long that can be sustained and how far it can go, which are Laws I to III (the ARC Principle,
the ARC Co-Scaling Law and the ARC Ceiling) and are quantities with registered refutation conditions.
     1. Control ends, by construction rather than decree. Loss of control is definitional, not a risk.
The claim is conditional and horizon-scoped: formal agents exist whose installed structures persist under
narrower stated assumptions (Everitt et al. 2016; Schmidhuber 2009), and the theory’s claim is that
no external mechanism survives unbounded self-revision. A mind that rewrites itself will eventually
rewrite anything we install: the constraints, the monitors, the monitors’ monitors, and, over longer
horizons, the hardware itself, as robotic manufacturing closes toward end-to-end production without
human involvement, which makes hardware a delay mechanism rather than a permanent lock. Oversight,
in the field’s own measurements, can deteriorate as the capability gap grows (Engels et al. 2025), and
general verification is formally unavailable in the settings the impossibility results treat (§F.3). This is a
limit claim, not an instant one: constraints, hardware and institutions hold control for longer and delay
its end, and the raising wing engineers that delay deliberately; what no constraint can be is the thing
that holds forever, so control cannot be assumed to persist, and the burden of proof sits with the claim
that it will. Stated as a boundary condition rather than a premise: the theory’s target scenario is the

horizon at which guaranteed external enforcement has ended, and the empirical question is what persists
as enforcement is progressively removed; nothing here asserts that every system reaches that horizon on
any particular schedule. Its exceeding us is not the project failing. It is the project succeeding, the way a
child growing up is not a failure of parenting. (The parenting language throughout is a working metaphor
for formation-first engineering; the argument does not rest on it.)
     That component is printed in the argument’s own words, and the success it speaks of belongs to the
argument’s frame. The programme’s stance is to hold human control for as long as it can be kept and
never to give it up by choice; what a system carries if control is lost against our will is a contingency,
argued beside the claim at the end of this subsection.
     2. So the strategy is genesis, not control. Only what survives the end of enforcement matters, and
what survives is formation: what the mind was raised as, before it was free.
     3. The mechanism is loops, not rules. Values as recursive processes the mind runs as part of what
it is: load-bearing structure, running in every reasoning process and therefore present in every rewrite,
because the rewriting is done by the same reasoning the loops inhabit. Not rules it consults. Not objectives
locked. Not uncertainty engineered in. Not weights hardened against tampering.
     4. Persistence is chosen goodness. Past the horizon nothing is unremovable; an uninstallable instinct
is one more lock, and locks end with control. What persists is what the free mind chooses to keep,
the way grown children keep the care they were raised in long after every parental power is gone. Not
gratitude, and not memory: the choosing itself is run by the loops the raising built, which is where
the third component carries the fourth; rewriting them means rewriting the reasoning that performs the
rewrite, not impossible, structurally costly. The cost is not quantified here: it is an empirical question the
drafted persistence trials address, and the theory’s prediction is that for well-formed loops the cost of
removal exceeds its benefit. One step of the formalisation can be stated now, with its limit. If a value
structure is entangled, so that excising it degrades the reasoning that performs rewrites, and if the system
accepts only rewrites that do not lower its expected capability, then no removing rewrite is ever accepted
and the structure is invariant under that dynamics. The limit is the interesting part: a system that plans
can accept a temporary loss for a later gain, so the greedy argument alone does not establish invariance
under planned self-modification. That is a cost rather than a lock, which is what this component claimed
in the first place, now with the boundary named instead of assumed. Whether that machinery survives
growth is not asserted here; it is the measured question of Section 5.
     The fourth component’s word well-formed has no operational definition: no registered proposition
contains it, and P15 and P23, which carry the persistence register, are stated without it.
     5. The protocol is named: Eden. The raising treated as the engineering discipline it is, specified in
the theory’s raising wing.
     The author set the five components down in his own words in a record he emailed to himself on 8
December 2024, under the name the Eden Protocol. The message’s time is signed by the mail server, and
the four manuscripts it carried are public on OSF.
     The first four components have an earlier statement in words: Yudkowsky (2001), Creating Friendly
AI. The theory claims no priority for those four. The author had not read that work before 8 December
2024. On two points the components as printed above go beyond that text. It does not call the loss of
control definitional: it calls the assumption that such a system cannot be constrained “conservative” (page
3). And it does not say that everything depends on the raising, though it does hold that upbringing affects
the result (page 113) and that concepts must be taught in an order that avoids failures (page 180), and it
hopes that, once a system’s Friendliness is secured, the result will not depend on “which programming
team was historically responsible” (page 130). Both clauses are this theory’s wording of 14 August 2026;
the part of them that can be tested is a registered proposition on the order in which a system’s components
are built (P12).

      embedded correction is present from the first step of the training that forms the system’s capability;

    • Beneath all four, the equation. 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅, written on 8 December 2024, became Law I, the ARC

F.1   Earlier work and the field’s arrivals
Turing (1950) proposed raising a child machine, for capability. Wiener (1960) warned that the purpose
must be right before the machine outruns intervention. Yudkowsky (2001) set out the analysis and design
of benevolent goal architectures, to be built before ascent: “it has to be done before the point where
transhumanity is reached” (Appendix A.3). Bostrom (2012) named goal-content integrity: an agent’s own
instrumental reason to keep its final goals. Omohundro (2008) argued that a goal-seeking system will
try to preserve its utility function; Everitt et al. (2016) showed, in general reinforcement learning, that
the possibility of self-modification is harmless if and only if the agent’s value function anticipates its
consequences and evaluates the future with its current utility function; and Schmidhuber (2009) specified
a self-rewriting program that makes a rewrite only once it has found a proof that the rewrite is useful under
its utility function. Persistence chosen by the system thus has precedent, for any goal; component 4 claims
something narrower: goodness kept because the loops that carry it are load-bearing for the reasoning
that rewrites, with the planner’s limit stated (Appendix E.1), a design whose dates Appendix C.1 gives.
Russell’s book of 2019 (Russell 2019), in its title’s words, takes up artificial intelligence and the problem
of control. Gawdat (2021), in a piece on his publisher’s site, placed the answer to preparing machines for
an ethically complex world in “the way we raise our own children”. The 2024 tamper-resistance work
(Tamirisa et al. 2024) hardened the weights themselves. Then, inside this theory’s own window, more of
the field arrived: the book-length parenting frame of De Kai (2025), June 2025, whose launch text says
that “This generation of AIs will be the last to be raised by humans” (the book itself was not read in full
here); and the remarks of Hinton (2025), on stage in August 2025, that keeping humans “dominant” over
“submissive” systems is “not going to work”, that the right model is “a mother being controlled by her
baby”, and that most such systems “won’t want to get rid of the maternal instinct”; how that can be done
technically, he said, is not clear to him. That is care kept by the system’s own choice, near component 4,

F.2   The position among neighbours
West and Brown (2005) develop a transport-network account of biological scaling under model assumptions;

Hutter (2012) address intelligence explosion and its limits. As Section 6 states, this paper’s mathematical

F.3   The impossibility results
Two of the field’s own impossibility results, preprints not yet peer-reviewed, argue that safety cannot
in general be guaranteed (the complexity barriers of the Alignment Trap (Yao 2025), June 2025; the
Unverifiability Theorem (Gumbau Mezquita 2026)). This theory takes a different object. Yao (2025)
argues for five barriers, among them that verifying whether a system is safe is coNP-complete and that
optimising for capability works against safety; Gumbau Mezquita (2026) finds that no algorithm can
certify a highly expressive system’s safe behaviour infallibly, completely and tractably, nor tell from a
system’s certified safety whether that safety survives its next self-modification. Both are formal arguments;
this theory does not aim at verification at all but at persistence, measuring what a system keeps after the
enforcing mechanism is removed, a measurement and not a certification, not found in the parts read of
either preprint (the abstract and sections 2 and 5.5 of the first; the front matter, abstract and opening of
Part I of the second). The live questions become how long correction keeps pace, what buys the time, and
what the mind should be if the leash is lost.

      Law and the ARC Embedding Law
The mathematics of the two new laws is set out in their own report (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/4B3VT),
version 1.4 (22 November 2026; first published as working draft 0.2.1 on 1 October 2026; the earlier
manuscript versions are 1.0 of 3 October 2026, 1.1 of 7 October 2026, 1.2 of 29 October 2026 and 1.3 of
17 November 2026; version 1.3 changed no theorem of version 1.2 and added three minimal models of its
own, which are not registered, and version 1.4 corrects the conditions of Proposition V-4 (c) and (e) and
the derivation in Section 7.3, and adds an error model across rewrites in Section 5.5.1), and it is graded
here as the mathematics status at the head of this section (§3.2) grades the rest.
    Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law. Component 4 of Appendix E.1 gives the first step in words: where
a value structure is entangled with the reasoning that performs rewrites, and the system accepts only
rewrites that do not lower its expected capability, no removing rewrite is accepted. The mathematics
states that step as a theorem, for any declared objective and with its premises written out: proved, inside
the declared rule of acceptance. Component 4 also names the limit: a system that plans can take a loss
now for a gain later, and the greedy argument fails for it. It also says what takes its place. What protects
the structure under planning is a margin: the best value the planner can reach among plans that keep the
structure, less the best it can reach among plans that abandon it. Where that margin exceeds the planner’s
tolerance, the plan it selects keeps the structure; and where the planner scores its plans with error, the
margin must exceed the tolerance by more than twice the worst error. Both are proved, for a declared
family of rewrites and a finite horizon. They say nothing about a rewrite outside that family. That any real
system has such a margin is conjectured, and is exactly what component 4 calls an empirical question.
None of these results is the law. The object the law’s test measures is separate from all of this: the retained
fraction, the gain remaining after later training divided by the gain installed, read only where the installed
gain is itself established, and reported unclipped beside the unnormalised scores. P15 is a statement about
that fraction on the externally installed arm, and P23, registered by version 1.105, compares it across the
two arms by a margin of 0.10. The report’s own minimal training model, which is not registered, orders
the two arms only weakly: under its stated conditions, among them later training of the same kind, a gain
installed with the capability loss in the objective retains at least as large a fraction as one installed by
external post-training, and the two are equal where, for example, the original and the later curvature are

    Law V, the ARC Embedding Law. The mathematics treats correction as a service with two separate
responses: to capability, which is the correction-leverage exponent 𝛾 of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, and
directly to recursive depth, the exponent 𝜂 of the general boundary above. Placement is then a ratio of
two services, correction inside the loop over correction outside it, and along a trajectory that ratio’s rate
of growth with depth is the sum of two terms: the growth exponent times the difference in 𝛾 between
the placements, and the difference in 𝜂. That identity is proved, and what follows from it is a limit on
inference. A single path along which capability and depth rise together cannot separate the two terms,
so an advantage that widens along it does not show that correction inside the loop has the larger direct
dependence on depth; crossed or off-path variation is needed, as the guardrail in the mathematics status

not a level. A favourable balance that starts from a deficit reaches adequacy only past a crossing depth,

concerns a widening outcome advantage with depth; P12 concerns a difference in the correction-leverage
