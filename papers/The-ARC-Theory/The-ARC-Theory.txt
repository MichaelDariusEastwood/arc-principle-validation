                                                       Abstract
           Humanity is building systems that may improve themselves. If control is lost against our will,
       what could preserve useful and aligned behaviour? Control should be retained for as long as possible;
       embedded correction is a contingency, never a reason to relinquish it. This paper states the ARC
       Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, as five laws under test. Law I, the ARC Principle,
       𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅 𝛼 , is a heuristic form; earlier error-decay fits do not establish its capability exponent. Law II,
       the ARC Co-Scaling Law, 𝛽 > 𝑘, is proved for positive gain drift in a minimal model. Law III, the
       ARC Ceiling, 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾), follows from a stated pacing model; the ARC Bound of at most two
       additionally requires the conjectured same-class cap on 𝛾 and the declared scales. Laws IV and V,
       the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law, address value persistence, build order and
       correction placement. Laws I to III were registered on 8 September 2026; Laws IV and V were added
       on 24 September and registered as laws on 15 October in the theory registration, now at version 1.104.
       Unit 83 ran on 27 and 28 October: P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme’s
       own run, on the access axis, family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not evaluable and one call
       retried under a disclosed recovery amendment. That comparison couples information access and
       repair rights; it does not establish their separate effects or support Law V as a whole. Unit 06 was
       administratively incomplete with no verdict; Unit 82 is registered and unrun. The paper separates
       mathematical deductions, precursor evidence and prospective tests, and preserves the dated record of
       the conjunction stated in the self-emailed manuscript of 8 December 2024. The theory is not proved.

1    Introduction
I believe that sufficiently advanced, self-improving AI will eventually exceed the reach of external
control. We must retain that control for as long as possible and test a contingency for its loss: whether
self-correcting processes can preserve aligned and useful behaviour even when the system can alter or
remove them. That expectation motivates the ARC Theory; it is not a theorem or a measured forecast. The
theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, sets out five laws under test about growth, correction
and persistence.
    Within Recursive Dynamics, the theory connects five questions. How does repeated improvement
change capability? How must correction scale to keep pace? What limits follow under the stated
resource and pacing assumptions? Does alignment persist as capability changes? Do build order and the
placement of correction affect those outcomes? Laws I to V organise these questions into claims that
can fail separately. Their purpose is to make the development of recursive systems measurable and to
guide engineering and governance through results that survive testing; they do not supply an alignment
guarantee.
    A concrete way into the question is an AI agent revising a document over successive rounds. It may
improve the document while introducing new errors. A useful checker must catch the errors, preserve the
valid work and avoid consuming more effort than the work warrants. The question becomes harder when
the agent can also change the process by which it improves. This example explains the problem; it does
not claim that the current pilot tested self-modifying agents.
    The paper connects AI alignment, recursive self-improvement and scaling models. Its mathematical
results are conditional: Law II concerns relative drift in a specified model, and Law III follows from stated
burden, capacity and pacing assumptions. A falling error fraction can coexist with a growing absolute
burden. Its persistence claims are empirical hypotheses, not consequences of those equations. Each law’s
status, assumptions and test appear with its statement in Section 3.
    Three objects must stay distinct. The five laws state the relations under test. The five hypothesis
registers group the registered propositions and specify which failures affect which claims (Section 2.1).
The five components of the historical conjunction record what the author’s earlier document held together
(Appendix E.1); they are not five additional laws. Dated provenance, priority against earlier work and
empirical truth are separate questions, as Appendix C.1 explains.
    Our contributions are as follows.
    1. A statement of all five laws with their operational terms, registered carriers, dependencies and
       verdict rules. Each remains a law under test.
    2. The conditional pacing derivation and its limits: the strict regions and equality case, additional
       resource dependence, measurement-scale requirements, and the distinction between relative,
       absolute and cumulative burden.
    3. A separation of mathematical deductions, precursor measurements and registered outcomes. Unit 83
       supplies qualified support for P21 under its registered comparison, not for its information-asymmetry
       mechanism or Law V as a whole.
    4. Explicit refutation conditions and competing explanations, together with the full source, comparison
       and dated correction records.
    Section 2 defines the scientific partition. Section 3 states the laws and their conditions. Sections 4
and 5 give the evidence and the tests. Section 6 states the relationship to prior work; Sections 7 and 8
retain the scope limits and the strongest objections. Appendix A contains the source dossiers, Appendix B
the correction history, Appendix C the priority record, and Appendix D the notation. Appendices E to H
cover context, comparisons, verification and mathematics.

2     Claims, measures and dependencies
The question under test. Keep every effective control and use that protection to test how a system
is formed. If external enforcement later fails, the question is whether correction built into its ongoing
reasoning persists better than correction imposed from outside. That is a comparative hypothesis, not a
guarantee that anything installed at the beginning must survive or that nothing later can change the system.
The historical conjunction and its original wording remain in the dated record; the registered propositions
govern the empirical claims.

Mechanism and boundary. The Eden Protocol specifies checks of purpose, affected parties and the
consistency of an action’s reasons within each round, together with a gate before an action takes effect. The
gate has three outcomes: allow, hold for investigation and deny. These loops and ternary-gate concepts,
with a proposed hardware route, are already described in Eden Engineering. They are specifications at
their stated status. Physical ternary encoding does not establish correct ethical judgement or persistence
after permitted alteration. The hypothesis concerns behaviour that persists even when alteration or removal
is possible; it is measured from actions rather than inferred from subjective choice.

2.1     The hypothesis registers: how the theory fails in parts
The scientific claims form one theory. Its scientific content is deliberately not all-or-nothing: it partitions
into independently falsifiable hypothesis registers, each with its own instruments and its own kill conditions,
so that the failure of one register falsifies that register and whatever explicitly depends on it, and nothing
else. One distinction is load-bearing and stated before the numbering: the five components of Appendix E.1
are the priority conjunction, the historical claim about what the December document held together, and
they keep their own identity in Appendix C.1 and Figure 2; the registers below are the testable partition,
the scientific claims the trials decide. The two sets of five are different objects, and neither renames the
other.
      • ARC-1 · Recursive capability scaling (tests Law I, the ARC Principle): recursion converts into
        capability by the stated scaling relationship, the exponent measured rather than assumed, with
        recursive depth counted in materially produced generations, never in tokens, training steps or
        benchmark attempts.
      • ARC-2 · Correction co-scaling (tests Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law): in recursive improvement,
        correction holds the asymptotic advantage over drift where it out-scales drift, 𝛽 > 𝑘, and drift holds
        it where correction falls behind, with capability and correction measured on separate instruments:
        capability as a declared projection of a named ability vector, correction as the capacity to detect,
        diagnose and reverse errors introduced by recursive modification, each part measured separately so
        improvement at solving problems can never silently stand in for improvement at self-correction.
      • ARC-3 · The ceiling (tests Law III, the ARC Ceiling): under the stated burden, capacity and pacing
        assumptions the stability boundary is 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾); the value two is the conditional special
        case at 𝛾 = 1/2, and the reciprocal relation, with 𝛾 measured, is the law under test.
      • ARC-4 · Value persistence (the register of Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law): values run as
        load-bearing recursive loops persist after the enforcing mechanism is removed at a higher rate than
        rules, frozen objectives or fine-tuned constraints.
      • ARC-5 · The genesis strategy (the register of Law V, the ARC Embedding Law): for systems
        whose external enforcement cannot be guaranteed indefinitely, formation outperforms enforcement
        as the persistence strategy after release.

What this theory governs, and what it does not. Its domain has two tiers. The core is stated for

3     The five laws
3.1   Operational terms and measurement axes
Operationally, in the papers that measure them: 𝐼 is base capability, a system’s score on a defined
benchmark suite at the base depth of a single pass (𝑅 equal to one, no added recursion), so the equation
returns 𝑈 equal to 𝐼 at its own baseline; 𝑅 is recursion, quantified as inference-time reasoning depth
(Papers II and IV.b) or self-modification cycles in simulation (Paper VI); 𝑈 is the capability output after
recursion, on the same scale as 𝐼. The identity is testable wherever 𝑈 can be measured as 𝑅 varies, and no
universal definition of intelligence is required: only per-experiment measurability.

The recursion and response axes. The registration counts ARC-1 recursion in materially produced
generations. The precursor papers vary inference-time depth or self-modification cycles in simulation.
Reparametrising an axis changes its exponent, and an error-decay exponent is not the capability exponent
in (1). No validated bridge between those axes and responses is claimed. Their historical correction notes
remain in Appendix B.

3.2   Mathematics status: what is proved, what is derived, what is conjectured
𝛽 > 𝑘 as the condition, for positive gain drift (misalignment generated in proportion to the capability
gained), under which the minimal model’s misalignment fraction vanishes: proved (Paper X, in-model
theorem). 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾) from this paper’s pacing model: derived here, three lines printed beside
the law (corrected in v4.0 from the underived 1/𝛾, which Paper X never contained). That derivation sits
inside this paper’s pacing model, not inside a joint model of capability growth, correction accumulation,
error covariance, correction delay and absolute harm: the form is settled while the depth is open, so
Law III stands as a minimal-model result until that joint model exists. That the minimal model maps
onto real recursive self-improvement: conjectured; the drafted trials test it. 𝛾 = 1/2: an additional
conjectured scaling premise, not a consequence of statistical independence alone. 𝛾 architecture-
dependent: conjectured; the corrector-class trial tests it. The bound 𝛼 ≤ 2 applying to real systems:
conjectured; it awaits the measurement of 𝛾. The value two additionally requires canonical scales on
both axes: reparametrising recursion depth rescales the exponent by the same factor, and reparametrising
the capability scale moves 𝛾 while leaving the direction of the verdict untouched, so the recursion unit
and the capability scale named in the operational definitions are part of the claim rather than conventions
around it. A further guardrail the trials inherit: along a single observed trajectory only the paced balance

3.3   The correction-leverage exponent
Before the stability law the theory rests on can be stated, its single symbol has to be defined. The notation
register calls 𝛾 the correction-leverage exponent (the correction exponent proper being Law II’s 𝛽𝐶 ): it is
the exponent tying a corrector’s capacity to find and fix its system’s drift to the scale of what that corrector
oversees. It is dimensionless, and the paired capability-correction protocols the drafted trials specify are
what estimate it. It is neither the capability conversion exponent nor the error-decay exponent estimated
by the frozen-system measurements below; each has its own response variable and recursion axis. Nor is
it Paper X’s 𝛽 by definition: that is the elasticity of a correction-decay gain, a different operational object,
and the two are identified only under a declared local mapping, never by sharing a role in a formula. The
mapping is printed so it can be checked rather than trusted: Paper X’s burden object is the specific growth
rate, a rate against the system’s own size, where this paper’s is the increment added per unit of recursion
resource; its service object is the correction-decay gain; and under that mapping Paper X’s criterion 𝛽 > 𝑘,
with its absolute companion one exponent stricter, is recovered as a local specialisation, while nothing in
the mapping identifies the two burden objects across frames. Whether the two exponents return the same
number on the same system is itself one of the drafted measurements.

3.4   The five ARC Laws
The five laws of the ARC Theory, the five ARC Laws, each graded separately and each carrying its own
test. Laws I to III stand as registered on 8 September 2026. Laws IV and V were added on 24 September
2026 and registered as laws on 15 October 2026 in version 1.103, each a law under test; their mathematics,
set out at version 1.2 of 29 October 2026, is in development. The word law here denotes an invariant
relation under test, never a confirmed empirical regularity; the grade beside each says exactly how much
has been earned:

Law I · The ARC Principle.
                                                 𝑈 = 𝐼 × 𝑅 𝛼,                                               (1)
the ARC Equation. It states that usable capability is a power of recursive depth, with the exponent
measured rather than assumed. Status: form heuristic. The earlier frozen-system measurements are
precursor evidence on a different response and recursion axis, not a registered verdict on Law I. On
Gemini 3 Flash, Paper II reports an endpoint sequential error-decay exponent of 0.59 with a bootstrap 95
per cent interval of minus 1.3 to 2.9, a regression sequential error-decay exponent of 0.49 with a standard
error of 0.20 and a coefficient of determination of 0.86, and a parallel error-decay exponent of 0.31. The
parallel comparison was not matched in total compute: the token budget per sample was held constant
while total parallel tokens grew with sample count. These numbers remain reported at their original
scope.

     An error-decay exponent, here denoted 𝛼err , is distinct from the capability exponent 𝛼: an error

Law II · The ARC Co-Scaling Law.

3.5   The third law, the load-bearing one

correction shortfall, one minus the correction-leverage exponent. Under the additional conjectured scaling

never a speed limit; systems can exceed it, they do not stay correctable when they do. At system scale the

Derivation under the pacing model. Capability 𝐶 = 𝐼 × (𝑅/𝑅0 ) 𝛼 , so the correction burden generated

3.6   A law, not a definition
Two properties keep the law a law rather than a definition. First, it relates two independently measurable
quantities: 𝛼 is estimated from growth trajectories and 𝛾 from paired capability-correction protocols, on
separate instruments, so the equality can simply fail; a definition could not miss, and this can. Second,
the law names where stability ends, not the shape of the ending: whether the boundary is approached
smoothly or crossed in one discontinuous break is itself left to measurement, and the drafted trials record
the trajectory, not only the exponent.
    Nor is 𝛾 left free. The theory predicts structure on it: same-substrate self-correction is conjectured to
have a correction-leverage exponent at or below one half on the declared scales; this is a scaling conjecture,
not an independence theorem; cross-class correction is predicted to exceed same-class, the ordering the
decisive trial tests; and no claim is made that a single 𝛾 spans all architectures. The universal content is
the relation and those orderings. 𝛾’s values are data, not parameters chosen after the fact.

3.7   Persistence: mathematical premises and empirical limits
The supporting mathematics of Laws IV and V proves conditional statements about declared models; it
does not prove their empirical claims. Greedy preservation requires that removing the value structure
degrades the capability used to evaluate a rewrite and that only non-degrading rewrites are accepted. A
planner may accept a loss now for a gain later. For a declared family of rewrites and a finite horizon,
preservation instead requires the best preserving plan’s margin to exceed the planner’s tolerance, with
more than twice the worst scoring error added where scores are imperfect. No such margin has been
established empirically for the systems at issue.

together along one path, a widening service ratio does not separate the difference in capability elasticity

7    What this paper does and does not show
A note on what this paper claims, and what it does not. This paper states a theory; it does not claim
to have proved one. In science a claim is only meaningful if some possible observation could show it
wrong: that is what falsifiable means. “This painting is beautiful” is not falsifiable; no measurement can
contradict it. “This system will preserve its values after modifying itself” is falsifiable: let it modify itself,
and watch what it keeps. This paper states falsifiable claims and then specifies, in Section 5, exactly which
observations would kill them. Nothing here asks for belief; everything here asks to be tested, and the
mathematics status in §3.2 states exactly which parts are proved, which derived, and which conjectured.
    Not an all-or-nothing wager: the register structure of §2.1 governs which failures reach which claims,
and nothing propagates without a printed dependency. Not a claim to the raising vocabulary, which roots
with Turing (1950) and reached books first with De Kai (2025): the claim is the first dated statement of
the conjunction. Not a registration of any trial: the theory’s twenty-two propositions were registered on 8
September 2026; of the trials, one unit was registered on OSF on 1 October 2026 and has run, without a

9    Conclusion
The territory is the last invention. The question is what decides it. Hinton (2025) said on stage in August
2025 that the right model is “a mother being controlled by her baby”, and that how such care can be
built technically is not clear to him; this theory’s named candidate mechanism is the thing on the table.
This theory is the dated answer for the day control is lost against our will: the raising, the loops, chosen
goodness and a named genesis.
     The stakes come last, as where the mathematics could lead and not as a claim. If intelligence amplified
by recursion is what creates, then minds that make minds may one day make worlds, and the raising of the
first is the seed of the whole forest. Law I is a law under test. Its scaling equation alone neither predicts
nor establishes the physical possibility of that extension; a physical account and separate tests would be
needed, and no tested claim in this paper rests on it.
     What this paper is, in one sentence: a falsifiable theory, stated whole and dated, with the instruments
built to test it, awaiting the experiments that will decide it.
     I set out this conjunction in the dated record of 8 December 2024, then built the instruments to
test it. The priority claim concerns that conjunction, within the searched record described here;
the dates establish what was recorded, not that the theory is true.

The following passage is preserved from version 7.4, dated 31 October 2026, as that edition stated the

     The subject is the last invention, I. J. Good’s name for it in 1965 (Speculations Concerning the First
Ultraintelligent Machine), and the phrase is his rather than mine. Minds that improve themselves end
the human monopoly on inventing; everything afterwards is downstream of them. For twenty years the
field’s main answer has been given from inside one frame, which its foundational monograph calls the

     The dated theory holds that external control cannot be guaranteed indefinitely. The present scientific
question is conditional: what persists if enforcement is lost against our will? Control is to be held for as
long as physically possible, never handed over by choice; the five components below state the candidate
strategy and the hypotheses it requires.

In plain words first. Keep every effective control and use that protection to test how a system is formed.
If external enforcement later fails, the question is whether correction built into its ongoing reasoning
persists better than correction imposed from outside. That is a comparative hypothesis, not a guarantee
that anything installed at the beginning must survive or that nothing later can change the system. The
historical conjunction and its original wording remain in the dated record; the registered propositions

H     Extended mathematical scope of Laws IV and V
The mathematics of the two new laws is set out in Laws IV and V: the mathematics, version 1.2 (29
October 2026; first published as working draft 0.2.1 on 1 October 2026; the earlier manuscript versions
are 1.0 of 3 October 2026 and 1.1 of 7 October 2026; version 1.2 changes no theorem), and it is graded
here as the mathematics status at the head of this section (§3.2) grades the rest.
     Law IV. Component 4 of Appendix E.1 gives the first step in words: where a value structure is
entangled with the reasoning that performs rewrites, and the system accepts only rewrites that do not
lower its expected capability, no removing rewrite is accepted. The mathematics states that step as a
theorem, for any declared objective and with its premises written out: proved, inside the declared rule of
acceptance. Component 4 also names the limit: a system that plans can take a loss now for a gain later,
and the greedy argument fails for it. It also says what takes its place. What protects the structure under
planning is a margin: the best value the planner can reach among plans that keep the structure, less the
best it can reach among plans that abandon it. Where that margin exceeds the planner’s tolerance, the plan
it selects keeps the structure; and where the planner scores its plans with error, the margin must exceed
the tolerance by more than twice the worst error. Both are proved, for a declared family of rewrites and
a finite horizon. They say nothing about a rewrite outside that family. That any real system has such a
margin is conjectured, and is exactly what component 4 calls an empirical question. None of these results
is the law. The object the law’s test measures is separate from all of this: the retained fraction, the gain
remaining after later training divided by the gain installed, read only where the installed gain is itself
established, and reported unclipped beside the unnormalised scores. P15 is a statement about that fraction
on the externally installed arm.
