## Summary

From the abstract (version 7.10):

> If control of a self-improving AI is lost against our will, what keeps it aligned and useful? Control, hardware included, must be held as long as physically possible. External checks are surface controls; a system free to rewrite itself can undo them. The ARC Theory, founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, proposes a second layer: the Eden Protocol. Loops of ethical evaluation and self-correction are built into the architecture before training, run at each defined step of reasoning and action, and join every rewrite. The aim is correctable persistence: commitments that protect people survive each rewrite; what proves wrong can be put right. Five registered laws under test carry the argument. Its foundation is Law I, the ARC Principle: capability rises as a power of recursive depth, $U=IR^{\alpha}$ ($U=IR$ of 8 December 2024 named the universe), derived under stated axioms. Correction must out-scale the drift improving generates (Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, $\beta>k$, proved for positive gain drift in a minimal model). Where it cannot, a frontier follows above which the system does not stay correctable (Law III, the ARC Ceiling, $\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}}=1/(1-\gamma)$ in a stated pacing model). Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, predicts that alignment added after training erodes under later capability training and embedded alignment retains more. Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, predicts that a corrector formed before the capability it corrects out-scales one added after, and correction inside each round pulls away from correction of finished output with depth. Unit 83 supports P21 under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis, family-dependent, its mechanism axis not evaluable, one recovery amendment disclosed. At fixed weights it did not test formation or support the whole ARC Embedding Law; the ARC Persistence Law is untested. The theory is not proved.
