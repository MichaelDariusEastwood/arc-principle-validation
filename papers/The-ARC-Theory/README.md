## Summary

From the abstract (version 7.6):

> Humanity is building systems that may improve themselves. If control is lost against our will, what could preserve useful and aligned behaviour? Control should be retained for as long as possible; embedded correction is a contingency, never a reason to relinquish it. This paper states the ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, as five laws under test. Law I, the ARC Principle, $U=I R^{\alpha}$, is a heuristic form; earlier error-decay fits do not establish its capability exponent. Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, $\beta>k$, is proved for positive gain drift in a minimal model. Law III, the ARC Ceiling, $\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}}=1/(1-\gamma)$, follows from a stated pacing model; the ARC Bound of at most two additionally requires the conjectured same-class cap on $\gamma$ and the declared scales. Laws IV and V, the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law, address value persistence, build order and correction placement. Laws I to III were registered on 8 September 2026; Laws IV and V were added on 24 September and registered as laws on 15 October in the theory registration, now at version 1.104. Unit 83 ran on 27 and 28 October: P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis, family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not evaluable and one call retried under a disclosed recovery amendment (results: DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/98HKR). That comparison couples information access and repair rights; it does not establish their separate effects or support Law V as a whole. Unit 06 was administratively incomplete with no verdict; Unit 82 is registered and unrun. The paper separates mathematical deductions, precursor evidence and prospective tests, and preserves the dated record of the conjunction stated in the self-emailed manuscript of 8 December 2024. The theory is not proved.
