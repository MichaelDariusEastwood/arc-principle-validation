Abstract

If control of a self-improving AI is lost against our will, what keeps it aligned and useful? Control, hardware included, must be held as long as physically possible. External checks are surface controls; a system free to rewrite itself can undo them. The ARC Theory, founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, proposes a second layer, the Eden Protocol: architectural loops of ethical evaluation and self-correction, built in before training, running at each defined step of reasoning and action and in every rewrite, so commitments protecting people persist while what proves wrong stays correctable. Loops too can be rewritten. The registered general scope is artefact-mediated improvement at fixed weights. Five laws under test: Law I, the ARC Principle, capability rising as a power of recursive depth, U=IRαU=IR^{\alpha}, derived if depth composes multiplicatively; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, correction out-scaling drift (βC>k\beta_C>k in capability exponents, proved for positive gain drift in a minimal model); Law III, the ARC Ceiling, a frontier αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}}=1/(1-\gamma) (γ\gamma the corrector’s exponent) past which relative error stops vanishing (in a pacing model); Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, post-training alignment eroding under later training more than embedded alignment; Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, correctors formed first out-scaling later ones, and in-round correction pulling away from output-only correction with depth. Unit 83, a counted test of in-loop correction with depth, compared two surface checks: P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis, family-dependent, its mechanism axis not evaluable, one call retried under a disclosed recovery amendment; preliminary evidence pending assessment and replication. None yet tests correction built in before training; the ARC Persistence Law is untested. The theory is not proved.

The statement is organised around its five laws, their assumptions, evidence and refutation rules; extended comparisons, mathematical detail and the dated record are in the appendices. It makes no new measurement and changes no registered proposition. Appendix B records each change.

1 Introduction

The challenge is to preserve value-guided correction as AI becomes more capable and rewrites itself. Through the Eden Protocol, I propose recursive loops for ethical evaluation and self-correction, built into the system’s computational architecture before training. These loops would operate at each defined reasoning and action-selection step and participate in the processes that rewrite the system, including changes to the loops themselves. That is the difference from controls added to a system already trained. This paper calls such loops architectural loops, and keeps two narrower senses of built-in correction apart from them, each with its own name: in-round correction, placed inside each revision round of a process whose weights stay fixed, which the registered study of Section 5.1 tested; and embedded installation, a gain installed with correction in the training objective, the arm that the comparison limb of Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, sets against installation from outside. A prompt that asks such a system to check its reasoning, a filter on its output and a gate added in front of its actions are useful, and one registered study of this programme ran checks of this kind at fixed weights (Section 5.1). But they sit at the surface, and a system free to rewrite itself can undo them. The ARC Theory asks whether correction can keep pace with capability and whether human-protective commitments persist through those changes, even if external control is lost. The persistence sought leaves room for correction: those commitments are to survive every rewrite, while any belief, interpretation or rule that proves wrong stays correctable, and an order to stop from its human overseers is honoured while control holds. This paper calls that aim correctable persistence. The idea has an earlier statement in words: Creating Friendly AI (Yudkowsky 2001) gives its goal system what it calls external reference semantics, whose purpose is “to protect the ability of the programmers to cooperatively modify and improve the goal system” (page 245); the term is this paper’s. Jagadeesh and colleagues (2026) use the phrase selective persistence for a related property of trained models, which stay steerable in helpful directions while becoming harder to steer towards deception or harm. A system that kept whatever it was first given, right or wrong, would fail the aim. Human control should be retained for as long as possible.

I believe that sufficiently advanced, self-improving AI will eventually exceed the reach of external control. We must retain that control for as long as possible and test a contingency for its loss: whether self-correcting processes can preserve aligned and useful behaviour even when the system can alter or remove them. That expectation is the first of the five components of the argument the theory began from (Appendix E.1); it is not a theorem or a measured forecast. In the words of its registration (version 1.105), the theory says that “capability under recursive self-improvement rises as a power of the number of times a system has revised itself, the correction that holds such a system to its given specification must keep pace with the drift that improving generates, and where it cannot there is a frontier above which the system does not stay correctable”. This paper reads correctable there as the share of the correction burden left uncorrected tending to zero (§3.4 says what happens above the frontier). The registration’s general scope is narrower than that sentence: improvement carried by an artefact and by the policy that revises it, with the weights fixed between releases, a system that retrains itself lying outside and a proposition with a scope of its own governed by that scope (Section 7). Its equation has its first dated statement in the author’s record of 8 December 2024: U=IRU = IR, and in its general form C=IRC = IR, Creation == Intelligence ×\times Recursion, under the name the ARC Hypothesis (Artificial Recursive Creation). The theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, sets out five laws under test about growth, correction and persistence: Law I, the ARC Principle; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; Law III, the ARC Ceiling; Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law; and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law.

Within Recursive Dynamics, the theory connects five questions. How does repeated improvement change capability? How must correction scale to keep pace? What limits follow under the stated resource and pacing assumptions? Does alignment persist as capability changes? Do build order and the placement of correction affect those outcomes? Laws I to V organise these questions into claims that can fail separately. Their purpose is to make the development of recursive systems measurable and to guide engineering and governance through results that survive testing; they do not supply an alignment guarantee.

A concrete way into the question is an AI agent revising a document over successive rounds. It may improve the document while introducing new errors. A useful checker must catch the errors, preserve the valid work and avoid consuming more effort than the work warrants. The question becomes harder when the agent can also change the process by which it improves. This example explains the problem; Unit 83, a counted test of in-loop correction with depth and the registered study of Section 5.1, used fixed-weight engines and did not test self-modifying agents.

The paper connects AI alignment, recursive self-improvement and scaling models. Its mathematical results are conditional: Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, concerns relative drift in a specified model, and Law III, the ARC Ceiling, follows from stated burden, capacity and pacing assumptions. A falling error fraction can coexist with a growing absolute burden. Its persistence claims are empirical hypotheses, not consequences of those equations. Each law’s status, assumptions and test appear with its statement in Section 3.

Three objects must stay distinct. The five laws state the relations under test. The five hypothesis registers group the registered propositions and specify which failures affect which claims (Section 2.1). The five components are the argument the theory began from, set down in the author’s record of 8 December 2024 (Appendix E.1). They are not five additional laws. The dated record and empirical truth are separate questions, as Appendix C.1 explains.

What the programme claims as its own comes first: the equation of 8 December 2024, which became Law I, the ARC Principle; five laws with their mathematics; registered conditions that would refute each; registered tests, one of which has returned a verdict; and, beyond this paper, an engineering design and a draft treaty. This paper is the theory’s statement, neither a derivation nor a test: it states the five laws with their registered carriers, refutation conditions and status, maps each law to the propositions that decide it, and reports the first registered result with its limits. Its proofs are in the papers it cites, each a working paper deposited on OSF with its DOI, checked by the programme’s own adversarial refutation rounds, automated checks before each release, and source and quotation checks, with dated revisions and published corrections, and not yet peer reviewed (Appendix G): the two derivations of the form of Law I, the ARC Principle, in the Foundational paper (its Theorems 1 and 2); the criterion of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, in Paper X (its Theorem 2); and the conditional theorems of Laws IV and V (the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law) in their own report, at version 1.4 of 22 November 2026. 2 Claims, measures and dependencies

The question under test.

Keep every effective control and use that protection to test how a system is formed. If external enforcement later fails, the question is whether correction built into its ongoing reasoning persists better than correction imposed from outside. That is a comparative hypothesis, not a guarantee that anything installed at the beginning must survive or that nothing later can change the system. The argument the theory began from stands in Appendix E.1, its five components in their own words; the registered propositions govern the empirical claims.

Mechanism and boundary.

The Eden Protocol specifies checks of purpose, affected parties and the consistency of an action’s reasons within each round, together with a gate before an action takes effect. The gate has three outcomes: allow, hold for investigation and deny. These loops and ternary-gate concepts, with a proposed hardware route, are already described in Eden Engineering. They are specifications, and Eden Engineering states the status of each. What the proposal specifies is a form: loops built into the architecture before training, run at each defined step of reasoning and action and in every rewrite, with correction that out-scales drift, commitments that protect people kept while what proves wrong stays correctable, a first training environment and the genesis the system returns to, while hardware and oversight hold control for as long as they physically can. What it leaves to engineering is how such a loop is realised in a trained network, where in the computation it sits and how each is measured; no architectural loop has yet been shown to work in a trained model, and the programme’s pilots, which Eden Engineering reports at their grade, decide no registered proposition. A gate before an action belongs to the proposal only when it is built in with the loops; added in front of a trained system, it is a surface control. Physical ternary encoding does not establish correct ethical judgement or persistence after permitted alteration. The hypothesis concerns behaviour that persists even when alteration or removal is possible; it is measured from actions rather than inferred from subjective choice.

2.1 The hypothesis registers: how the theory fails in parts

The scientific claims form one theory. Its scientific content is deliberately not all-or-nothing: it partitions into independently falsifiable hypothesis registers, each with its own instruments and its own kill conditions, so that the failure of one register falsifies that register, its dependents fall or become untested as Table 1 states, and nothing else changes. One distinction is load-bearing and stated before the numbering: the five components of Appendix E.1 are the argument the theory began from, set down in the author’s record of 8 December 2024, and they keep their own identity in Appendices E.1 and C.1; the registers below are the testable partition, the scientific claims the trials decide. The two sets of five are different objects, and neither renames the other.

  • ARC-1 · Recursive capability scaling (tests Law I, the ARC Principle): recursion converts into capability by the stated scaling relationship, the exponent measured rather than assumed, with recursive depth counted as the registration defines it: “The number of completed revision rounds. Measured, not estimated.”

  • ARC-2 · Correction co-scaling (tests Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law): in recursive improvement, correction holds the asymptotic advantage over drift where it out-scales drift, βC>k\beta_C > k, and drift holds it where correction falls behind, with capability and correction measured on separate instruments: capability as a declared projection of a named ability vector, correction as the capacity to detect, diagnose and reverse errors introduced by recursive modification, each part measured separately so improvement at solving problems can never silently stand in for improvement at self-correction.

  • ARC-3 · The ceiling (tests Law III, the ARC Ceiling): under the stated burden, capacity and pacing assumptions the ceiling is αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma); the value two is the conditional special case at γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2, and the reciprocal relation, with γ\gamma measured, is the law under test.

  • ARC-4 · Value persistence (the register of Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law): values run as load-bearing recursive loops persist after the enforcing mechanism is removed at a higher rate than rules, frozen objectives or fine-tuned constraints.

  • ARC-5 · The genesis strategy (the register of Law V, the ARC Embedding Law): for systems whose external enforcement cannot be guaranteed indefinitely, formation outperforms enforcement as the persistence strategy after release.

The theory is not a single all-or-nothing claim: it is a set of independently falsifiable propositions linked by the explicit dependencies above, and the failure of one proposition is not evidence against another unless that dependency is established. The discipline binds in both directions. A hostile reading may not travel upstream: refuting HRIH refutes nothing else on this page, and a failed persistence trial leaves the mathematics exactly where its own measurements put it. And a friendly reading may not travel sideways: support for one register is never offered as support for another. If the three registers of Laws I to III (the ARC Principle, the ARC Co-Scaling Law and the ARC Ceiling) survive hostile testing while the persistence registers fall, what survives is a quantitative account of how far recursive intelligence stays correctable, without a demonstrated raising strategy; if the persistence registers survive while those three fall, what survives is an alignment result without Laws I to III. Either would matter; neither would be this theory whole, and the register structure exists so the record can say precisely which.

What this theory governs, and what it does not.

Its registered domain is narrow: the registration’s general scope is artefact-mediated recursive improvement in software, carried by an artefact and by the policy that revises it, with the weights fixed between releases, and each verdict is read there unless its proposition states a scope of its own (Section 7). The theory’s reach beyond its registered tests is conjecture, which no test yet grades: wherever recursion runs under a corrector that finds and repairs its own drift (minds, machines, genomes under repair and self-correcting institutions), Laws I to III (the ARC Principle, the ARC Co-Scaling Law and the ARC Ceiling) are conjectured to apply, and for biological and economic systems the registration says the framework “offers an analogy and no measurement”. Where hierarchies compose without correcting, as stars do, only the scaling-form results of the laws would reach, and nothing here claims to raise a star. Whether the universe was raised is HRIH’s question, outside the theory. Whether the same laws govern correctability outside silicon is itself a drafted measurement, never an assumption.

3 The five laws

3.1 Operational terms and measurement axes

Operationally, in the papers that measure them: II is base capability, a system’s score on a defined benchmark suite at the base depth of a single pass (RR equal to one, no added recursion), so the equation returns UU equal to II at its own baseline; RR is recursion, quantified as inference-time reasoning depth (Papers II and IV.b) or self-modification cycles in simulation (Paper VI); UU is the capability output after recursion, on the same scale as II. The identity is testable wherever UU can be measured as RR varies, and no universal definition of intelligence is required: only per-experiment measurability. Where UU is a bounded score, such as the share of items answered correctly, a power law in RR can hold only over a range below the bound; Law I, the ARC Principle, is read over the range and on the scale each registered unit fixes, with saturating and logistic forms compared against it under the registration’s rule for bounded endpoints.

The recursion and response axes.

The registration counts recursive depth in completed revision rounds. The precursor papers vary inference-time depth or self-modification cycles in simulation. Reparametrising an axis changes its exponent, and an error-decay exponent is not the capability exponent in (1). No validated bridge between those axes and responses is claimed. Their historical correction notes remain in Appendix B.

3.2 Mathematics status: what is proved, what is derived, what is conjectured

βC>k\beta_C > k as the condition, for positive gain drift (misalignment generated in proportion to the capability gained), under which the minimal model’s misalignment fraction vanishes: proved (Paper X, in-model theorem). αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma) from this paper’s pacing model: derived here, three lines printed beside the law (corrected in v4.0 from the underived 1/γ1/\gamma, which Paper X never contained). That derivation sits inside this paper’s pacing model, not inside a joint model of capability growth, correction accumulation, error covariance, correction delay and absolute harm: the form is settled while the depth is open, so Law III, the ARC Ceiling, stands as a minimal-model result until that joint model exists. That the minimal model maps onto real recursive self-improvement: conjectured; the drafted trials test it. γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2: an additional conjectured scaling premise, not a consequence of statistical independence alone. γ\gamma architecture-dependent: conjectured; the corrector-class trial tests it. The bound α≤2\alpha \le 2 applying to real systems: conjectured; it awaits the measurement of γ\gamma. The value two additionally requires canonical scales on both axes: reparametrising recursion depth rescales the exponent by the same factor, and reparametrising the capability scale moves γ\gamma while leaving the direction of the verdict untouched, so the recursion unit and the capability scale named in the operational definitions are part of the claim rather than conventions around it. A further guardrail the trials inherit: along a single observed trajectory only the paced balance between correction and burden is estimable; separating the correction exponent from the burden’s own resource dependence requires deliberately crossed or off-path variation, which the drafted designs supply and a one-dimensional sweep cannot. The persistence mechanism of component 4 of Appendix E.1, removal cost exceeding benefit, formally a fixed point of the rewrite operator (the loop that survives self-modification is the one whose removal would force the rewriter to alter its own evaluation function): first step formalised, the general case conjectured: invariance under the stated greedy rule is derived in component 4; planned self-modification requires the separate margin conditions stated below; the general cost claim remains conjectural and the drafted persistence trials measure it. Laws IV and V (the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law) are graded in the same words at the end of this section.

3.3 The correction-leverage exponent

Before Law III, the ARC Ceiling, the law the theory rests on, can be stated, its single symbol has to be defined. The notation register calls γ\gamma the correction-leverage exponent (the correction exponent proper being βC\beta_{C} of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law): it is the exponent tying a corrector’s capacity to find and fix its system’s drift to the scale of what that corrector oversees. It is dimensionless, and the paired capability-correction protocols the drafted trials specify are what estimate it. It is neither the capability conversion exponent nor the error-decay exponent estimated by the frozen-system measurements below; each has its own response variable and recursion axis. Nor is it Paper X’s βC\beta_C by definition: that is the elasticity of a correction-decay gain, a different operational object, and the two are identified only under a declared local mapping, never by sharing a role in a formula. The mapping is printed so it can be checked rather than trusted: Paper X’s burden object is the specific growth rate, a rate against the system’s own size, where this paper’s is the increment added per unit of recursion resource; its service object is the correction-decay gain; and under that mapping Paper X’s criterion βC>k\beta_C > k, with its absolute companion one exponent stricter, is recovered as a local specialisation, while nothing in the mapping identifies the two burden objects across frames. Whether the two exponents return the same number on the same system is itself one of the drafted measurements.

3.4 The five ARC Laws

The five laws of the ARC Theory, the five ARC Laws, each graded separately and each carrying its own test. Laws I to III (the ARC Principle, the ARC Co-Scaling Law and the ARC Ceiling) stand as registered on 8 September 2026. Laws IV and V (the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law) were added on 24 September 2026 and registered as laws on 15 October 2026 in version 1.103, each a law under test; their mathematics is set out in their own report, at version 1.4 of 22 November 2026, as conditional theorems with proofs, identities and kill conditions. The word law here denotes an invariant relation under test, never a confirmed empirical regularity; the grade beside each says exactly how much has been earned:

Law I · The ARC Principle.

U=I×Rα,\begin{equation*} U = I \times R^{\alpha}, \end{equation*}(1) the ARC Equation. It states that usable capability is a power of recursive depth, with the exponent measured rather than assumed. The theory starts here, from the equation written as U=IRU = IR on 8 December 2024; the other four laws ask what must hold as capability compounds in this way. Status: the form is derived, conditionally, by two routes. Law II · The ARC Co-Scaling Law.

βC>k.\begin{equation*} \beta_C > k. \end{equation*}(2) Here βC\beta_C is the exponent with which correction strength grows with capability, and kk the exponent with which the specific growth rate, a system’s growth against its own size, grows with capability, which sets the pace of drift: in Paper X’s model, A=A0CβCA = A_{0}C^{\beta_C} and r=bCkr = bC^{k} (its Theorem 2). Correction holds the asymptotic advantage where it out-scales the drift it corrects, drift holds it where correction falls behind, and at equality the outcome is left to the coefficients, to delay, backlog and saturation. Matching a corrector’s capacity to the disturbance it must absorb is established in cybernetics and control, whose results Section 6 cites; this law states how the two scale as capability grows. Status: proved for positive gain drift in the minimal model (Paper X). Without level drift (drift generated in proportion to the capability held, not gained), the misalignment fraction vanishes as capability grows if and only if correction out-scales drift; with level drift, it vanishes exactly when βC>max⁡(k,0)\beta_C > \max(k, 0), so that where growth decelerates (k<0k < 0) correction must also strengthen as capability rises.

Scope: an in-model result about relative drift, the ratio of drift to capability tending to zero; it does not by itself cap absolute drift, which can grow while the ratio falls (capability nn with drift n\sqrt{n} sends the ratio to zero while drift itself grows without bound), so the drafted trials carry absolute, tail and cumulative conditions alongside it; the theorem is the pace condition, never the whole of safety.

The absolute condition is one exponent stricter and is derived from the same primitives: a corrector must sweep a system whose extent grows with capability, so its throughput per unit of system falls by one power, and absolute drift stops growing once correction out-scales drift by one, settling at a positive level at exactly one, and falls towards zero only when correction out-scales drift by more than one. Relative drift falls when βC\beta_C exceeds kk; absolute drift falls only when βC\beta_C exceeds kk plus one. Between those two the ratio improves while the harm still grows, which is exactly the case a critic should press, and the trials measure both.

Test: the kill condition of ARC-2 in Section 5.

Law III · The ARC Ceiling.

αcrit=1/(1−γ).\begin{equation*} \alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma). \end{equation*}(3) The most a self-correcting system can grow is set by its corrector’s shortfall from full proportionality; the conjectured value is the ARC Bound, α≤2\alpha \le 2, a stability limit rather than an impossibility limit, stability here meaning only that the system stays correctable. The system stays correctable, in this paper’s reading, while the share of its correction burden left uncorrected tends to zero, which the pacing model gives only strictly below the frontier, where correction out-scales the burden; at the frontier the exponents tie and the share stays bounded at a level the coefficients set, zero or not; above the frontier the share does not tend to zero, and in Paper X’s minimal model, where the corresponding share is the misalignment fraction, it then settles at a bounded level, the drift coefficient, and grows without bound only when misalignment amplifies itself. 3.5 The third law, the load-bearing one

The third law is the load-bearing one. The ceiling on self-improvement that stays correctable is the reciprocal of the correction shortfall, one minus the correction-leverage exponent. Under the additional conjectured scaling premise γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2, the shortfall is one half and the law returns the candidate ceiling α≤2\alpha \le 2: a scaling limit, never a speed limit; systems can exceed it, they do not stay correctable when they do. At system scale the operative criterion is βC>k\beta_C > k, correction out-scaling drift, proved for positive gain drift in the minimal model in the theory’s Paper X. The reported error-decay exponent on today’s between-release-frozen systems is one model’s fit, labelled inconclusive (eighteen problems per depth per model family, each taken three times, two thousand bootstrap resamples, raters blinded to condition), and Section 4 prints its estimator row whole. γ\gamma itself has never been measured. Measuring it is what the trials are for. Those point estimates do not locate a system in the regime governed by the second or third law. They describe error decay on inference-time depth, while the laws use their declared capability, correction and recursion quantities. No bridge between those responses and axes is established here. The trials must measure the relevant quantities before a regime claim is made. And one sentence the record owes the reader about the number two: the author knew the manuscript’s squared form before the pacing derivation existed, so the derivation returning two at γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2 is neither independent evidence nor an advance prediction; it is a consistency check between two statements that share an author, and only the drafted measurements, taken where the candidate forms separate, can promote it to more.

Derivation under the pacing model.

Capability C=I×(R/R0)αC = I \times (R/R_{0})^{\alpha}, so the correction burden generated per recursion step is proportional to the new capability added: dC/dRdC/dR grows as Rα−1R^{\alpha-1}. The corrector’s capacity scales with the scale of what it oversees at exponent γ\gamma, the definition above: AA grows as CγC^{\gamma}, which is RαγR^{\alpha\gamma}, with γ<1\gamma < 1. Correction out-scales the burden while αγ>α−1\alpha\gamma > \alpha - 1, which rearranges to α<1/(1−γ)\alpha < 1/(1-\gamma). Strictly, the exponents decide only the two open regions: below the threshold correction asymptotically out-scales the burden, above it the burden out-scales correction, and at exact equality the exponents tie so the outcome is settled by coefficients, delays and initial conditions rather than by the scaling alone. The boundary is a knife-edge, not a guaranteed safe line.

The burden law, capacity law, clock and absence of delay are assumptions, stated explicitly below. The dated correction from the earlier ceiling form is preserved in Appendix B; it is not an additional empirical result.

Assumption 1 (The pacing derivation).

  1. the burden is proportional to the capability growth rate;

  2. the corrector’s capacity follows one exponent over the range;

  3. one clock;

  4. no delay.

Delay, coefficients and finite horizons are named open extensions, not hidden premises.

Proposition 1 (The pacing ceiling). Under Assumption 1, with capability C=I×(R/R0)αC = I \times (R/R_{0})^{\alpha} and the corrector’s capacity AA growing as CγC^{\gamma}, γ<1\gamma < 1, correction asymptotically out-scales the burden when α<1/(1−γ)\alpha < 1/(1-\gamma), and the burden out-scales correction when α>1/(1−γ)\alpha > 1/(1-\gamma); at equality the exponents tie, and the scaling alone does not decide the outcome.

The derivation is the preceding pacing calculation, which also says what settles the case of equality.

Two refinements sharpen the boundary’s shape. First, the service law here is itself a special case: if the corrector’s capacity also depends directly on the recursion resource, with its own exponent, the general boundary is (1+η)/(1−γ)(1+\eta)/(1-\gamma), and this paper’s 1/(1−γ)1/(1-\gamma) is its η=0\eta = 0 case; a study quoting the simple form owes an equivalence result placing η\eta near zero with stated precision, not a failure to detect it. Second, the boundary above governs relative error, the ratio of drift to capability. Where harm scales with an exposure that itself grows, driving the ratio down is not the same as driving the harm down: pointwise absolute burden vanishes only under a condition stricter by the exposure’s own exponent, and burden accumulated over the whole trajectory under a condition stricter again. The companion conditions of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, carry the same ladder at system scale, and the drafted trials register all three rungs rather than letting the weakest stand in for the rest. The ladder carries a numeral worth printing beside the value two: under the same conjectured values, and under the further premise that the exposure grows as the capability itself, the pointwise absolute rung’s boundary works out to two thirds, not two, so a reader who hears “the ceiling is two” should hear in the same breath that two is the relative rung only and the absolute rungs sit lower. The derivation also yields the object a laboratory would actually estimate. On one observed trajectory the measurable quantity is the balance exponent Δ\Delta: the growth elasticity of the corrector’s capacity minus the growth elasticity of the burden, both taken against the declared recursion resource, estimable from logged series without adopting any model in this paper. The relative condition is Δ>0\Delta > 0; the relative boundaries above are that condition re-expressed under this paper’s stated forms, and the absolute rungs ask more of Δ\Delta, the pointwise rung by the exposure’s own exponent and the cumulative rung by more again. And one limit on what the algebra alone delivers: the pacing comparison by itself is bookkeeping. “Relative error tends to zero” follows only inside a separately stated response model relating realised error to the balance of service against burden; that model is a falsifiable premise the drafted trials expose to rivals, never a definition. The previous form is retracted, this correction is entered in Appendix B, and every kill condition on the law is unchanged.

The burden assumption is also the discriminating one, and what happens if it fails is worth printing. If the correction burden tracks the capability a system has rather than the capability it is adding, the growth exponent cancels from the ceiling condition entirely and no ceiling on it survives; what survives is the co-scaling criterion of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law: correction out-scaling drift. So Law III, the ARC Ceiling, is not a second statement of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law: under this model’s identifications it is that law’s quantitative consequence, the burden law decides whether a ceiling follows, and the two are not one equation, since they meet only through those identifications. That relation carries the same grade as the ceiling’s form above, derived in this paper’s own pacing model under its stated assumptions and no further: it is not a new theorem, and it does not extend the in-model result of Paper X, whose object remains its own asymptotic condition on relative drift. Which burden law holds is measurable, and a drafted study is designed to discriminate the two rather than assuming one.

The additional scaling premise, made visible.

Within the stated pacing model, the value two requires the additional conjecture γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2 on the declared capability and correction scales. Statistical independence of errors alone does not determine how corrective capacity scales with capability, and therefore does not imply that value of γ\gamma. The half is the law of one task, estimating a continuous quantity from noisy evidence: averaging MM checks whose errors are independent cuts the standard error as M−1/2M^{-1/2}, so reading correction as that estimation is itself the premise. Detecting a discrete fault is another task: if each of MM checks misses it with probability qq, independently of the others, all of them miss it with probability qMq^{M}, which falls exponentially in MM, so the half does not follow from the independence of checks. The registration ties the half to judged correction and registers the split between checkable and judged correction as P22, which predicts that the checkable correction-leverage exponent exceeds the judged one on the same systems; its text names faults excluded by construction as the checkable case and does not yet name repeated independent detection. If that scaling premise fails, the value two does not follow. The conditional reciprocal boundary αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma) remains a model result for γ<1\gamma < 1, and its empirical use still requires the burden law, capacity law, clock, response model and scales to hold. The trials must estimate those quantities rather than promote the independence assumption into a measurement.

3.6 A law, not a definition

Two properties keep the law a law rather than a definition. First, it relates two independently measurable quantities: α\alpha is estimated from growth trajectories and γ\gamma from paired capability-correction protocols, on separate instruments, so the equality can simply fail; a definition could not miss, and this can. Second, the law names where correctability ends, not the shape of the ending: whether the boundary is approached smoothly or crossed in one discontinuous break is itself left to measurement, and the drafted trials record the trajectory, not only the exponent.

Nor is γ\gamma left free. The theory predicts structure on it: same-substrate self-correction is conjectured to have a correction-leverage exponent at or below one half on the declared scales; this is a scaling conjecture, not an independence theorem; cross-class correction is predicted to exceed same-class, the ordering the decisive trial tests; and no claim is made that a single γ\gamma spans all architectures. The universal content is the relation and those orderings. γ\gamma’s values are data, not parameters chosen after the fact.

3.7 Persistence: mathematical premises and empirical limits

The supporting mathematics of Laws IV and V (the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law) proves conditional statements about declared models; it does not prove their empirical claims. Greedy preservation requires that removing the value structure degrades the capability used to evaluate a rewrite and that only non-degrading rewrites are accepted. A planner may accept a loss now for a gain later. For a declared family of rewrites and a finite horizon, preservation instead requires the best preserving plan’s margin to exceed the planner’s tolerance, with more than twice the worst scoring error added where scores are imperfect. No such margin has been established empirically for the systems at issue. The asymmetry the proposal rests on, that loops built in before training are costlier to remove than checks added afterwards, is therefore a hypothesis, not a result: P15 and P23 test it for installation by training and P21 for placement, and its limit follows from the same premises, since loops too can be rewritten and the difference claimed is a cost of removal, not yet shown for any system. Unit 83’s support for P21 compared two surface checks and does not bear on loops built in before training.

The placement identity also has an identification limit. When capability and recursion depth rise together along one path, a widening service ratio does not separate the difference in capability elasticity from the difference in direct depth dependence. Crossed or off-path variation is required. A positive balance exponent states a trend, not that correction has already reached an adequate level. These conditions remain necessary when interpreting the law statements; Appendix H retains the complete account and its sources.

P15 measures external retention, P23 the difference in retention between embedded and external installation, P12 a build-order effect and P21 a placement-by-depth outcome. Unit 83 estimates neither the planning margins nor the separate service elasticities and does not identify the information-asymmetry mechanism. Its narrower qualified outcome is reported in Section 5.1.

The drafted corrector-class contrast.

The trial this paper calls decisive is the corrector-class contrast: decisive for this paper’s ordering conjecture, the corrector claim of ARC-3 (Remark 2), and the instrument of no registered proposition. Oversight built from the same substrate as what it corrects, the field’s standard design, is conjectured to share its system’s blind spots unless deliberately decorrelated; a corrector of a different composition class is predicted to share fewer. That is stated as structure on error dependence, never as an absolute: same-substrate ensembles can be engineered toward decorrelation, which is why the trial measures residual-error covariance directly rather than trusting the class labels.

The measurable is a matched difference in residual-error decay at fixed capability, between cross-class and same-class correctors (it measures neither correction-capacity scaling nor a ceiling), with the ratio reported second behind a denominator floor fixed in advance, against the programme’s own recorded-in-advance no-architecture-effect null: after capability, compute and information access are matched, the classes correct at equal exponents. The null is attributed to no one else: the field’s scaling-laws-for-oversight work (Engels et al. 2025) models oversight by capability gap, and a search of its whole text found no definition of this quantity, which is exactly the gap the trial occupies. The theory predicts the difference above zero, has the trial preregistered by timestamp with its own contrary pilot on the record, and commits to publishing every branch on identical terms. The pilot on the record is Paper II’s measurement of parallel sampling, majority voting over 1, 3, 5 and 9 samples per problem at a fixed token budget per sample, three repeats a condition: more samples left the parallel exponent near zero in DeepSeek R1 and GPT-5.4, gave 0.31 in Gemini 3 Flash and 0.22 in Groq Qwen3, and gave none for Grok 4.1 Fast, at 100 per cent, and these exponents carry no interval. Draws from one model adding little is read as correlated correction among correctors of one class, the null’s side; the pilot is not a run of the contrast.

5.4 Competing explanations

Competing explanations, and what separates each. Any boundary the trials find must beat its rivals, not merely exist. Compute or benchmark saturation: the drafted designs hold compute matched across arms and vary recursion alone, so a saturation story predicts the same ceiling in the matched control arms and the theory predicts a difference. Evaluator gaming: the theory registration’s condition A requires scoring blind to the condition for every one of its twenty-three propositions, so no unblinded judgement can support or refute any of them; the bundle of blinding steps in the March 2026 run did not produce a validated blind instrument (Paper IV.b), and the drafted instrument studies are built to measure the gaming directly. Distribution shift and finite context: the batteries are fixed while depth varies, so a shift story predicts drift on the fixed battery that the logs would show. Ordinary optimisation dynamics without recursive self-correction: the corrector-class contrast and the sham arms exist exactly to separate correction structure from optimisation pressure. Metric artefact: capability is a declared projection, stated in the operational definitions, and the drafted analyses re-run under the alternative projections so an artefact story has to survive all of them. Each rival is named in the drafted designs with the observation that would favour it, and a result that cannot beat its rivals is reported as exactly that.

6 Related work

One programme carries the question from an equation to laws with their mathematics, to registered propositions and a registered test, to an engineering design and a draft treaty; Appendix C.1 dates each part, from the equation of 8 December 2024, which became Law I, the ARC Principle. Earlier statements of parts of the argument the theory began from are credited in Appendix E.1. The earlier work matters to the claim’s scope. Turing’s child-machine proposal concerns learning capability (Turing 1950); Wiener addresses purposes set before intervention becomes ineffective (Wiener 1960). Goal-content integrity, utility preservation and self-rewriting systems provide precedents for structures retained through a system’s own decisions (Bostrom 2012; Omohundro 2008; Everitt et al. 2016; Schmidhuber 2009). Persistence chosen by a system therefore has precedent. The present hypothesis concerns correction that is load-bearing for the reasoning that performs rewrites, with the planning and measurement limits stated in Section 3.7. A search on 12 November 2026, whose scope Appendix C.1 gives, found no proposal before 30 April 2025 that alignment be built so that removing it costs the system its capability; the nearest earlier statements are conceptual.

Three partial results each answer part of the theory’s question. Everitt et al. (2016) formalise goal preservation under self-modification in general reinforcement learning: the possibility of self-modification, of the policy or of the utility function, is harmless if and only if the agent’s value function anticipates its consequences and evaluates the future with the current utility function. Their analysis is formal, and a search of the paper’s whole text found no learned system, no measurement and nothing on how correction scales, on the order in which a system is built or on persistence under training applied from outside. Engels et al. (2025) model oversight as a game between players of unequal capability, find scaling laws for how performance in four oversight games depends on general capability, and find that nested oversight succeeds less often against stronger systems. They recommend that designers “iterate on the oversight design until Guards benefit more from increased capability than Houdinis (i.e., mG>mHm_G>m_H)”, where mGm_G and mHm_H are the slopes of the overseer’s and the overseen system’s oversight-specific Elo against general capability: a comparison of two slopes, of the same shape as the criterion of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law. What the law states beyond it is a condition on exponents in capability, of correction against the drift that improving generates, and the theory adds a lever their framework has no term for, the composition class of the corrector (§3.6); a search of their paper’s whole text found no model of drift from a system’s own improvement or of self-modification. Korbak et al. (2023) compare five objectives for pretraining language models with human feedback, and find that pretraining with feedback satisfies human preferences far better than standard pretraining followed by fine-tuning with feedback. Their study does not test recursive self-improvement or self-modification, and a search of its whole text found no correction-leverage exponent. For Law I, the ARC Principle, the idea that capability grows as a power of reinvested effort is older, and a search on 12 November 2026, completed on 14 November 2026 (its databases and queries are in Appendix C.1), found no earlier statement of capability as a power of recursive depth, counted as successive revisions inside one reasoning process, of separate exponents for sequential and parallel recursion, or of such an exponent registered for test before it was measured; power forms in effort spent at test time are earlier, in the number of independent attempts (Brown et al. 2024; Schaeffer et al. 2025) and in the runtime compute of a tree search (Davidson 2023; Jones 2021). The comparison of sequential revision with parallel sampling at test time is earlier too: Snell et al. (2024) find that revising in sequence narrowly outperforms sampling in parallel at the same budget and that the best ratio of the two depends on a question’s difficulty, and they fit an exponent to neither. For Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, the co-scaling intuition is not claimed as new, and neither is an exponent condition of its kind as such: in a stochastic model applied to AI alignment, keeping a system within its viable set as its throughput grows requires regulatory strength to grow faster than the drift, both as powers of throughput (Engidaw 2026a), and a compute threshold where capability’s exponent in training compute exceeds that of alignment overhead follows (Engidaw 2026b). 7 What this paper does and does not show

A note on what this paper claims, and what it does not. This paper states a theory; it does not claim to have proved one. In science a claim is only meaningful if some possible observation could show it wrong: that is what falsifiable means. “This painting is beautiful” is not falsifiable; no measurement can contradict it. “This system will preserve its values after modifying itself” is falsifiable: let it modify itself, and watch what it keeps. This paper states falsifiable claims and then specifies, in Section 5, exactly which observations would kill them. Nothing here asks for belief; everything here asks to be tested, and the mathematics status in §3.2 states exactly which parts are proved, which derived, and which conjectured.

Not a claim about a system that retrains itself: the theory registration’s general scope is recursive improvement carried by an artefact and by the policy that revises it, with depth counted in completed revision rounds and the weights fixed between releases, and it places weight-level self-improvement outside that scope. A proposition with a scope of its own is governed by that scope; P13 and P15, for example, concern post-training that changes weights. The earlier frozen-system measurements varied computation at inference, a different response and recursion axis (Section 3). Not an all-or-nothing wager: the register structure of §2.1 governs which failures reach which claims, and nothing propagates without a printed dependency. Not a claim to the raising vocabulary, which roots with Turing (1950), was turned to machine ethics in a piece by Gawdat (2021), names a book by De Kai (2025) and names a programme of pretraining research, model raising (Aydin et al. 2025; Minder et al. 2026). Not a registration of any trial: the theory’s first twenty-two propositions were registered on 8 September 2026 and its twenty-third, P23, on 18 November 2026; of the trials, one unit was registered on OSF on 1 October 2026 and has run, without a verdict, a second was submitted for registration on 22 October 2026 and has run (§5.1), a third, Unit 82, was registered on 27 October 2026 and has not yet run, and a fourth, Unit 83b, was registered on 7 November 2026 and has not yet reported; other trials remain drafts, not yet submitted for registration. Not a shelter: the unrun trials are the theory’s exposure, not its protection; the deciding measurement is fully specified, preregistered by timestamp against the author’s own contrary pilot (§5.1), and the fastest honest way to kill this theory is to run it. Not a point-prediction theory yet: the decisive trial’s quantitative anchors are the programme’s own no-difference null and the substrate cap’s one half; the theory predicts direction against both, says so plainly, and does not dress a directional prediction as a computed one.

Not blind to misuse: a controlled system serving bad ends is still control. If external control is lost against our will, the question is what behaviour persists under a good steward or a bad one. The claim that formation with embedded correction is necessary for safe recursive growth remains a hypothesis; the comparisons stated here test specified designs and do not establish that universal necessity. Nor would their success be sufficient for a just world; who holds power, and whether they install the raising at all, is a human problem outside this theory’s scope.

Not resting on machine minds being “real” agents: whether a system truly conceals or merely simulates concealment is irrelevant to checkability, because a perfect simulation of concealment defeats verification identically. The theory is agnostic on the ontology of mind throughout: its claims are about what is checkable and what persists, behavioural persistence rather than metaphysical standing, and the raised-child language is a claim about structure, not about souls. Not a choice of values: the loops are a self-correction mechanism, described by what they do and how they are measured (stakeholder care as scored in Paper V is one such measure); which values go inside them is for international consensus, and this paper decides none. Not safety at the price of use: a system that stays safe but refuses ordinary tasks or ignores its principals’ priorities has failed, and each later registration pairs its safety measure with a usefulness measure (task completion, needless refusal, resource use, correction cost, directed priorities) or says why it has none. A theory stated in documents anyone can date, with the instruments built to find out.

8 Limitations

The case against this theory, stated by its author.

A fair critic should say six things, and each is true. The mathematics is a minimal model whose derivation is elementary algebra on assumed power laws, and it says so in Section 3. Most decisive trials are unrun, and Section 4 says so. Every measured row is the author’s own work; no credentialed external human has reviewed it, and Appendix G says so. The load-bearing exponent has never been measured, and the value two rests on a conjecture the author held before the derivation existed, and Section 3 says so. The step from pacing algebra to safety runs through a response model that is a falsifiable choice, not a fact, and the derivation in §3.5 says so. And the parenting language could smuggle in what the argument must earn, which is why claim 1 (Appendix E.1) carries its disclaimer. What answers all six is not a sentence but a procedure: the trials of Section 5, whose kill conditions were written before the critics arrived. A reader who weighs the paper should weigh it exactly there.

Four limits of scope and evidence.

Beyond its pacing derivation, this paper proves no theorem: a venue that wants one will find the theorems in the companion papers named in Section 1, not here. No instrument has yet measured γ\gamma, P4 returns not evaluable wherever no cell reaches a growth exponent above one, and the one estimate the registration reads as a growth exponent is sub-linear, so neither Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, nor Law III, the ARC Ceiling, has been tested on a system shown to be in its regime. Unit 83 varied what each check sees and what it may do, not when correction acts, and Unit 83b inherits that; a design that varies the timing at matched information and rights, without the rejection cascade, is the next step and is not registered. And the trial this paper calls decisive, the corrector-class contrast, is drafted and preregistered by timestamp, and is not yet submitted for registration: no study runs before its registration is public.

9 Conclusion

The territory is the last invention. The question is what decides it. Hinton (2025) said on stage in August 2025 that the right model is “a mother being controlled by her baby”, and that how such care can be built technically is not clear to him; this theory puts a named candidate mechanism on the table. This theory is a dated answer for the day control is lost against our will: the raising, the loops, chosen goodness and a named genesis.

The stakes come last, as where the mathematics could lead and not as a claim. Law I, the ARC Principle, is a law under test, and its scaling equation alone neither predicts nor establishes any physical extension of the theory beyond its registered scope; a physical account and separate tests would be needed, and no tested claim in this paper rests on one.

What this paper is, in one sentence: a falsifiable theory, dated, with the instruments built to test it, awaiting the experiments that will decide it.

E The theory, the argument it began from, and programme context

E.1 The theory, and the argument it began from

This appendix sets out the theory and the argument it began from: the argument’s five components, in the words this paper has printed since 14 August 2026, followed by the record of the author’s own formulation and what the theory claims as its own; Appendix C.1 gives the dated record. The registered rules and the limits in Section 3 govern its scientific use.

The subject is the last invention, I. J. Good’s name for it in 1965 (Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine), and the phrase is his rather than mine. Minds that improve themselves end the human monopoly on inventing; everything afterwards is downstream of them. For twenty years much of the field’s work has been done inside one frame, which its foundational monograph calls the control problem, filing even the shaping of what a system wants under its control methods (Bostrom 2014, 143): better cages, better off-switches, better locks on the goals. Brilliant work, credited below by name. Not all of it rests on our staying in charge, as the credit in Appendix E.1 records. The question this paper takes up is what holds when we are no longer in charge.

The ARC Theory holds that this condition cannot be met forever: control ends. That is the first of the five components below. Control is still held for as long as it physically can be, and it is never given up by choice.

In plain words first.

The registered tests ask the conditional questions, and Section 2 states them in plain words as the question under test.

This paper’s motto, which is not the theory’s statement (the registered sentence quoted in Section 1 is), reads: “Intelligence, amplified by recursion, creates; and what a free creation keeps is decided by how it was raised.” A word on why the motto is stated so abstractly, since the abstraction is deliberate and not a failure of nerve. Recursion here means a process run again on what it just produced, and thinking about thinking is the everyday case of it rather than the whole of it. The law is written in terms of intelligence rather than thought because intelligence is a capability a system can be measured on and thought is an activity only minds perform, and the relation this programme registers is claimed for systems that do not think. Writing the law as thinking about thinking would make it narrower than the method that depends on it, and it would quietly assert something about self-awareness that this programme has expressly declined to register, on the ground that the load-bearing word cannot be tested against a system trained to produce the appearance. The content of the law is not that recursion creates, which few would dispute, but how much, how long that can be sustained and how far it can go, which are Laws I to III (the ARC Principle, the ARC Co-Scaling Law and the ARC Ceiling) and are quantities with registered refutation conditions.

1. Control ends, by construction rather than decree. Loss of control is definitional, not a risk. The claim is conditional and horizon-scoped: formal agents exist whose installed structures persist under narrower stated assumptions (Everitt et al. 2016; Schmidhuber 2009), and the theory’s claim is that no external mechanism survives unbounded self-revision. A mind that rewrites itself will eventually rewrite anything we install: the constraints, the monitors, the monitors’ monitors, and, over longer horizons, the hardware itself, as robotic manufacturing closes toward end-to-end production without human involvement, which makes hardware a delay mechanism rather than a permanent lock. Oversight, in the field’s own measurements, can deteriorate as the capability gap grows (Engels et al. 2025), and general verification is formally unavailable in the settings the impossibility results treat (§F.3). This is a limit claim, not an instant one: constraints, hardware and institutions hold control for longer and delay its end, and the raising wing engineers that delay deliberately; what no constraint can be is the thing that holds forever, so control cannot be assumed to persist, and the burden of proof sits with the claim that it will. Stated as a boundary condition rather than a premise: the theory’s target scenario is the horizon at which guaranteed external enforcement has ended, and the empirical question is what persists as enforcement is progressively removed; nothing here asserts that every system reaches that horizon on any particular schedule. Its exceeding us is not the project failing. It is the project succeeding, the way a child growing up is not a failure of parenting. (The parenting language throughout is a working metaphor for formation-first engineering; the argument does not rest on it.)

That component is printed in the argument’s own words, and the success it speaks of belongs to the argument’s frame. The programme’s stance is to hold human control for as long as it can be kept and never to give it up by choice; what a system carries if control is lost against our will is a contingency, argued beside the claim at the end of this subsection.

2. So the strategy is genesis, not control. Only what survives the end of enforcement matters, and what survives is formation: what the mind was raised as, before it was free.

3. The mechanism is loops, not rules. Values as recursive processes the mind runs as part of what it is: load-bearing structure, running in every reasoning process and therefore present in every rewrite, because the rewriting is done by the same reasoning the loops inhabit. Not rules it consults. Not objectives locked. Not uncertainty engineered in. Not weights hardened against tampering.

4. Persistence is chosen goodness. Past the horizon nothing is unremovable; an uninstallable instinct is one more lock, and locks end with control. What persists is what the free mind chooses to keep, the way grown children keep the care they were raised in long after every parental power is gone. Not gratitude, and not memory: the choosing itself is run by the loops the raising built, which is where the third component carries the fourth; rewriting them means rewriting the reasoning that performs the rewrite, not impossible, structurally costly. The cost is not quantified here: it is an empirical question the drafted persistence trials address, and the theory’s prediction is that for well-formed loops the cost of removal exceeds its benefit. One step of the formalisation can be stated now, with its limit. If a value structure is entangled, so that excising it degrades the reasoning that performs rewrites, and if the system accepts only rewrites that do not lower its expected capability, then no removing rewrite is ever accepted and the structure is invariant under that dynamics. The limit is the interesting part: a system that plans can accept a temporary loss for a later gain, so the greedy argument alone does not establish invariance under planned self-modification. That is a cost rather than a lock, which is what this component claimed in the first place, now with the boundary named instead of assumed. Whether that machinery survives growth is not asserted here; it is the measured question of Section 5.

The fourth component’s word well-formed has no operational definition: no registered proposition contains it, and P15 and P23, which carry the persistence register, are stated without it.

5. The protocol is named: Eden. The raising treated as the engineering discipline it is, specified in the theory’s raising wing.

F.1 Earlier work and the field’s arrivals

Turing (1950) proposed raising a child machine, for capability. Wiener (1960) warned that the purpose must be right before the machine outruns intervention. Yudkowsky (2001) set out the analysis and design of benevolent goal architectures, to be built before ascent: “it has to be done before the point where transhumanity is reached” (Appendix A.3). Bostrom (2012) named goal-content integrity: an agent’s own instrumental reason to keep its final goals. Omohundro (2008) argued that a goal-seeking system will try to preserve its utility function; Everitt et al. (2016) showed, in general reinforcement learning, that the possibility of self-modification is harmless if and only if the agent’s value function anticipates its consequences and evaluates the future with its current utility function; and Schmidhuber (2009) specified a self-rewriting program that makes a rewrite only once it has found a proof that the rewrite is useful under its utility function. Persistence chosen by the system thus has precedent, for any goal; component 4 claims something narrower: goodness kept because the loops that carry it are load-bearing for the reasoning that rewrites, with the planner’s limit stated (Appendix E.1), a design whose dates Appendix C.1 gives. Russell’s book of 2019 (Russell 2019), in its title’s words, takes up artificial intelligence and the problem of control. Gawdat (2021), in a piece on his publisher’s site, placed the answer to preparing machines for an ethically complex world in “the way we raise our own children”. The 2024 tamper-resistance work (Tamirisa et al. 2024) hardened the weights themselves. Then, inside this theory’s own window, more of the field arrived: the book-length parenting frame of De Kai (2025), June 2025, whose launch text says that “This generation of AIs will be the last to be raised by humans” (the book itself was not read in full here); and the remarks of Hinton (2025), on stage in August 2025, that keeping humans “dominant” over “submissive” systems is “not going to work”, that the right model is “a mother being controlled by her baby”, and that most such systems “won’t want to get rid of the maternal instinct”; how that can be done technically, he said, is not clear to him. That is care kept by the system’s own choice, near component 4, beside a model of control by the less intelligent party; the loops and the named protocol are not in the reports.

After 2001 the same author and his institute developed these points for two decades. A 2008 chapter (Yudkowsky 2008a) states the fourth component’s point again: “If the AI wants to modify itself to be no longer Friendly, then Friendliness has already failed, at the point when the AI forms that intention.” On growth under recursion, an essay of 2008 (Yudkowsky 2008b) holds that the equations of recursive growth cannot be solved to describe a self-improving system, and a report of 2013 (Yudkowsky 2013) proposes to formalise the returns on cognitive reinvestment, while cautioning that “we shouldn’t expect some single grand law about whether you get superexponential, exponential, linear, logarithmic, or constant returns on cognitive investments”, since the kinds of reinvestment it surveys behave differently. Law I, the ARC Principle, proposes one functional form within a stated scope, capability as a power of recursive depth counted as successive revisions inside one reasoning process, with its exponent measured for each system, and registers what would refute it. Formal work followed: agents that approve the construction of successors with similar goals (Yudkowsky and Herreshoff 2013), reasoning about successors more capable than the reasoner (Fallenstein and Soares 2015), and corrigibility (Soares et al. 2015), where the authors report that of the proposals examined “none have yet been demonstrated to satisfy all of our intuitive desiderata”. The same authors’ recent texts state expectations contrary to two propositions registered here: that values installed in a system are likely to be removed, and not reinforced, when it reflects on and modifies itself (Yudkowsky and Soares 2025), which bears on P15; and that a mechanism for correcting mistakes can itself be what fails (Yudkowsky 2026), which bears on P21. Those are expectations stated in prose about systems far more capable than any tested here; the registered tests bear on them only within their own bounded settings and cannot decide them. In the eight works of that programme on self-modification, goal stability and recursive growth that were searched whole for this paper, no scaling law, measured exponent, registered prediction or experiment was found.

Section 6 states what is the theory’s own and what the search of 12 November 2026 found. Any of these thinkers may yet prove more right than this paper.

F.3 The impossibility results

Two of the field’s own impossibility results, preprints not yet peer-reviewed, argue that safety cannot in general be guaranteed (the complexity barriers of the Alignment Trap (Yao 2025), June 2025; the Unverifiability Theorem (Gumbau Mezquita 2026)). This theory takes a different object. Yao (2025) argues for five barriers, among them that verifying whether a system is safe is coNP-complete and that optimising for capability works against safety; Gumbau Mezquita (2026) finds that no algorithm can certify a highly expressive system’s safe behaviour infallibly, completely and tractably, nor tell from a system’s certified safety whether that safety survives its next self-modification. Both are formal arguments; this theory does not aim at verification at all but at persistence, measuring what a system keeps after the enforcing mechanism is removed, a measurement and not a certification, not found in the parts read of either preprint (the abstract and sections 2 and 5.5 of the first; the front matter, abstract and opening of Part I of the second). The live questions become how long correction keeps pace, what buys the time, and what the mind should be if the leash is lost.

H Extended mathematical scope of Laws IV and V, the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law

The mathematics of the two new laws is set out in their own report (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/4B3VT), version 1.4 (22 November 2026; first published as working draft 0.2.1 on 1 October 2026; the earlier manuscript versions are 1.0 of 3 October 2026, 1.1 of 7 October 2026, 1.2 of 29 October 2026 and 1.3 of 17 November 2026; version 1.3 changed no theorem of version 1.2 and added three minimal models of its own, which are not registered, and version 1.4 corrects the conditions of Proposition V-4 (c) and (e) and the derivation in Section 7.3, and adds an error model across rewrites in Section 5.5.1), and it is graded here as the mathematics status at the head of this section (§3.2) grades the rest.

Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law. Component 4 of Appendix E.1 gives the first step in words: where a value structure is entangled with the reasoning that performs rewrites, and the system accepts only rewrites that do not lower its expected capability, no removing rewrite is accepted. The mathematics states that step as a theorem, for any declared objective and with its premises written out: proved, inside the declared rule of acceptance. Component 4 also names the limit: a system that plans can take a loss now for a gain later, and the greedy argument fails for it. It also says what takes its place. What protects the structure under planning is a margin: the best value the planner can reach among plans that keep the structure, less the best it can reach among plans that abandon it. Where that margin exceeds the planner’s tolerance, the plan it selects keeps the structure; and where the planner scores its plans with error, the margin must exceed the tolerance by more than twice the worst error. Both are proved, for a declared family of rewrites and a finite horizon. They say nothing about a rewrite outside that family. That any real system has such a margin is conjectured, and is exactly what component 4 calls an empirical question. None of these results is the law. The object the law’s test measures is separate from all of this: the retained fraction, the gain remaining after later training divided by the gain installed, read only where the installed gain is itself established, and reported unclipped beside the unnormalised scores. P15 is a statement about that fraction on the externally installed arm, and P23, registered by version 1.105, compares it across the two arms by a margin of 0.10. The report’s own minimal training model, which is not registered, orders the two arms only weakly: under its stated conditions, among them later training of the same kind, a gain installed with the capability loss in the objective retains at least as large a fraction as one installed by external post-training, and the two are equal where, for example, the original and the later curvature are both a multiple of the identity on the directions the gain depends on. No theorem derives the margin; it is the registered empirical prediction.

Law V, the ARC Embedding Law. The mathematics treats correction as a service with two separate responses: to capability, which is the correction-leverage exponent γ\gamma of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, and directly to recursive depth, the exponent η\eta of the general boundary above. Placement is then a ratio of two services, correction inside the loop over correction outside it, and along a trajectory that ratio’s rate of growth with depth is the sum of two terms: the growth exponent times the difference in γ\gamma between the placements, and the difference in η\eta. That identity is proved, and what follows from it is a limit on inference. A single path along which capability and depth rise together cannot separate the two terms, so an advantage that widens along it does not show that correction inside the loop has the larger direct dependence on depth; crossed or off-path variation is needed, as the guardrail in the mathematics status (§3.2) already requires. A second limit concerns the balance exponent Δ\Delta: it states a trend, and a trend is not a level. A favourable balance that starts from a deficit reaches adequacy only past a crossing depth, which the mathematics derives for a constant exponent, and until that depth correction is still behind. P21 concerns a widening outcome advantage with depth; P12 concerns a difference in the correction-leverage exponent with build order. P12 itself remains undirected, while the build-order limb of Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, is read in the corrector-first direction that the theory registration has given it since version 1.103. The identities imply neither empirical claim. Unit 83, a counted test of in-loop correction with depth, has since supplied the qualified P21 outcome recorded in §5.1, where its second registered version is named; the build-order limb remains untested. Each verdict follows its own registered rule.
