# Correction That Out-Scales Drift: when does correction keep pace with a system that improves itself?

**Version:** 1.17
**Revised:** 7 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-07-03
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 1.17):

> Can correction keep pace with a system that improves itself? I analyse a minimal model in which misalignment $D$ arrives in proportion to capability $C$ gained and is removed in proportion to its size, so the fraction $d=D/C$ behaves as a feedback loop whose gain is correction strength $A$ over the specific growth rate $r$; the proofs are elementary. With positive gain drift $\gamma_1>0$, $d$ tends to zero when $A/r$ grows without bound and, for $A=A_0C^{\beta}$ and $r=bC^{k}$, if and only if $\beta>k$; along the trajectory $d\sim(\gamma_1 b/A_0)C^{-(\beta-k)}$. At $\beta=k$ it settles at $\gamma_1 b/(A_0+b)$, so the coefficients decide whether a tolerance is met, and at $\gamma_1$ when $\beta<k$. Absolute misalignment $D=Cd$ is bounded only when $\beta\ge k+1$. The verdict is set by $\beta-k$, not by the growth prefactor $b$, and without compounding it holds through a finite-time blow-up of capability. Under the power laws, after its transient, $d$ depends on capability only through $A/r$, so a change in the level of correction shifts every tolerance crossing equally and a change of $\beta-k$ shifts them unequally. Unless correction and the growth rate both decay with capability, unbounded $d$ needs self-amplifying misalignment, $\gamma_3\ge1$, with a constant-coefficient threshold $(\gamma_3-1)r/A<1$ of the epidemic and branching kind; its mapping onto the quantum error-correction threshold is a hypothesis. Extensions cover vector drift, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise and a saturating corrector, under which, with accelerating growth, the fraction vanishes only if capacity scales with $\beta_{\mathrm{cap}}>k$. The theorems concern a modelled index, not a guarantee of AI safety. The open test is to predict, from separately measured terms, where $d$ crosses stated tolerances on held-out systems, before and after one corrective mechanism is changed; an estimator and a blind protocol are given.
