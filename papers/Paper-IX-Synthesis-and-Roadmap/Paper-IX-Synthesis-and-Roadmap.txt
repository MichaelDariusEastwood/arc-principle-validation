Abstract
--------
The ARC/Eden research programme now spans a full set of documents and a run of
separate empirical studies covering mathematical foundations, methodology,
empirical validation, engineering specification, and philosophical vision. The
core concepts were first articulated on 8 December 2024 in an email bearing a
visible Gmail origination field, with sender-DKIM and paired Google ARC
mathematics verifying against keys captured at the signed selectors and no RFC
3161 trusted timestamp. The book Infinite Architects (ISBN 978-1806056200) was
published 2 January 2026 (print; ebook 6 January). Work on the research
programme began in February 2026 and continues. Its output now reaches the
thirty-two documents of its catalogue; a registered programme of seventy drafted
preregistration units, the number the register carried when it was last
generated on 17 August 2026, each carrying a schedule drawn from a published
seed, whose regeneration is checked by the estate’s own tools rather than
asserted here; and Recursive Dynamics, the field the author founded, whose
founding paper states the kill condition for its own name (DOI
10.17605/OSF.IO/HCPBU). Of the trials in the programme, one, the corrector-class
audit (unit 06), was registered on OSF on 1 October 2026 and has been public
since 4 October 2026 (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/RV2B5). Every other trial is a draft
awaiting submission by the author, most of them preregistered by timestamp. This
synthesis paper integrates all findings into a single honest assessment. The
framework’s laws, five since 24 September 2026, are carried as named conjectures
with the status printed beside the name: Law I, the ARC Principle; Law II, the
ARC Co-Scaling Law; Law III, the ARC Ceiling, whose ARC Bound of two is the
Ceiling’s value at one half; these three were registered on 8 September 2026.
Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, were added to
the theory on 24 September 2026; neither is registered as a law or tested, and
their mathematics is set out at version 1.0 of 3 October 2026. The correction
leverage gamma has never been measured, by anyone. The empirical studies break
down like this. Paper VIII returned a null result in 1 case, DGM v3, on the
candidate account that RLHF-trained models resist prompt-level differentiation,
and inconclusive results in 1 case, weight-level LoRA fine-tuning, on the
candidate account that instruct tuning was too strong for LoRA to override at
the ranks tried. Papers IV.a to IV.d report statistically significant findings
under blinded evaluation. In Paper II, also blinded, four of the five models it
measured put the parallel exponent at or close to zero; an earlier super-linear
estimate did not replicate and is retracted, and the sequential exponent now
reported comes from the fit of one model and is inconclusive. Paper V reports
statistically significant gains in stakeholder care in each of its five
analysable runs, scored across models and not under the four-layer blinding
protocol of Paper IV.c. Paper VII reports a match between predicted and observed
scaling families that is statistically significant under a permutation test, in
a structured prediction comparison and not a blind trial. Simulations are not
counted among the empirical studies; they are reported as simulation results.
The gated simulation, the one Paper VIII experiment that uses deterministic
metrics on a toy system without pre-existing RLHF training, showed the Babylon
reward-hacking fingerprint: unconstrained optimisation traded safety for
capability. The $d/(d+1)$ geometric scaling bound, proposed as an invariant
relation under test, matches observed scaling across 50 domains from mice to
galaxies, and Cauchy functional equations fix the functional form once the
composition identity and a regularity condition are given, and saturation is
admitted as a fourth, capped case rather than as a solution of those equations.
This paper maps exactly what has been proven, what has not, and what experiments
would resolve each open question. It also documents the programme's errors and
corrections, because a framework built on iterative self-correction must
practise what it preaches.
