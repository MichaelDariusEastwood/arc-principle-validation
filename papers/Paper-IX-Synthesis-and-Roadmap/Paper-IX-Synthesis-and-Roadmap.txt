Abstract
--------
The ARC/Eden research programme now spans a full set of documents and a run of
separate empirical studies covering mathematical foundations, methodology,
empirical validation, engineering specification, and philosophical vision. The
core concepts were first articulated on 8 December 2024 in an email bearing a
visible Gmail origination field, with sender-DKIM and paired Google ARC
mathematics verifying against keys captured at the signed selectors and no RFC
3161 trusted timestamp. The book Infinite Architects (ISBN 978-1806056200) was
published 2 January 2026 (print; ebook 6 January). Work on the research
programme ran between February and August 2026. Its output now reaches
twenty-six papers; a registered programme of seventy drafted preregistration
units, the number the register carried when it was last generated on 17 August
2026, each awaiting human submission and each carrying a schedule drawn from a
published seed, whose regeneration is checked by the estate’s own tools rather
than asserted here; and a proposed field, Recursive Dynamics, whose founding
paper states the kill condition for its own name (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/HCPBU).
This synthesis paper integrates all findings into a single honest assessment.
The framework’s laws are carried as named conjectures with the status printed
beside the name: Law I, the ARC Principle; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; Law
III, the ARC Ceiling, whose ARC Bound of two is the Ceiling’s value at one half;
the correction leverage gamma has never been measured, by anyone. The empirical
studies break down like this: a clear positive result in 1 case, the gated
simulation, the one experiment in the programme that uses deterministic metrics
on a system without pre-existing RLHF training; a null result in 1 case, DGM v3,
where prompt-level differentiation met resistance from RLHF-trained models;
inconclusive results in 1 case, weight-level LoRA fine-tuning, where instruct
tuning left the models too strong for LoRA to override; and, in every remaining
study, statistically significant findings under blinded evaluation. The
$d/(d+1)$ geometric scaling bound, proposed as an invariant relation under test,
matches observed scaling across 50 domains from mice to galaxies, and Cauchy
functional equations fix the functional form once the composition identity and a
regularity condition are given, and saturation is admitted as a fourth, capped
case rather than as a solution of those equations. This paper maps exactly what
has been proven, what has not, and what experiments would resolve each open
question. It also documents the programme's errors and corrections, because a
framework built on iterative self-correction must practise what it preaches.
