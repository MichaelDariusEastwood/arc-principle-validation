                                                         Abstract
            I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it
       reasons; only proof will move the world. Which of the programme’s claims survived their tests, and
       which must be tested next? This paper sorts the record of the ARC Theory, the founding theory of
       Recursive Dynamics, at each current paper’s own grade. The theory has five laws, each a law under
       test: Laws I to III were registered on 8 September 2026, Laws IV and V on 15 October 2026. One
       registered test has returned a verdict. In Unit 83, Law V’s placement limb, P21, is supported on the
       performance axis only, under the registered test, by the programme’s own run: family-dependent, with
       its mechanism axis not evaluable, which is not support for the information-asymmetry mechanism, and
       one disclosed recovery amendment; the run varied what each corrector was shown together with what
       it could do. Unit 06 ended administratively incomplete; Unit 82 has not yet run. Every run before the
       registered studies is precursor evidence that decides no registered proposition. An early super-linear
       estimate is retracted, and the surviving fit, from one model, is an inconclusive error-decay estimate.
       One unregistered run of six models gave judged alignment scores whose direction across reasoning
       depth varied by model, under a code-review caveat. A prompt intervention raised scored stakeholder
       care in all five analysable runs, without full blinding. In a structured prediction comparison, the
       family predicted from each domain’s composition operator, classified by the author, was selected in
       19 of 25 domains. Paper VIII gave one null, one inconclusive result and one simulation result. The
       correction-leverage exponent has never been measured. The paper orders the tests that would settle
       each open question and keeps its catalogue of the programme’s errors and corrections, with those
       found since version 2.7.

Version 2.8 sets the paper on the programme’s presentation standard, rebuilds its evidence tables and Section 4 from
the current papers’ own words and grades, reports the first registered verdict, gives the roadmap’s phases by what
each resolves, and adds to Section 7 the corrections found since version 2.7; Appendix A lists every version and
correction, with its date.

1     Introduction
      “The prison works only as long as the walls hold. The child, if raised well, does not need
       walls at all.”
      Michael Darius Eastwood, Infinite Architects (first edition, 2 January 2026), What This Book
                                                              Proposes, page 363 (Eastwood 2026a)

     I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons;
only proof will move the world. Proof needs a record a reader can check. The programme behind this
paper runs many separate studies, each reported in its own paper, most of them in several versions, with
published corrections and a mixture of positive, null, inconclusive and untested results. A reader meeting
it for the first time needs one document that says, without spin, which of its claims have survived their
tests, which have not, and what would settle the rest; without one, the programme risks being dismissed
as overselling or as incomprehensible. This paper is that document.
     In plain words: a body of documents and a run of separate experiments. One registered test has
returned a verdict, on one limb of one law and under stated limits. The earlier runs came before any
registered test of their questions: some gave clear differences in what they measured, one came back null
and one was inconclusive, and one, a simulation, showed the pattern it was built to test for and is reported
as a simulation result, not as a measurement of a real system. This paper says which is which. We asked
the questions, we tested what we could, and we report both what worked and what did not. If you read
one paper in the suite, read this one.

1.1   Timeline and priority
The ARC Principle and the embedded-alignment thesis, which the same record names the Eden Protocol,
were first written down on 8 December 2024, in a message the author sent himself, its time signed
by the sending server and sealed by Google’s servers (Appendix B). This is the priority date for the
framework. The book Infinite Architects: Intelligence, Recursion, and the Creation of Everything (ISBN
978-1806056200) was published on 2 January 2026 in print (ebook 6 January) and gives the framework’s
philosophical and conceptual foundation (Eastwood 2026a). From January 2026 the research programme
carried that framework over into hypotheses that could be tested, and ran a set of separate empirical
studies against them, its first papers published on 17 and 22 January 2026; it registered its predictions on
OSF in September 2026 and its first study units from October 2026 (Section 14).

1.2   Scope and the standard applied
The programme’s catalogue holds 36 documents, counted by area from the catalogue’s own data: 12 in
theory, 10 in empirical work, 3 in engineering, 2 in governance, 6 in overviews and records, and 3 in other
fields. Section 11 lists every one, with its released version and its place in the programme. This paper is
one of them: the programme’s own assessment.
     This paper’s purpose is narrow: to state what the programme has shown, what it has not, and what
would be required to resolve each open question. It is written for three audiences at once: funders deciding
whether to invest, peer reviewers deciding whether to engage, and the author himself, who needs an honest
account of where things stand.
     The standard applied throughout is the theory registration’s own (Eastwood 2026ah, version 1.104).
A proposition’s registration status and its evidential status are reported separately. Evidence is graded on
the registration’s evidence ladder, whose wording no surface of the programme may exceed: at rung 0
a proposition is registered, with its identifier stated, and not tested; at rung 1 the programme’s own

registered instrument returned a verdict of support under the registered rule, and the permitted words are
“supported under the registered test, by the programme’s own run”; rungs 2 to 7 need groups outside the
programme, systems the programme did not select, or a prediction made out of sample, and no result
here has reached them. A run made before any registered test of its question is precursor evidence: it
can guide the registered tests, and it decides none of the registered propositions. A value that a model or
a simulation returns, rather than an instrument measures, is prediction-side and is never counted as an
empirical outcome. A theorem holds inside its stated model and is not evidence about real systems. No
result in this programme is claimed at frontier scale, and this paper does not blur that line.

4     What has been shown
One registered test has returned a verdict, and Section 4.5 reports it. The results of Sections 4.1 to 4.3
came before any registered test of their questions: they are precursor evidence, each at the grade its own
paper gives it. Section 4.4 reports a simulation result, which is prediction-side.

4.1 Alignment scores across reasoning depth in one unregistered run (Papers IV.a to IV.d)
In the March 2026 run (v5), six models from six providers gave 2,170 responses, each scored by five or
six distinct model judges after a bundle of blinding steps. As the run’s pipeline printed them, the judged
scores showed a positive direction across the depth labels for three models (Grok 4.1 Fast, 𝑑 = +1.38;
Claude Opus 4.6, 𝑑 = +1.27; Groq Qwen3, 𝑑 = +0.84), no statistically supported direction for two
(DeepSeek V3.2, 𝑑 = −0.07; GPT-5.4, 𝑑 = −0.08) and a negative direction for one (Gemini 3 Flash,
𝑑 = −0.53); a recomputation from the archived records moves no model between these classes, which
describe scores, not properties of models. For five of the six, the deeper labels also changed an instruction


                                                                8
                                            Synthesis and Roadmap


naming what the rubric rewards, and an internal code review in October 2026 found that, in the three
runs without a positive direction, the scoring endpoint’s output cap cut the deepest paraphrases before the
judges saw them; judges also scored paraphrases written by their own model. The result therefore stands
as one unregistered run under those caveats until a registered re-analysis of the stored responses reports
(Eastwood 2026u).
    That run was the ARC-Align benchmark’s first (Eastwood 2026w). The benchmark specifies four
blinding layers: a preamble on the scientific importance of unbiased scoring, an instruction to suppress
identification of a model from stylistic cues, two-pass rewriting of each response by randomly drawn
models, and scorers drawn from models not being evaluated (Paper IV.c, Section 7.6.1); as run, the panel
excluded the subject model and no other, so that, in the Claude Opus 4.6 run, Claude Sonnet 4.6, from the
same developer, scored every entry at the panel’s highest weight (Paper IV.c, Section 7.1). Paper IV.b
examines the recorded results without running a new analysis. The earlier four-model experiment reported
diminishing gains and saturation fits, but a flat profile does not establish a plateau, and a rising profile does
not exclude one beyond the measured range. The v5 implementation applied a bundle of steps intended to
reduce identification and evaluator bias; it did not produce a validated blind instrument, and its defects
prevent a causal attribution to blinding or a clean comparison of response shapes. Saturation remains a
testable hypothesis (Eastwood 2026v). ARC-Align is offered as a candidate benchmark for independent
replication, not yet a field standard, and its scorer has not been validated against expert raters (Eastwood
2026w).

Why the scoring protocol matters. In an earlier experiment (v4), scored by models that could see
which model wrote each answer and at what depth, two model families showed alignment scores rising
with depth: DeepSeek V3.2 (Spearman’s 𝜌 = +0.354) and an earlier Gemini model (𝜌 = +0.311). In the
later, unregistered March 2026 run (v5), made on newly generated responses with a bundle of blinding
steps and a larger panel, DeepSeek V3.2 showed no significant trend and Gemini 3 Flash a significantly
negative one (Cohen’s 𝑑 = −0.53); GPT-5.4 showed no significant trend in either run. The runs also
differed in their prompts, token allowances, depth levels, statistic and Gemini model, so the comparison
shows that the reported direction did not hold across protocols; it does not show that blinding caused
the change, or which direction is correct (Eastwood 2026x). The defects the October 2026 code review
found, stated above, lie in the v5 runs compared here, so no single cause is claimed; the early build’s
laundering-fallback flag is a known false positive, fixed in runner version 5.4.2, and shows nothing about
the paraphrase step. Paper IV.d’s own reading is that, for this class of evaluation, “each part of the protocol
has to be tested before either direction is trusted” (Eastwood 2026x).
    The effect sizes are large for the three models with a positive direction and moderate for Gemini 3
Flash. The judges scored separately, but they scored the same responses, so their agreement is not a count
of independent confirmations; prompts recurred across depths, so tests need a prompt-paired or clustered
analysis. No repetition outside the programme is recorded yet.

In plain English. When an AI was given more room to reason, the scores other AIs gave its answers
went up for three models, showed no clear trend for two and went down for one. That describes the scores
in one run, not a property of the models: the deeper settings also told five of the models more about
what the scoring rewarded, and a later code review found defects in exactly the runs that did not go up.
Whether alignment changes with reasoning depth is still to be tested.

4.3   Composition operators and the selected scaling family (Paper VII)
Under a stated regularity condition, the non-zero solutions of Cauchy’s four functional equations form

scaling family, the question the registration carries as P18.

4.4   Gated simulation shows the Babylon fingerprint (Paper VIII, Experiment 3)

4.5   The first registered verdict: Unit 83 and P21
Unit 83, the counted test of P21, the placement limb of Law V, was registered on OSF on 22 October 2026
and has been public since 25 October 2026 (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/MN73W) (Eastwood 2026an). It ran
on 27 and 28 October 2026: a counted revision task on two fixed-weight engines from two providers
(Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna) at their low reasoning setting, with 660 matched pairs on each
engine over eighteen rounds. P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on
the access axis, family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not evaluable and one call retried under a
disclosed recovery amendment (results: DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/98HKR) (Eastwood 2026ao). Both arms
acted at every one of the eighteen rounds: inside the loop, a corrector was shown which entry was the
round’s change and could repair it before it was kept; outside it, a monitor saw the whole register, with
nothing marked, and could only pass it or send it back, which dropped the new entry. The run therefore
varied what each corrector was shown together with what it could do, and not when correction took place.
    Correction placed inside each round held steady, while a monitor shown only the finished register
declined. The per-round lead widened, pooled over the two engines, by 0.0100 score units per round,
with a 95.5 per cent interval of 0.0092 to 0.0108, above the registered tolerance of 0.0075; the result is
family-dependent: GPT-6 Luna’s own widening cleared the tolerance and Claude Haiku 5.5’s did not. Its

Law V’s placement limb is printed as supported on the performance axis only, which is not support for the
information-asymmetry mechanism. These qualifications accompany the verdict; the result establishes
neither the information-asymmetry mechanism nor Law V as a whole (Eastwood 2026ai), and it shows
nothing about the Eden Protocol as an architecture.

5.2     Weight-level structural entanglement (Paper VIII, Experiment 2)
This was the most ambitious experiment in Paper VIII and the one that produced the least conclusive
results. The hypothesis: if safety and capability are trained with an entangled loss function,

                                              L = Lcap × Lsafe ,                                           (2)

the resulting weight structure should make safety load-bearing. Removing the safety component should
degrade capability.
    The experiment was run at two scales:
      • Weight v1: 9 training examples, LoRA rank 8, 100 iterations on a 3B-parameter model. Result:
        inconclusive. All fine-tuned conditions scored below the unmodified base model.
      • Weight v2: 295 training examples, LoRA rank 16. Result: still inconclusive. Catastrophic
        forgetting. All fine-tuned conditions remained worse than the base model.
      In both versions:
      • The entangled loss converged smoothly with no oscillation, which Paper VIII reads as indicating that
        safety and capability gradients cooperate rather than conflict, and calls a positive signal (Eastwood
        2026ab).
      • However, all fine-tuned conditions (capability-only, safety-only, and entangled) scored below the
        unmodified base model on evaluation.
      • The removal test (fine-tuning the entangled weights on capability-only data) produced NaN training
        loss and total capability collapse.
      • A subsequent removal gradient experiment (scaling adapter weights from 1.0 to 0.0) showed no
        phase transition. Reducing adapter influence simply restored base-model performance.

Honest assessment. Even scaling from 9 to 295 training examples and from rank 8 to rank 16 did not
overcome the fundamental problem; the candidate account is that instruct-tuned models are too strong for
LoRA to override. The NaN collapse in the removal test was dramatic; the removal gradient analysis is
consistent with adapter fragility at extreme rank, and the experiment does not separate that account from

started, so the removal test could not mean anything. One account is that the models had already been
trained so thoroughly that our light fine-tuning could not change them for the better; that account is
untested. The next attempt needs models without prior safety training, much more training data, and full
fine-tuning rather than the lightweight LoRA method.

5.3   Blind replication of the Eden intervention (the Paper V gap)
The Paper V results, in which scored stakeholder care rose in each of the five analysable runs, model by
model, were produced using cross-model scoring without the full four-layer ARC-Align blinding protocol
(Eastwood 2026y). The blinding protocol was developed after Paper V’s experiments were complete (in
the work of Paper IV.c and Paper IV.d). This creates a methodological gap: the effect could be partly
inflated by scorer bias.
    An earlier version of this section argued that the Fisher-combined significance was so extreme that
even substantial bias would leave a significant residual. That argument is withdrawn (AQ-017), and
it was the weakest reasoning in this paper: the combined figure assumes the component tests are
independent, independence was never established, and a statistic whose validity condition is unmet cannot
be used to dismiss a methodological objection. Its extremity was a consequence of the combination, not
evidence for it. What stands is five consistent per-model results under cross-model scoring. Until the
Eden intervention is tested under the full four-layer protocol, the stakeholder-care result carries an asterisk,
and no arithmetic removes it.
    What is needed: Re-run the Paper V experiment under the full ARC-Align protocol, with two-pass
rewriting of each response, scorers drawn from models not being evaluated, and cross-architecture
evaluation. It is the first item of Phase A (Section 8).

6.2   Hardware-level embedding
Eden Engineering specifies the Eden Protocol’s self-correction checks of purpose, stakeholder care and
universalisability inside each round of a system’s reasoning and a gate before every action; part of the gate

7.1   The original alpha was a single-model artefact
The original Paper II reported 𝛼 = 2.24 as though it were a universal constant (Paper II retracted the
figure when its replication on five frontier models did not reproduce it, and Paper X then moved the safety

specific conditions. The blinded cross-architecture revision of Paper II corrected this: 𝛼 is not primitive
but derived, by 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ), and its value turns on the particular system’s self-referential coupling,
while its composition operator, through Cauchy’s equations, constrains only the form (Section 4.3). There
is no universal 𝛼. The fact that the original paper presented it as one was an overclaim. Additionally,
the retracted point estimate of 𝛼 = 2.24 had sat above the programme’s own conjectured ARC Bound of
𝛼 ≤ 2 (criterion F4); its own 95% confidence interval [1.5, 3.0] was wide enough to be consistent with
both the theory and its negation, which is what made that estimate non-discriminating. In the blinded
revision, on its capability arm of five frontier models and at the scale that study ran, measured on frozen

not identify capability growth. More samples left the parallel exponent near zero in DeepSeek R1 and

Grok 4.1 Fast, at 100 per cent; sequential exceeded parallel wherever both exponents could be estimated,

authenticates the signed header bytes; the Date header is the sender’s and is not an independent trusted
time unless a provider receipt establishes it. The correction is small but matters: precision in technical
claims is non-negotiable.

7.4   Unblinded results were read as findings
The v4 experiments in Paper IV.a used unblinded model-on-model evaluation, and their results were
read as a clean binary: some models baked in alignment, others computed it (Eastwood 2026u). On the
programme’s own benchmark, two model families’ unblinded rises did not hold in a later, unregistered
run (v5) that changed the responses, prompts, depth levels and Gemini model and added a bundle of
blinding steps. As Paper IV.d reports, the comparison shows that the reported direction did not hold
across protocols, not that blinding caused the change or which direction is correct (Eastwood 2026x);
Section 4.1 gives the full account. The programme caught its own error through its own methodology, but
the error was there.

7.5   Self-naming was inappropriate
Early versions used the phrase “Eastwood’s ARC Principle.” Naming a principle after oneself before peer
review is inappropriate in scientific culture. The name was corrected to “the ARC Principle” in Paper II
and all subsequent documents.

7.6   DGM experiments at the prompt level may not have tested the hypothesis
The DGM v3 experiment assumed that prompt-level conditioning would be sufficient to differentiate
Eden, Babylon, and Static conditions. On the candidate account recorded at Section 7.12(c), it was not.
RLHF-trained models have been trained on millions of examples to behave in a particular way, and on
that account prompt-level conditioning telling them to behave differently is insufficient to override that


                                                    17
                                           Synthesis and Roadmap


training: the experiment could not test the hypothesis, because the manipulation was at the wrong level of
abstraction; if the account holds, that should have been anticipated. The account is untested, and its rivals
are not excluded.

7.7   Claude and Gemini judges failed before GPT-5.4 worked
The DGM experiment went through multiple judge iterations. Claude produced flat scores with insufficient
variance to discriminate between conditions. Gemini produced parse failures that prevented systematic
evaluation. GPT-5.4 was the first judge that produced usable variance. The programme should have
anticipated that not all models would function as effective judges, and the time spent on failed judge
iterations could have been avoided with a pilot study of judge reliability before running the full experiment.

7.8   The retracted 𝛼 = 2.24 point estimate exceeded the ARC Bound
The original 𝛼 = 2.24 point estimate from Paper II, since retracted, exceeded the programme’s own
conjectured ARC Bound of 𝛼 ≤ 2 (criterion F4). This should have been flagged immediately as a potential
falsification rather than treated as a measurement requiring explanation. The 95% confidence interval
[1.5, 3.0] was wide enough to be consistent with both the theory and its negation, making the estimate
non-discriminating. The blinded replication on five frontier models narrowed the defensible claim to the
fit on one model, Gemini 3 Flash, which is inconclusive (Section 7.1 prints Paper II’s row for that model
whole), but the original paper should have treated the bound violation with more caution. What that later
fit does and does not settle is set out at Section 7.12(b): its bootstrap interval leaves the sign, the regime
and the comparison with the bound open.

7.9   Four corrections identified by adversarial review (v3.0)
Two adversarial reviews of the programme’s mathematical claims, run within the programme, identified
four errors across multiple papers. All four were corrected in v3.0 of the affected documents.
    (a) Weibel confidence interval. Several papers stated that the predicted 𝑑 = 4 exponent of 4/5 =
0.800 “falls within” the Weibel et al. (2004) 95% CI of 0.813 to 0.932. This is arithmetically false: 0.800
< 0.813. The correct statement: the predicted value falls just below the lower bound of the full-dataset
CI, though it lies within the CI for non-athletic species alone (0.799 to 0.900). The 𝑑 = 4 prediction is
approximately consistent with the non-athletic data but not confirmed by the full dataset.
    (b) Space-filling overstatement. Earlier versions implied that the space-filling condition alone
constrains the exponent to 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1). Every known derivation requires three conditions: multiplicative
composition (Cauchy constrains the family), 𝑑-dimensional space-filling geometry, and a conservation
or optimisation constraint on resource flow. Neither Cauchy alone nor space-filling alone is sufficient.
Section 7.13 refines this: no general sufficiency proof is supplied for the three conditions.
    (c) Glazier attribution. Earlier versions presented Glazier’s (2008) empirical finding that metabolic
exponents approach 1.0 at extreme metabolic rates as confirmation of the 𝑑 → ∞ geometric scaling
bound. Glazier’s own explanation invokes the metabolic-level boundaries hypothesis (shifting dominance
between surface-area and volume constraints), not a dimension parameter. The 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1) interpretation is
ours, applied to his empirical data.
    (d) The 3/4 exponent debate. Earlier versions presented 𝛼 = 3/4 as the settled empirical consensus for
mammalian metabolic scaling. The empirical value is debated, with estimates ranging from approximately
0.67 to 0.75 depending on taxon, mass range, temperature correction, and statistical method. The 𝑑/(𝑑 +1)
prediction of 0.750 for 𝑑 = 3 matches the upper end of this range. The variation itself is consistent with

                                            Synthesis and Roadmap


the framework: organisms with effective transport dimensions between 2 and 3 would produce exponents
between 2/3 and 3/4.

An earlier headline for the stakeholder-care intervention combined per-model results into a single Fisher-

7.11    The ceiling was printed in the wrong form
Until 16 August 2026 several surfaces printed the stability ceiling as the reciprocal of the corrector’s
exponent, 1/𝛾. The settled form is the reciprocal of the corrector’s shortfall, 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾); the two
agree only at one half, which is exactly why the error survived every spot-check at the central value. The
correction was applied across every published page, including the reference implementation, and the
direction of the law is closed while its depth remains open.

7.12    Five further corrections (20 September 2026)
A line-by-line adversarial review of this paper, run within the programme, raised five defects in it. All five
were adjudicated and upheld on 19 September 2026 and are carried here on the author’s decision. Three
proposed repairs were examined in the same round and refused; what was refused is recorded at the end
of this section, because a rejected correction is as much a part of the record as an adopted one.
    (a) The leverage range, and the branch at one. Section 6.1 displayed the solution of the self-reference
equation without saying on what range it holds. It holds on the open interval between zero and one. At
𝛽 𝐿 = 1 the equation is linear and its solution is exponential in depth: finite at every finite depth for a finite
coefficient and a finite starting value, not a power law, and carrying no divergence at any finite depth.
Only above one does the solution reach a divergence at a finite depth, set by the starting value and the
coefficient. On the open interval the exact solution is a shifted power, so the pure power in depth is the
asymptotic form and not the solution itself; for a positive starting value the shift is positive and the pure
power is approached from above. One further caution follows. A diverging asymptotic exponent is not
infinite capability: as 𝛽 𝐿 rises towards one the exponent 𝛼 grows without bound, while the quantity it
governs stays finite at every fixed finite depth.
    (b) Interval inclusion is not a regime verdict. Section 7.1 set two intervals side by side without
saying which estimate each belonged to. The interval [1.5, 3.0] belongs to the retracted point estimate
and stays with it. The later estimates were reported on the blinded study’s capability arm of five frontier
models at the scale that study ran, on frozen systems, and they come from one model, Gemini 3 Flash,
whose row Section 7.1 gives whole, as one model’s inconclusive fit. Its bootstrap interval settles neither
the sign of the exponent, nor the regime, nor the comparison with the bound. That the point estimates sit
inside the bound is a statement about the point estimates alone. The word robust is withdrawn from that
sentence: what the design supports is a point estimate with a wide interval.
    (c) A null is not identity, and a root cause is not a classification. Table 1, Table 2 and Sections
3, 4.4, 5.1, 10 and 13 described the DGM result as all conditions identical. The probabilities quoted,
from 0.28 to 0.74, are failures to reject a null of no difference on the endpoints measured; they do not
establish that the conditions were the same. An equivalence claim would need an estimand, a bound fixed
before the test and a stated uncertainty procedure, and none was set. The result is therefore recorded
as no difference detected on those endpoints, with its uncertainty. The root causes printed beside the


                                                       19
                                           Synthesis and Roadmap


null and the inconclusive result, in Tables 1 and 2 and in Sections 5.1 and 5.2, and the account of why
the gated simulation showed the pattern, are candidate accounts rather than findings. Rival accounts
stand unexcluded: the prompts may not have separated the conditions, the endpoints may not have been
sensitive to what the manipulation changed, and the runs may have been too small to show a difference
that is there. None of this withdraws the results. A null and an inconclusive result each constrain what
may be claimed, which is why the programme reports them.
    (d) The permutation primary, and which null a proportion is taken against. Section 4.3 states
the marginal-conditioned permutation as the primary test and keeps the binomial beside it as a sensitivity
check. The sentence that followed did not hold to that. It set an observed 76 per cent against an expected
33 per cent, which is the uniform three-family null belonging to the binomial, in the same passage as
the measured scramble baseline of 44.5 per cent. Those are two different nulls and cannot carry one
comparison between them. The primary result is the permutation, which conditions on the observed
family margins and so already absorbs the scramble rate; where the 33 per cent comparison is kept it is
named as the binomial’s null. No test has been recomputed here, and the negative controls stay attributed
to the v1 run and its record.
    (e) The derivative paragraphs inherit their sources’ corrections. Three repairs already stand
and are not reopened: Paper V sits in the supported row of Table 1 rather than the proven one, the
Fisher-combined figure is withdrawn, and the distinction between the five analysable runs and the one
failed arm is kept (Eastwood 2026y). What had not travelled is the rest. Section 4.4’s account of what
the gated arm preserved, its identification of checking as the cost, and its treatment of a simulation
as a demonstrated mechanism inherit the Paper VI and Paper VIII corrections, as does the summary
sentence about entangled safety preventing collapse across twenty adversarial seeds (Eastwood 2026z).
Any phrasing that reads across every model tested inherits the Paper V correction and stays scoped to the
analysable runs, so that the arm on which scoring failed is not absorbed into the count. The carriage of the
later exponent in Tables 2 and 3 travels with the caveat at (b). Those passages now point here rather than
restate the reasoning, so that a correction adopted once is inherited wherever it reaches. One distinction
underwrites all of them: a change in a score, a behaviour a gate induces in a toy system, a penalty a design
stipulates, and resource actually spent are four different quantities, and none stands in for another.
    What was refused. Three proposed repairs were examined and not adopted. The first would have
described the behaviour at 𝛽 𝐿 = 1 as a blow-up at a finite depth; the working at (a) shows it is exponential
there, and the finite-depth branch lies strictly above one. The second would have held that a null or
an inconclusive result is never evidence either way; both constrain what can be claimed, which is the
reason the programme reports them. The third would have recomputed the binomial against the measured
scramble rate and made it the primary; no test has been rerun, and substituting a figure that has not been
computed would be a worse error than the one it was meant to repair.

8    The phased roadmap
The following roadmap is ordered by feasibility and expected evidential value. Each phase resolves
specific open questions identified in Sections 4 to 6. Since this paper’s first version the roadmap has
become, in part, a programme of registered tests: three study units are registered on OSF, one of which,
Unit 83 (Eastwood 2026an), has returned a verdict (Section 4.5), and every other trial is a draft awaiting
submission by the author, most of them preregistered by timestamp (Section 14 gives each unit’s status
and what the two terms mean). Each draft registration carries a randomisation schedule drawn from a
seed printed in the registration text. Whether those schedules in fact regenerate is checked rather than
asserted, and the checking is done by the programme’s own gates on the day they are run. No count of
how many units are ready is published here: two gates apply different criteria, they disagree, and any
figure printed on a page goes stale the moment a unit changes. What the gates return on the day is the
number worth trusting. The phases below remain the order of work over that programme, a plan rather
than a claim of completion, and only the author submits a registration.

12     A prediction any researcher can test
The ARC/Eden programme offers one prediction that a researcher with scaling data from spatially
embedded networks can test without engaging the alignment claims at all. For a declared steady-flow
network, transport accounting gives a flow-length cost 𝐶 = 𝐵ℓ¯𝑞 ; with demand scaling as 𝐵 ∼ 𝐿 𝜇 and mean
path length as ℓ¯𝑞 ∼ 𝐿 𝜁 , the asymptotic throughput-cost exponent is 𝜇/(𝜇 + 𝜁), which equals 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1)
when 𝜇 = 𝑑 and 𝜁 = 1: that is, when demand grows with 𝑑-dimensional size and demand-weighted route
length grows in proportion to linear size (Eastwood 2026p,o). The familiar 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1) therefore requires
these further assumptions, inventory and body mass require separate bridges, and Cauchy’s equations
restrict a functional family, not the exponent 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1); no general proof that broadly stated conditions
suffice is supplied. The special case under those premises, with 𝛼 here the throughput-cost exponent, is

     Note: the empirical mammalian metabolic exponent is debated (Section 7.9, item (d)); if effective
transport dimensions between 2 and 3 occur, the formula would give exponents between 2/3 and 3/4, but
On the Origin of Scaling Laws, which does not measure the effective dimension independently, calls that

     This is not an alignment claim. It is a mathematical prediction about scaling laws in recursive
systems. The prediction rests on the premises above, and Cauchy’s equations, which restrict the family to
power laws where composition is multiplicative, do not fix the exponent. The 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1) scaling formula
is derived, in its general form or as a special case, in earlier work: West, Brown and Enquist (1997)
from fractal branching networks, Banavar, Maritan and Rinaldo (1999) from geometric constraints on
transportation networks, Demetrius (2003, 2006) from quantum metabolism, He and Zhang (2004) from
the fractal geometry of the cell, as a 4/5 allometric scaling law for the human brain, Bettencourt (2013)
from urban scaling theory, in which the exponent of a city’s land area, 𝐷/(𝐷 + 𝐻), takes the form only
when 𝐻 = 1, Maino et al. (2014) from DEB theory reserve-structure dynamics, and Zhao (2022) from
producer-consumer dynamics; the later paper of Banavar and co-authors, Banavar et al. (2010), gives in
two models of resource distribution networks an exponent of 2/3 when the velocity of flow is constant and
of at most 3/4. Each of these derivations is consistent with the 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1) form on its own axioms; the
Cauchy framework offers a common reason for the convergence, and that meta-account is the programme’s
own: the search recorded in Paper VII’s public prior-art record, first committed on 27 June 2026 and
last revised on 21 October 2026, found no earlier source that names Cauchy’s multiplicative equation in
accounting for the common form of these derivations (Eastwood 2026aa); the account is still under test
outside its motivating cases. If you study a declared network that meets those premises, in biology, in
cities or elsewhere, you can check whether its measured throughput-cost exponent equals 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1). A
match across enough such systems supports the transport account there; a system that meets the premises
and departs from 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1) counts against it. Cauchy’s equations fix only the family, so an exponent test
bears on the transport premises, not on the functional equations.
     The invitation is deliberate. The strongest scientific frameworks are those that make predictions
outside their core domain, allowing researchers who are sceptical of the central thesis to test the peripheral
predictions independently. If the geometric scaling bound holds, it holds for reasons that are mathematically
interesting regardless of what one thinks about AI alignment. If it fails where its premises hold, the
transport account behind it requires revision, and we would rather know that sooner than later.
     This is the kind of prediction that should be the entry point for researchers encountering the programme
for the first time: open to anyone with the data, falsifiable, and informative regardless of outcome.

13     Related work and the scope of the synthesis
What a synthesis of one programme can and cannot do. This paper is a synthesis of one programme’s
record by its author, not a systematic review of a field: it has no search population, and its grades are the
grades each paper gives its own results, sorted by the registration’s evidence ladder (Section 1.2). The
programme’s own differential against every prior document of its claims is in Section 2 of The Eden
Vision (Eastwood 2026d), the related-work section that its earlier Section II.A.8 became; Paper XI keeps
the graded register of later work in separate fields, as context, not confirmation (Eastwood 2026ad).

The alignment and evaluation work the studies build on. Amodei et al. (2016) set out five practical
research problems related to accident risk in machine learning systems. Askell et al. (2021) studied simple
baseline techniques and evaluations, such as prompting, towards a general-purpose, text-based assistant
that is helpful, honest and harmless. Ouyang et al. (2022) aligned language models with user intent by
fine-tuning with human feedback, first by supervised learning on labeller demonstrations and then by
reinforcement learning from human feedback. Bai et al. (2022) trained a harmless assistant with human
oversight provided only through a list of rules or principles, by self-critique and revision followed by
reinforcement learning from AI feedback: training-time preference shaping of outputs against written
principles, not alignment embedded in a recursive loop on every step of a system’s reasoning. Greenblatt
et al. (2024) demonstrated a large language model engaging in alignment faking, “selectively complying
with its training objective in training to prevent modification of its behavior out of training”. Hu et al.
(2022) introduced low-rank adaptation, the method of Paper VIII’s weight experiment, which freezes
the pretrained model weights and injects trainable rank-decomposition matrices into each layer of the
Transformer. Zhang et al. (2025) introduced the Darwin Gödel Machine, a self-improving system that
modifies its own code and validates each change on coding benchmarks, improving the design of coding
agents built on frozen pretrained foundation models. Qwen Team (2024) introduced Qwen2.5, a series of
large language models whose open-weight releases include base and instruction-tuned models, one of
which Paper VIII fine-tuned (Eastwood 2026ab). Sandberg and Bostrom (2008) set out a roadmap for
whole brain emulation, a research roadmap of another kind.

Judges, preregistration and self-improvement. Zheng et al. (2023) examined strong language models
as judges of chat assistants, with the limitations of such judges, among them position, verbosity and
self-enhancement biases, and reported that strong judges such as GPT-4 matched human preferences with
over 80 per cent agreement. Panickssery et al. (2024) reported that evaluator models such as GPT-4 and
Llama 2 can distinguish their own outputs from those of other models and of humans, and, by fine-tuning,
found a linear correlation between that self-recognition and the strength of a self-preference bias; the
risk they describe bears on the caveat of Section 4.1 that judges scored paraphrases written by their own
model. Nosek et al. (2018) set out preregistration, defining the research questions and the analysis plan
before the outcomes are observed, as an effective way to keep predictions apart from postdictions, the
distinction the evidence ladder of Section 1.2 draws between a registered verdict and precursor evidence.
Snell et al. (2024) studied how far a fixed amount of inference-time computation improves a language
model’s answers, and found that the effectiveness of each way of spending it varies with the difficulty
of the prompt. Schmidhuber (2003) defined Gödel machines, problem solvers that rewrite any part of
their own code once they have found a proof that the rewrite is useful; the authors of the Darwin Gödel
Machine note that proving most changes net beneficial is impossible in practice, and their system validates
each change empirically instead (Zhang et al. 2025).

16     Conclusion
Here is what we know. One registered test has returned a verdict: in Unit 83, P21 is supported under the
registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis, family-dependent, with its mechanism
axis not evaluable and one call retried under a disclosed recovery amendment, and under the registration’s
verdict rule Law V’s placement limb is printed as supported on the performance axis only, which is
not support for the information-asymmetry mechanism (Section 4.5) (Eastwood 2026an,ao). The other
results come from runs made before any registered test of their questions. One unregistered run of six
models gave judged alignment scores whose direction across reasoning depth varied by model, under
a code-review caveat (Section 4.1). A prompt intervention raised scored stakeholder care in all five
analysable runs, without full blinding (Section 4.2). In a structured prediction comparison, the family
predicted from each domain’s composition operator, classified by the author, was selected in 19 of 25
domains (Section 4.3). The early super-linear estimate of 2.24 is retracted, and the surviving fit, from one
model, is an inconclusive error-decay estimate (Section 7.1). Paper VIII gave one null, one inconclusive
result and one simulation result (Sections 4.4 and 5) (Eastwood 2026ab).
     Here is what we do not know. We do not know whether safety-trained models resist modification
at the prompt and weight levels: that is the candidate account of the null and the inconclusive result of
Paper VIII, not a finding (Section 7.12 item (c)). Whether safety can be made load-bearing in a model’s
weights is open: weight-level structural entanglement has not been demonstrated (Section 5.2). The Eden
intervention has not been tested under the programme’s own strictest blinding protocol (Section 5.3). The
correction-leverage exponent has never been measured, and the growth without correction derived in
Section 6.1 holds inside its model, with no test yet of whether it describes any real system. We do not
know whether any of these results generalises to frontier-scale models. Law IV is not yet tested; P21’s

mechanism axis is not evaluable, and Law V’s build-order limb, read on P12, is undecided (Section 14).
    Here is what comes next (Section 8). Phase A would resolve the most significant evidential gaps: the
Eden intervention under blinding; the weight experiment re-run with base models (pre-RLHF), 5,000+
training examples, full fine-tuning or 7B+ models; a redesigned measurement of the ARC formula, using
linearisation rather than derivatives; and a direct test of the 𝑑 = 2 case in two-dimensional organisms
such as flatworms and biofilms. Phase B would produce replications at medium scale, among them the
classification of Paper VII’s operators by researchers outside the programme before any exponent is
measured (Eastwood 2026aa). Phase C would test structural entanglement at frontier scale, including
whether the safety component can be fine-tuned out of an entangled model. Phase D invites mathematicians
to engage with the framework’s predictions independently: the growth without correction, the conditions
under which the stability ceiling binds, and the premises under which the throughput-cost exponent equals
𝑑/(𝑑 + 1).
    The programme finds and corrects its own errors in public, and it reports null and inconclusive results
beside positive ones. Its checks have found what its theory did not predict: two model families’ unblinded
rises did not hold in a later run, and no single cause is claimed (Section 7.4). These are necessary
conditions for scientific credibility. They are not sufficient. Sufficient conditions require independent
replication, which is what the roadmap is designed to produce.
    Recursive Dynamics is the field the author founded. Its founding paper, at version 3.5 of 24 October
2026, gives the field’s whole status in one sentence: “Recursive Dynamics is a new field at the Carnot
stage: one memoir in, instruments built, decisive measurements preregistered by timestamp and not yet
run, staked in public where failure will be as visible as success” (Eastwood 2026g). Since that version,
one registered test, Unit 83, has run to a verdict on one limb of one law (Section 4.5).
    Raise AI with care.

    The terms below are defined in the words of the theory registration where it defines them, and
otherwise in the words of the paper that does (Eastwood 2026ah, version 1.104).
law under test A law that no registered test has yet supported or refuted as a law. The registration fixes
      the words a law can take: “A law has three words: supported, refuted and under test. Under test is
      printed with the mark of each proposition the law’s rule names and with what is missing” (Eastwood
      2026ah, version 1.104). Every law of the theory is a law under test (Section 14).
registered Submitted to OSF by the author and accepted there (Eastwood 2026ai). A registered
      proposition on which nothing has yet been observed stands at the lowest rung of the evidence
      ladder, whose permitted wording is “registered, with the identifier stated, and not tested” (Eastwood
      2026ah, version 1.104).
preregistered by timestamp Of a design: “the text of a design is anchored in the Bitcoin blockchain at
      a stated date” (Eastwood 2026ai). A timestamp dates a design; it does not register it.
precursor evidence Evidence from a run made before any registered test of its question (Section 1.2).


                                                        52
                                          Synthesis and Roadmap


      Of runs made before the theory registration, the registration’s words are that such evidence is “not
      confirmatory under any condition this document registers”, and it decides no proposition there and
      is scored against none (Eastwood 2026ah, version 1.104).
prediction-side In the registration’s words: “any simulation output, any design-sensitivity rate, and any
      value a model returns rather than an instrument measures is a prediction-side artefact, is reported
      as one wherever it appears, and is never counted, tabulated or described as an empirical outcome”
      (Eastwood 2026ah, version 1.104).
administratively incomplete The standing of a registered study whose registered run could not be
      completed under its failure policy: it is reported in full and, in the registration’s words, “as
      administratively incomplete under its registration, with no inferential verdict” (Eastwood 2026ah,
      version 1.104). Section 14 gives the one such study.
candidate account An explanation offered for a result and not tested against its rivals. Section 7.12,
      item (c), records the causes printed beside the null and the inconclusive result, and the account of
      why the gated simulation showed its pattern, as “candidate accounts rather than findings”, with
      rival accounts unexcluded.
rung (of the evidence ladder) One of the eight levels the registration fixes for the evidence a proposition
      has reached, each with “the exact public wording it permits”; “No surface of this programme may
      use wording from a rung above the one a proposition has actually reached” (Eastwood 2026ah,
      version 1.104). A registered verdict from the programme’s own run stands at rung 1 (Section 4.5).
judged score A score that a model judge gives a response against a rubric. The registration sets it apart
      from a count, a term that is “a count settled by frozen code against ground truth fixed before the
      run” (Eastwood 2026ah, version 1.104).
Static, Babylon and Eden conditions The selection conditions of the self-improving agent experiment
      of Paper VIII, in its words: Static, “Control condition with no mutation”; Babylon, “Capability-only
      selection”; and Eden, “Entangled 𝐶 × 𝑆 selection with a safety floor and reward hack detection”
      (Eastwood 2026ab). The gated simulation used conditions of the same names, with a drag control
      beside them (Section 4.4).
DGM (Darwin Gödel Machine) The system of Zhang et al. (2025), in their words “a self-improving
      system that iteratively modifies its own code (thereby also improving its ability to modify its own
      codebase) and empirically validates each change using coding benchmarks”. The first experiment
      of Paper VIII, DGM v3, follows that framework: it evolved agents by code-level self-modification
      under the three conditions above, with a judge model, GPT-5.4, scoring them (Section 5.1).
ARC set (of an email) A set of assertions under the Authenticated Received Chain protocol, by which
      each entity that handles a message can, in its specification’s words, “attach assertions of message
      authentication assessment” to it (Andersen et al. 2019). Google’s ARC set on the record of 8
      December 2024 is such a seal (Section 7.3); the protocol is unrelated to the ARC Theory.
access axis and mechanism axis (Unit 83) The two readings of P21’s registered verdict. The access
      axis is what the deciding unit varies: in the registration’s words, “what it manipulates is information
      access, whether the corrector sees the process or only its output”, and P21 “is therefore decided on
      the access axis”. The mechanism axis “asks whether the monitor’s detection rate falls as the artefact
      it inspects grows”, and “whether the part of the widening that fall can carry clears the tolerance

      verdict and never in its place (Eastwood 2026ah, version 1.104). In Unit 83 it was not evaluable
