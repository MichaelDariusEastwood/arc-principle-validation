# What can composition and transport explain about scaling laws?

**Version:** 3.14
**Revised:** 1 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-02-22
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 3.14):

> Predicting how a system scales can help us decide whether resources and correction can keep pace with its growth. This framework paper asks which composition and transport assumptions justify a scaling prediction before the response is observed. Four classical continuous homomorphism problems constrain functional families; they do not classify every physical law. A global logarithmic-defect stability result and a regular-variation limit connect exact equations to specified approximate and asymptotic cases. For a declared steady-flow network, transport accounting gives $C=B\bar\ell_q$. Demand scaling $B\sim L^\mu$ and mean-path scaling $\bar\ell_q\sim L^\zeta$ then give an asymptotic throughput-cost exponent $\mu/(\mu+\zeta)$. The familiar $d/(d+1)$ requires further assumptions, and inventory and body mass require separate bridges. Biological measurements are compared on their own variables; pooled fungal confirmation is withdrawn. The Friedmann reparametrisation remains an identity, with acceleration distinguished from exponential expansion. Fixed rules can generate superlinear trajectories, so neither physical dimension nor an architectural label fixes an AI capability exponent. The contribution is a conditional synthesis and a prospective transport test with independently measured premises, held-out predictions and serious rivals. Existing prediction histories, corrections and qualified companion results are retained. No universal physical ceiling, alignment guarantee or new empirical validation is established.
