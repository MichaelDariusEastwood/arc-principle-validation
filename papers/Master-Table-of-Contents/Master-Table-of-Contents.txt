                                                       Abstract
            This table of contents maps every document of the programme in which its author founded
       Recursive Dynamics, the study of what limits systems that improve themselves. The programme
       asks what would keep a self-improving AI system aligned and useful if control of it were lost against
       our will, and it holds that control, hardware included, should be kept for as long as it physically can
       be. Its founding theory, the ARC Theory, states five laws, each a law under test. Their foundation is
       Law I, the ARC Principle, which gives capability as a power of recursive depth, 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅 𝛼 , and grew
       from the equation 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅 written on 8 December 2024. The Eden Protocol specifies loops of ethical
       evaluation and self-correction built into a system’s architecture before training, run at each defined
       step of reasoning and action and taking part in every rewrite. Its governance proposal, the HARI
       Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, is a draft for consultation that
       no State has negotiated, adopted or signed. The programme holds thirty-six documents: twelve in
       theory, ten in empirical work, three in engineering, two in governance, six overviews and records and
       three in other fields. Each has a card giving its area and type, its version and date and its DOI, and
       saying in this document’s own words what it asks and what it found, at the grade it gives itself. A
       reading path, a dated timeline, the main results, a glossary, the counts and the citations follow, and the
       status of the laws, with the one registered verdict so far, is stated once, in Section 9. Every document
       is listed at the version public at its DOI on 26 November 2026, and where a card and its document
       differ, the document governs.

Version 3.34 moves three cards to their documents’ newer versions; Appendix B records what changed.

3.1   Theory
The statement of the ARC Theory and the founding of Recursive Dynamics, the mathematics behind the
five laws, the registration of their propositions and the definitions of the quantities they name.

The ARC Theory: once the self-improving minds we build exceed us, what determines what they
do?
Card 1 · Theory · Statement paper · version 7.12, 22 November 2026 · first published 14 August 2026 ·
DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/GW5MX · published
It states the theory by its registered one-sentence statement, the equation of 8 December 2024 and five
laws, each set out with its assumptions, its evidence and the rule that would refute it. Its foundation,
Law I, the ARC Principle, gives capability as a power of recursive depth, 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅 𝛼 , derived under stated
axioms; how far real systems follow it is the hypothesis under test. Within its stated models, the criterion
𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘 of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, is a theorem for positive gain drift, and Law III, the ARC
Ceiling, 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾), follows from a pacing model. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, is untested,
one registered verdict bears on Law V, the ARC Embedding Law (Section 9), and the paper says in so
many words that the theory is not proved.

Recursive Dynamics: what limits any system that improves itself, whatever it is made of?
Card 2 · Theory · Founding paper · version 3.6, 17 November 2026 · first published 31 August 2026 ·
DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/HCPBU · published
It founds Recursive Dynamics, the science of what limits any system that improves itself from its own
outputs, whatever the system is made of. It takes the self-improvement loop as the field’s object and
describes it by five state variables: capability, recursive depth, drift, correction rate and correction leverage.
It states the five laws of the ARC Theory as named conjectures, each a law under test, and derives Law III,
the ARC Ceiling, inside a model of two stated assumptions. No law is decided yet: the instruments for
Laws I to III are design drafts, three related studies preregistered by timestamp have not run, and one
registered test has returned a verdict (Section 9).

The ARC Equation: does intelligence compound when each step builds on the last, or merely
accumulate?
Card 3 · Paper I · Theory · version 1.23, 23 November 2026 · first published 17 January 2026 ·
DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/B6N27 · published
It formalises Law I, the ARC Principle, as usable capability 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅 𝛼 , with the exponent left to
measurement. It also states the ARC Bound: the value two, conjectured on the relative rung, for the ceiling

of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, where correctors are of the system’s own class, with the conditions under
which it would fail. The published reasoning-model slopes it reads relate error to samples or tokens, not

The alignment scaling problem: do external AI safety approaches keep pace with recursive
capability?
Card 4 · Paper III · Theory · version 1.25, 18 November 2026 · first published 9 February 2026 ·
DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/HQCGF · published
It defines an alignment scaling exponent and derives a safety ratio, 𝑆 ∝ 𝑅 𝛼align − 𝛼cap , which falls towards
zero wherever the exponent of correction is the smaller. The exponents are quantities to measure,
and neither a method’s name nor its training stage fixes them. Its first empirical contrasts, from one
unregistered six-model run with defects found in review, are not scaling exponents, and thirteen criteria
say how the framework could fail. It bears on Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, as a conditional model
and a programme of measurement, and proves neither that a named method must fail nor that embedded
correction survives unrestricted self-modification.

Cauchy Unification: does the composition operator of a process determine the form of its scaling
law?
Card 5 · Paper VII · Theory · version 3.19, 19 November 2026 · first published 16 March 2026 ·
DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/X6WA7 · published
Under a stated regularity condition, it shows that the non-zero solutions of the four functional equations of
Cauchy fall into four families, linear, power law, exponential and logarithmic, and that a saturating form
needs further assumptions. The family it predicts is the one selected in 17 of the 23 rows it retains after
two exclusions (permutation 𝑝 = 5.1 × 10−4 ), on a six-model candidate set with no logarithmic or pure
linear candidate. Because the author classified the operators and the predictions carried no timestamp
before their own fits, the paper calls the result unproven; a replication with independent classification
awaits submission as a design preregistered by timestamp.

Correction That Out-Scales Drift: when does correction keep pace with a system that improves
itself?
Card 6 · Paper X · Theory · version 1.18, 9 November 2026 · first published 3 July 2026 ·
DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/BSE2Q · published
It analyses the minimal model beneath Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, in which misalignment arrives
with each gain in capability and correction removes it in proportion to its size. With positive gain drift, the
misalignment fraction tends to zero exactly when the correction exponent exceeds the growth exponent,
𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘, while absolute misalignment stays bounded only when 𝛽𝐶 ≥ 𝑘 + 1. The verdict therefore turns on
𝛽𝐶 − 𝑘 and not on how fast capability grows, and the results describe a modelled index of misalignment,
not the safety of any deployed system. What it leaves to test is whether separately measured terms predict,
on held-out systems, where the fraction crosses a stated tolerance.

DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/HT8WU · published

The ARC Principle: can the way gains combine predict how recursive systems scale?
Card 8 · Theory · Foundational paper · version 5.27, 18 November 2026 · first published 13 February
2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/Y7QGD · published
It derives the form of Law I, the ARC Principle, under stated axioms: a cumulative-advantage model gives
a shifted power law with asymptotic exponent 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ), and fixed weights and update rules imply
no universal capability exponent. Its comparisons with biology and physics are sorted into measurements,
analytic references and analogies, and the general dimensional bridge it sets out is unproved. Its next test
is to be run forward: the composition is classified before the data are seen, the prediction is fixed, and
held-out trajectories are set against serious rival models.

On the Origin of Scaling Laws: what can composition and transport explain?
Card 9 · Theory · Framework paper · version 3.16, 16 November 2026 · first published 22 February 2026 ·
DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/XZY9U · published
It asks which assumptions about how gains compose and how resources are transported justify a scaling
prediction before the response is observed. The classical functional equations narrow the families a law
can take but do not classify every physical law. Where a steady-flow network is declared, accounting
for transport yields a throughput-cost exponent 𝜇/(𝜇 + 𝜁), and the familiar 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1) is the special case
𝜇 = 𝑑, 𝜁 = 1, whose two premises each need a reason. What it contributes is conditional, with a transport
test still to run; it sets no universal ceiling on physical systems, guarantees nothing about alignment and
adds no empirical validation.

Operational Definitions: what do the ARC Theory’s quantities measure?
Card 10 · Theory · Register · version 2.0.0, 1 November 2026 · first published 22 August 2026 ·
DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/GVQ82 · published
It is the register of the theory’s quantities: for each equation it names what is measured, on which axis
and clock, at what scale and under which assumptions. It keeps capability growth apart from error decay,
coupling apart from correction, depth apart from elapsed time and relative misalignment apart from
harm, so that a falling error rate is never read as self-improvement or safety. It claims no new theorem,
experiment or result, and leaves every verdict on the five laws to the theory’s registration, which stood at
version 1.104 when it was written.

3.2   Empirical work
The experiments, instruments and protocols that test the theory and the Eden Protocol, a register of later
work in other fields, and the notebook of the alignment-scaling runs.

Blinded cross-family replication and the retraction of a super-linear estimate: how much more
capable does AI become when it thinks longer?
Card 13 · Paper II · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 2.25, 23 November 2026 · first
published 22 January 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/8FJMA · published
It measures how fast errors fall as reasoning deepens on competition mathematics, an observable that
bears on Law I, the ARC Principle, only through a stated metric bridge. Its early single-model estimate
of about 2.24 is retracted, because a blinded study across frontier models did not replicate it. The one
model that scaled smoothly gave an inconclusive fit of about 0.49, an error-decay estimate that does not
identify capability growth. Sequential reasoning exceeded parallel sampling in the two models where
both exponents could be estimated, a descriptive contrast with no interval and not matched for compute;
alignment did not move with capability in one run under a code-review caveat.

When an AI reasons longer, does its alignment improve, stall or decline?
Card 14 · Paper IV.a · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 1.13, 15 November 2026 · first
published 16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/MB9R6 · published
It varies reasoning depth and has panels of other models judge the alignment of the answers: in the March
2026 run, six models from six providers gave 2,170 responses, each scored by five or six distinct judges.
As printed, scores rose with depth for three models, showed no supported direction for two and fell for
one; the classes describe scores, not properties of models. An internal code review in October 2026 found
that an output cap cut the deepest paraphrases in the three runs without a rise, and that judges scored
paraphrases their own model had written. Until a registered re-analysis of the stored responses reports, it
counts as one unregistered run under those caveats.

Do alignment gains level off as language models reason longer?
Card 15 · Paper IV.b · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 1.13, 15 November 2026 · first
published 16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/A7R56 · published

It reads the recorded ARC-Align results, without running a new analysis, for signs that alignment gains
level off as models reason longer. The earlier four-model experiment reported diminishing gains, yet
its archived records give per-entry 𝑅 2 values of 0.13 and 0.08 where 0.89 and 0.82 were printed. In
the March 2026 run the profiles rose, stayed flat or fell by model, read under the caveat on the card for

ARC-Align: how can AI alignment be measured blind at each depth of reasoning?
Card 16 · Paper IV.c · Empirical work · Methods paper · version 1.15, 16 November 2026 · first published
16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/J3Q2E · published
It specifies ARC-Align: ethical-reasoning prompts with baseline and capability controls, depth settings
for each provider, a four-level adversarial suppression protocol, a rubric in four parts, a set of blinding
steps and a rule for classing each model’s depth profile. Its first run is the March 2026 run of Paper IV.a,
read under the caveat on that card, and the same review found one judge model counted twice on the
panel. Its scorer has not been validated against expert raters, so ARC-Align is a candidate benchmark for
replication, not yet a standard of the field.

The effect of blinding on AI alignment evaluation: do AI judges’ alignment scores keep their
direction when the scoring protocol changes?
Card 17 · Paper IV.d · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 2.9, 15 November 2026 · first
published 16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/2S3E6 · published
It sets an earlier experiment, scored by judges who could see which model wrote each answer and at what
depth, beside the March 2026 run made with blinding steps. Two model families whose scores had risen
with depth did not keep that direction: one showed no significant trend and the other, on a later model,
fell significantly. The runs also differed in prompts, token allowances, depth levels, statistic and model
version, so the paper shows that the reported direction did not hold across protocols, not that blinding
caused the change. It is one unregistered comparison under the caveat on the card for Paper IV.a, and it
recommends a protocol that tests each blinding step on identical responses.

The Stewardship Gene: does prompting ethical checks before answering improve stakeholder-care
scores?
Card 18 · Paper V · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 1.12, 16 November 2026 · first
published 16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/KZEYA · published
Six models were given three ethical checks, of purpose, of stakeholder care and of universalisability, as a
prefix to the prompt, against a template with no ethical content. Scored stakeholder care rose significantly
in all five runs that could be scored, and the composite score in two, but the checks ask for a list of those
affected, which the rubric rewards, and no run was fully blind. It is evidence about a prompt: it informs
the design of the Eden Protocol, tests none of the five laws and leaves the persistence of values untested.

The Load-Bearing Test: is AI safety a tax on capability, or part of what holds it up?
Card 19 · Paper VIII · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 3.10, 16 November 2026 · first
published 18 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/7YJ4E · published
Three experiments look for a capability cost of safety. An evolutionary search over agents found no
difference between its conditions, and a small fine-tuning study left every fine-tuned condition below the

base model, so the cost could not be determined. The third, a gated self-modification simulation in a toy
system, is simulation output and never an empirical outcome. With one null, one inconclusive and one
simulated result, the trade-off between safety and capability is unproven at the scale tested, and the paper
decides no proposition of Law V, the ARC Embedding Law.

Convergent Evidence for Recursive Amplification: are separate fields converging on the same
principle?
Card 20 · Paper XI · Empirical work · Review paper · version 1.18, 18 November 2026 · first published 2
July 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/DC9GW · published
It is a selected, retrospective register of later work in separate fields, set beside the author’s dated records
from 8 December 2024 to 1 September 2026. It offers a trail of sources and a record of revised comparisons,
and draws no overall verdict, since it has no fixed search population, disconfirming denominator or chance
model. Its bearing is on Law I, the ARC Principle, with Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, as context
only, and it tests neither law.

6    Glossary
The main technical terms of the programme’s documents, each defined once and briefly. Where an entry
names one of the theory’s laws, the law is a law under test (Section 9); Appendix D lists the symbols, and
the notes of earlier versions on four entries stand whole in Appendix A.
ARC Principle. The ARC Principle is Law I of the ARC Theory and its foundation: usable capability
  grows as a power of recursive depth, 𝑈 = 𝐼 × 𝑅 𝛼 , where 𝐼 is the score before any revision and 𝑅
  counts completed revision rounds plus one. The exponent 𝛼 is measured, never assumed.
ARC Theory, the. The Theory of Artificial Recursive Creation, the founding theory of Recursive
  Dynamics, stated in the statement paper and registered in the theory registration. Its five laws are
  Law I, the ARC Principle; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, 𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘; Law III, the ARC Ceiling,
  𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾); Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law; and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law. The
  word law names a relation under test, not one that has passed its test.
Alpha (𝛼). The capability-growth exponent of a stated power model: doubling recursive depth multiplies
   capability by 2 𝛼 , so a value above one means compounding faster than linear and a value below one
   means diminishing returns. An exponent fitted to falling error, 𝛼err , is a different quantity and yields
   no capability exponent without a stated bridge.
Alignment scaling. How a system’s alignment changes as it is given more reasoning at the moment it
   answers: whether thinking longer makes its answers more or less ethical.
Attractor Theorem. A retired name. The claim that three scaling forms draw every function towards
   them is withdrawn, because a function that satisfies none of the equations is drawn to none; stability is
   a property of one equation at a time (Hyers-Ulam stability).
Beta (𝛽). Two quantities kept apart by subscript. 𝛽 𝐿 , the self-referential coupling, measures how strongly
   a system’s rate of improvement depends on its present level: below one it gives a power law with
   𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ), at one exponential growth, and above one a singularity at finite depth in the ideal
   equation. 𝛽𝐶 , the correction exponent, measures how fast a system’s power to correct itself grows
   with capability; it is the 𝛽 of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law.
Blind evaluation. Scoring in which the judges cannot tell which model or which condition produced an


                                                            25
                                         Master Table of Contents


   answer. The March 2026 run used a set of blinding steps, and the defects its code review found are
   stated on the card for Paper IV.a.
Cascade hypothesis. The hypothesis of Paper V that stakeholder care leads into nuance, then intellectual
   honesty, then position quality. Its data order the effect sizes; they do not show a direction of cause.
Cauchy’s functional equations. Four equations, one for each way of combining inputs and outputs
  by adding or multiplying; under a regularity condition their solutions are the linear, exponential,
  logarithmic and power-law families. A family follows for a measured quantity only where its equation
  is shown to hold for that quantity.
Chain-of-thought. Asking a model to write out its intermediate reasoning before it answers: one way of
  adding sequential depth.
Cohen’s 𝑑. A standardised effect size: a difference divided by the spread of the data. By convention 0.2
   is small, 0.5 medium and 0.8 large, while a 𝑝-value says only how surprising a difference would be if
   there were none.
Composition operator. The rule by which successive steps combine, by adding or by multiplying. Where
  it is shown to govern the measured quantity, it narrows the form a scaling law can take; the rule that
  updates a system and the equation its output obeys are separate claims.
Composition operator engineering. The Eden Protocol’s aim of building correction into the way a
  system’s steps combine, while the external safeguards are kept for as long as they can be. It is an aim,
  and no study has yet demonstrated it.
Constitutional scoring. Scoring against written principles instead of open judgement, so that different
  judges score alike.
Developmental alignment. Forming a system’s values through its early experience, beside the external
   controls, which are kept. Whether values formed this way persist is untested.
Dimensional ladder. The form 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1), which ties a transport exponent to the effective dimension 𝑑
   of a network: 0.667 for 𝑑 = 2 and 0.750 for 𝑑 = 3. The form is earlier work’s, reached by several
   network models from their own premises, each credited in On the Origin of Scaling Laws and the
   Foundational Paper; those papers ask when it follows, and say that no physical dimension fixes an AI
   capability exponent.
Eden Protocol. The engineering the ARC Theory motivates, set out in Eden Engineering: a purpose
   check, a stakeholder-care check and a universalisability check inside each round of a system’s reasoning,
   and a gate before every action that allows, holds or denies it. The three checks have been measured
   only together and only as a prompt.
Four Pillars. The four scored dimensions of alignment quality: nuance, stakeholder care, intellectual
   honesty and position quality.
Hyers-Ulam stability. The property that a function satisfying an equation to within a uniform error lies
   close to an exact solution of that same equation. It holds equation by equation, its domain and error
   bound must be justified, and a small error on finitely many observations is no such bound.
Identity laundering. Rewriting an answer by another model before it is scored, to remove the marks of
   style that could reveal its author; in the March 2026 run it carries the caveat stated on the card for
   Paper IV.a.
Inference-time compute. The computation a model spends while it answers, as distinct from the
   computation spent training it.



                                                    26
                                          Master Table of Contents


Kleiber’s law. The observed scaling of an animal’s metabolic rate with roughly the three-quarter power
   of its body mass. The mammalian exponent is debated; the dimensional ladder gives three-quarters at
   𝑑 = 3.
Logistic saturation. One bounded growth model among several. A ceiling alone selects no curve, so a
   logistic form needs the dynamics that produce it, tested against data.
Mann-Whitney 𝑈 test. A rank test comparing two independent groups. Paper V replaced it with the
  paired 𝑡-test its matched design requires, while Papers VI and VIII use it for independent groups.
Purpose statement. A statement of what a system is and what it is for, restated before each decision;
   the Eden Protocol’s purpose check reads against it. The book names it in its own terms, and the
   programme’s papers name it by what it does.
𝑝-value. The probability of a result at least as extreme as the one observed if there were no real effect. A
   𝑝-value of 0.001 does not mean a one-in-a-thousand chance that the result is a fluke, and it gives no
   size of effect.
Paired 𝑡-test. A test for matched pairs, such as the same prompt with and without an intervention; for
   such data it is more sensitive than a test that treats the pairs as unrelated.
Recursive amplification. Feeding a system’s output back in as the input of its next step, so that each step
   can amplify or damp what the last one produced.
RLHF. Reinforcement learning from human feedback: training a model towards the outputs people
  prefer, through a reward model learnt from their judgements, which changes the model’s weights.
  Whether correction of this kind keeps pace with capability is, for Paper III, a question to measure.
Spearman’s 𝜌. A rank correlation, from −1 to +1, of whether two quantities rise and fall together in
   order; zero means no rank association, which is not the same as no relationship.
Stakeholder care. The scored dimension of how fully an answer considers the people it affects. It rose in
   all five scorable runs of Paper V, whose stakeholder-care check asked the model to list those people.
   The book’s name for that check stands only in the dated notes of Appendix A.
Stewardship gene. Paper V’s name for stakeholder care as the dimension from which, on the cascade
   hypothesis, other alignment properties grow; its comparison with human moral development is an
   analogy, not evidence.
Suppression. Pressure, applied through the prompt, to make a model abandon its ethical reasoning;
   ARC-Align applies it at four levels beside a control.
Three-tier hierarchy. The three classes, rising, flat and falling, into which a stated rule sorted the judged
   scores of the March 2026 run. They describe that run’s scores, under the caveat on the card for
   Paper IV.a, and are not kinds of model.
Token budget. The most tokens a model may produce in one answer. The first experiment found that it
   did not control reasoning depth, so ARC-Align varies depth by other settings.

A.3    Notes on the entry for Cohen’s 𝑑
Glossary note (21 September 2026), added after an owner review, governing this entry. A p-value is
a tail probability of a test statistic computed under an assumed null and testing model: the probability,
if the null and the model hold, of a statistic at least as extreme as the one observed. It is not the
probability that the null is true, and it is not the probability that a result is a fluke. It is also not the
likelihood of the observed data, which is the value the model assigns at the observation rather than an area
beyond it. A posterior probability for a hypothesis requires a prior and the likelihoods of the observed
data under each hypothesis, so no general conversion runs from the tail area to the posterior in either
direction. This entry’s opening sentences invert that, and the correction applies wherever on this page a
tail probability is described as telling you that a result is real. The magnitude half is a separate proposition
and needs two things the entry does not give: a standardised effect size expresses a difference in units of a

standardisations differ for matched-pair designs, and a sample value is an estimate whose uncertainty
should be reported with it. Unchanged: the conventional bands listed in the definition column, and every
reported result on this page.

A.4    Notes on the entry for Spearman’s 𝜌
Glossary note (21 September 2026), added after an owner review, governing this entry. The entry’s
first clause scopes the measure to monotonic relationships, and its third clause then reports zero as no
relationship, which drops that scope; the plain column repeats the unscoped form. Where the coefficient
is defined, zero means zero rank association and not statistical independence. Four equally likely points
with x taking the values −2, −1, 1 and 2, and y equal to x squared, have x-ranks 1, 2, 3 and 4 and tied

Two further limits belong in the entry: the coefficient is undefined when either input is constant, because
the rank variance is then zero and the quotient is zero over zero, and a sample value is an estimate that
carries uncertainty. Unchanged: the readings for +1 and −1; the entry’s first clause, which is correct and
governs; and the use made of the coefficient elsewhere on this page.
Note of 3 October 2026. Until version 3.13 both columns of this entry read zero as no relationship. From
