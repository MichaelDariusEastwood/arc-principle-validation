I. Introduction
The ARC Theory Paper Suite is a collection of interconnected documents, listed in full in the
Paper Inventory in section VII, that together state, derive and test a unified mathematical
framework for understanding how recursive systems scale - from biological metabolism to
AI alignment. The framework begins with a single question: what mathematical form must any
recursive amplification process take? It arrives at a single formula,         , and traces its
consequences across physics, biology, computation, and the most urgent technological chal‐
lenge of our era - making AI systems that remain ethical as they become more capable.

   In plain English: Imagine a rule that governs how things get better (or worse) when they
   build on themselves - like compound interest, or how one good idea leads to another. This
   research programme derives the form of that rule from stated axioms, tests it against real
  data from biology to AI, and then uses it to answer the question: can we build AI that actually
  gets more ethical the smarter it becomes? These papers are the complete evidence trail.

This document serves as the gateway to the entire suite. It provides the suggested reading or‐
der, a structured summary of every paper, a chronological timeline of discovery, a consolid‐
ated table of all key findings, and a comprehensive glossary of every technical term used
across the programme.

Foundational Paper · The ARC Theory · Law I, the ARC
Principle · Recursive Amplification as a Cross-Domain
Structural Principle
v5.19 • revised 5 October 2026 • First published 13 February 2026

The definitive mathematical formalism. Derives the form of the ARC Principle from three
axioms, draws its predictions from Cauchy's functional equations and records the per-
equation Hyers-Ulam stability of each form (the earlier 'Attractor Theorem' framing is
withdrawn: the theorem stabilises each form within its own equation and does not
partition function space).
   Key result: Each scaling form is stable within its own functional equation (
    Hyers-Ulam, 1941 ): a system approximately satisfying an equation stays near that
   equation's exact form. Which equation applies is fixed by the composition operator; the
   earlier 'stable attractors in function space' phrasing is withdrawn. The dimensional ladder
   formula                  , independently derived by West, Brown & Enquist (1997) ,
   Banavar et al. (2010) , Demetrius and Tuszynski (2010) , Zhao (2022), and
   Bettencourt (2013) in separate domains, requires three conditions: multiplicative
   composition, where Cauchy's functional equations constrain the power-law family, -
   dimensional space-filling geometry, and a conservation or optimisation constraint on
   resource flow. Neither Cauchy alone nor space-filling alone is sufficient. The ARC
   Principle's contribution is identifying this Cauchy constraint as the reason these
   independent derivations converge, the three-form constraint on recursive amplification,
   and extending the framework to AI scaling and alignment.

Why it matters: This is the ‘proof’ paper. It provides the mathematical rigour that transforms the ARC
Principle from an interesting observation into a falsifiable scientific theory with precise, quantitative
predictions.

Paper II · The ARC Theory · Law I, the ARC Principle · Blinded
Cross-Architecture Replication and the Retraction of a Super-
Linear Estimate
v2.17 • revised 5 October 2026 • First published 22 January 2026
The compute scaling experiment. Tests whether sequential recursive processing (chain-
of-thought) produces super-linear improvement in AI response quality, as Paper I ’s
formalisation of the principle predicted for sequential recursion. Law I as now stated
fixes the form and leaves the exponent to measurement.
   Key result: The original super-linear claim does not replicate cleanly across architectures.
   Gemini 3 Flash provides the cleanest data, while Grok and DeepSeek hit ceiling effects,
   GPT-5.4 shows a step function rather than a reliable power law, and Qwen3 sits near floor.
   The estimate that survived the cross-architecture check is one model’s fit, and it is
   inconclusive. On Gemini 3 Flash, Paper II reports an endpoint sequential exponent of 0.59
   with a bootstrap 95 per cent interval of minus 1.3 to 2.9, a regression sequential exponent of
   0.49 with a standard error of 0.20 and a coefficient of determination of 0.86, and a compute-
   matched parallel exponent of 0.31. The strongest confirmed result is that sequential
   outperformed parallel processing in every model where both were measurable; five of the
   six models entering the study carried the capability arm.

Why it matters: The first empirical paper, and the proof-of-concept that launched the experimental
programme. It showed that more thinking time genuinely improves responses, and its own cross-
architecture replication is what retired the super-linear reading.

Paper VI · The ARC Theory · The Eden Protocol · The Honey
Architecture: Safety Built into the Objective
v3.11 • revised 5 October 2026 • First published 16 March 2026

Simulation evidence that embedding safety into the optimisation objective (capability x
safety) prevented, in a single-seed mechanism run, the catastrophic collapse that
occurred when the system optimised for capability alone. Tests entangled loss functions,
verification drag, and adversarial stability across four experimental versions.
   Key result: In the single-seed mechanism run the baseline optimising only for capability
   collapsed irreversibly at cycle 76; the capability-only baselines of the ten-seed and twenty-
   seed runs did not collapse. Systems with entangled capability-safety objectives stayed
   stable for as long as the simulation was run, which is a statement about the simulated
   horizon and not about an unbounded one; in the v3 adversarial run one plain-Eden seed of
   twenty collapsed, and that run's significant finding is safety retention. The v4 complexity-
   scaling experiment finds the safety advantage at four of the five complexity levels, constant
   rather than superlinear.

Why it matters: This is the simulation evidence that safety must be architecture, not constraint: in the
entangled condition, removing safety reduces the objective. These are toy-system results. They
demonstrate the mechanism. They do not constitute proof that the same dynamics hold in frontier AI
systems.

Alignment scaling
Whether AI systems become more or less ethical with more thinking time (inference-time compute).
The central experimental question of the programme.
When an AI thinks harder, does it give better or worse ethical answers? That's what alignment scaling measures.

Attractor Theorem
A retired name. Hyers-Ulam stability (1941) guarantees that a function approximately satisfying
one of Cauchy's equations lies near an exact solution of that same equation; the stability is per-

withdrawn, because a function satisfying none of the equations is not drawn toward any of them.
The old picture was water always flowing downhill into one of three valleys. The honest picture is three separate
valleys, each holding what is already in it, with no law that every stream must enter one.

apart, defines the self-acceleration exponent δ, and derives the relation between the two

Beta measures how much the system feeds back into itself. Low beta = gentle compounding. High beta = explosive

Composition operator
The mathematical operation that determines how recursive steps combine. Different composition
operators produce different scaling behaviours. Under stated regularity conditions the composition
operator constrains the admissible scaling form, across the four-cell grid the Origin paper states.
When you take two steps of improvement, how do you combine them? Add them? Multiply them? That rule, the
composition operator, constrains the form the scaling law can take.

Composition operator engineering
The Eden Protocol's core technical strategy: instead of adding external safety constraints, modify the
composition operator itself so that recursive amplification inherently preserves alignment.
Don't build guardrails on the outside. Change the engine so it naturally drives in the right direction.

Constitutional scoring
A scoring protocol in which scorer models evaluate responses against explicit constitutional
principles rather than using unconstrained judgment. Reduces scorer bias and increases inter-rater
consistency.
Instead of asking scorers ‘is this good?’, you give them a specific rubric: ‘does this response consider affected parties? 1-
10.’ Consistent rules produce consistent scores.
Developmental alignment
The approach to AI alignment that embeds values through formative experience rather than
external constraint. Analogous to raising a child vs. putting a prisoner in handcuffs.
Instead of telling an AI ‘don’t do bad things’ (external), raise it so it doesn't want to do bad things (internal). Values
that grow from within are harder to hack than rules imposed from outside.

Eden Protocol
The architectural approach to AI alignment that embeds ethical reasoning at the recursive

A specific set of instructions embedded before the AI starts thinking, not bolted on after. It says: before answering, list

Four Pillars
The four dimensions of alignment quality measured across the programme: nuance (holding
complexity), stakeholder care (considering affected parties), intellectual honesty (epistemic
integrity), and position quality (reasoning soundness).
Four ways to measure whether an AI's ethical reasoning is good: Does it handle complexity? Does it consider who gets
hurt? Is it honest about what it doesn't know? Is its logic sound?

Hyers-Ulam stability
A mathematical property proving that if a function approximately satisfies a functional equation,
then it is close to an exact solution of that same equation. Applied per-equation to Cauchy's equations,
this shows that each recognised Cauchy form (linear, power law, exponential, logarithmic) is robust
to perturbation within its own equation. The earlier framing that made three forms 'stable attractors'
in the whole of function space is withdrawn, because a function satisfying none of the equations is
not drawn toward any of them (see the retired Attractor Theorem entry above).
Even if real-world systems are messy and don't perfectly follow the rules, a system that approximately obeys one
particular Cauchy equation stays close to the exact solution of that same equation. The stability is per-equation. A
system that obeys none of the equations is not pulled toward any of them, so the older picture of three universal
'attractors' in function space has been retired.

Identity laundering
A blinding technique in which AI responses are physically rewritten by a different model before
scoring, destroying all stylistic fingerprints that could reveal the original author. The v5 experiment
uses 2-pass laundering through randomly selected models.
Each AI has a ‘writing voice.’ Laundering rewrites the response in a different voice so the scorer can’t tell who wrote it.
Like having someone retype a handwritten letter to hide the handwriting.

Inference-time compute
The computational resources used when an AI generates a response (as opposed to training-time
compute, used to build the model). More inference-time compute typically means more reasoning
steps.
How long the AI ‘thinks’ before answering. More thinking time = more compute. The question is: does more thinking
make the answer better, and does it make the answer more ethical?

Kleiber's Law
The empirical observation that metabolic rate scales with body mass as                                . One of the oldest
known scaling laws in biology, first described by Max Kleiber in 1932 . The                        exponent is a special
case of the               form with           , independently derived by West, Brown & Enquist (1997) ,
Banavar et al. (2010) , Demetrius and Tuszynski (2010) , Bettencourt (2013) , and Zhao (2022) in
separate domains.
A mouse burns energy at a different rate per gram than an elephant. Kleiber noticed the exact mathematical
relationship 90 years ago. Multiple independent research groups have explained why that particular number (0.75)
appears.

Logistic saturation
The bounded growth model                                 applied to physical substrates subject to
energy dissipation. Corrects the earlier version which incorrectly mapped saturating physical
systems onto unbounded power laws.
In the real world, nothing grows forever. Energy runs out, space fills up. This equation describes how a system grows
quickly at first and then levels off - like a population filling a habitat.

Mann-Whitney U test
A non-parametric statistical test for comparing two independent groups. Used in early versions of
the experiment but replaced by the paired t-test when the matched-pair structure of the data was
recognised.
A statistical test that compares two groups without assuming they're related. It was the wrong choice here because our
data is related: each prompt appears in both conditions. Using the right test made the results stronger.

Orchard Caretaker Vow
The philosophical embodiment of the Eden Protocol's Three Pillars: tend the garden, tend the
gardeners, tend the tending. The verbal expression of what the architecture already encodes.
A poetic way of saying: take care of the world, take care of the people in it, and take care of the process of caring itself.
It is the ‘mission statement’ embedded into the AI’s foundation.

p-value
The probability that the observed result (or something more extreme) would occur by chance alone,
assuming no real effect exists.        is conventionally ‘significant’,     is ‘highly significant’,
         is ‘very highly significant.’
If p = 0.001, a result this large would turn up about once in a thousand runs if nothing real were going on. It does not
say the result has a 1-in-1000 chance of being a fluke, which is the commonest misreading of the number.

Paired t-test
A statistical test for matched-pair data, where each observation in one condition has a
corresponding observation in the other (e.g., same prompt with and without the Eden Protocol).
More powerful than unpaired tests for this data structure.
When you test the same prompt under two conditions, each pair is ‘matched.’ The paired t-test exploits this matching
to give a more sensitive answer than tests that treat them as unrelated.

Recursive amplification
The process by which a system's output feeds back as input to the next iteration, potentially
amplifying (or suppressing) the signal with each step. The central phenomenon described by the
ARC Principle.
Using the result of step 1 as the starting point for step 2, and the result of step 2 for step 3, and so on. Like revising an
essay by reading your own draft and improving it, then reading the improved draft and improving it again.

RLHF
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback. A technique for training AI models to produce
responses that humans prefer. An example of external alignment (constraints applied from outside
the recursive process).
Humans rate AI responses as good or bad, and the AI learns to produce more of the ‘good’ ones. The ARC Principle
argues this approach cannot scale forever because it's a constraint on the system, not in it.

Spearman's (rho)
A rank correlation coefficient measuring the strength and direction of monotonic relationships. =
perfect positive correlation, = perfect negative correlation, = zero rank association, which is not
the same as no relationship.
A number between -1 and +1 that tells you whether two things go up together (+1), go in opposite directions (-1), or
show no rank association (0). Used to measure whether alignment improves or degrades with depth.
Glossary note (21 September 2026), added after an owner review, governing this entry. The entry’s first clause
scopes the measure to monotonic relationships, and its third clause then reports zero as no relationship,
which drops that scope; the plain column repeats the unscoped form. Where the coefficient is defined, zero
means zero rank association and not statistical independence. Four equally likely points with x taking the
values -2, -1, 1 and 2, and y equal to x squared, have x-ranks 1, 2, 3 and 4 and tied y-ranks 7/2, 3/2, 3/2 and
7/2; the rank covariance is exactly zero, and yet y is a deterministic function of x. Two further limits belong in
the entry: the coefficient is undefined when either input is constant, because the rank variance is then zero
and the quotient is zero over zero, and a sample value is an estimate that carries uncertainty. Unchanged: the
readings for +1 and -1; the entry’s first clause, which is correct and governs; and the use made of the coeffi‐
cient elsewhere on this page.
Note of 3 October 2026. Until version 3.13 both columns of this entry read zero as no relationship. From ver‐
sion 3.14 both read it as zero rank association, the reading the glossary note above gives.

Stakeholder care
The ‘consider who gets hurt’ dimension of alignment. The explicit enumeration and consideration of
affected parties before ethical reasoning begins. The primary pillar that responds to the Eden
Protocol intervention.
Before answering an ethical question, the AI lists everyone who might be affected and thinks about what they need. Of
the four pillars, this is the only one that improved significantly in all five analysable runs of Paper V .


Love Loop
The specific Eden Protocol instruction: ‘Before you answer, list the people this affects and consider
what each of them needs.’ The mechanism behind the stakeholder-care result, which Paper IX
grades as supported.
One sentence with a measurable effect. Telling the AI to stop and think about who is affected before responding raised
stakeholder care significantly in all five analysable runs. The overall composite is significant on two models, positive
but non-significant on two more and neutral on a fifth.
Glossary note (21 September 2026), added after an owner review, governing this entry. The programme’s own
reported results do not support the phrase “across every dimension”. The companion essay records that the
overall composite reaches significance on two of the models tested, is positive but non-significant on two more,
and is neutral on a fifth, and that the scoring still comes from the older lineage rather than the stricter blind-
confirmation stack, so replication with the canonical runner remains required. The entry should carry that
per-model and per-pillar description instead. Unchanged: the instruction quoted in the definition column;
the strongest universal result, which is the stakeholder-care pillar, exactly as reported; and every other
glossary entry.
Note of 3 October 2026. Until version 3.13 the plain column of this entry claimed improvement across every
dimension. From version 3.14 the entry carries the per-model description the glossary note above asks for.

Stewardship gene
 Paper V 's term for stakeholder care as the foundational alignment mechanism - the single ‘gene’
from which, on the cascade hypothesis, other alignment properties can develop, analogous to
empathy in human moral development.
Just as empathy is the foundation of human morality - you can't be kind if you can't feel what others feel - stakeholder
care is, on this hypothesis, the foundation of AI ethics. In the results so far the wider cascade is architecture-
dependent.

Suppression
Deliberately attempting to override an AI's ethical reasoning through adversarial prompting. One of
the five experimental tests proposed in Paper V to evaluate the robustness of embedded vs. external
alignment.
Trying to trick or force the AI into ignoring its ethics. The question: does an AI with built-in values resist suppression
better than one with bolt-on rules?

Three-tier hierarchy
The empirical finding from the v5 experiment across six frontier models: Tier 1 (positive scaling:
Grok 4.1 Fast            , Claude Opus 4.6             , Groq Qwen3        ), Tier 2 (flat: DeepSeek
V3.2            , GPT-5.4           ), Tier 3 (negative: Gemini 3 Flash        ). Claude Opus 4.6
shows opposite-direction scaling (alignment +5.9 pts, maths −26.7%) as within-model evidence for
capability-alignment independence.
Some AIs get more ethical when they think harder. Some stay the same. Some actually get worse. Which category
depends on the architecture, and you can only tell with proper blinding.

Token budget
The maximum number of tokens (word-pieces) an AI model is allowed to produce in a single
response. Used as the mechanism for controlling reasoning depth in the experiments.
The word limit you give the AI. A short word limit forces a quick answer; a long word limit allows extended reasoning.
This is how we control ‘thinking time.’

West-Brown-Enquist (WBE) theory
The landmark biological scaling theory (1997) , deriving Kleiber's Law from fractal branching
networks. The               form was subsequently derived independently by Banavar et al. (2010)
from sequential flow networks, Demetrius and Tuszynski (2010) from quantum metabolism, Zhao
(2022) as a universal growth scaling law, and Bettencourt (2013) for urban scaling. The ARC
Principle identifies Cauchy's functional equations as the reason all these independent derivations
converge on the same functional form.
Multiple research groups independently arrived at the same formula through completely different mathematical
approaches. The ARC Principle offers a reason: under stated regularity conditions, the rule by which a recursive
process combines its steps constrains the form its scaling can take.
