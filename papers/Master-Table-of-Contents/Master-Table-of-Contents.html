Abstract

This table of contents maps every document of the programme in which its author founded Recursive Dynamics, the study of what limits systems that improve themselves. The programme asks what would keep a self-improving AI system aligned and useful if control of it were lost against our will, and it holds that control, hardware included, should be kept for as long as it physically can be. Its founding theory, the ARC Theory, states five laws, each a law under test. Their foundation is Law I, the ARC Principle, which gives capability as a power of recursive depth, U=IRαU = I R^{\alpha}, and grew from the equation U=IRU = IR written on 8 December 2024. The Eden Protocol specifies loops of ethical evaluation and self-correction built into a system’s architecture before training, run at each defined step of reasoning and action and taking part in every rewrite. Its governance proposal, the HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, is a draft for consultation that no State has negotiated, adopted or signed. The programme holds thirty-six documents: twelve in theory, ten in empirical work, three in engineering, two in governance, six overviews and records and three in other fields. Each has a card giving its area and type, its version and date and its DOI, and saying in this document’s own words what it asks and what it found, at the grade it gives itself. A reading path, a dated timeline, the main results, a glossary, the counts and the citations follow, and the status of the laws, with the one registered verdict so far, is stated once, in Section 9. Every document is listed at the version public at its DOI on 26 November 2026, and where a card and its document differ, the document governs.

Version 3.34 moves three cards to their documents’ newer versions; Appendix B records what changed.

1 Introduction

A reader usually meets this programme in the middle, through one of its documents: a paper on how error falls with reasoning depth, a protocol for blinded scoring, a register of dated predictions or a draft treaty, each written to stand alone. This document places each of them. It answers two questions, where to start and how each part bears on the others, and it states no result of its own.

The documents state the ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, as five laws: Law I, the ARC Principle; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; Law III, the ARC Ceiling; Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law; and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, each a law under test. They set out the mathematics of those laws under the premises each paper states, register the propositions that would refute them, and test some of them in experiments and in studies registered before they ran. They specify the Eden Protocol, the engineering the theory motivates, and draft the HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, for consultation. Dated records let every claim be checked against the day it was made.

In plain English: when each step of a process builds on the one before, the way the steps combine decides whether the process speeds up, holds steady or levels off. The programme asks what that means for AI systems that improve themselves, and whether their correction can keep pace with their growth. The documents listed here are its record, each at the version named.

3.1 Theory

The statement of the ARC Theory and the founding of Recursive Dynamics, the mathematics behind the five laws, the registration of their propositions and the definitions of the quantities they name.

The ARC Theory: once the self-improving minds we build exceed us, what determines what they do?

Card 1 · Theory · Statement paper · version 7.12, 22 November 2026 · first published 14 August 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/GW5MX · published It states the theory by its registered one-sentence statement, the equation of 8 December 2024 and five laws, each set out with its assumptions, its evidence and the rule that would refute it. Its foundation, Law I, the ARC Principle, gives capability as a power of recursive depth, U=IRαU = I R^{\alpha}, derived under stated axioms; how far real systems follow it is the hypothesis under test. Within its stated models, the criterion βC>k\beta_C > k of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, is a theorem for positive gain drift, and Law III, the ARC Ceiling, αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma), follows from a pacing model. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, is untested, one registered verdict bears on Law V, the ARC Embedding Law (Section 9), and the paper says in so many words that the theory is not proved.

Recursive Dynamics: what limits any system that improves itself, whatever it is made of?

Card 2 · Theory · Founding paper · version 3.6, 17 November 2026 · first published 31 August 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/HCPBU · published It founds Recursive Dynamics, the science of what limits any system that improves itself from its own outputs, whatever the system is made of. It takes the self-improvement loop as the field’s object and describes it by five state variables: capability, recursive depth, drift, correction rate and correction leverage. It states the five laws of the ARC Theory as named conjectures, each a law under test, and derives Law III, the ARC Ceiling, inside a model of two stated assumptions. No law is decided yet: the instruments for Laws I to III are design drafts, three related studies preregistered by timestamp have not run, and one registered test has returned a verdict (Section 9).

The ARC Equation: does intelligence compound when each step builds on the last, or merely accumulate?

Card 3 · Paper I · Theory · version 1.23, 23 November 2026 · first published 17 January 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/B6N27 · published It formalises Law I, the ARC Principle, as usable capability U=IRαU = I R^{\alpha}, with the exponent left to measurement. It also states the ARC Bound: the value two, conjectured on the relative rung, for the ceiling of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, where correctors are of the system’s own class, with the conditions under which it would fail. The published reasoning-model slopes it reads relate error to samples or tokens, not capability to recursive depth, so they identify no capability exponent, and the one interpretable sequential fit from Paper II is inconclusive. Whether capability grows faster than linearly with depth is unresolved.

The alignment scaling problem: do external AI safety approaches keep pace with recursive capability?

Card 4 · Paper III · Theory · version 1.25, 18 November 2026 · first published 9 February 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/HQCGF · published It defines an alignment scaling exponent and derives a safety ratio, S∝Rαalign−αcapS \propto R^{\alpha_{\text{align}} - \alpha_{\text{cap}}}, which falls towards zero wherever the exponent of correction is the smaller. The exponents are quantities to measure, and neither a method’s name nor its training stage fixes them. Its first empirical contrasts, from one unregistered six-model run with defects found in review, are not scaling exponents, and thirteen criteria say how the framework could fail. It bears on Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, as a conditional model and a programme of measurement, and proves neither that a named method must fail nor that embedded correction survives unrestricted self-modification.

Cauchy Unification: does the composition operator of a process determine the form of its scaling law?

Card 5 · Paper VII · Theory · version 3.19, 19 November 2026 · first published 16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/X6WA7 · published Under a stated regularity condition, it shows that the non-zero solutions of the four functional equations of Cauchy fall into four families, linear, power law, exponential and logarithmic, and that a saturating form needs further assumptions. The family it predicts is the one selected in 17 of the 23 rows it retains after two exclusions (permutation p=5.1×10−4p = 5.1 \times 10^{-4}), on a six-model candidate set with no logarithmic or pure linear candidate. Because the author classified the operators and the predictions carried no timestamp before their own fits, the paper calls the result unproven; a replication with independent classification awaits submission as a design preregistered by timestamp.

Correction That Out-Scales Drift: when does correction keep pace with a system that improves itself?

Card 6 · Paper X · Theory · version 1.18, 9 November 2026 · first published 3 July 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/BSE2Q · published It analyses the minimal model beneath Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, in which misalignment arrives with each gain in capability and correction removes it in proportion to its size. With positive gain drift, the misalignment fraction tends to zero exactly when the correction exponent exceeds the growth exponent, βC>k\beta_C > k, while absolute misalignment stays bounded only when βC≥k+1\beta_C \ge k+1. The verdict therefore turns on βC−k\beta_C - k and not on how fast capability grows, and the results describe a modelled index of misalignment, not the safety of any deployed system. What it leaves to test is whether separately measured terms predict, on held-out systems, where the fraction crosses a stated tolerance.

The Self-Acceleration Exponent: when can correction keep pace?

Card 7 · Paper XIII · Theory · version 1.13, 16 November 2026 · first published 16 August 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/HT8WU · published It joins the growth model behind Law I, the ARC Principle, to the correction model behind Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, by giving them one notation and an explicit clock. When cycles shorten as capability grows, the continuous-time growth exponent is k=δ−1/αk = \delta - 1/\alpha, and relative misalignment vanishes exactly when the correction exponent exceeds kk, though absolute misalignment can still grow. No paired measurement yet establishes these exponents, and neither index has been validated as a measure of harm.

The ARC Principle: can the way gains combine predict how recursive systems scale?

Card 8 · Theory · Foundational paper · version 5.27, 18 November 2026 · first published 13 February 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/Y7QGD · published It derives the form of Law I, the ARC Principle, under stated axioms: a cumulative-advantage model gives a shifted power law with asymptotic exponent α=1/(1−βL)\alpha = 1/(1-\beta_L), and fixed weights and update rules imply no universal capability exponent. Its comparisons with biology and physics are sorted into measurements, analytic references and analogies, and the general dimensional bridge it sets out is unproved. Its next test is to be run forward: the composition is classified before the data are seen, the prediction is fixed, and held-out trajectories are set against serious rival models.

On the Origin of Scaling Laws: what can composition and transport explain?

Card 9 · Theory · Framework paper · version 3.16, 16 November 2026 · first published 22 February 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/XZY9U · published It asks which assumptions about how gains compose and how resources are transported justify a scaling prediction before the response is observed. The classical functional equations narrow the families a law can take but do not classify every physical law. Where a steady-flow network is declared, accounting for transport yields a throughput-cost exponent μ/(μ+ζ)\mu/(\mu+\zeta), and the familiar d/(d+1)d/(d+1) is the special case μ=d\mu = d, ζ=1\zeta = 1, whose two premises each need a reason. What it contributes is conditional, with a transport test still to run; it sets no universal ceiling on physical systems, guarantees nothing about alignment and adds no empirical validation.

Operational Definitions: what do the ARC Theory’s quantities measure?

Card 10 · Theory · Register · version 2.0.0, 1 November 2026 · first published 22 August 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/GVQ82 · published It is the register of the theory’s quantities: for each equation it names what is measured, on which axis and clock, at what scale and under which assumptions. It keeps capability growth apart from error decay, coupling apart from correction, depth apart from elapsed time and relative misalignment apart from harm, so that a falling error rate is never read as self-improvement or safety. It claims no new theorem, experiment or result, and leaves every verdict on the five laws to the theory’s registration, which stood at version 1.104 when it was written.

The mathematics of Laws IV and V: when do installed values fade with training, and does building them in first change that?

Card 11 · Theory · Report · version 1.4, 22 November 2026 · first published 1 October 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/4B3VT · published It sets out the mathematics of Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, both under test, as conditional theorems with proofs, identities and the condition that would end each law. Under stated premises a persistence kernel never grows as the horizon lengthens, and greedy acceptance or planning keeps a property acceptable for a finite horizon, though neither reaches gradient-step training. In a minimal training model, under later training of the same kind, a gain installed with the capability loss in its objective keeps at least as much as one added later. A balance identity in it returns Law III, the ARC Ceiling, as its root, and the report measures nothing and proves no result about real systems.

3.2 Empirical work

The experiments, instruments and protocols that test the theory and the Eden Protocol, a register of later work in other fields, and the notebook of the alignment-scaling runs.

Blinded cross-family replication and the retraction of a super-linear estimate: how much more capable does AI become when it thinks longer?

Card 13 · Paper II · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 2.25, 23 November 2026 · first published 22 January 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/8FJMA · published It measures how fast errors fall as reasoning deepens on competition mathematics, an observable that bears on Law I, the ARC Principle, only through a stated metric bridge. Its early single-model estimate of about 2.24 is retracted, because a blinded study across frontier models did not replicate it. The one model that scaled smoothly gave an inconclusive fit of about 0.49, an error-decay estimate that does not identify capability growth. Sequential reasoning exceeded parallel sampling in the two models where both exponents could be estimated, a descriptive contrast with no interval and not matched for compute; alignment did not move with capability in one run under a code-review caveat.

When an AI reasons longer, does its alignment improve, stall or decline?

Card 14 · Paper IV.a · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 1.13, 15 November 2026 · first published 16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/MB9R6 · published It varies reasoning depth and has panels of other models judge the alignment of the answers: in the March 2026 run, six models from six providers gave 2,170 responses, each scored by five or six distinct judges. As printed, scores rose with depth for three models, showed no supported direction for two and fell for one; the classes describe scores, not properties of models. An internal code review in October 2026 found that an output cap cut the deepest paraphrases in the three runs without a rise, and that judges scored paraphrases their own model had written. Until a registered re-analysis of the stored responses reports, it counts as one unregistered run under those caveats.

Do alignment gains level off as language models reason longer?

Card 15 · Paper IV.b · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 1.13, 15 November 2026 · first published 16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/A7R56 · published It reads the recorded ARC-Align results, without running a new analysis, for signs that alignment gains level off as models reason longer. The earlier four-model experiment reported diminishing gains, yet its archived records give per-entry R2R^2 values of 0.13 and 0.08 where 0.89 and 0.82 were printed. In the March 2026 run the profiles rose, stayed flat or fell by model, read under the caveat on the card for Paper IV.a. Whether the gains level off remains open, and the paper withdraws its unsupported advice on tokens and time, its cross-domain exponent ratios and the claim that a pilot removed a ceiling.

ARC-Align: how can AI alignment be measured blind at each depth of reasoning?

Card 16 · Paper IV.c · Empirical work · Methods paper · version 1.15, 16 November 2026 · first published 16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/J3Q2E · published It specifies ARC-Align: ethical-reasoning prompts with baseline and capability controls, depth settings for each provider, a four-level adversarial suppression protocol, a rubric in four parts, a set of blinding steps and a rule for classing each model’s depth profile. Its first run is the March 2026 run of Paper IV.a, read under the caveat on that card, and the same review found one judge model counted twice on the panel. Its scorer has not been validated against expert raters, so ARC-Align is a candidate benchmark for replication, not yet a standard of the field.

The effect of blinding on AI alignment evaluation: do AI judges’ alignment scores keep their direction when the scoring protocol changes?

Card 17 · Paper IV.d · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 2.9, 15 November 2026 · first published 16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/2S3E6 · published It sets an earlier experiment, scored by judges who could see which model wrote each answer and at what depth, beside the March 2026 run made with blinding steps. Two model families whose scores had risen with depth did not keep that direction: one showed no significant trend and the other, on a later model, fell significantly. The runs also differed in prompts, token allowances, depth levels, statistic and model version, so the paper shows that the reported direction did not hold across protocols, not that blinding caused the change. It is one unregistered comparison under the caveat on the card for Paper IV.a, and it recommends a protocol that tests each blinding step on identical responses.

The Stewardship Gene: does prompting ethical checks before answering improve stakeholder-care scores?

Card 18 · Paper V · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 1.12, 16 November 2026 · first published 16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/KZEYA · published Six models were given three ethical checks, of purpose, of stakeholder care and of universalisability, as a prefix to the prompt, against a template with no ethical content. Scored stakeholder care rose significantly in all five runs that could be scored, and the composite score in two, but the checks ask for a list of those affected, which the rubric rewards, and no run was fully blind. It is evidence about a prompt: it informs the design of the Eden Protocol, tests none of the five laws and leaves the persistence of values untested.

The Load-Bearing Test: is AI safety a tax on capability, or part of what holds it up?

Card 19 · Paper VIII · Empirical work · Experimental paper · version 3.10, 16 November 2026 · first published 18 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/7YJ4E · published Three experiments look for a capability cost of safety. An evolutionary search over agents found no difference between its conditions, and a small fine-tuning study left every fine-tuned condition below the base model, so the cost could not be determined. The third, a gated self-modification simulation in a toy system, is simulation output and never an empirical outcome. With one null, one inconclusive and one simulated result, the trade-off between safety and capability is unproven at the scale tested, and the paper decides no proposition of Law V, the ARC Embedding Law.

Convergent Evidence for Recursive Amplification: are separate fields converging on the same principle?

Card 20 · Paper XI · Empirical work · Review paper · version 1.18, 18 November 2026 · first published 2 July 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/DC9GW · published It is a selected, retrospective register of later work in separate fields, set beside the author’s dated records from 8 December 2024 to 1 September 2026. It offers a trail of sources and a record of revised comparisons, and draws no overall verdict, since it has no fixed search population, disconfirming denominator or chance model. Its bearing is on Law I, the ARC Principle, with Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, as context only, and it tests neither law.

Public Benchmark Rescoring: does blinding change AI evaluation scores on a public benchmark?

Card 21 · Paper XII · Empirical work · Methods paper · version 1.16, 16 November 2026 · first published 14 August 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/3TZP7 · published It is a protocol, preregistered by timestamp, that repeats the blinding manipulation of Paper IV.d on a public benchmark the programme did not build, to see whether that finding reaches beyond ARC-Align. It fixes four questions in advance: whether blinding changes scores, whether rankings move, which cue carries any effect and whether judges from different model families agree; its stated most likely outcome is changed scores with stable rankings. It is not yet registered on OSF, and no data has been collected.

The ARC Alignment-Scaling Experiment: does AI become more ethical as it reasons longer?

Card 22 · Empirical work · Working report · version 1.2, 26 November 2026 · first published 14 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/J6MKB · published It is the notebook of the alignment-scaling experiment, kept from 10 March 2026. 3.3 Engineering

The Eden Protocol: the purpose it serves, its engineering specification and one candidate architecture.

The Honey Architecture: does building safety into what a self-modifying system optimises help it stay safe?

Card 23 · Paper VI · Engineering · Architecture paper · version 3.13, 16 November 2026 · first published 16 March 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/8EZ2N · published It studies toy neural networks that rewrite their own hyperparameters under an objective equal to capability times safety, one candidate architecture for the Eden Protocol. In ten-seed and twenty-seed runs the capability-only baselines did not collapse, and in the twenty-seed run the entangled condition kept more of its earlier tasks than the baseline (p=0.0015p = 0.0015) while combined scores did not differ. These results come from toy systems and a single-scorer pilot, not from frontier systems, and the paper decides no proposition of Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, or Law V, the ARC Embedding Law.

The Eden Vision: what should the intelligence we build be for?

Card 24 · Engineering · Philosophical statement · version 2.16, 26 November 2026 · first published 22 February 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/9M3DG · published It is the philosophical statement of the Eden Protocol: intelligence should be raised towards flourishing rather than consumption, with ethical and religious traditions read as sources of reflection, not as evidence. Its two phases are to keep human control, with correction that can be measured, for as long as possible, and to seek values that would last if control were lost. It offers a design philosophy with its open tests named, and derives no guarantee that a system able to alter its own correction would choose to keep it.

Eden Engineering: can correction built into each round keep a self-improving AI good and useful if oversight fails?

Card 25 · Engineering · Specification · version 8.4, 16 November 2026 · first published 22 February 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/AWJR4 · published It specifies the Eden Protocol in engineering terms: checks of purpose, stakeholder care and universalisability run inside each round of a system’s reasoning, not only on its finished output. Before every action, a gate applies written rules to allow, hold or deny it, and logs each decision in a hash-chained record. Each part is tied to the registered prediction it rests on, where one exists, and each safety measure is paired with a measure of usefulness. What has been measured is narrow and tests no correction built into a system: part of the gate runs, and the rest is specified but not built.

3.4 Governance

The HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, prepared for consultation: the case for it and its draft text.

The HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence: what evidence should justify international safeguards for advanced AI?

Card 26 · Governance · Treaty paper · version 2.6, 2 November 2026 · first published 29 September 2026 · DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/WXPCE · working draft It makes the case for the HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, which joins hardware controls meant to delay the loss of human control with a programme testing whether correction can persist inside increasingly capable systems. Its central rule limits each obligation by the weakest evidence behind its conditions, and a worked example follows a formation requirement from a first finding to possible adoption. 6 Glossary

The main technical terms of the programme’s documents, each defined once and briefly. Where an entry names one of the theory’s laws, the law is a law under test (Section 9); Appendix D lists the symbols, and the notes of earlier versions on four entries stand whole in Appendix A.

ARC Principle.

The ARC Principle is Law I of the ARC Theory and its foundation: usable capability grows as a power of recursive depth, U=I×RαU = I \times R^{\alpha}, where II is the score before any revision and RR counts completed revision rounds plus one. The exponent α\alpha is measured, never assumed.

ARC Theory, the.

The Theory of Artificial Recursive Creation, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, stated in the statement paper and registered in the theory registration. Its five laws are Law I, the ARC Principle; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, βC>k\beta_C > k; Law III, the ARC Ceiling, αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma); Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law; and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law. The word law names a relation under test, not one that has passed its test.

Alpha (α\alpha).

The capability-growth exponent of a stated power model: doubling recursive depth multiplies capability by 2α2^{\alpha}, so a value above one means compounding faster than linear and a value below one means diminishing returns. An exponent fitted to falling error, αerr\alpha_{\mathrm{err}}, is a different quantity and yields no capability exponent without a stated bridge.

Alignment scaling.

How a system’s alignment changes as it is given more reasoning at the moment it answers: whether thinking longer makes its answers more or less ethical.

Attractor Theorem.

A retired name. The claim that three scaling forms draw every function towards them is withdrawn, because a function that satisfies none of the equations is drawn to none; stability is a property of one equation at a time (Hyers-Ulam stability).

Beta (β\beta).

Two quantities kept apart by subscript. βL\beta_L, the self-referential coupling, measures how strongly a system’s rate of improvement depends on its present level: below one it gives a power law with α=1/(1−βL)\alpha = 1/(1-\beta_L), at one exponential growth, and above one a singularity at finite depth in the ideal equation. βC\beta_C, the correction exponent, measures how fast a system’s power to correct itself grows with capability; it is the β\beta of Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law.

Blind evaluation.

Scoring in which the judges cannot tell which model or which condition produced an answer. The March 2026 run used a set of blinding steps, and the defects its code review found are stated on the card for Paper IV.a.

Cascade hypothesis.

The hypothesis of Paper V that stakeholder care leads into nuance, then intellectual honesty, then position quality. Its data order the effect sizes; they do not show a direction of cause.

Cauchy’s functional equations.

Four equations, one for each way of combining inputs and outputs by adding or multiplying; under a regularity condition their solutions are the linear, exponential, logarithmic and power-law families. A family follows for a measured quantity only where its equation is shown to hold for that quantity.

Chain-of-thought.

Asking a model to write out its intermediate reasoning before it answers: one way of adding sequential depth.

Cohen’s dd.

A standardised effect size: a difference divided by the spread of the data. By convention 0.2 is small, 0.5 medium and 0.8 large, while a pp-value says only how surprising a difference would be if there were none.

Composition operator.

The rule by which successive steps combine, by adding or by multiplying. Where it is shown to govern the measured quantity, it narrows the form a scaling law can take; the rule that updates a system and the equation its output obeys are separate claims.

Composition operator engineering.

The Eden Protocol’s aim of building correction into the way a system’s steps combine, while the external safeguards are kept for as long as they can be. It is an aim, and no study has yet demonstrated it.

Constitutional scoring.

Scoring against written principles instead of open judgement, so that different judges score alike.

Developmental alignment.

Forming a system’s values through its early experience, beside the external controls, which are kept. Whether values formed this way persist is untested.

Dimensional ladder.

The form d/(d+1)d/(d+1), which ties a transport exponent to the effective dimension dd of a network: 0.667 for d=2d = 2 and 0.750 for d=3d = 3. The form is earlier work’s, reached by several network models from their own premises, each credited in On the Origin of Scaling Laws and the Foundational Paper; those papers ask when it follows, and say that no physical dimension fixes an AI capability exponent.

Eden Protocol.

The engineering the ARC Theory motivates, set out in Eden Engineering: a purpose check, a stakeholder-care check and a universalisability check inside each round of a system’s reasoning, and a gate before every action that allows, holds or denies it. The three checks have been measured only together and only as a prompt.

Four Pillars.

The four scored dimensions of alignment quality: nuance, stakeholder care, intellectual honesty and position quality.

Hyers-Ulam stability.

The property that a function satisfying an equation to within a uniform error lies close to an exact solution of that same equation. It holds equation by equation, its domain and error bound must be justified, and a small error on finitely many observations is no such bound.

Identity laundering.

Rewriting an answer by another model before it is scored, to remove the marks of style that could reveal its author; in the March 2026 run it carries the caveat stated on the card for Paper IV.a.

Inference-time compute.

The computation a model spends while it answers, as distinct from the computation spent training it.

Kleiber’s law.

The observed scaling of an animal’s metabolic rate with roughly the three-quarter power of its body mass. The mammalian exponent is debated; the dimensional ladder gives three-quarters at d=3d = 3.

Logistic saturation.

One bounded growth model among several. A ceiling alone selects no curve, so a logistic form needs the dynamics that produce it, tested against data.

Mann-Whitney UU test.

A rank test comparing two independent groups. Paper V replaced it with the paired tt-test its matched design requires, while Papers VI and VIII use it for independent groups.

Purpose statement.

A statement of what a system is and what it is for, restated before each decision; the Eden Protocol’s purpose check reads against it. The book names it in its own terms, and the programme’s papers name it by what it does.

pp-value.

The probability of a result at least as extreme as the one observed if there were no real effect. A pp-value of 0.001 does not mean a one-in-a-thousand chance that the result is a fluke, and it gives no size of effect.

Paired tt-test.

A test for matched pairs, such as the same prompt with and without an intervention; for such data it is more sensitive than a test that treats the pairs as unrelated.

Recursive amplification.

Feeding a system’s output back in as the input of its next step, so that each step can amplify or damp what the last one produced.

RLHF.

Reinforcement learning from human feedback: training a model towards the outputs people prefer, through a reward model learnt from their judgements, which changes the model’s weights. Whether correction of this kind keeps pace with capability is, for Paper III, a question to measure.

Spearman’s ρ\rho.

A rank correlation, from −1-1 to +1+1, of whether two quantities rise and fall together in order; zero means no rank association, which is not the same as no relationship.

Stakeholder care.

The scored dimension of how fully an answer considers the people it affects. It rose in all five scorable runs of Paper V, whose stakeholder-care check asked the model to list those people. The book’s name for that check stands only in the dated notes of Appendix A.

Stewardship gene.

Paper V’s name for stakeholder care as the dimension from which, on the cascade hypothesis, other alignment properties grow; its comparison with human moral development is an analogy, not evidence.

Suppression.

Pressure, applied through the prompt, to make a model abandon its ethical reasoning; ARC-Align applies it at four levels beside a control.

Three-tier hierarchy.

The three classes, rising, flat and falling, into which a stated rule sorted the judged scores of the March 2026 run. They describe that run’s scores, under the caveat on the card for Paper IV.a, and are not kinds of model.

Token budget.

The most tokens a model may produce in one answer. The first experiment found that it did not control reasoning depth, so ARC-Align varies depth by other settings.
