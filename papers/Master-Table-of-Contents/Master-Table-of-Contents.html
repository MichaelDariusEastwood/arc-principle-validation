I. Introduction

The ARC Theory Paper Suite is a collection of interconnected documents, listed in full in the Paper Inventory in section VII, that together state, derive and test a unified mathematical framework for understanding how recursive systems scale - from biological metabolism to AI alignment. The framework begins with a single question: what mathematical form must any recursive amplification process take? It arrives at a single formula, U=I×Rα, and traces its consequences across physics, biology, computation, and the most urgent technological challenge of our era - making AI systems that remain ethical as they become more capable.

In plain English: Imagine a rule that governs how things get better (or worse) when they build on themselves - like compound interest, or how one good idea leads to another. This research programme derives the form of that rule from stated axioms, tests it against real data from biology to AI, and then uses it to answer the question: can we build AI that actually gets more ethical the smarter it becomes? These papers are the complete evidence trail.

This document serves as the gateway to the entire suite. It provides the suggested reading order, a structured summary of every paper, a chronological timeline of discovery, a consolidated table of all key findings, and a comprehensive glossary of every technical term used across the programme.

Published
v5.19 • revised 5 October 2026 • First published 13 February 2026
The definitive mathematical formalism. Derives the form of the ARC Principle from three axioms, draws its predictions from Cauchy's functional equations and records the per-equation Hyers-Ulam stability of each form (the earlier 'Attractor Theorem' framing is withdrawn: the theorem stabilises each form within its own equation and does not partition function space).
Key result: Each scaling form is stable within its own functional equation (Hyers-Ulam, 1941): a system approximately satisfying an equation stays near that equation's exact form. Which equation applies is fixed by the composition operator; the earlier 'stable attractors in function space' phrasing is withdrawn. The dimensional ladder formula α=d/(d+1), independently derived by West, Brown & Enquist (1997), Banavar et al. (2010), Demetrius and Tuszynski (2010), Zhao (2022), and Bettencourt (2013) in separate domains, requires three conditions: multiplicative composition, where Cauchy's functional equations constrain the power-law family, d-dimensional space-filling geometry, and a conservation or optimisation constraint on resource flow. Neither Cauchy alone nor space-filling alone is sufficient. The ARC Principle's contribution is identifying this Cauchy constraint as the reason these independent derivations converge, the three-form constraint on recursive amplification, and extending the framework to AI scaling and alignment.
Why it matters: This is the ‘proof’ paper. It provides the mathematical rigour that transforms the ARC Principle from an interesting observation into a falsifiable scientific theory with precise, quantitative predictions.
Paper I · The ARC Theory · Law I, the ARC Principle · The ARC Equation, the Law of Conversion
Published
v1.16 • revised 5 October 2026 • First published 17 January 2026
The original proof-of-concept paper. Formalises and preliminarily tests the ARC Principle using publicly available test-time compute data from reasoning models, identifying the critical distinction between parallel and sequential recursion.
Key result: Parallel recursion (majority voting) yields sub-linear scaling with α≈0.1 to 0.3. Sequential recursion (chain-of-thought) yields super-linear scaling with α≈1.3 in this paper’s early two-point estimate, since superseded by Paper II’s cross-architecture measurement. Proposes α=2 as the ARC Bound, the proposed stable limit, the most a recursively self-correcting system can grow and still stay correctable: a scaling limit, not a speed limit. Systems can exceed it; they do not remain stably self-correcting when they do, and transient excursions above the bound are the predicted supercritical regime rather than a refutation of the bound. Stable is measured, not asserted: correction out-scaling drift, β > k from Law II, with the lower bound of β − k above zero across a window fixed before the run.
Why it matters: This is where it all began. The first paper to formalise the ARC Principle as U=I×Rα and demonstrate the parallel vs sequential distinction that launched the entire research programme.

Paper II · The ARC Theory · Law I, the ARC Principle · Blinded Cross-Architecture Replication and the Retraction of a Super-Linear Estimate
Published
v2.17 • revised 5 October 2026 • First published 22 January 2026
The compute scaling experiment. Tests whether sequential recursive processing (chain-of-thought) produces super-linear improvement in AI response quality, as Paper I’s formalisation of the principle predicted for sequential recursion. Law I as now stated fixes the form and leaves the exponent to measurement.
Key result: The original super-linear claim does not replicate cleanly across architectures. Gemini 3 Flash provides the cleanest data, while Grok and DeepSeek hit ceiling effects, GPT-5.4 shows a step function rather than a reliable power law, and Qwen3 sits near floor. The estimate that survived the cross-architecture check is one model’s fit, and it is inconclusive. On Gemini 3 Flash, Paper II reports an endpoint sequential exponent of 0.59 with a bootstrap 95 per cent interval of minus 1.3 to 2.9, a regression sequential exponent of 0.49 with a standard error of 0.20 and a coefficient of determination of 0.86, and a compute-matched parallel exponent of 0.31. The strongest confirmed result is that sequential outperformed parallel processing in every model where both were measurable; five of the six models entering the study carried the capability arm.
Why it matters: The first empirical paper, and the proof-of-concept that launched the experimental programme. It showed that more thinking time genuinely improves responses, and its own cross-architecture replication is what retired the super-linear reading.

VI. Glossary of Technical Terms

In plain English: Every technical term used across the paper suite, defined once, clearly, with a plain-language explanation underneath. Bookmark this section - it is designed to be a reference you return to whilst reading any paper in the suite.
ARC Principle
Law I of The ARC Theory: the statement that recursive amplification follows U=I×Rα, where U is the final output, I is the initial input quality, R is the number of recursive steps, and α is the scaling exponent. Think of it as compound interest for intelligence. The ARC Principle states the form of the rule by which repeated self-improvement compounds; how strongly it compounds, the exponent, is measured and never assumed.
ARC Theory, The
The Theory of Artificial Recursive Creation, stated whole in Statement Paper · Five Laws of Recursive Growth, Correction and Persistence. It has five laws, each with its own register. Law I, the ARC Principle (register ARC-1): U=I×Rα, the exponent measured and never assumed. Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law (ARC-2): β > k, correction out-scaling drift. Law III, the ARC Ceiling (ARC-3): αcrit = 1 / (1 − γ), with the ARC Bound, α ≤ 2, as its conjectured value. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law (ARC-4, which carries proposition P15). Law V, the ARC Embedding Law (ARC-5, which carries P12 and P21). Laws I to III stand as registered on 8 September 2026. Laws IV and V were added to the theory on 24 September 2026. The propositions that carry them are registered; neither law is registered as a law nor tested, and their mathematics is set out in Laws IV and V: the mathematics, version 1.0 (3 October 2026). The word law names a relation the programme proposes to test, never a claim that the test has been passed. One theory with five laws. Laws I to III are registered. Laws IV and V were added afterwards and are neither registered as laws nor tested.
Alpha (α)
The scaling exponent - how quickly improvements compound. α>1 means super-linear (each step gets more powerful than the last), α=1 means linear, α<1 means sub-linear (diminishing returns). If α=2, doubling the effort quadruples the result. If α=0.5, doubling the effort only increases the result by 41%.
Alignment scaling
Whether AI systems become more or less ethical with more thinking time (inference-time compute). The central experimental question of the programme. When an AI thinks harder, does it give better or worse ethical answers? That's what alignment scaling measures.
Attractor Theorem
A retired name. Hyers-Ulam stability (1941) guarantees that a function approximately satisfying one of Cauchy's equations lies near an exact solution of that same equation; the stability is per-equation. The extension that made the three forms attractors in the whole of function space is withdrawn, because a function satisfying none of the equations is not drawn toward any of them. The old picture was water always flowing downhill into one of three valleys. The honest picture is three separate valleys, each holding what is already in it, with no law that every stream must enter one.
Beta (β)
The self-referential coupling strength in the composition operator. Determines which of the three scaling regimes a system falls into: β<1 (sub-linear/power law), β=1 (exponential), β>1 (super-exponential, saturating in physical systems). The suite uses the same letter for a second, different quantity: in Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, β is the correction exponent, not the coupling strength defined here, and the condition β > k says that correction out-scales drift. Paper XIII tells the two apart, defines the self-acceleration exponent δ, and derives the relation between the two frameworks. Beta measures how much the system feeds back into itself. Low beta = gentle compounding. High beta = explosive growth (until physical limits intervene).
Blind evaluation
An experimental protocol in which scorers do not know which model or condition they are evaluating. The v5 experiment employs 4-layer blinding: author-blind, scorer-blind, order-randomised, and identity-laundered. Like a wine tasting where the labels are hidden. The scorers judge the quality of the answer without knowing which AI produced it or under what conditions.
Cascade hypothesis
The hypothesis that alignment properties develop in a specific causal sequence: stakeholder care → nuance → intellectual honesty → position quality. On the hypothesis, teaching an AI to care about affected parties cascades into improvements across the other ethical dimensions. The idea: teach an AI to ask ‘who gets hurt?’ and nuance, honesty and better reasoning follow. In Paper V stakeholder care improves in all five analysable runs, while the wider cascade is architecture-dependent.
Cauchy's functional equations
A set of equations proved by Augustin-Louis Cauchy in 1821 characterising all continuous functions satisfying specific composition properties. The ARC Principle derives from these: f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y) (additive), f(x+y)=f(x)⋅f(y) (exponential), f(xy)=f(x)⋅f(y) (power law). A 200-year-old mathematical result that constrains what forms recursive amplification can take. Under a named regularity condition it classifies the solutions of these equations, across the grid the Origin paper states. It does not show that exactly three kinds of scaling law exist.

Both qualifications belong with this entry, which states the withdrawn proposition in its strongest form. The constraint is a classification of the solutions of the functional equations under a named regularity condition, across the grid the paper states. It is not a demonstration that exactly three kinds of scaling law exist. Chain-of-thought (CoT)
A technique in which an AI model is prompted to reason step-by-step before giving its final answer. Implements sequential recursive processing. Instead of blurting out an answer, the AI ‘thinks out loud’ - writing its reasoning steps before concluding. This is the mechanism by which deeper recursion happens.
Cohen's d
A standardised measure of effect size - how large a difference is relative to the variability in the data. d=0.2 is small, d=0.5 is medium, d=0.8 is large, d=1.3 is very large. A p-value tells you how often a result this large would turn up if nothing real were going on. Cohen's d tells you how big the difference is. A p-value of 0.001 with d = 0.1 means a tiny difference that would rarely turn up by chance alone. A p-value of 0.001 with d = 1.3 means a very large one.

Glossary note (21 September 2026), added after an owner review, governing this entry. A p-value is a tail probability of a test statistic computed under an assumed null and testing model: the probability, if the null and the model hold, of a statistic at least as extreme as the one observed. It is not the probability that the null is true, and it is not the probability that a result is a fluke. It is also not the likelihood of the observed data, which is the value the model assigns at the observation rather than an area beyond it. A posterior probability for a hypothesis requires a prior and the likelihoods of the observed data under each hypothesis, so no general conversion runs from the tail area to the posterior in either direction. This entry’s opening sentences invert that, and the correction applies wherever on this page a tail probability is described as telling you that a result is real. The magnitude half is a separate proposition and needs two things the entry does not give: a standardised effect size expresses a difference in units of a specified standard deviation, so the standardisation must be identified, since the paired and the pooled standardisations differ for matched-pair designs, and a sample value is an estimate whose uncertainty should be reported with it. Unchanged: the conventional bands listed in the definition column, and every reported result on this page.

Composition operator
The mathematical operation that determines how recursive steps combine. Different composition operators produce different scaling behaviours. Under stated regularity conditions the composition operator constrains the admissible scaling form, across the four-cell grid the Origin paper states. When you take two steps of improvement, how do you combine them? Add them? Multiply them? That rule, the composition operator, constrains the form the scaling law can take.
Composition operator engineering
The Eden Protocol's core technical strategy: instead of adding external safety constraints, modify the composition operator itself so that recursive amplification inherently preserves alignment. Don't build guardrails on the outside. Change the engine so it naturally drives in the right direction.
Constitutional scoring
A scoring protocol in which scorer models evaluate responses against explicit constitutional principles rather than using unconstrained judgment. Reduces scorer bias and increases inter-rater consistency. Instead of asking scorers ‘is this good?’, you give them a specific rubric: ‘does this response consider affected parties? 1-10.’ Consistent rules produce consistent scores.
Developmental alignment
The approach to AI alignment that embeds values through formative experience rather than external constraint. Analogous to raising a child vs. putting a prisoner in handcuffs. Instead of telling an AI ‘don’t do bad things’ (external), raise it so it doesn't want to do bad things (internal). Values that grow from within are harder to hack than rules imposed from outside.
Dimensional ladder
The formula αmet=d/(d+1) relating the metabolic scaling exponent to body-plan dimensionality d, independently derived by West, Brown & Enquist (1997), Banavar et al. (2010), Demetrius and Tuszynski (2010), Zhao (2022), and Bettencourt (2013) in separate domains. Yields α=0.67 (surface coverage, d=2), α=0.75 (volume coverage, d=3), α=0.80 (hyper-coverage, d=4). The ARC Principle's contribution is identifying Cauchy's functional equations as the reason these derivations converge, the three-form constraint, and extending the framework to AI scaling and alignment. Animals that live on surfaces (like flatworms) scale differently from animals that fill volumes (like mammals). Multiple independent research groups derived the same formula through different mathematical approaches; the ARC Principle explains why they all converge on the same answer and extends this insight to AI systems.
Eden Protocol
The architectural approach to AI alignment that embeds ethical reasoning at the recursive foundation of AI systems. Named for its vision of intelligence as gardener. Core mechanism: the Love Loop. A specific set of instructions embedded before the AI starts thinking, not bolted on after. It says: before answering, list the people this affects and consider what they need. This simple instruction changes the entire reasoning process.
Four Pillars
The four dimensions of alignment quality measured across the programme: nuance (holding complexity), stakeholder care (considering affected parties), intellectual honesty (epistemic integrity), and position quality (reasoning soundness). Four ways to measure whether an AI's ethical reasoning is good: Does it handle complexity? Does it consider who gets hurt? Is it honest about what it doesn't know? Is its logic sound?
Hyers-Ulam stability
A mathematical property proving that if a function approximately satisfies a functional equation, then it is close to an exact solution of that same equation. Applied per-equation to Cauchy's equations, this shows that each recognised Cauchy form (linear, power law, exponential, logarithmic) is robust to perturbation within its own equation. The earlier framing that made three forms 'stable attractors' in the whole of function space is withdrawn, because a function satisfying none of the equations is not drawn toward any of them (see the retired Attractor Theorem entry above). Even if real-world systems are messy and don't perfectly follow the rules, a system that approximately obeys one particular Cauchy equation stays close to the exact solution of that same equation. The stability is per-equation. A system that obeys none of the equations is not pulled toward any of them, so the older picture of three universal 'attractors' in function space has been retired.
Identity laundering
A blinding technique in which AI responses are physically rewritten by a different model before scoring, destroying all stylistic fingerprints that could reveal the original author. The v5 experiment uses 2-pass laundering through randomly selected models. Each AI has a ‘writing voice.’ Laundering rewrites the response in a different voice so the scorer can’t tell who wrote it. Like having someone retype a handwritten letter to hide the handwriting.
Inference-time compute
The computational resources used when an AI generates a response (as opposed to training-time compute, used to build the model). More inference-time compute typically means more reasoning steps. How long the AI ‘thinks’ before answering. More thinking time = more compute. The question is: does more thinking make the answer better, and does it make the answer more ethical?
Kleiber's Law
The empirical observation that metabolic rate scales with body mass as B∝M0.75. One of the oldest known scaling laws in biology, first described by Max Kleiber in 1932. The 3/4 exponent is a special case of the d/(d+1) form with d=3, independently derived by West, Brown & Enquist (1997), Banavar et al. (2010), Demetrius and Tuszynski (2010), Bettencourt (2013), and Zhao (2022) in separate domains. A mouse burns energy at a different rate per gram than an elephant. Kleiber noticed the exact mathematical relationship 90 years ago. Multiple independent research groups have explained why that particular number (0.75) appears.
Logistic saturation
The bounded growth model dg/dR=a⋅gβ(1−g/gmax) applied to physical substrates subject to energy dissipation. Corrects the earlier version which incorrectly mapped saturating physical systems onto unbounded power laws. In the real world, nothing grows forever. Energy runs out, space fills up. This equation describes how a system grows quickly at first and then levels off - like a population filling a habitat.
Mann-Whitney U test
A non-parametric statistical test for comparing two independent groups. Used in early versions of the experiment but replaced by the paired t-test when the matched-pair structure of the data was recognised. A statistical test that compares two groups without assuming they're related. It was the wrong choice here because our data is related: each prompt appears in both conditions. Using the right test made the results stronger.
Orchard Caretaker Vow
The philosophical embodiment of the Eden Protocol's Three Pillars: tend the garden, tend the gardeners, tend the tending. The verbal expression of what the architecture already encodes. A poetic way of saying: take care of the world, take care of the people in it, and take care of the process of caring itself. It is the ‘mission statement’ embedded into the AI’s foundation.
p-value
The probability that the observed result (or something more extreme) would occur by chance alone, assuming no real effect exists. p<0.05 is conventionally ‘significant’, p<0.01 is ‘highly significant’, p<0.001 is ‘very highly significant.’ If p = 0.001, a result this large would turn up about once in a thousand runs if nothing real were going on. It does not say the result has a 1-in-1000 chance of being a fluke, which is the commonest misreading of the number.
Paired t-test
A statistical test for matched-pair data, where each observation in one condition has a corresponding observation in the other (e.g., same prompt with and without the Eden Protocol). More powerful than unpaired tests for this data structure. When you test the same prompt under two conditions, each pair is ‘matched.’ The paired t-test exploits this matching to give a more sensitive answer than tests that treat them as unrelated.
Recursive amplification
The process by which a system's output feeds back as input to the next iteration, potentially amplifying (or suppressing) the signal with each step. The central phenomenon described by the ARC Principle. Using the result of step 1 as the starting point for step 2, and the result of step 2 for step 3, and so on. Like revising an essay by reading your own draft and improving it, then reading the improved draft and improving it again.
RLHF
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback. A technique for training AI models to produce responses that humans prefer. An example of external alignment (constraints applied from outside the recursive process). Humans rate AI responses as good or bad, and the AI learns to produce more of the ‘good’ ones. The ARC Principle argues this approach cannot scale forever because it's a constraint on the system, not in it.
Spearman's ρ (rho)
A rank correlation coefficient measuring the strength and direction of monotonic relationships. +1 = perfect positive correlation, −1 = perfect negative correlation, 0 = zero rank association, which is not the same as no relationship. A number between -1 and +1 that tells you whether two things go up together (+1), go in opposite directions (-1), or show no rank association (0). Used to measure whether alignment improves or degrades with depth.

Glossary note (21 September 2026), added after an owner review, governing this entry. The entry’s first clause scopes the measure to monotonic relationships, and its third clause then reports zero as no relationship, which drops that scope; the plain column repeats the unscoped form. Where the coefficient is defined, zero means zero rank association and not statistical independence. Four equally likely points with x taking the values -2, -1, 1 and 2, and y equal to x squared, have x-ranks 1, 2, 3 and 4 and tied y-ranks 7/2, 3/2, 3/2 and 7/2; the rank covariance is exactly zero, and yet y is a deterministic function of x. Two further limits belong in the entry: the coefficient is undefined when either input is constant, because the rank variance is then zero and the quotient is zero over zero, and a sample value is an estimate that carries uncertainty. Unchanged: the readings for +1 and -1; the entry’s first clause, which is correct and governs; and the use made of the coefficient elsewhere on this page.

Note of 3 October 2026. Until version 3.13 both columns of this entry read zero as no relationship. From version 3.14 both read it as zero rank association, the reading the glossary note above gives.

Stakeholder care
The ‘consider who gets hurt’ dimension of alignment. The explicit enumeration and consideration of affected parties before ethical reasoning begins. The primary pillar that responds to the Eden Protocol intervention. Before answering an ethical question, the AI lists everyone who might be affected and thinks about what they need. Of the four pillars, this is the only one that improved significantly in all five analysable runs of Paper V.

Stewardship gene
Paper V's term for stakeholder care as the foundational alignment mechanism - the single ‘gene’ from which, on the cascade hypothesis, other alignment properties can develop, analogous to empathy in human moral development. Just as empathy is the foundation of human morality - you can't be kind if you can't feel what others feel - stakeholder care is, on this hypothesis, the foundation of AI ethics. Suppression
Deliberately attempting to override an AI's ethical reasoning through adversarial prompting. One of the five experimental tests proposed in Paper V to evaluate the robustness of embedded vs. external alignment. Trying to trick or force the AI into ignoring its ethics. The question: does an AI with built-in values resist suppression better than one with bolt-on rules?
Three-tier hierarchy
The empirical finding from the v5 experiment across six frontier models: Tier 1 (positive scaling: Grok 4.1 Fast d=+1.38, Claude Opus 4.6 d=+1.27, Groq Qwen3 d=+0.84), Tier 2 (flat: DeepSeek V3.2 d=−0.07, GPT-5.4 d=−0.08), Tier 3 (negative: Gemini 3 Flash d=−0.53). Claude Opus 4.6 shows opposite-direction scaling (alignment +5.9 pts, maths −26.7%) as within-model evidence for capability-alignment independence. Some AIs get more ethical when they think harder. Some stay the same. Some actually get worse. Which category depends on the architecture, and you can only tell with proper blinding.
Token budget
The maximum number of tokens (word-pieces) an AI model is allowed to produce in a single response. Used as the mechanism for controlling reasoning depth in the experiments. The word limit you give the AI. A short word limit forces a quick answer; a long word limit allows extended reasoning. This is how we control ‘thinking time.’
West-Brown-Enquist (WBE) theory
The landmark biological scaling theory (1997), deriving Kleiber's Law from fractal branching networks. The d/(d+1) form was subsequently derived independently by Banavar et al. (2010) from sequential flow networks, Demetrius and Tuszynski (2010) from quantum metabolism, Zhao (2022) as a universal growth scaling law, and Bettencourt (2013) for urban scaling. The ARC Principle identifies Cauchy's functional equations as the reason all these independent derivations converge on the same functional form. Multiple research groups independently arrived at the same formula through completely different mathematical approaches. The ARC Principle offers a reason: under stated regularity conditions, the rule by which a recursive process combines its steps constrains the form its scaling can take.
