# Does intelligence compound when each step builds on the last, or merely accumulate?

**Version:** 1.18
**Revised:** 1 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-01-17
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 1.18):

> I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons; only proof will move the world. Does intelligence compound when each step builds on the last, or merely accumulate? This paper formalises Law I of the ARC Theory, the ARC Principle (Artificial Recursive Creation): usable capability $U=I R^{\alpha}$, with its exponent measured rather than assumed. Its stronger hypothesis is that sequential recursion scales super-linearly and faster than parallel sampling. Published reasoning-model data give error-decay slopes of about 0.1 to 0.3 for parallel sampling and 1.34 from two sequential points. These compare error against sample count or average tokens, not directly capability against recursive depth; the series also differ in model and benchmark, and the sequential points are two model versions. Under a positive-error power model, the proxy $1/E$ has the same exponent on the same resource axis. Reading it as the capability exponent requires an explicit metric and depth mapping. The later five-model study, Paper II, has one interpretable sequential fit, on Gemini 3 Flash: error-decay regression 0.49 (standard error 0.20) and endpoint estimate 0.59 (bootstrap 95 per cent interval $-1.3$ to $2.9$). That one-model result is inconclusive. Neither set independently measures self-referential coupling. The paper states the ARC Bound, the conjectured value two of Law III’s stability ceiling, with its conditions and falsification criteria. Super-linear capability growth remains unresolved; Law I remains a law under test.
