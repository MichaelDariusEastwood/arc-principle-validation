# The ARC Equation: does intelligence compound when each step builds on the last, or merely accumulate?

**Version:** 1.22
**Revised:** 18 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-01-17
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 1.22):

> Does intelligence compound when each step builds on the last, or merely accumulate? Under the ARC Theory the answer is a question of safety: the rate at which capability grows with each revision, set against the rate at which correction improves, decides whether a self-improving system stays correctable. This paper formalises Law I of the ARC Theory, the ARC Principle (Artificial Recursive Creation): usable capability $U=I R^{\alpha}$, with its exponent measured rather than assumed. Its stronger hypothesis is that sequential recursion scales super-linearly and faster than parallel sampling. Published reasoning-model data give error-decay slopes of about 0.1 to 0.3 for parallel sampling and 1.34 from two sequential points (1.22 to 1.49 under the rounding of the published token averages alone). These measure how error falls with samples or average tokens at inference, not how capability grows with recursive depth (counted in completed revision rounds); the series also differ in model and benchmark, and the sequential points are two model versions. Reading any of them as the law’s exponent needs a capability metric and a depth instrument that this paper does not yet supply. The later five-model study, Paper II, has one interpretable sequential fit, on Gemini 3 Flash: error-decay regression 0.49 (standard error 0.20, from three points) and endpoint estimate 0.59 (bootstrap 95 per cent interval $-1.3$ to $2.9$). That one-model result is inconclusive. The paper states the ARC Bound, the conjectured value two, on the relative rung, of the stability ceiling of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, for correctors of the system’s own class, with its conditions and falsification criteria, each decided by a three-outcome rule fixed in advance: supported, refuted or insufficient precision. Super-linear capability growth remains unresolved; Law I, the ARC Principle, remains a law under test.
