Abstract

I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons; only proof will move the world. Does intelligence compound when each step builds on the last, or merely accumulate? This paper formalises Law I of the ARC Theory, the ARC Principle (Artificial Recursive Creation): usable capability U=IRαU=I R^{\alpha}, with its exponent measured rather than assumed. Its stronger hypothesis is that sequential recursion scales super-linearly and faster than parallel sampling. Published reasoning-model data give error-decay slopes of about 0.1 to 0.3 for parallel sampling and 1.34 from two sequential points. These compare error against sample count or average tokens, not directly capability against recursive depth; the series also differ in model and benchmark, and the sequential points are two model versions. Under a positive-error power model, the proxy 1/E1/E has the same exponent on the same resource axis. Reading it as the capability exponent requires an explicit metric and depth mapping. The later five-model study, Paper II, has one interpretable sequential fit, on Gemini 3 Flash: error-decay regression 0.49 (standard error 0.20) and endpoint estimate 0.59 (bootstrap 95 per cent interval −1.3-1.3 to 2.92.9). That one-model result is inconclusive. Neither set independently measures self-referential coupling. The paper states the ARC Bound, the conjectured value two of Law III’s stability ceiling, with its conditions and falsification criteria. Super-linear capability growth remains unresolved; Law I remains a law under test.

1 Introduction

I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons; only proof will move the world. If control of a capable AI system were lost and could not be won back, what the system itself carries would decide what it does. The programme’s position is that people keep oversight, containment and shutdown for as long as they physically can, and never give them up by choice; correction built into a system is the contingency for control lost against our will, not a reason to let go of it. Whether any correction can keep pace depends first on how capability grows when a system works on its own output, and that is this paper’s question: does intelligence compound when each step builds on the last, or merely accumulate? In plain words, its answer so far is this. Published reasoning-model data give steeper error reduction against average tokens in one sequential pair than against sample count in a parallel series. These are different response-to-resource comparisons, with other differences between the systems; they do not isolate the form of reasoning or directly measure capability against recursive depth. The one later interpretable error-decay fit is inconclusive. The question stays open, and the paper states it in a form that can be tested and refuted.

This paper belongs to the ARC Theory (the Theory of Artificial Recursive Creation), the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, the science of systems that improve themselves, which the author founded (the founding paper). The theory is carried by five laws, each a law under test: Law I, the ARC Principle, that usable capability is a power of recursive depth, with the exponent measured rather than assumed; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; Law III, the ARC Ceiling; and Laws IV and V, the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law, added on 24 September 2026 and registered as laws on 15 October 2026, their mathematics in development. The laws and twenty-two propositions are registered on OSF (the theory registration), and the statement paper states the theory whole. This paper states Law I formally and examines error-decay evidence under the metric and depth premises stated in Section 4.2, and it states the ARC Bound, the conjectured value of Law III’s ceiling; Appendix E places it among the laws.

The scaling laws governing artificial intelligence have been extensively studied. Kaplan et al. (2020) established power-law relationships between model performance and parameters, while Hoffmann et al. (2022) refined these with compute-optimal training prescriptions. These laws govern what to scale but do not address why scaling produces intelligent behaviour.

The emergence of reasoning models in 2024 and 2025 introduced a new variable: test-time compute. OpenAI’s o1 (September 2024; OpenAI 2024) and DeepSeek’s R1 (January 2025; DeepSeek-AI 2025a) allocate computational resources at inference time to reason before responding, producing substantial capability improvements on reasoning benchmarks.

This paper’s hypothesis is that test-time compute serves as a proxy for recursive depth, and that recursive depth may be a fundamental driver of capability amplification in artificial intelligence systems.

2 Related work, antecedents and the rival instrument

This section states each debt to prior work in the source’s own terms, beginning with the work nearest to this paper’s own empirical question, the scaling of test-time compute, then the scaling premise the programme borrows, and ending with the nearest work on how oversight scales and its antecedents.

Test-time compute, studied directly.

The scaling of test-time compute in language models has been studied directly, and the contrast this paper draws between sequential and parallel recursion was drawn earlier within single models. Snell et al. (2024), first posted on 6 August 2024, compare sequential revisions with parallel samples under one generation budget and report that “while increased sequential revisions tends to outperform more parallel compute, at higher generation budgets there is an ideal ratio that strikes a balance between the two extremes”, the ratio depending on the difficulty of the question. Brown et al. (2024), first posted on 31 July 2024, report that under repeated sampling “the relationship between coverage and the number of samples is often log-linear and can be modelled with an exponentiated power law”, while majority voting and reward models “plateau beyond several hundred samples and fail to fully scale with the sample budget”. Muennighoff et al. (2025), first posted on 31 January 2025, lengthen one model’s thinking by budget forcing, which “allows extrapolating beyond its performance without test-time intervention: from 50% to 57% on AIME24”. Zeng et al. (2025), first posted on 17 February 2025, find that the longer chains of thought of o1-like models “do not consistently enhance accuracy” and, comparing “sequential and parallel scaling strategies on QwQ, R1 and LIMO”, that “parallel scaling achieves better coverage and scalability”. The published evidence within single models is therefore mixed, and the comparison of Zeng et al. (2025), which holds the system fixed, runs against this paper’s hypothesis. This paper states the contrast as a hypothesis about the capability exponent in equation (1), reports two-point error-decay estimates in Section 4, and states the stability ceiling of Section 3.3. Relating the error estimates to the capability hypothesis requires the metric and depth bridge of Section 4.2.

The pipes premise, with its contest.

The premise that transport networks set the pace of biological growth is not this programme’s claim to defend; it is the premise of a leading account in quantitative biology, and a contested one. West, Brown and Enquist derived the allometric scaling laws of biology from the geometry of nutrient-distribution networks (West et al. 1997, 4 April 1997; one of the most cited papers in its field, with an OpenAlex citation count of 5,046 as of 12 August 2026, a single-source and index-dependent count). The exact exponent remains contested: White and Seymour report basal metabolic rate proportional to body mass to the two-thirds power rather than three-quarters (White and Seymour 2003; 725 citations, OpenAlex), and Kozłowski and Konarzewski have twice asked whether the model is “mathematically correct and biologically relevant” (Kozłowski and Konarzewski 2004, 2005). This programme’s premise needs only the mechanism, that the delivery network sets the ceiling, and not any particular exponent; the dispute over the exponent’s value leaves it untouched, but the critics question the mechanism too, so the programme takes it as a premise, and its biological reading stands or falls with it.

The exception West’s own group found.

The escape from the pipe limit is also not this programme’s assertion; it is measured in the urban scaling literature by the same senior author. Bettencourt et al. (2007) (2,704 citations, OpenAlex, 12 August 2026, a single-source count) report, in the paper’s own words, read from its abstract (their β\beta is a city-scaling exponent, not this paper’s coupling βL\beta_L):

“Quantities reflecting wealth creation and innovation have β≈1.2>1\beta\approx1.2>1 (increasing returns), whereas those accounting for infrastructure display β≈0.8<1\beta\approx0.8<1 (economies of scale).”

Infrastructure, the literal pipes, scales sublinearly. Information-mediated activity scales superlinearly. They state the contrast with biology directly: “we discuss how cities are similar to, and differ from, biological organisms, for which β<1\beta<1”. They derive “growth equations, which quantify the dramatic difference between growth fueled by innovation versus that driven by economies of scale” (spelling as in the original). And their closing inference names the consequence: “This difference suggests that, as population grows, major innovation cycles must be generated at a continually accelerating rate to sustain growth and avoid stagnation or collapse.”

On this paper’s reading, that is the shape of the open problem this programme addresses: in the findings of West’s group, information-mediated growth escapes the throughput limit, and the only brake in their framework is external and must be applied again and again, faster and faster. No internal limit is derived. The replacement limit, a ceiling belonging to the growing system itself, is the gap.

The pipes argument at fixed throughput.

Everything that grows is fed through a channel, and for everything before software, fixing the channel fixes the growth: starve a tumour’s blood supply and its growth is held back; fix a chain reaction’s fuel and geometry and it stops; the Eddington limit is a luminosity ceiling where radiation pressure balances gravity, not a hard ceiling on the rate of accretion: ultraluminous X-ray sources sit well above it, one accreting neutron star at about a hundred times it (Bachetti et al. 2014). Cosmic inflation is excluded by scope (expansion of space, not growth of a structure on a substrate); evolution is the sharpest case and fits (complexity rising for billions of years at roughly fixed solar throughput, glacially, with no internal corrector, so on this framework evolution would carry a measurable exponent far below the ceiling, a hypothesis about biology stated without a criterion or a planned test, and outside the theory registration’s scope). Software is the first thing that keeps growing when the pipes are held fixed, within the finite number of states and the maximum rate a fixed machine allows (Margolus and Levitin 1998; Lloyd 2000), and the operational definition is load-bearing: an artefact loop on a fixed substrate, measured on a substrate-fixed clock; a model merely thinking longer does not qualify. The physicist’s objection is Landauer’s (1961), that computation is physically implemented; the fixed-substrate clock is the answer, because the regime under study holds the substrate fixed and asks what still grows.

The rival instrument.

The nearest work to this programme’s question, and the right paper to weigh it against, is Engels et al. (2025), arXiv:2504.18530, first posted 25 April 2025 at 17:54:27 UTC (arXiv’s record; a NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight Poster, checked on 15 August 2026 against the NeurIPS virtual page for poster 115536, as recorded in the statement paper’s reference dossier). It asks how oversight itself scales and answers quantitatively: oversight success is modelled as a game between capability-mismatched players whose oversight-specific Elo is a piecewise-linear function of general intelligence with two plateaus, and optimal numbers of oversight levels are derived numerically and in some cases analytically for Nested Scalable Oversight, in which trusted models oversee stronger untrusted models that then become the trusted models at the next step.

The instrument is the difference. Their variable is the capability gap between overseer and overseen, measured in Elo. This programme’s variable is the composition class of the corrector, measured through the corrector’s own scaling exponent. They also state the design condition in slope form: oversight improves with capability when the overseer’s game-specific Elo rises faster with general Elo than the overseen system’s (mG>mHm_G > m_H), and they recommend iterating an oversight design until it does. Their framework has no term for what the overseer is made of. The programme’s separate prediction, outside this paper’s framework, is that iterated same-class oversight, of which Nested Scalable Oversight is an instance, stays capped however many rungs are added, while a cross-class corrector is not held to that cap. The theory registration’s P6 states the cap for same-class correctors, at or below one half, as a conjecture on the correction-leverage exponent of Law III (the ARC Ceiling, a law under test), an exponent the registration keeps distinct from Law II’s βC\beta_C. That is where the two programmes could disagree about a measurable quantity, and this paper does not decide it.

Antecedents and near-misses, each with its differential.

On the question. Hutter asked directly whether intelligence can explode (Hutter 2012, arXiv, 28 February 2012), undertaking to separate speed from intelligence explosion and to consider possible bounds on intelligence, augmenting the analysis of Chalmers (2010). The question itself goes back to Good (1966). What that literature does not contain, so far as has been surveyed, is a derived ceiling with an architecture dependence; growth exponents of the form used here are older, as the item on the form below concedes, and power laws in inference compute have been fitted directly (the paragraph on test-time compute above). A prior source that supplies a derived ceiling with an architecture dependence defeats the corresponding priority here, and readers who locate one are invited to report it.

On the form. The relation α=1/(1−βL)\alpha = 1/(1-\beta_L) that Figure 1 and Section 5.2 take from the Foundational paper is the exponent of the elementary solution of a separable growth equation. The same structure, a stock that grows at a rate proportional to a power below one of itself, is the knowledge production function of Jones (1995), whose long-run growth rate carries the factor 1/(1−ϕ)1/(1-\phi).

On impossibility. Three 2025 arXiv papers argue for limits on alignment or its control: Yao (2025b), “The Alignment Trap: Complexity Barriers” (arXiv:2506.10304, version 1 of 12 June 2025 at 02:30:30 UTC, independently observed by the Internet Archive on 13 June 2025; cited by arXiv:2512.03048); Yao (2025a), “On the Mathematical Impossibility of Safe Universal Approximators” (arXiv:2507.03031, 3 July 2025, the only paper in the arXiv abstract corpus containing the phrase “irreducible uncontrollability”, abstract-search total of one, measured 12 August 2026); and Ball et al. (2025), “On the Impossibility of Separating Intelligence from Judgment” (arXiv:2507.07341, 9 July 2025). All three are worst-case and qualitative: measure zero, coNP-completeness, cryptographic hardness. None reports an average-case scaling exponent or a rate. The third concludes that “safety cannot be achieved by designing filters external to the LLM internals (architecture and weights)” and argues that “an aligned AI system’s intelligence cannot be separated from its judgment”; that argument from filtering intractability is consistent with this programme’s architecture dependence, though it does not test it. Two of the three are by one author. The measured object of all three is verification of a fixed system, and it is closed within its own terms; this programme’s measured object is a different one, persistence of alignment as capability compounds under recursive amplification, and closure of the first does not settle the second.

On the mechanism. That anti-correlated estimates average better than independent ones is textbook variance reduction (antithetic variates). The mechanism is not the claim. The claim is that architecture determines whether anti-correlation is available at all, and that this caps a safety-relevant exponent.

The nearest structural analogue. The quantum error-correction threshold theorem (Aharonov and Ben-Or 2008) also converts a qualitative worry into a critical value. It concerns physical error rates in a fixed architecture, not a corrector’s scaling exponent, so it is a near-miss rather than an occupant; the analogy is one of method. This analogue was identified by the programme’s own search rather than by a referee, and is disclosed accordingly.

On recursive creation. Smolin’s cosmological natural selection (Smolin 1992) is the antecedent for selection-shaped universes, and the programme’s own December-era notes cite it contemporaneously (“Echoing Smolin’s cosmological natural selection, AI could create recursive universes with their own laws”, from the author’s notes).

On feedback and stability (classical). The general proposition that inadequate corrective gain relative to system gain causes instability has a long control-theory lineage (small-gain theorems; Zames 1966). The differential: those are gain conditions on interconnected systems, not a power-law exponent criterion on a corrector’s scaling with the capability of a recursively improving system. The general proposition is never claimed; the exponent formulation, dynamics and estimator are the contribution surface.

The nearest quantitative neighbour. Liu and Meng (2026), “Self-Correction as Feedback Control: Error Dynamics, Stability Thresholds, and Prompt Interventions in LLMs” (arXiv:2604.22273) recasts self-correction as a closed-loop control problem via a two-state Markov model and derives a directly measurable stability threshold: iterate only when the error-correction rate over the error-introduction rate exceeds Acc/(1−Acc)\mathrm{Acc}/(1-\mathrm{Acc}). The differential: theirs is a per-step rate threshold at a fixed capability level, deciding whether another iteration helps now; this programme’s criterion is a scaling relation across capability, deciding whether corrective strength keeps pace as the system improves. Theirs has no scaling exponent on the corrector and no capability-growth exponent, the two quantities the programme’s co-scaling criterion compares; the two models meet at zero growth, as Paper X shows. Two adjacent empirical results from the same search: Li (2025), “Decomposing LLM Self-Correction: The Accuracy-Correction Paradox and Error Depth Hypothesis” (arXiv:2601.00828, first posted on 24 December 2025; an accuracy-correction paradox, weaker models showing higher intrinsic correction rates) and Tsui (2025), “Self-Correction Bench: Uncovering and Addressing the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Large Language Models” (arXiv:2507.02778, first posted on 3 July 2025; a systematic self-correction blind spot across open models).

3 The ARC Principle and its framework

3.1 The ARC Equation

The ARC Principle (Artificial Recursive Creation), stated in an earlier form, as the ARC Hypothesis, in the author’s manuscripts of 8 December 2024 (Eastwood 2024) and articulated in Infinite Architects (Eastwood 2026d), where its exponent is two, states, in the form this paper gives it: U=I×Rα\begin{equation*} U = I \times R^{\alpha} \end{equation*}(1) Capability scales with intelligence multiplied by recursive depth raised to a power, where:

  • UU = Effective capability on a specified metric (benchmark performance requires the metric bridge of Section 4.2; the letter’s earlier senses in this programme are in Appendix D.2)

  • II = Base intelligence (single-pass processing capacity without recursive reasoning)

  • RR = Recursive depth (number of self-referential processing iterations)

  • α\alpha = Scaling exponent (empirically determined; hypothesised stability ceiling = 2, a scaling limit above which self-correction is not stably maintained)

The principle’s core claim: recursion does not merely add to capability; it multiplies it according to a power law. The letter α\alpha has an earlier sense in this programme, recorded in Appendix D.2.

3.2 Recursion and its two forms

Definition 1 (Recursion). Recursion is self-reference: a process whose output becomes its input. It is distinct from mere iteration (repeating the same operation) because each cycle operates on the transformed results of previous cycles.

Definition 2 (Parallel recursion, weak). Multiple independent solutions generated simultaneously. No information transfer between branches. Example: generating NN samples and selecting by majority vote. Expected scaling: diminishing returns as redundancy increases.

Definition 3 (Sequential recursion, strong). Each processing step builds explicitly on previous steps. Errors can be detected and corrected iteratively. Example: chain-of-thought reasoning (Wei et al. 2022) that folds self-reflection into each step. Expected scaling: compounding returns as depth enables self-correction.

The ARC Principle predicts that sequential recursion should produce higher α\alpha values than parallel recursion.

3.3 The quadratic stability ceiling hypothesis

We hypothesise that α=2\alpha = 2 represents the stable limit, the most a recursively self-correcting system can grow and stay correctable: a scaling limit, not a speed limit. The exactly quadratic speedup belongs to the quantum search algorithm of Grover (1996); Bennett et al. (1997) then established the lower bound that matches it, which is what makes that speedup optimal for unstructured search. The correspondence invoked here is structural, the same quadratic form of a search-optimality bound, not a claim that recursive reasoning implements amplitude amplification. If a similarly shaped limit applies to recursive intelligence for related reasons, quadratic scaling may mark the ceiling above which self-correction is not stably maintained; systems can be driven beyond it, they do not remain stably self-correcting there, and any excursion above 2 is the predicted supercritical regime rather than a refutation of the bound. A durably stable system above 2 would refute the bound. Stable is measured, not asserted: correction has to out-scale drift, which Law II states as βC>k\beta_C > k, and the lower bound on βC−k\beta_C - k has to sit above zero throughout a window fixed before the run began.

Law before value.

The ceiling of stable self-improvement is set by how fast a system can correct itself: it is the reciprocal of one minus the correction-leverage exponent γ\gamma, αcrit=11−γ.\begin{equation*} \alpha_{\text{crit}} = \frac{1}{1-\gamma}. \end{equation*}(2) For a corrector built from the same substrate as the engine, that reciprocal is two at γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2, the ARC Bound’s point value, which the theory registration conjectures; what it claims is the inequality, a ceiling of at most two for correctors of the system’s own class. The point value rests on two assumptions the registration names and does not present as established: the burden model, under which the drift exponent follows the rate of capability gain, and the route to one half, that internally accumulated corrections combine like independent samples. The law is the claim. Two is what the law returns at γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2, and the conditions behind one half are what the drafted and registered units examine: Unit 82, registered on 27 October 2026, tests P17, which bears on one of them and establishes neither the value nor the bound (Section 6.3). If two dies, the law survives completely: measure γ\gamma and report the ceiling it implies. If the law dies, two has no independent support, because the derivation runs through 1/(1−γ)1/(1-\gamma). Version 1.104 of the theory registration states the relation as “a conditional specialisation of the second law, not a third finding standing beside it”, on which a result is “never independent corroboration of the second law”. The relation is derived inside a pacing model and has not been derived from a joint model of capability growth, correction accumulation, error covariance, correction delay and absolute harm, so Law III is stated here as a minimal-model result rather than as a general law. One consequence should be met where the law is met, because it cuts both ways: since, on the domain γ<1\gamma < 1 of Proposition 1, 1/(1−γ)≤21/(1-\gamma) \le 2 is exactly equivalent to γ≤1/2\gamma \le 1/2, the ceiling and the half-value are one commitment written in two notations rather than two independent claims, and neither may ever be offered as evidence for the other.

Proposition 1 (The domain of the equivalence). For γ<1\gamma < 1, 1/(1−γ)≤21/(1-\gamma) \le 2 holds if and only if γ≤1/2\gamma \le 1/2. At γ=1\gamma = 1 the reciprocal inequality has no truth value while γ≤1/2\gamma \le 1/2 is false, and for γ>1\gamma > 1 the reciprocal inequality holds while γ≤1/2\gamma \le 1/2 fails. The equivalence therefore holds exactly on γ<1\gamma < 1, the range on which αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\text{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma) is positive and finite.

The proof is in Appendix A. The restriction is the model’s own rather than an assumption added to it, and it is what the clauses above already imply where they state that the relation is derived inside a pacing model and is offered as a minimal-model result rather than as a general law. Read inside that domain, the point of the sentence stands entire.

4.1 Data sources

The sequential series.

DeepSeek-R1 and its update DeepSeek-R1-0528, as the developer’s model card for DeepSeek-R1-0528 reports them (28 May 2025; DeepSeek-AI 2025b); the model is described in its technical report, arXiv:2501.12948 (DeepSeek-AI 2025a). The two points are two versions of the model, each at its own average token use, not two thinking budgets of one version.

4.2 Method

Equation (3) estimates an error-decay exponent, denoted αerr\alpha_{\mathrm{err}} here, over a declared resource coordinate RresR_{\mathrm{res}}. For positive errors at two distinct positive resource levels, it is the slope implied by E(Rres)∝Rres−αerrE(R_{\mathrm{res}})\propto R_{\mathrm{res}}^{-\alpha_{\mathrm{err}}}. In the parallel series the resource is sample count; in the sequential pair it is the developer’s average thinking-token count. Neither coordinate is a measured count of completed self-improvement revisions. This is the same endpoint estimator used in Paper II, version 2.19, equation (2); its equation (1) models the proportion of incorrect responses.

αerr=−ln⁡(Error2/Error1)ln⁡(Rres,2/Rres,1)\begin{equation*} \alpha_{\mathrm{err}} = -\frac{\ln(\text{Error}_2 / \text{Error}_1)}{\ln(R_{\mathrm{res},2} / R_{\mathrm{res},1})} \end{equation*}(3)

The metric bridge.

On the same resource coordinate, defining a proxy as Cerr=1/EC_{\mathrm{err}}=1/E, or a fixed positive multiple of it, gives the same numerical exponent when the stated error power model holds and E>0E>0. Accuracy, 1−E1-E, is a different response and approaches its ceiling; it does not thereby grow as a power with that exponent. Identifying αerr\alpha_{\mathrm{err}} with the capability exponent α\alpha in equation (1) therefore requires an explicitly justified capability metric and a mapping from the resource coordinate to recursive depth. 5.2 What would refute the principle

The ARC Principle in this paper’s original, stronger form, that capability scales super-linearly with recursive depth, U=I×RαU = I \times R^{\alpha}, with the sequential/parallel distinction determining the regime, is refutable. It would be overturned by any of the following:

  1. No super-linear depth scaling. If capability scaled only linearly or sub-linearly with recursive depth RR across reasoning models (i.e. α≤1\alpha \le 1 where the principle predicts α>1\alpha > 1 for sequential recursion), the central super-linearity claim fails.

  2. Exponent-composition mismatch. Given independently measured coupling βL\beta_L, a declared capability metric and depth axis, and the growth-model premises and asymptotic regime of Figure 1, systematic disagreement with α=1/(1−βL)\alpha=1/(1-\beta_L) would reject that model's applicability to the measured system. It would not refute the algebraic solution under its premises. Inferring βL\beta_L from the same fitted exponent and substituting it back supplies no such test. The coupling remains distinct from Law II's correction exponent βC\beta_C.

  3. Regime non-distinction. If sequential and parallel recursion produced the same scaling regime (no super-linear vs sub-linear split), the paper’s central distinction collapses.

  4. Better-fitting alternative. If a functional form outside the ARC family fit the test-time compute data materially better across models, ARC would not be the operative law.

  5. Domain-independence failure. If U=I×RαU = I \times R^{\alpha} held for reasoning models but robustly failed in other recursive domains meeting the premise, the domain-independent claim would have to narrow to “a property of current reasoning models”.

  6. The one-half reference. On Gemini 3 Flash, Paper II's error-decay regression is 0.49 (standard error 0.20), one model's inconclusive fit (Section 4.4). One half is a reference value, not an automatic null exponent for the proportion of incorrect responses: the central limit theorem's square-root standard-error scaling does not by itself supply a law for that proportion. A competing sampling, aggregation and decision model must predict the same measured outcome before a comparison can distinguish mechanisms. The earlier objection is retained with its correction in Appendix F; its programme record is FALS-022 and Objection 5 of Paper II, Section 5.4.

5.3 The decision form

The decision form these criteria inherit. Section 5.1 records status against this paper’s original criteria; the deciding registrations use a three-outcome form, and every criterion above inherits it: SUPPORTED when the interval lies wholly beyond the minimum effect fixed in advance; REFUTED when the interval lies wholly within the equivalence margin fixed in advance (two-one-sided-tests logic); INSUFFICIENT PRECISION when the interval is wider than the margin, reported in exactly those words and never as support or refutation. The margin, the minimum effect and a measured power figure are fixed before filing. “Refuted if the confidence interval contains the null” lets an underpowered study refute by default and is not licensed anywhere in this programme.

5.4 An objection pre-empted

One objection pre-empted, because a physicist reaches it in one step. Fault-tolerant quantum error correction suppresses logical error exponentially in code distance below threshold, which looks like a counterexample to any square-root cap (the same-class limit γ≤1/2\gamma \le 1/2 of Section 3.3). Two scope facts answer it: the exponential suppression runs on the redundancy axis at fixed capability, not on the coupling of corrective strength to the capability of the corrected system, which is the exponent this programme measures; and the threshold theorem itself requires sufficiently uncorrelated physical noise, which is the independence premise again, wearing hardware. Concentration bounds likewise give exponentially falling error probability while estimator width falls as the square root: different quantities, and conflating them manufactures a refutation.

5.5 A programme hypothesis beyond this paper

Oversight arrangements that place a human in the loop, as amplification (Christiano et al. 2018) and reinforcement learning from human feedback (Christiano et al. 2017) do, are cross-class by construction, because the human corrector does not share the model’s substrate. The programme therefore hypothesises, beyond what this paper’s framework decides, that human-in-the-loop oversight shows a higher correction exponent than pure-model oversight, for a structural reason rather than a sentimental one. This is testable on existing data and does not require new systems. 7 Limitations

Scientific integrity requires explicit acknowledgement of limitations:

  • Limited data points: The DeepSeek-R1 analysis relies on only two data points, from two versions of the model (Section 4.1). Additional measurements would strengthen or refute the finding.

  • Token counts as averages: The thinking-token counts are the developer’s published averages a question, about 12,000 for the earlier version at 70% and about 23,000 for DeepSeek-R1-0528 at 87.5%; no count for each problem is published.

  • Domain specificity: Current evidence is limited to mathematical reasoning (AIME 2024 and AIME 2025). Generalisation to other domains remains untested.

  • Confounded comparison: The parallel and sequential series differ in system, developer, benchmark, evaluation context and resource axis as well as in the form of recursion (Section 4.5), and published comparisons within single models are mixed (Section 2); the contrast is a hypothesis.

  • Functional form: Two points fix the exponent of a power law through them and cannot tell a power law from a logarithmic, exponential or saturating form; equation (3) assumes the power law, and the registered analysis of Law I compares it with rival forms fixed in advance.

  • Metric and axis: Equation (3) measures error decay against samples or average tokens. Reciprocal error has the same exponent only on the same axis under the stated positive-error power model; accuracy does not. A capability interpretation requires a justified metric and mapping to recursive depth, and a finite-window exponent need not equal the growth model's asymptotic exponent. Neither algebraic inversion nor fixed model weights independently measures coupling.

  • Developer-reported figures: Every number in Tables 1 and 2 is the developer’s own published figure; none was measured for this paper.

8 Implications

8.1 For AI development

If the ARC Principle holds, recursive depth constitutes a third scaling axis alongside parameters and data. Investment in recursive architectures may yield better returns than scaling model size alone.

8.2 For AI safety

If recursion amplifies not only capability but also embedded values, then well-aligned initial values should strengthen through recursive self-improvement. Misaligned values would also compound, making early alignment critical. The paper measures nothing about values and nothing about usefulness (legitimate task completion, needless refusal, resource use, correction cost), so neither half of that conditional says whether a system whose values compound stays useful. Neither half is a reason to relax oversight, containment or shutdown, which stay in human hands for as long as they can be held; correction built in is the contingency for control lost against our will.

8.3 For scientific understanding

The ARC Principle connects to several frameworks, including the scaling laws of Kaplan et al. (2020), Integrated Information Theory (Tononi 2008) and the optimality proof for Grover’s quantum search (Bennett et al. 1997).

9 Conclusion

We have formalised the ARC Principle and presented preliminary evidence:

  1. Parallel sampling gives error-decay slopes αerr≈0.1\alpha_{\mathrm{err}}\approx0.1 to 0.30.3 on the sample-count axis

  2. The sequential pair gives αerr≈1.34\alpha_{\mathrm{err}}\approx1.34 on the average-token axis, from two versions of one model. Paper II's later one-model interpretable error fit is inconclusive. Neither independently identifies a capability exponent or coupling; the reciprocal-error reading needs the metric, power-model and axis premises in Section 4.2

  3. The form of recursion may determine whether capability compounds, which the paper holds as a hypothesis (Section 4.5)

In plain terms, the original stronger hypothesis claims: “Thinking about thinking makes you smarter. Not linearly smarter, but disproportionately smarter, if the thinking is sequential rather than parallel.”

The principle stands as a law under test. The research continues.
