                                                        Abstract
           I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it
       reasons; only proof will move the world. Predicting how a system improves requires separating a
       mathematical model from evidence that the system follows it. This cross-domain foundation of the
       ARC Theory asks whether the way gains combine can predict later scaling. Specified continuous
       functional equations restrict scaling families; associativity of an update rule alone does not. A
       cumulative-advantage model gives the shifted power law 𝑔(𝑟) = [1 + (1 − 𝛽 𝐿 )𝑎𝑟] 1/(1−𝛽𝐿 ) , with
       asymptotic exponent 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ). Its characteristic scale requires a declared depth coordinate and
       an operational crossover criterion. Fixed model weights and fixed update rules imply no universal
       capability exponent. Biological and physical comparisons are separated into measurements, analytic
       references and analogies; the proposed general dimensional bridge remains unproved. The one
       interpretable AI error-suppression fit is inconclusive and does not identify a capability-growth
       exponent. A registered pilot supports its pooled performance contrast under the registered scoring
       rule, with engine dependence, a disclosed recovery amendment and a mechanism test that was not
       evaluable; it validates none of the exponent, crossover or ceiling claims. The ARC Ceiling remains a
       law under test with conditional premises, not a universal safety guarantee. The decisive next test is
       prospective: classify composition independently, lock the scaling prediction, and compare held-out
       trajectories with serious rival models. The contribution is a framework for predictions that can fail,
       with each mathematical and empirical claim kept at its demonstrated scope.

1    Introduction
I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons;
only proof will move the world. If control of a capable AI system were lost and could not be won back,
what the system itself carries would decide what it does. People should keep oversight, containment and
shutdown for as long as they physically can, and never surrender them by choice. Embedded correction is
the programme’s contingency for control lost against our will. Testing that ambition requires knowing
what improves, on which axis, and under which constraints.
     This paper develops one part of that foundation: can the way a system combines gains predict
how its later performance scales? It compares computational, biological and physical examples, while
distinguishing model assumptions from observations. A useful answer would let a researcher make a
prediction before seeing the full curve, and identify where that prediction fails. No exponent derived here
establishes that a deployed AI system remains safe or correctable.
     This paper belongs to the ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, the science of
systems that improve themselves, which the author founded (the founding paper). The theory is carried
by five laws, each a law under test: Law I, the ARC Principle, that usable capability is a power of
recursive depth, with the exponent measured rather than assumed; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law;
Law III, the ARC Ceiling; and Laws IV and V, the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law,
added on 24 September 2026, their mathematics in development. The laws and twenty-two propositions
are registered on OSF (the theory registration), and the statement paper states the theory whole. The
programme’s engineering hypothesis is the Eden Protocol, correction built into a system’s own reasoning,
whose pilot Section 6.5 reports; its end goal is to learn, by registered tests, whether correction built into a
self-improving system can keep pace with its capability, as the contingency for control lost against our
will. This paper brings four fields together, AI reasoning, quantum error correction, acoustic physics and
consciousness science, and assumes no reader knows the others: Section 1.1 says what each reported, and
Appendix G places the paper among the laws.
     The mathematical statements are conditional. Specified functional equations constrain a family; the
cumulative-advantage ODE selects an asymptotic exponent under its own assumptions. Neither result
proves that a real system satisfies those premises. The structural properties hold in every substrate whose
recursion satisfies the axioms and the premises Theorem 4 names, because the axioms name no substrate.
The empirical hypothesis is that an independently justified composition description helps predict fresh
trajectories better than alternative models. That separates the framework’s mathematical content from the
prospective success it still needs to demonstrate.
     Our contributions are:
    • A formalism. Three axioms, separability, cumulative advantage and continuity (Section 3.1), and
      the recursive composition operator ⊕, which characterises how incremental gains combine with
      accumulated capability (Definition 1), giving the generalised ARC Equation (2).
    • A classification. Under each case’s hypotheses, multiplicative ⊕ yields power-law scaling (𝑅 𝛼 ),
      additive ⊕ yields exponential scaling (𝑒 𝛼𝑟 in the added passes 𝑟 = 𝑅 − 1), and bounded ⊕ yields
      saturation (Theorem 1), with the domain and error conditions of any stability claim stated separately
      (Section 3.2).
    • The exponent derived, not fitted. Within the stated model, the asymptotic exponent is 𝛼 =
      1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) (Theorem 2); no additive decomposition of capability is consistent with the axioms
      (Theorem 2a); and the characteristic scale 𝑅 ∗ = 𝛼/𝑎 is independent of base potential only while 𝑎
      and 𝛽 𝐿 remain fixed (Theorem 3).
    • Conditional consequences and limits. The model supplies a characteristic scale, depth dependence
      and a multiplicative dependence on base quality. A capacity ceiling requires further premises.

1.1     The problem
Between August 2024 and February 2026, four research programmes reported the following results:
    Google Quantum AI (results announced 9 December 2024; the team’s preprint public from 24
August 2024) demonstrated exponential suppression of the logical error rate below the surface-code
threshold on the Willow processor, by a factor Λ = 2.14 ± 0.02 for each increase of two in code distance
(Google Quantum AI and Collaborators 2024), a result graded here as convergent timing, not a prediction.
    DeepSeek-AI (preprint January 2025; in Nature, September 2025) showed that “the reasoning abilities
of LLMs can be incentivized through pure reinforcement learning (RL)”, with the emergence of reasoning
patterns such as self-reflection and verification (Guo et al. 2025). Sharma and Chopra (2025) reported that
sequential refinement, in which chains build on previous attempts, outperformed parallel self-consistency
at matched compute in 95.6% of configurations, across five open-source models and three reasoning
benchmarks. Their paper is dated before the experiment reported here, so it is the earlier demonstration of
that advantage and not a confirmation of this work. The direction is also not settled: Gu et al. (2026),
posted in April 2026, report the opposite result, parallel sampling outperforming sequential across three
model families on mathematics and coding, attributing it to reduced exploration when a chain conditions
on its own previous answers.
    NYU Physics (February 2026) created a continuous classical time crystal with a pair of acoustically
levitated beads: “Unless the particles have identical scattering properties, their wave-mediated interactions
are nonreciprocal”, and pairs can use this nonreciprocity “to harvest energy from the wave to sustain

steady-state oscillations despite viscous drag and the absence of periodic driving” (Morrell et al. 2026).
The oscillation is a linearly stable limit cycle. In one experiment the antisymmetric mode’s coherence time
is 𝜏 = (100 ± 10) s, about 6,700 cycles at 66.787 Hz, comparable to the 108 s recording, and the authors
add: “Sustained oscillations persist for hours longer than the measurement window, which means that the
true coherence time of the nonequilibrium steady state may be substantially larger.” Every recording the
paper reports is of a steady state, and no growth exponent has been measured in this system.
    The COGITATE Consortium (2025) found information about conscious content in visual, ventro-
temporal and inferior frontal cortex, content-specific decoding in posterior regions generalising for about
the duration of the stimulus (0.5 to 1.5 s) while in prefrontal regions its temporal generalisation was
restricted to about 0.2 to 0.4 s (Cogitate Consortium et al. 2025). COGITATE tested two theories of con-
sciousness, not a recursion depth, and its results challenge key tenets of both; this paper reads the sustained,
content-specific posterior responses as structurally consistent with depth-dependent, feedback-driven
processing, which COGITATE did not measure.
    These programmes address different problems in different physical substrates. They report different
things: exponential suppression of error in quantum error correction, a contested sequential advantage in
AI reasoning, a sustained steady-state limit cycle in a classical time crystal, and no measured form in
neuroscience. They are separate programmes in separate fields.
    Yet, on this paper’s reading, they share a structural pattern: in each, recursive or recurrent processing
(where the output of one cycle becomes the input for the next), operating on structured asymmetry, is
what the reported gain or order depends on. Those programmes are cited for convergence and not for
mechanism: whether any of their systems accumulates, meaning retains something from each pass whose
contribution grows with depth, rather than merely conditioning on the previous pass, is untested in each
case. The question this paper addresses is whether that pattern reflects a single underlying principle or
mere coincidence.

1.2   The ARC Equation
We formalise the question as follows. For AI systems, the equation is

                                                𝑈 = 𝐼 × 𝑅 𝛼,                                                (1)

the ARC Equation (AI power-law form). Separately, the ARC Bound is the registration’s claim that the
ceiling of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, a law under test, is at most two for correctors of the system’s own
class; Paper X supersedes this fixed-exponent framing as the operative safety criterion and re-scopes,
without retracting, the equation and the bound (Section 6.4). Here 𝛼 is the exponent of the declared
capability observable; the error-suppression fit reported below is a different estimand (Section 3.1). The
two is not a property of the equation: it is the value Law III’s ceiling, 1/(1 − 𝛾), returns where the
correction-leverage exponent 𝛾 is one half, a point value the registration conjectures and does not assert
(Section 6.4). Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, a law under test, is stated as the registration states it,
𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘: correction has the asymptotic scaling advantage where its exponent exceeds the drift exponent,
burden has it where the correction exponent is the smaller, and at equality coefficients, delay, backlog and
saturation decide the outcome. In Paper X’s minimal model, with positive gain drift, 𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘 decides
whether the misalignment fraction vanishes, and with level drift as well the condition is 𝛽𝐶 > max(𝑘, 0).

Appendix D.1), 𝐼 denotes base potential (structured asymmetry; the notation register’s base intelligence,
what the system could do before any self-improvement), 𝑅 denotes recursive depth, and 𝛼 denotes the
scaling exponent. Within the stated model this paper derives 𝛼 rather than fitting it: the asymptotic
exponent follows from the self-referential coupling 𝛽 𝐿 via 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ). That the exponent is bounded
by two is not a result of this model: it belongs to Law III, and Section 6.4 states it as the registration

2    Related work, antecedents and the rival instrument
The formalism rests on mathematics far older than this programme, and much of its cross-domain evidence
is other researchers’ work, beside the programme’s own experiment, comparisons and pilot (Sections 4.3,
6.1 and 6.5); this section states each debt in the source’s own terms and then weighs the nearest work.

Exact equations, asymptotic scaling and noisy observations. Cauchy’s functional equations supply
the exact families (Cauchy 1821); stability results impose their own domain and error assumptions (Hyers
1941; Baker et al. 1979). Regular variation is an established setting for asymptotic behaviour near powers
(Bingham et al. 1987); Bingham and Ostaszewski (2015) connect Cauchy and related functional equations
with asymptotic limits under explicit side conditions. These results motivate separating an exact identity
from an asymptotic approximation and an empirical fit. None establishes a universal scaling form for
arbitrary recursive updates.

Fitting is part of the hypothesis. Xiao et al. (2011) show why the observation-error model matters
when choosing between log-transformed regression and nonlinear regression. Model comparisons must
refer to the same measured response with comparable likelihoods. Appendix E therefore requires the
error model, diagnostics and uncertainty procedure to be specified before selecting a scaling family.

The dimensional formula. The physical models discussed in Section 6.4 have different observables and
premises (West et al. 1997, 2004; Banavar et al. 1999, 2010; Demetrius 2008; Demetrius and Tuszynski
2010; Zhao 2022; Bettencourt 2013). The conditional transport result identifies when 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1) follows for
delivery against flow-length cost; a general common derivation for metabolic, urban and other exponents
remains unproved. Neither that formula nor the classical Cauchy families are claimed as new.

The forms, conceded. The relation 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) of Theorem 2 is the exponent of the elementary
solution of the separable equation Axiom 2 states. The same structure, a stock that grows at a rate
proportional to a power below one of itself, is the knowledge production function of Jones (1995), whose
long-run growth rate carries the factor 1/(1 − 𝜙); the expression 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) is also the sum of a geometric
series, familiar as the Keynes multiplier (Keynes 1936) and as Dyson resummation (Dyson 1949), as
Paper X’s novelty ledger records of this paper. Axiom 2’s name, cumulative advantage, is the term of

The four programmes and their neighbours. The programmes of Section 1.1 are cited for convergence,
not mechanism. A photonic metamaterial analogue of a continuous time crystal (Liu et al. 2023) and a
continuous space-time crystal state driven by nonreciprocal optical forces (Raskatla et al. 2024) have
also been reported; recurrent processing as a basis of consciousness has its own literature (Lamme 2006;
Zheng et al. 2025); and the scaling of test-time compute in language models has been studied directly
(Snell et al. 2024; Zeng et al. 2025; Li et al. 2025).

The pipes premise, with its contest. The premise that transport networks set the pace of biological
growth is not this programme’s claim to defend; it is the premise of a leading account in quantitative biology,
and a contested one. West, Brown and Enquist derived the allometric scaling laws of biology from the
geometry of nutrient-distribution networks (West et al. 1997; one of the most cited papers in its field, with
an OpenAlex citation count of 5,046 as of 12 August 2026, a single-source and index-dependent count).
The exact exponent remains contested: White and Seymour report basal metabolic rate proportional to
body mass to the two-thirds power rather than three-quarters (White and Seymour 2003), and Kozłowski
and Konarzewski have twice asked whether the model is “mathematically correct and biologically relevant”
(Kozłowski and Konarzewski 2004, 2005). This programme’s premise needs only the mechanism, that the
delivery network sets the ceiling, and not any particular exponent; the dispute over the exponent’s value
leaves it untouched, but the critics question the mechanism too, so the programme takes it as a premise,
and its biological reading stands or falls with it.

The exception West’s own group found. The escape from the pipe limit is also not this programme’s
assertion; it is measured in the urban scaling literature by the same senior author. Bettencourt, Lobo,
Helbing, Kühnert, and West (2007) report, in the paper’s own words (their 𝛽 is a city-scaling exponent,
not this paper’s coupling 𝛽 𝐿 ): “Quantities reflecting wealth creation and innovation have 𝛽 ≈ 1.2 > 1
(increasing returns), whereas those accounting for infrastructure display 𝛽 ≈ 0.8 < 1 (economies of scale).”
Infrastructure, the literal pipes, scales sublinearly; information-mediated activity scales superlinearly.
They state the contrast with biology directly: “we discuss how cities are similar to, and differ from,
biological organisms, for which 𝛽 < 1”. They derive “growth equations, which quantify the dramatic
difference between growth fueled by innovation versus that driven by economies of scale” (spelling as
in the original). And their closing inference names the consequence: “This difference suggests that, as
population grows, major innovation cycles must be generated at a continually accelerating rate to sustain
growth and avoid stagnation or collapse.” On this paper’s reading, that is the shape of the open problem
this framework addresses: in the findings of West’s group, information-mediated growth escapes the
throughput limit, and the only brake in their framework is external and must be applied again and again,
faster and faster. No internal limit is derived. The replacement limit, a ceiling belonging to the growing
system itself, is the gap.

The pipes argument at fixed throughput. Everything that grows is fed through a channel, and for
everything before software, fixing the channel fixes the growth. Starve a tumour’s blood supply and
its growth is held back. Fix a chain reaction’s fuel and geometry and it stops. Exhaust an epidemic’s
susceptible hosts and it burns out. Astrophysics has a named, quantitative constraint of the same kind: the
Eddington limit, a luminosity ceiling where radiation pressure balances gravity, not a hard ceiling on
accretion, since ultraluminous X-ray sources sit well above it (Bachetti et al. 2014). The claim here is a
generalisation of limits physics already accepts, not a new species of assertion. Two hard cases are handled

deliberately. Cosmic inflation is excluded by scope: it is expansion of space, not growth of a structure on a
substrate, and it ended by field dynamics rather than by running out of anything. Evolution is the sharpest
case and it fits: biological complexity increased for billions of years at roughly fixed solar throughput,
which is growth on information at fixed pipes, and it is also glacial, and it has no internal corrector at all,

physical substrate fixed and asks what still grows, which is why exogenous speed-ups are excluded by
definition. The functional form of capability growth in recursive depth remains an empirical question,

registered rivals, “the logarithmic form, the exponential form, a saturating form, a broken or piecewise
power law with one change of regime, and the shifted finite-window solution of this framework’s own
growth equation”, and a declining outcome is admissible.

The rival instrument. The nearest work to this programme’s question, and the right paper to weigh it
against, is Engels et al. (2025), first posted 25 April 2025. It asks how oversight itself scales and answers
quantitatively: oversight success is modelled as a game between capability-mismatched players whose
oversight-specific Elo is a piecewise-linear function of general intelligence with two plateaus, and optimal
numbers of oversight levels are derived for Nested Scalable Oversight, in which trusted models oversee
stronger untrusted models that then become the trusted models at the next step. The instrument is the
difference. Their variable is the capability gap between overseer and overseen, measured in Elo. This
programme’s variable is the composition class of the corrector, measured through the corrector’s own
scaling exponent. They also state the design condition in slope form: oversight improves with capability
when the overseer’s game-specific Elo rises faster with general Elo than the overseen system’s (𝑚 𝐺 > 𝑚 𝐻 ),
and they recommend iterating an oversight design until it does. Their framework has no term for what the
overseer is made of, and this paper’s formalism has none either. The programme’s separate prediction,
outside this paper’s model, is that iterated same-class oversight, of which Nested Scalable Oversight is an
instance, stays capped however many rungs are added, while a cross-class corrector is not held to that cap.
The theory registration’s P6 states the cap for same-class correctors, at or below one half, as a conjecture
on the correction-leverage exponent of Law III (the ARC Ceiling, a law under test), an exponent the
registration keeps distinct from Law II’s 𝛽𝐶 . That is where the two programmes could disagree about a
measurable quantity, and this paper does not decide it.

Antecedents and near-misses, each with its differential. On the question: Hutter asked directly
whether intelligence can explode (Hutter 2012, arXiv, 28 February 2012), undertaking to separate speed
from intelligence explosion and to consider possible bounds on intelligence, augmenting the analysis of
Chalmers (2010); the question itself goes back to Good (1966). What that literature does not contain is a
derived ceiling with an architecture dependence; growth exponents of the form used here are older, as
this section has conceded. On impossibility: three 2025 arXiv papers argue for limits on alignment or
its control (Yao 2025b, public by 13 June 2025, attested by arXiv and independently observed by the
Internet Archive; Yao 2025a, 3 July 2025; and Ball et al. 2025, 9 July 2025). All three are worst-case
and qualitative: measure zero, coNP-completeness, cryptographic hardness. Their measured object is
verification of a fixed system, and it is closed within its own terms; this programme’s measured object
is a different one, persistence of alignment as capability compounds under recursive amplification, and

closure of the first does not settle the second. None reports an average-case scaling exponent or a rate.
The third concludes that “safety cannot be achieved by designing filters external to the LLM internals
(architecture and weights)” and argues that “an aligned AI system’s intelligence cannot be separated from
its judgment”; that argument from filtering intractability is consistent with this programme’s architecture

The claim is that architecture determines whether anti-correlation is available at all, and that this caps
a safety-relevant exponent. The nearest structural analogue is the quantum error-correction threshold
theorem (Aharonov and Ben-Or 2008), which also converts a qualitative worry into a critical value; it
concerns physical error rates in a fixed architecture, not a corrector’s scaling exponent, so it is a near-miss
rather than an occupant, and the analogy was identified by this programme’s own search rather than by
a referee. On feedback and stability, the general proposition that inadequate corrective gain relative to
system gain causes instability has a long control-theory lineage (small-gain theorems; Zames 1966); those
are gain conditions on interconnected systems, not a power-law exponent criterion on a corrector’s scaling
with the capability of a recursively improving system. The nearest quantitative neighbour is Liu and Meng
(2026), which recasts self-correction as a closed-loop control problem via a two-state Markov model
and derives a directly measurable stability threshold, iterate only when the error-correction rate over the
error-introduction rate exceeds Acc/(1 − Acc). The differential: theirs is a per-step rate threshold at a
fixed capability level, deciding whether another iteration helps now; this programme’s criterion is a scaling
relation across capability, deciding whether corrective strength keeps pace as the system improves. Theirs
has no comparison of correction and drift exponents across capability, the quantities the programme’s
co-scaling criterion compares; the two models meet where growth stops, as Paper X shows. Smolin’s
cosmological natural selection (Smolin 1992) is the antecedent for selection-shaped universes, and the
programme’s own December-era notes cite it contemporaneously.

Feedback, resources and elapsed time. Cunningham et al. (2026) distinguish algorithmic improvement,
capability and aggregate economic effects; feedback elasticities and bottlenecks determine whether a
narrow acceleration propagates. This is relevant to the programme’s distinction between recursive depth
and elapsed time. It is not a measurement of the correction exponent or a derivation of the ARC Ceiling.
Comparing the models requires an explicit mapping of observables, clocks and fixed resources; no
equivalence or confirmation is claimed here.

A programme hypothesis beyond the model. Oversight arrangements that place a human in the loop,
as amplification and reinforcement learning from human feedback do, are cross-class by construction,
because the human corrector does not share the model’s substrate. The programme therefore hypothesises,
beyond what this paper’s equations decide, that human-in-the-loop oversight shows a higher correction
exponent than pure-model oversight, for a structural reason rather than a sentimental one. This is testable
on existing data and does not require new systems.

Proof. Suppose 𝑈 = 𝑓 (𝐼) + ℎ(𝑟), with depth counted in added passes, 𝑟 = 𝑅 − 1. By Axiom 1:
𝑈 = 𝐼 × 𝑔(𝑟) with 𝑔(0) = 1. At 𝑟 = 0 (𝑅 = 1): 𝑓 (𝐼) + ℎ(0) = 𝐼, so 𝑓 (𝐼) = 𝐼 − ℎ(0). Substituting:
𝐼 × 𝑔(𝑟) = 𝐼 + [ℎ(𝑟) − ℎ(0)], giving 𝑔(𝑟) = 1 + [ℎ(𝑟) − ℎ(0)]/𝐼. Since 𝑔 depends only on depth
(Axiom 1), but the right side depends on 𝐼, we require ℎ(𝑟) = ℎ(0) for all 𝑟, giving 𝑔(𝑟) = 1 (no
amplification). But Axiom 2 with 𝛽 𝐿 > 0 guarantees 𝑑𝑔/𝑑𝑟 = 𝑎 · 𝑔 𝛽𝐿 = 𝑎 > 0 at 𝑟 = 0, so 𝑔 is
non-constant. Contradiction.                                                                    □

     This result has a concrete interpretation. A recursive system with zero base quality (𝐼 = 0) produces
zero capability regardless of depth, and a system at zero added passes (𝑟 = 0, the first pass) produces only
its base potential, unamplified. Complexity emerges from the interaction between structured input and
recursive processing (neither alone is sufficient).

4.2   Property-by-property evidence
Property 1: threshold behaviour. Quantum: The surface code threshold 𝑝 thr ≈ 1% is a sharp phase
boundary. Above it (𝑝 > 𝑝 thr ), a larger code distance makes performance worse. Below it (𝑝 < 𝑝 thr ), each
increase of two in code distance suppresses the logical error rate by a further factor, so the suppression
is exponential in distance. This is the most precisely measured threshold in the evidence base: Google
varied physical error rates through controlled error injection and observed the threshold directly (Google
Quantum AI and Collaborators 2024).
    Physics: The time crystal’s exceptional point (where two eigenvalues of the stability matrix coalesce)
marks, on this paper’s reading, the transition from symmetric (bounded) to antisymmetric (compounding)
dynamics. Below threshold: active oscillator. At threshold: exceptional point. Above threshold: time
crystal (Morrell et al. 2026). The exceptional point is computed from the source’s model; none is measured.
Here and throughout this section, the time crystal is a consistency check and a cross-domain analogy, not
a test of the framework, and no study of it is registered.
    AI: The sequential-over-parallel comparison in Paper II is observed precursor evidence, with one
interpretable sequential fit and a second comparison the paper classes as unreliable (Section 4.3). Its arms
were not matched in compute. Comparing two procedures does not establish a threshold in a continuously
varied parameter.

Property 2: recursive depth dependence. Quantum: Logical error rate decreases as 𝜀 𝑑 ∝ Λ− (𝑑+1)/2
with code distance 𝑑. Each increase of two in 𝑑 enlarges the code’s lattice of physical qubits, and the
suppression factor Λ applies for each such step (Google Quantum AI and Collaborators 2024); the paper
reads code distance as its depth variable by analogy, since the surface code is not a concatenated, recursive
code.
    AI: Sharma and Chopra (2025) tested seven voting methods for aggregating sequential chain outputs.
Methods favouring later reasoning steps (inverse-entropy weighting) achieved optimal performance
in 97% of configurations; methods favouring earlier steps achieved optimality in only 17%. This
80-percentage-point gap indicates that later recursive steps carry systematically more information about
the correct answer, consistent with capability that compounds with depth (Sharma and Chopra 2025).
    Physics: In the source’s linear stability analysis the antisymmetric (breathing) mode grows when the
pair lies in its active region, “leading to spontaneous breathing-mode oscillations” (Morrell et al. 2026).
Every recording is of a steady state, so that growth has not been observed and no dependence on cycle
count has been measured.

quality (𝑝). Worse qubits (𝑝 closer to 𝑝 thr ) give Λ → 1 and error correction barely helps. Active leakage
removal (DQLR), which maintains qubit quality during operation, improved Λ by 35% at distance 5

5.1   AI: multiplicative composition
A later reasoning step can use an earlier answer, but that fact alone establishes neither 𝑔(𝑅1 𝑅2 ) =
𝑔(𝑅1 )𝑔(𝑅2 ) nor the cumulative-advantage ODE. The proposed model is that the rate of gain depends
on the current amplification by the specified power, at fixed resources and parameters. That is what an
independent estimate of 𝛽 𝐿 and a held-out trajectory would test.
    The 𝛽 𝐿 -dynamics equation 𝑑𝑔/𝑑𝑟 = 𝑎𝑔 𝛽𝐿 models this directly. If 𝛽 𝐿 ≈ 0.5 (moderate coupling),
Theorem 2 gives 𝛼 ≈ 2; the available error-suppression fit is a different estimand (Section 3.1).
    Two derivations reach that value and they are not the same derivation, which this paper now states
rather than leaves to be noticed. Theorem 2 gives the exponent the coupling sustains, 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ),
where 𝛽 𝐿 is the elasticity of marginal amplification in Axiom 2. The ceiling is that of Law III, the ARC
Ceiling, a law under test, 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾), and it comes from the correction shortfall rather than from the
coupling, where 𝛾 is the correction-leverage exponent (the registration’s letter, written 𝜒 in the notation
register). Both return two when their exponent is one half; in general they agree exactly where 𝛽 𝐿 = 𝛾 on
the same system, which is an open question and is preregistered by timestamp as one (first anchored on 12

one and the same system and tests whether the asymptotic growth exponent the coupling sustains respects
the ceiling the correction exponent sets (𝛽 𝐿 ≤ 𝛾), and discloses in advance the precision that test needs.
Written in the coupling, the stability condition 𝛼 ≤ 𝛼crit reads 𝛽 𝐿 ≤ 𝛾, which says that a system stays
correctable exactly while the leverage it takes per step does not outrun the capacity it has to correct what
that leverage costs, under the conditions the registration states for Law III (Section 6.4).

5.2   Quantum: additive composition
In surface code quantum error correction, each increase of two in code distance multiplies the suppression
of the logical error rate by the same factor Λ. The surface code is not concatenated: a larger distance
enlarges one lattice, and syndrome extraction repeats in time, cycle by cycle, not distance by distance;
the paper reads code distance as its depth variable by analogy. The gains accumulate additively (in code
distance) while composing multiplicatively (in error suppression).
    For the conditional common-Λ model, first choose a reference distance 𝑑0 and count added pairs
of distance as 𝑛 = (𝑑 − 𝑑0 )/2. The normalised suppression factor is 𝑓 (𝑛) = 𝜀 𝑑0 /𝜀 𝑑0 +2𝑛 = Λ𝑛 when
the prefactor is fixed. It satisfies 𝑓 (𝑛1 + 𝑛2 ) = 𝑓 (𝑛1 ) 𝑓 (𝑛2 ) on the admissible integer increments. A
continuous exponential interpolation additionally assumes the regularity of Theorem 1(2). The measured
Λ = 2.14 ± 0.02 is the source’s suppression factor for each increase of two in code distance (Google
Quantum AI and Collaborators 2024).
    As functions of a common mathematical coordinate, an exponential with positive rate eventually
exceeds any fixed power. That ordering alone does not rank quantum error correction above AI reasoning:
code distance, recursive depth, observables and resource costs differ. This paper uses the quantum
comparison as an analogy; Λ is the source’s measured error-suppression factor, not a capability-growth
exponent.

5.3   Physics: bounded composition
In the time crystal, the source’s linear stability analysis gives a growing antisymmetric (breathing) mode
for pairs in its active region; higher-order damping, which that analysis leaves out, can stabilise the
growing mode, and the pair settles into steady-state oscillation on a linearly stable limit cycle (Morrell
et al. 2026).
    This is bounded composition, on this paper’s reading of the system: 𝑄 𝑟+1 = min(𝑄 𝑟 + 𝛿𝑄 𝑟 , 𝑄 max ).

The strongest test of this framework is not whether it describes systems already studied. It is whether it

Forward prediction. Identify a candidate recursive system not used to select the framework, for example

proposed relation; that the missing network explains them is this paper’s reading, to be derived for each
system rather than inferred from the numbers.

8    What this paper does and does not show
The contribution is a cross-domain framework within the ARC Theory and Recursive Dynamics:
conditional mathematical results, explicit limits, and a prospective test of whether composition information
improves scaling predictions. The classical functional-equation families are established mathematics.
Their application to a particular recursive system requires a separate argument. The cumulative-advantage
ODE supplies a shifted power law and an asymptotic exponent on its stated premises; it does not make
those premises true of an AI, organism or physical device.

The empirical standing. The March 2026 runs are precursor evidence that decides no registered
proposition (Section 4.3). A sequential advantage was observed only where both exponents could be
estimated, while Gu et al. (2026) report the opposite direction in their comparison. The one interpretable
error-suppression fit is inconclusive; its metric is defined in Section 3.1. The alignment comparison
cannot decide F13 (Section 6.5). These observations do not validate the exponent identity, a crossover
criterion or a universal ceiling. The mathematical corrections in this revision are evaluated through their
assumptions and proofs, separately from those measurements.
    The framework’s predictive usefulness remains to be demonstrated. Appendix E sets out the
prospective design; any study applying it needs its own fixed plan before observations. Section 7
retains the earlier criteria with their current dispositions, including the corrected F4 and F7. The former
non-claims table and contribution wording are preserved in Appendix B as superseded history.

9    Limitations
Evidential limitations. The earlier estimate of about 2.2 is retracted, and the one interpretable fit of the
v5 cross-architecture experiment, Gemini 3 Flash’s, is what survived it, one model’s fit and inconclusive,
not a correction of the same measurement (Section 4.3); the five capability runs of March 2026 are
precursor evidence that decides no registered proposition. No 𝛼 has been measured in time crystals. The
COGITATE connection is structural, not quantitative. The 𝛽 𝐿 -to-𝛼 relationship is an identity proved
in Appendix A.2 and checked numerically (𝑅 2 = 1.00000000), an internal consistency check and not a
validation of the relation in any system; the empirical measurement of 𝛽 𝐿 in physical systems requires
independent confirmation. The 𝑅 ∗ crossover is untested empirically. The former numerical 𝛽 𝐿 bands
have no established phase-boundary interpretation; Section 5.4 separates the ODE parameters from the
functional-equation premises. Conjecture 5 (⊕ transitions) has descriptive local-estimate checks in three
computational systems, beside a Bernoulli control (Appendix F.6), and untested in physical experiments.

The five-property test (Theorem 4) did not reach statistical significance (𝑝 = 0.095) and requires larger
samples. The alignment run of March 2026, a single unregistered run, cannot decide criterion F13: its
statistics are rank correlations and contrasts, not exponents, and its three flat and negative runs are the
three the October 2026 code review found defective (Section 6.5).

Blind prediction testing. A blind prediction test was conducted on three computational systems
(Barabási-Albert networks, gradient descent with momentum, Kuramoto oscillators). The measured 𝛼
values were 3 to 20× smaller than predicted by 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ). However, forensic analysis identified
two independent confounds: (1) the numerical-derivative 𝛽 𝐿 estimation method is fatally biased, giving
𝛽 𝐿 ≈ 0.95 regardless of true 𝛽 𝐿 , even for pure Bernoulli systems with 3 per cent Gaussian noise added;
(2) none of the tested systems satisfy Axiom 2 (constant coupling coefficient 𝑎), and the BA network’s
effective coupling decreases ∼ 50× over the simulation. Applied to systems built to satisfy Axiom 2, the
linearisation method recovers the relation with 𝑅 2 = 0.9999, which checks the method, not the prediction
for the three systems tested. The prospective predictions failed on those systems. The subsequent
diagnostics can explain why that design was not a clean test of the conditional identity; they do not make
the predictions successful. A new blind test with the method fixed in advance is required, and identifying
natural systems that satisfy the axioms remains the central empirical challenge. Full methodology and
forensic analysis are available as supplementary material (see Paper III).

Theoretical limitations. The framework formalises the pattern of recursive amplification but does
not identify the mechanism. Theorem 2 is the solution of the growth equation Axiom 2 assumes, and
Theorem 2a follows from the product form Axiom 1 assumes; their weight lies in what they make
measurable, and the axioms themselves are untested in any physical system. It tells you what happens
(scaling exponents, crossover depths, functional forms) but not why at a microscopic level (analogous
to thermodynamics before statistical mechanics). Connecting the composition operator to microscopic
dynamics remains an open problem.


10    Conclusion
The central question is whether a description of how gains combine can predict a later scaling curve. The
paper supplies conditional mathematical results and a prospective way to test their usefulness. Cauchy’s
equations constrain families under stated hypotheses; the cumulative-advantage ODE gives a shifted
power law, an asymptotic exponent and a coordinate-dependent characteristic scale. Neither a fixed update
rule nor frozen model weights fixes an empirical exponent.
     Every system discussed in this paper (silicon neural networks, superconducting qubits, millimetre
polymer beads, cortical neural circuits) maintains a low-entropy state far from equilibrium and, on this
paper’s reading, feeds its outputs back into its inputs. Under the cumulative-advantage model their gains
would change smoothly from approximately linear towards compounding over a characteristic scale of
depth (Theorem 3), universal in structure and domain-specific in form on the framework’s premises; no
such scale has yet been measured in any of them (criterion F7).
     The present record does not establish a universal law across domains. The AI error-suppression estimate
is inconclusive. The biological comparisons require distinct observables and independent dimension
assignments. The physical analogies and exact reference relations motivate questions without validating
the proposed common mechanism. Unit 83 supplies a qualified result for its own correction-placement
contrast, with the full limits in Section 8; it decides none of this paper’s scaling or ceiling claims.
     The ARC Ceiling remains a law under test whose conditional premises are stated in Section 6.4. A
declining error fraction, an absolute error burden and growth in elapsed time are different questions. No


                                                    38
                                     Recursive amplification across domains


safety guarantee follows from a fitted exponent. The next contribution should be a locked prediction that
succeeds or fails on held-out trajectories against serious alternatives, with the outcome published either way.
    If the predictions fail, we will have learned where the structural analogy breaks down. If they hold,
we will have identified a principle that connects intelligence, computation, and the physics of order across
substrates. Intelligence may not be a property of particular materials. It may be what happens where
recursion compounds its own gains, a hypothesis this paper states and does not establish.

Conjectural extrapolation. The furthest reading of the framework comes last, as a conclusion the
mathematics does not deny rather than a result: Law I, a law under test, taken to the limit of capability
does not rule out a universe or its creation. Established physics asks about both in its own terms. On the
universe’s settings, Steven Weinberg wrote: “Astronomical observations indicate that the cosmological
constant is many orders of magnitude smaller than estimated in modern theories of elementary particles”
(Weinberg 1989). On the formation of a new universe, Blau, Guendelman and Guth found that, for a range
of initial conditions, a region of false vacuum inflates, and that “an observer in the interior will describe a
closed universe which completely disconnects from the original spacetime” (Blau et al. 1987). Neither work
concerns intelligence or this framework, and neither supports the reading. No measurement in this paper
bears on that reading, no prediction of it is registered, and no test of it is in view, so it is graded as conjecture
and is not established. Its premise is testable: the reading needs the framework to hold beyond AI, and
the forward prediction of Section 6.1 (registered as P18) and criterion F10 test whether the composition
description predicts held-out scaling in other systems; their failure would remove that premise. The reading
belongs to the long-horizon interpretive layer of Infinite Architects (Eastwood 2026a), requires independent
argument and evidence beyond what is reported here, and is a reason for nothing else in this paper.
    The predictions are specified. The falsification criteria are public. The data will decide.

A.1    Proof of Theorem 1 (classification)
Case 1: multiplicative composition.
Proof. Hypothesis: 𝑔 : R+ → R+ continuous with 𝑔(1) = 1 and 𝑔(𝑅1 · 𝑅2 ) = 𝑔(𝑅1 ) · 𝑔(𝑅2 ).
   Define ℎ(𝑥) = ln 𝑔(𝑒 𝑥 ). Then:

                 ℎ(𝑥 + 𝑦) = ln 𝑔(𝑒 𝑥+𝑦 ) = ln 𝑔(𝑒 𝑥 · 𝑒 𝑦 ) = ln[𝑔(𝑒 𝑥 ) · 𝑔(𝑒 𝑦 )] = ℎ(𝑥) + ℎ(𝑦)            (A.1)

This is Cauchy’s additive functional equation. Under the continuity of 𝑔 (hence ℎ), every solution has
the form ℎ(𝑥) = 𝛼𝑥 for a constant 𝛼; monotonicity makes 𝛼 ≥ 0. Therefore ln 𝑔(𝑒 𝑥 ) = 𝛼𝑥, giving
𝑔(𝑅) = 𝑅 𝛼 .                                                                                        □

Case 2: additive composition.
Proof. Hypothesis: 𝑓 : [0, ∞) → R+ continuous with 𝑓 (0) = 1 and 𝑓 (𝑟 1 + 𝑟 2 ) = 𝑓 (𝑟 1 ) · 𝑓 (𝑟 2 ), in the
added passes 𝑟 = 𝑅 − 1.
    Taking logarithms: ln 𝑓 (𝑟 1 +𝑟 2 ) = ln 𝑓 (𝑟 1 ) +ln 𝑓 (𝑟 2 ). This is again Cauchy’s additive equation, whose
continuous solutions have the form ln 𝑓 (𝑟) = 𝛼𝑟, with 𝛼 ≥ 0 under monotonicity, giving 𝑓 (𝑟) = 𝑒 𝛼𝑟 , so
that 𝑔(𝑅) = 𝑓 (𝑅 − 1) = 𝑒 𝛼(𝑅−1) and 𝑔(1) = 1, as Axiom 1 requires.                                              □

E      A prospective measurement protocol
This protocol asks whether composition predicts fresh observations better than rival models. It is a
prospective design proposal. Existing registrations retain their own definitions, margins and rules.
    Step 1: fix the system and quantity. Name the observable, its units, the population of tasks or
physical states, the depth origin and the resource budget. State whether weights, code, memory, prompts
or tools may change. Define usable performance and any error burden separately. A common letter does
not make accuracy, metabolic rate and physical error suppression commensurable.
    Step 2: measure the baseline and justify composition. Measure 𝐼 at the declared first pass in
the same units as 𝑈. Specify how a composition class or parameter is assigned before inspecting the


                                                      81
                                  Recursive amplification across domains


validation trajectory. Use mechanistic evidence, independent observers blinded to the answer where
appropriate, or an expressly separated calibration sample. Record the rule, exclusions and uncertainty; an
assignment inferred from the observed exponent is not a prediction.
    Step 3: lock the prediction. From that information, specify the candidate family, the parameter
prediction and interval, and an operational crossover if one is claimed. Freeze the held-out data split,
resource controls, measurement schedule and analysis before validation. Parameter estimates and
validation outcomes must come from disjoint records or a sealed early-window/later-window design. A
transform of the same fitted curve is an internal check, not independent confirmation.
    Step 4: establish what the design can distinguish. Size and place the observations by a design
analysis that reports its sensitivity to the named alternatives, noise, saturation and dependence. A small
depth ladder is not automatically decisive. Predetermine the tolerances, multiplicity treatment, failure
conditions and inconclusive outcomes. A post-hoc change of domain or effective dimension cannot rescue
a failed prediction.
    Step 5: compare adequate rivals on a common response. Include the exact power, the shifted
cumulative-advantage solution, exponential, logarithmic, saturating and broken-power candidates where
justified. Respect the baseline: for example 𝑔(𝑅) = 𝑒 𝜆(𝑅−1) and 𝑔(𝑅) = 1 + 𝑐 log 𝑅 satisfy 𝑔(1) = 1;
unshifted versions need not. Prespecify observation-error models and comparable likelihoods, including
transformation Jacobians where required, before using information criteria (Xiao et al. 2011). Evaluate
held-out prediction as well as fit. For a claimed mechanism, specify an intervention that distinguishes it
from a descriptive curve with the same shape.
    Step 6: report the whole test. Publish the locked prediction, code version, inclusion record,
observations, uncertainty and all deviations. Report supported, refuted or inconclusive only under the
registered rule. A failed predictive test is evidence about that prediction even when a later diagnostic
identifies misspecification; the diagnostic motivates a new test and does not turn failure into support.
    The earlier six-step protocol is retained in Appendix B. Its proposed common entropy unit was not
validated, and its bare curve-selection instructions did not specify an observation-error model. This
revision makes neither a new data analysis nor a change to a study already registered.

F.2   Theorem 2 validation (𝛽 𝐿 → 𝛼 identity)
The relationship 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) was tested against 30 exact Bernoulli ODE solutions with 𝛽 𝐿 spanning

F.3   Theorem 2a validation (non-additivity)
The non-additivity proof was verified numerically. For multiple (𝐼1 , 𝐼2 , 𝑅1 , 𝑅2 ) combinations across
all 𝛽 𝐿 ∈ (0, 1): if 𝑈 were additive (𝑈 = 𝑓 (𝐼) + ℎ(𝑅)), then 𝑈 (𝐼1 , 𝑅1 ) − 𝑈 (𝐼1 , 𝑅2 ) would equal

makes those differences unequal. This checks a consequence of the assumed product form; it does not
establish that form empirically.
    Scope of the recorded check. The reported numerical examples concern non-additivity under a
product model. They do not prove the earlier universal claim for 𝑆 = 𝑈 (𝐼, 𝑅)/[𝐼 + ((1 − 𝛽 𝐿 )𝑎𝑅) 𝛼 ], which

interpretable. The non-additivity theorem follows from varying 𝐼 and a non-constant 𝑔 on a product
domain, not from 𝑆 > 1.

F.5   Theorem 4 validation (five properties)
Ten recursive systems and five non-recursive controls were scored on the five derived properties. Recursive

are required.

F.8   Failures and boundary cases
Two results fell short of expectations:
     𝑅 ∗ absolute value: The historical 62% figure and the 2 to 7% figure above remain unresolved and

case where the Bernoulli ODE model does not apply.
     These failures are reported for completeness and scientific integrity. They bound the framework’s
applicability and inform future refinement.
