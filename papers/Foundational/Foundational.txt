                                                        Abstract
           I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it
       reasons; only proof will move the world. If control of a capable AI system were lost and could not
       be won back, what the system itself carries would decide what it does. This paper asks whether
       recursion amplifies capability in the same way across very different fields, so that a self-improving
       system’s growth could be predicted before it scales. Between 2024 and 2026, programmes in AI
       reasoning, quantum error correction, acoustic physics and consciousness science reported results
       that bear on recursive or recurrent processing, in different forms. We formalise the question with
       three axioms (separability, cumulative advantage, continuity) and a recursive composition operator.
       Under each case’s hypotheses, Cauchy’s functional equations give multiplicative composition a power
       law and additive composition an exponential; capped composition stays bounded. Inside the model
       the asymptotic exponent is derived, not fitted: 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) for coupling 𝛽 𝐿 ; base potential and
       depth must interact multiplicatively; and a crossover depth 𝑅 ∗ = 𝛼/𝑎, independent of base potential,
       separates approximately linear from compounding gains. Five structural properties follow, two on
       premises added to the axioms. The ARC Bound is stated as registered: the ceiling of Law III, the
       ARC Ceiling, a law under test, is at most two for same-class correctors, and the point value two is
       a conjecture. The evidence is graded: the AI exponent’s one interpretable fit is inconclusive, the
       time crystal is an analogy with no exponent measured, and the neuroscience mapping is structural.
       Thirteen falsification criteria and a cross-domain forward prediction are stated; a computational suite
       checks the closed forms but tests no real system. Each result holds inside its stated model.

1    Introduction
I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons;
only proof will move the world. If control of a capable AI system were lost and could not be won back,
what the system itself carries would decide what it does. The programme’s position is that people keep
oversight, containment and shutdown for as long as they physically can, and never give them up by choice;
correction built into a system is the contingency for control lost against our will, not a reason to let go
of it. If the way a recursive process combines its gains fixes how its capability grows with depth, then
the scaling of a self-improving system could be predicted from a property measured before it scales: if
you can measure the composition operator in a new system, you can predict its scaling function before
measuring the full scaling curve (Section 3.2). On that reading, structural alignment (embedding values
within the composition operator itself; the values are for international consensus, Section 6.5) is to be
built while systems remain frozen and their scaling is predictable, and judged on whether it keeps a
system useful as well as safe. This paper asks whether recursion amplifies capability in the same way
across very different fields: is the pattern that separate research programmes have reported (Section 1.1) a
coincidence, or are we observing different expressions of a single structural principle?
     This paper belongs to the ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, the science of
systems that improve themselves, which the author founded (the founding paper). The theory is carried
by five laws, each a law under test: Law I, the ARC Principle, that usable capability is a power of
recursive depth, with the exponent measured rather than assumed; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law;
Law III, the ARC Ceiling; and Laws IV and V, the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law,
added on 24 September 2026, their mathematics in development. The laws and twenty-two propositions
are registered on OSF (the theory registration), and the statement paper states the theory whole. The
programme’s engineering hypothesis is the Eden Protocol, correction built into a system’s own reasoning,
whose pilot Section 6.5 reports; its end goal is to learn, by registered tests, whether correction built into a
self-improving system can keep pace with its capability, as the contingency for control lost against our
will. This paper brings four fields together, AI reasoning, quantum error correction, acoustic physics and
consciousness science, and assumes no reader knows the others: Section 1.1 says what each reported, and
Appendix G places the paper among the laws.
     We argue that the pattern reflects a principle, but not the one that might be expected. We do not claim
that one equation fits all domains. We claim something more specific: that, for any recursive system that
satisfies the axioms, a measurable property (its composition operator) determines its scaling function,
and that five qualitative properties follow from the recursive architecture, two of them on premises added
to the axioms (Theorem 4). The mathematical form differs across domains because the composition
operator differs. The structural properties hold in every substrate whose recursion satisfies the axioms,
because the axioms name no substrate.
     Our contributions are:
    • A formalism. Three axioms, separability, cumulative advantage and continuity (Section 3.1), and
      the recursive composition operator ⊕, which characterises how incremental gains combine with
      accumulated capability (Definition 1), giving the generalised ARC Equation (2).
    • A classification. Under each case’s hypotheses, multiplicative ⊕ yields power-law scaling (𝑅 𝛼 ),
      additive ⊕ yields exponential scaling (𝑒 𝛼𝑟 in the added passes 𝑟 = 𝑅 − 1), and bounded ⊕ yields
      saturation (Theorem 1), each form stable within its own functional equation (Section 3.2).
    • The exponent derived, not fitted. Within the stated model, the asymptotic exponent is 𝛼 =
      1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) (Theorem 2); no additive decomposition of capability is consistent with the axioms
      (Theorem 2a); and the crossover depth 𝑅 ∗ = 𝛼/𝑎 is independent of base potential (Theorem 3).
    • Five structural properties, threshold behaviour, recursive depth dependence, base quality de-

1.1     The problem
Between August 2024 and February 2026, four research programmes reported the following results:
    Google Quantum AI (results announced 9 December 2024; the team’s preprint public from 24
August 2024) demonstrated exponential suppression of the logical error rate below the surface-code
threshold on the Willow processor, by a factor Λ = 2.14 ± 0.02 for each increase of two in code distance
(Google Quantum AI and Collaborators 2024), a result graded here as convergent timing, not a prediction.
    DeepSeek-AI (preprint January 2025; in Nature, September 2025) showed that “the reasoning abilities
of LLMs can be incentivized through pure reinforcement learning (RL)”, with the emergence of reasoning
patterns such as self-reflection and verification (Guo et al. 2025). Sharma and Chopra (2025) reported that
sequential refinement, in which chains build on previous attempts, outperformed parallel self-consistency
at matched compute in 95.6% of configurations, across five open-source models and three reasoning
benchmarks. Their paper is dated before the experiment reported here, so it is the earlier demonstration of
that advantage and not a confirmation of this work. The direction is also not settled: Gu et al. (2026),
posted in April 2026, report the opposite result, parallel sampling outperforming sequential across three
model families on mathematics and coding, attributing it to reduced exploration when a chain conditions
on its own previous answers.
    NYU Physics (February 2026) created a continuous classical time crystal with a pair of acoustically

levitated beads: “Unless the particles have identical scattering properties, their wave-mediated interactions
are nonreciprocal”, and pairs can use this nonreciprocity “to harvest energy from the wave to sustain

The oscillation is a linearly stable limit cycle. In one experiment the antisymmetric mode’s coherence time
is 𝜏 = (100 ± 10) s, about 6,700 cycles at 66.787 Hz, comparable to the 108 s recording, and the authors
add: “Sustained oscillations persist for hours longer than the measurement window, which means that the
true coherence time of the nonequilibrium steady state may be substantially larger.” Every recording the
paper reports is of a steady state, and no growth exponent has been measured in this system.
    The COGITATE Consortium (2025) found information about conscious content in visual, ventro-
temporal and inferior frontal cortex, content-specific decoding in posterior regions generalising for about
the duration of the stimulus (0.5 to 1.5 s) while in prefrontal regions its temporal generalisation was
restricted to about 0.2 to 0.4 s (Cogitate Consortium et al. 2025). COGITATE tested two theories of con-
sciousness, not a recursion depth, and its results challenge key tenets of both; this paper reads the sustained,
content-specific posterior responses as structurally consistent with depth-dependent, feedback-driven
processing, which COGITATE did not measure.
    These programmes address different problems in different physical substrates. They report different
things: exponential suppression of error in quantum error correction, a contested sequential advantage in
AI reasoning, a sustained steady-state limit cycle in a classical time crystal, and no measured form in
neuroscience. They are separate programmes in separate fields.
    Yet, on this paper’s reading, they share a structural pattern: in each, recursive or recurrent processing
(where the output of one cycle becomes the input for the next), operating on structured asymmetry, is
what the reported gain or order depends on. Those programmes are cited for convergence and not for
mechanism: whether any of their systems accumulates, meaning retains something from each pass whose
contribution grows with depth, rather than merely conditioning on the previous pass, is untested in each
case. The question this paper addresses is whether that pattern reflects a single underlying principle or
mere coincidence.

1.2   The ARC Equation
We formalise the question as follows. For AI systems, the equation is

                                           𝑈 = 𝐼 × 𝑅 𝛼,     𝛼 ≤ 2,                                          (1)

the ARC Equation (AI power-law form), with the ARC Bound, the registration’s claim that the ceiling
of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, a law under test, is at most two for correctors of the system’s own class;
Paper X supersedes this fixed-exponent framing as the operative safety criterion and re-scopes, without
retracting, the equation and the bound (Section 6.4). Here 𝛼 is the measured exponent (on today’s frozen
models, one model’s fit and inconclusive; Section 4.3 prints its row whole). The two is not a property of
the equation: it is the value Law III’s ceiling, 1/(1 − 𝛾), returns where the correction-leverage exponent
𝛾 is one half, a point value the registration conjectures and does not assert (Section 6.4). Law II, the
ARC Co-Scaling Law, a law under test, is stated as the registration states it, 𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘: correction has the
asymptotic scaling advantage where its exponent exceeds the drift exponent, burden has it where the
correction exponent is the smaller, and at equality coefficients, delay, backlog and saturation decide the
outcome. In Paper X’s minimal model, with positive gain drift, 𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘 decides whether the misalignment
fraction vanishes, and with level drift as well the condition is 𝛽𝐶 > max(𝑘, 0).
    Here 𝑈 denotes effective capability (the letter’s earlier senses in this programme are recorded in
Appendix D.1), 𝐼 denotes base potential (structured asymmetry; the notation register’s base intelligence,
what the system could do before any self-improvement), 𝑅 denotes recursive depth, and 𝛼 denotes the
scaling exponent. Within the stated model this paper derives 𝛼 rather than fitting it: the asymptotic

the generalised ARC Equation. Under the regularity and composition hypotheses of Theorem 1:

passes 𝑟 = 𝑅 − 1), and bounded ⊕ yields saturation.


2    Related work, antecedents and the rival instrument
The formalism rests on mathematics far older than this programme, and much of its cross-domain evidence
is other researchers’ work, beside the programme’s own experiment, comparisons and pilot (Sections 4.3,
6.1 and 6.5); this section states each debt in the source’s own terms and then weighs the nearest work.

Functional equations and their stability. The classification of Theorem 1 rests on Cauchy’s functional
equations (Cauchy 1821); that a function approximately satisfying one of them lies near an exact solution
of the same equation is the stability theorem of Hyers and Ulam (Hyers 1941; Ulam 1960). The
renormalisation-group parallel of Section 3.2 draws on Kadanoff’s scaling picture for Ising models near
the critical point (Kadanoff 1966) and on Wilson’s renormalisation group (Wilson 1971).

The dimensional formula. Section 6.4 sets out the earlier derivations of 𝛼 = 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1), a formula this
paper does not claim (West et al. 1997, 2004; Banavar et al. 1999, 2010; Demetrius 2008; Demetrius
and Tuszynski 2010; Zhao 2022; Bettencourt 2013); what it adds is an account of why those derivations
converge.

The forms, conceded. The relation 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) of Theorem 2 is the exponent of the elementary
solution of the separable equation Axiom 2 states. The same structure, a stock that grows at a rate
proportional to a power below one of itself, is the knowledge production function of Jones (1995), whose
long-run growth rate carries the factor 1/(1 − 𝜙); the expression 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) is also the sum of a geometric
series, familiar as the Keynes multiplier (Keynes 1936) and as Dyson resummation (Dyson 1949), as
Paper X’s novelty ledger records of this paper. Axiom 2’s name, cumulative advantage, is the term of
Price (1976). What this paper adds is not these forms but the axioms that select among them and their
reading across domains.

The pipes premise, with its contest. The premise that transport networks set the pace of biological
growth is not this programme’s claim to defend; it is the premise of a leading account in quantitative biology,
and a contested one. West, Brown and Enquist derived the allometric scaling laws of biology from the
geometry of nutrient-distribution networks (West et al. 1997; one of the most cited papers in its field, with
an OpenAlex citation count of 5,046 as of 12 August 2026, a single-source and index-dependent count).
The exact exponent remains contested: White and Seymour report basal metabolic rate proportional to
body mass to the two-thirds power rather than three-quarters (White and Seymour 2003), and Kozłowski
and Konarzewski have twice asked whether the model is “mathematically correct and biologically relevant”
(Kozłowski and Konarzewski 2004, 2005). This programme’s premise needs only the mechanism, that the
delivery network sets the ceiling, and not any particular exponent; the dispute over the exponent’s value
leaves it untouched, but the critics question the mechanism too, so the programme takes it as a premise,
and its biological reading stands or falls with it.

The exception West’s own group found. The escape from the pipe limit is also not this programme’s
assertion; it is measured in the urban scaling literature by the same senior author. Bettencourt, Lobo,

(increasing returns), whereas those accounting for infrastructure display 𝛽 ≈ 0.8 < 1 (economies of scale).”
Infrastructure, the literal pipes, scales sublinearly; information-mediated activity scales superlinearly.
They state the contrast with biology directly: “we discuss how cities are similar to, and differ from,
biological organisms, for which 𝛽 < 1”. They derive “growth equations, which quantify the dramatic
difference between growth fueled by innovation versus that driven by economies of scale” (spelling as
in the original). And their closing inference names the consequence: “This difference suggests that, as
population grows, major innovation cycles must be generated at a continually accelerating rate to sustain
growth and avoid stagnation or collapse.” On this paper’s reading, that is the shape of the open problem
this framework addresses: in the findings of West’s group, information-mediated growth escapes the
throughput limit, and the only brake in their framework is external and must be applied again and again,
faster and faster. No internal limit is derived. The replacement limit, a ceiling belonging to the growing
system itself, is the gap.

The pipes argument at fixed throughput. Everything that grows is fed through a channel, and for
everything before software, fixing the channel fixes the growth. Starve a tumour’s blood supply and
its growth is held back. Fix a chain reaction’s fuel and geometry and it stops. Exhaust an epidemic’s
susceptible hosts and it burns out. Astrophysics has a named, quantitative constraint of the same kind: the
Eddington limit, a luminosity ceiling where radiation pressure balances gravity, not a hard ceiling on
accretion, since ultraluminous X-ray sources sit well above it (Bachetti et al. 2014). The claim here is a
generalisation of limits physics already accepts, not a new species of assertion. Two hard cases are handled
deliberately. Cosmic inflation is excluded by scope: it is expansion of space, not growth of a structure on a
substrate, and it ended by field dynamics rather than by running out of anything. Evolution is the sharpest
case and it fits: biological complexity increased for billions of years at roughly fixed solar throughput,
which is growth on information at fixed pipes, and it is also glacial, and it has no internal corrector at all,
since selection is external. On this framework, evolution would carry a measurable exponent far below
the ceiling; that is a hypothesis about biology falling out of a framework built for software, stated without

Antecedents and near-misses, each with its differential. On the question: Hutter asked directly

than independent ones is textbook variance reduction (antithetic variates); the mechanism is not the claim.

A programme hypothesis beyond the model. Oversight arrangements that place a human in the loop,

beyond what this paper’s equations decide, that human-in-the-loop oversight shows a higher correction
exponent than pure-model oversight, for a structural reason rather than a sentimental one. This is testable
on existing data and does not require new systems.

3.1   Three axioms
We begin with three axioms that define the recursive amplification systems this paper treats.

Axiom 1 (Separability). Effective capability decomposes as 𝑈 = 𝐼 × 𝑔(𝑅), where 𝐼 > 0 is base potential
(structured asymmetry; the notation register’s base intelligence), 𝑅 ≥ 1 is recursive depth, and 𝑔(1) = 1

multiplicative channels. A system with zero base potential (𝐼 = 0) achieves zero capability regardless of
recursive depth: recursion has nothing to amplify.

3.2     The composition operator

Frozen models versus recursive self-modification: two distinct regimes. A critical application of
this classification concerns the distinction between current AI systems and hypothetical self-modifying
ones. Current frontier AI models are frozen during inference: when they generate extended reasoning
chains, the underlying weights, and with them the composition rule, remain fixed (attention patterns still
vary with the input). The composition operator ⊕ does not change between steps. This architectural
constraint confines such systems to multiplicative composition through a finite-dimensional parameter
space, producing power-law fits with exponents below one, which Paper II names the physical regime,
𝛼 < 1 (Section 5.1). Empirically, Paper II’s estimate of the exponent rests on one model, Gemini 3 Flash,
and is inconclusive, with both sequential point estimates below one (Section 4.3 prints its row whole).
Such a fit does not by itself place the model outside Axiom 2: 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) is the asymptotic exponent
of the axiom’s solution, and over a finite window, below the crossover depth of Theorem 3, that solution
can return a fitted exponent below one for a positive coupling.
     The Cauchy classification reveals precisely why. In a frozen system the same fixed operator composes
every reasoning step, and with 𝛼 < 1 the resulting gains are real but diminishing. By contrast, a system
capable of recursive self-modification, one that rewrites its own composition function ⊕ during operation,
would occupy a qualitatively different position in the classification. If step 𝑟 improves the operator used
at step 𝑟 + 1, the self-referential coupling 𝛽 𝐿 would, on this model, be positive and could rise, yielding
𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) > 1. This transition does not require quantum hardware or additive composition; it
requires only that the system can modify its own reasoning architecture during inference.
     No current AI system achieves this. Earlier versions of this paper did predict super-linear scaling
(𝛼 > 1) for current, frozen models (the original ARC Bound prediction, “testable within current model
generations”, now kept in Appendix B.5), and that prediction was not borne out; the estimate of about
2.2 is retracted (Section 4.3). On the present reading, super-linear scaling belongs to a regime that
does not yet exist. The practical consequence, previewed in Section 1, is that structural alignment is to
be built while systems remain frozen and their scaling is predictable, and judged beside a measure of
usefulness, as every safety measure is (Section 6.5). Once self-modification arrives, the composition
operator becomes a moving target, and on this model a fixed external safeguard would fall behind a
capability that compounds. Correction built into the operator is the contingency for control lost against
our will; oversight, containment and shutdown stay in human hands for as long as they can be held.
Guaranteed controllability after self-modification is not an established result of this programme.

5     The composition operator across domains
The composition operator ⊕ is not merely a classification tool. It explains why different domains exhibit
different scaling functions.

5.1   AI: multiplicative composition
In chain-of-thought reasoning, each step builds hierarchically on accumulated insight. Step 𝑟 + 1 does not
merely add new information; it restructures the problem representation using everything accumulated
through step 𝑟. This is multiplicative composition: the gain at each step depends on the quality of the
accumulated representation, less than proportionally (were it strictly proportional, growth would be
exponential).
    The 𝛽 𝐿 -dynamics equation 𝑑𝑔/𝑑𝑟 = 𝑎𝑔 𝛽𝐿 models this directly. If 𝛽 𝐿 ≈ 0.5 (moderate coupling),
Theorem 2 gives 𝛼 ≈ 2; the measured value is reported below.
    Two derivations reach that value and they are not the same derivation, which this paper now states
rather than leaves to be noticed. Theorem 2 gives the exponent the coupling sustains, 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ),
where 𝛽 𝐿 is the leverage fraction of the recursive step. The ceiling is that of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, a
law under test, 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾), and it comes from the correction shortfall rather than from the coupling,
where 𝛾 is the correction-leverage exponent (the registration’s letter, written 𝜒 in the notation register).
Both return two when their exponent is one half; in general they agree exactly where 𝛽 𝐿 = 𝛾 on the same
system, which is an open question and is preregistered by timestamp as one (first anchored on 12 August
2026; Appendix C): a drafted study, AG, estimates the coupling and the correction exponent on one and
the same system and tests whether the growth exponent the leverage fraction sustains respects the ceiling
the correction exponent sets (𝛽 𝐿 ≤ 𝛾), and discloses in advance the precision that test needs. Written in
the coupling, the stability condition 𝛼 ≤ 𝛼crit reads 𝛽 𝐿 ≤ 𝛾, which says that a system stays correctable
exactly while the leverage it takes per step does not outrun the capacity it has to correct what that leverage
costs, under the conditions the registration states for Law III (Section 6.4).

The coupling of today’s systems. The original prediction that optimised language models with explicit
self-correction would converge toward 𝛼 ≈ 2 (𝛽 𝐿 ≈ 0.5), testable within current model generations, was
not borne out; it and its version 3.1 update stand in Appendix B.5. Paper II reads a sequential exponent
below one as the physical regime, 𝛼 < 1, and its one interpretable fit, Gemini 3 Flash’s, is inconclusive,
with both point estimates below one (Section 4.3). Read through 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) alone, such an exponent
would ask for a coupling below zero, outside Axiom 2’s range; but that relation gives the asymptotic
exponent, and over a finite window the axiom’s solution can return a fitted exponent below one for a
positive coupling (Section 3.2). Whether any deployed system lies inside the regime that Theorem 2 and
Laws II and III, each a law under test, govern is therefore unmeasured: no coupling has been estimated
independently on any system, and that comparison is what P5 registers (Section 6.2).

5.2   Quantum: additive composition
In surface code quantum error correction, each increase of two in code distance multiplies the suppression
of the logical error rate by the same factor Λ. The surface code is not concatenated: a larger distance
enlarges one lattice, and syndrome extraction repeats in time, cycle by cycle, not distance by distance;

                                        Recursive amplification across domains


the paper reads code distance as its depth variable by analogy. The gains accumulate additively (in code
distance) while composing multiplicatively (in error suppression).
    This is additive composition: 𝑓 (𝑑1 + 𝑑2 ) = 𝑓 (𝑑1 ) · 𝑓 (𝑑2 ). Theorem 1(2) gives exponential scaling:
𝜀 𝑑 ∝ Λ− (𝑑+1)/2 . The measured Λ = 2.14 ± 0.02 is the error suppression factor for each increase of two in
code distance (Google Quantum AI and Collaborators 2024).
    On this paper’s analogy, the composition operator would order quantum scaling above AI scaling:
exponential functions eventually dominate any power law, and additive composition, where each step’s
correction is independent of accumulated state, produces stronger scaling than multiplicative composition,
where each step depends on (and is limited by) accumulated context. The ordering is this paper’s reading;
Λ is the source’s measured quantity, not an exponent of this framework.

5.3    Physics: bounded composition
In the time crystal, the source’s linear stability analysis gives a growing antisymmetric (breathing) mode
for pairs in its active region; higher-order damping, which that analysis leaves out, can stabilise the
growing mode, and the pair settles into steady-state oscillation on a linearly stable limit cycle (Morrell
et al. 2026).
    This is bounded composition, on this paper’s reading of the system: 𝑄 𝑟+1 = min(𝑄 𝑟 + 𝛿𝑄 𝑟 , 𝑄 max ).
The source records only the established oscillation, so no rise toward the cycle, no ceiling and no growth
exponent has been measured (Section 1.1). On the framework’s reading, a recursive channel with intrinsic
losses saturates; the time crystal is consistent with that reading and does not test it.

5.5   Composition operator transitions
The framework as presented assigns a single composition operator ⊕ to each system. This may be an
idealisation. In bounded or dissipative systems, the composition operator itself can transition between
regimes as recursive depth increases.

Conjecture 5 (Composition operator transitions). A single physical system can transition between different
composition operator regimes as a function of recursive depth or an internal state parameter. This
extends the framework from fixed ⊕ to depth-dependent ⊕(𝑅), modelled by generalising Axiom 2 to
𝑑𝑔/𝑑𝑟 = 𝑎(𝑔, 𝑟) · 𝑔 𝛽𝐿 (𝑔,𝑟 ) where the coupling itself depends on accumulated amplification.

    Computational checks. Computational tests show that logistic growth, gradient descent with
momentum, and Kuramoto oscillator synchronisation all exhibit analogous transitions when measuring
local 𝛽 𝐿 in sliding windows. By contrast, unbounded pure-Bernoulli systems maintain constant 𝛽 𝐿 to
machine precision (𝜎 < 10−6 ).
    Implications for contradictory AI evidence. If reasoning systems begin in an additive regime
(shallow thinking), transition to multiplicative (deep reasoning with cumulative advantage), and eventually
saturate, then studies measuring at different recursive depths would observe different scaling in the same
system. The finding of Zeng et al. (2025) that correct solutions are often shorter than incorrect ones may
mark the transition between multiplicative and bounded composition. This is testable: measure local
𝛽 𝐿 at different reasoning depths within a single system. Against the registered P1 the reading is no exit:
the theory registration says that the reading that a system changed its own composition operator “is not
available after the result is seen”, and that the papers’ statement that a self-modifying system escapes the
attention bound “is not a licence to move this proposition’s boundary afterwards”.

Epistemic status. Conjecture 5, supported in three computational systems and untested in physical
experiments, is not derivable from the original three axioms. It extends the framework’s scope but requires
independent experimental confirmation.

The strongest test of this framework is not whether it describes systems already studied. It is whether it

Forward prediction. Identify a recursive system not studied in this paper: evolutionary algorithms,

6.2   The within-system regime transition
Regime transition prediction. The same physical system should transition between scaling regimes
as the coupling strength 𝛽 𝐿 is experimentally varied. In AI: vary the fraction of context available to
each reasoning step (e.g., by truncating the attention window). As the effective 𝛽 𝐿 varies, the fitted

the measured coupling, within the margin of 0.10 that P5 registers for that comparison. This tests the
𝛽 𝐿 -to-𝛼 relationship within a single system, eliminating cross-domain confounds.

6.3   The 𝑅 ∗ crossover
When plotting capability against recursive depth, there should exist a distinct crossover at depth 𝑅 ∗ = 𝛼/𝑎
(Theorem 3). Below 𝑅 ∗ , scaling appears approximately linear. Above 𝑅 ∗ , scaling follows the domain-
appropriate super-linear form. Since 𝑅 ∗ depends only on the architecture (𝛼 and 𝑎), it should be consistent
across different input qualities for the same system.
     If separately measured 𝛼 and 𝑎 place the crossover 𝑅 ∗ inside the measured window and no transition
from approximately linear to compounding gains appears there, the framework’s transitional regime
prediction is falsified; a pure power from the first pass is the case 𝑎 = 𝛼 of Theorem 4 and refutes nothing.
𝑅 ∗ is a unique signature that distinguishes recursive amplification from simple redundancy.

6.4   The ARC Bound
The bound as registered. The ARC Bound, at most two, is the inequality the ceiling relation returns
from the same-class limit on the correction exponent, and it needs P6 and P20 and nothing else. That the

programme has not registered; the registration claims the inequality and conjectures the equality, and
neither has yet been tested. Same-class means a system corrected by mechanisms of its own class (P6,
which the registration holds with low confidence): a cross-class system measured above two does not
refute the bound, and the corrector’s class is declared and evidenced before the run. Under the registration
the bound takes its word from P6 and P20 alone. It is refuted only where P6 is refuted, by “a same-class
corrector whose measured correction exponent interval lies entirely above one half”, a measurement made

printed supported, since P6 admits no supported verdict. Law III’s own test of a sustainable maximum is
P3’s, against the value the ceiling relation returns from the correction exponent measured on the same

analytic identity, and two against reference values without a named source. In each case the physical
system contains a hierarchical branching network of dimension 𝑑 subject to a conservation constraint: a
KPZ growth front divides 2D space along a 1D network; a percolation cluster takes the form of a 2D
fractal; a propagating crack cuts branching networks through the material. The formula has clear failures
where this network structure or conservation condition is absent (Ising model, polymer scaling, galaxy
clustering), clarifying its domain of applicability: 𝛼 = 𝑑/(𝑑 + 1) applies wherever a 𝑑-dimensional
hierarchical network, subject to conservation or optimisation of resource flow, optimally partitions a
(𝑑 + 1)-dimensional space.

6.5    Conditional safety implication
If the framework is correct, alignment properties embedded within the recursive process may scale with
capability (𝛼align ≈ 𝛼), while external constraints applied post-hoc may not (𝛼align ≈ 0). The safety ratio
𝑆 = Alignment/Capability ∝ 𝑅 𝛼align − 𝛼 remains constant when alignment is ‘in the loop’ and decays to
zero when it is not. The ratio sets alignment against capability and measures no usefulness (legitimate task
completion, needless refusal, resource use, correction cost), so neither half of the conditional says whether
built-in correction keeps a system useful. This is the conditional prediction as originally stated; the run of
March 2026 reported below cannot decide its external half, and its in-loop half is untested. Unit 83’s
registered result (Section 8) does not test it either. The corrector acted inside each round at fixed weights:
that is placement, not correction built into a system. (In plain English: on this model, values built into the
reasoning process would keep pace with capability, while constraints added afterwards would fall behind
it. Neither half has been decided. Neither half is a reason to relax oversight, containment or shutdown,
which stay in human hands for as long as they can be held; correction built in is the contingency for
control lost against our will.)

8     What this paper does and does not show
What this paper shows is mathematical and holds inside its stated model: under the axioms and each
case’s hypotheses, the classification (Theorem 1), the exponent relation (Theorem 2), the necessity of
the multiplicative interaction (Theorem 2a) and the crossover (Theorem 3); and the five properties on
the premises Theorem 4 names. What it does not show is that any real system satisfies the axioms: the
𝛽 𝐿 -to-𝛼 relation, the crossover and the classification are untested in physical systems, the AI exponent’s
best fit is one model’s and inconclusive, no exponent has been measured in a time crystal, and the ARC
Bound, registered as a ceiling of at most two for same-class correctors, is untested, its point value two a
conjecture. The table below sets what the paper does not claim beside what it does.

9    Limitations
Evidential limitations. The earlier estimate of about 2.2 is retracted, and the one interpretable fit of the
v5 cross-architecture experiment, Gemini 3 Flash’s, is what survived it, one model’s fit and inconclusive,
not a correction of the same measurement (Section 4.3); the five capability runs of March 2026 are
precursor evidence that decides no registered proposition. No 𝛼 has been measured in time crystals. The
COGITATE connection is structural, not quantitative. The 𝛽 𝐿 -to-𝛼 relationship is an identity proved
in Appendix A.2 and checked numerically (𝑅 2 = 1.00000000), an internal consistency check and not a
validation of the relation in any system; the empirical measurement of 𝛽 𝐿 in physical systems requires
independent confirmation. The 𝑅 ∗ crossover is untested empirically. The 𝛽 𝐿 boundary values in the
phase diagram are approximate. Conjecture 5 (⊕ transitions) is supported in three computational systems,
beside a Bernoulli control (Appendix F.6), and untested in physical experiments. The five-property
test (Theorem 4) did not reach statistical significance (𝑝 = 0.095) and requires larger samples. The
alignment run of March 2026, a single unregistered run, cannot decide criterion F13: its statistics are rank
correlations and contrasts, not exponents, and its three flat and negative runs are the three the October
2026 code review found defective (Section 6.5).

Blind prediction testing. A blind prediction test was conducted on three computational systems
(Barabási-Albert networks, gradient descent with momentum, Kuramoto oscillators). The measured 𝛼
values were 3 to 20× smaller than predicted by 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ). However, forensic analysis identified
two independent confounds: (1) the numerical-derivative 𝛽 𝐿 estimation method is fatally biased, giving
𝛽 𝐿 ≈ 0.95 regardless of true 𝛽 𝐿 , even for pure Bernoulli systems with 3 per cent Gaussian noise added;
(2) none of the tested systems satisfy Axiom 2 (constant coupling coefficient 𝑎), and the BA network’s
effective coupling decreases ∼ 50× over the simulation. Applied to systems built to satisfy Axiom 2, the
linearisation method recovers the relation with 𝑅 2 = 0.9999, which checks the method, not the prediction
for the three systems tested. The blind test failed for reasons found after it ran, so it neither supports nor
refutes the relation; a new blind test with the method fixed in advance is required, and identifying natural
systems that satisfy the axioms remains the central empirical challenge. Full methodology and forensic
analysis are available as supplementary material (see Paper III).

Theoretical limitations. The framework formalises the pattern of recursive amplification but does
not identify the mechanism. Theorem 2 is the solution of the growth equation Axiom 2 assumes, and
Theorem 2a follows from the product form Axiom 1 assumes; their weight lies in what they make
measurable, and the axioms themselves are untested in any physical system. It tells you what happens
(scaling exponents, crossover depths, functional forms) but not why at a microscopic level (analogous
to thermodynamics before statistical mechanics). Connecting the composition operator to microscopic
dynamics remains an open problem.


10     Conclusion
Every system discussed in this paper (silicon neural networks, superconducting qubits, millimetre polymer
beads, cortical neural circuits) maintains a low-entropy state far from equilibrium and, on this paper’s
reading, feeds its outputs back into its inputs. The framework predicts that below a critical depth the gains
are ordinary and above it they compound, with the transition universal in structure and domain-specific in
form; no such depth has yet been measured in any of them (criterion F7), and a claim that the transition is
sharp would need a criterion of its own.

                                     Recursive amplification across domains


    We have formalised this observation. The composition operator ⊕ sorts recursive systems by the
functional equation their scaling satisfies: under the stated hypotheses it determines the functional form of
scaling from measurable algebraic properties. The five derived properties (threshold, depth-dependence,
base-quality dependence, multiplicative interaction, regime boundaries) follow as theorems from three
axioms and, for two of them, the premises Theorem 4 names, not as empirical generalisations.
    The framework issues predictions that the programme has been unable to locate in any theory confined
to a single domain. It predicts the scaling behaviour of new recursive systems from their composition
operator alone. It predicts a scaling crossover at a derived depth 𝑅 ∗ that is independent of base quality
and consistent across input qualities for a given architecture. It predicts that a measured 𝛽 𝐿 determines
𝛼 via 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ), a relationship proved for the axiom’s solutions and checked numerically to
𝑅 2 = 1.00000000; the blind test of Section 9 neither supports nor refutes it for other systems. It states the
ARC Bound as the theory registration holds it, a ceiling of at most two for correctors of the system’s own
class, which needs P6 and P20 and nothing else, with the point value two a conjecture. Its own earlier
form, the coupling at which the sustained exponent reaches two (𝛽 𝐿 = 0.5, 𝛼 = 2), is an early-framework
conjecture that decides nothing registered (criteria F5 and F11), with the 𝑂 (𝑁 2 ) tally of self-attention
pathways only a heuristic for the frozen regime; on that conjecture two is a scaling limit, not a speed
limit, which systems can exceed in transient supercritical excursions without staying stably self-correcting.
And it conjectures that bounded systems exhibit composition operator transitions that could explain
why different studies of the same system can observe different scaling regimes (Conjecture 5). These
predictions and conjectures are specific, falsifiable, and forward-looking.
    If the predictions fail, we will have learned where the structural analogy breaks down. If they hold,
we will have identified a principle that connects intelligence, computation, and the physics of order across
substrates.
    Intelligence may not be a property of particular materials. It may be what happens on the far side of
the recursive threshold.

Conjectural extrapolation. The furthest reading of the framework comes last, as a conclusion the
mathematics does not deny rather than a result: Law I, a law under test, taken to the limit of capability
does not rule out a universe or its creation. No measurement in this paper bears on that reading, no
prediction of it is registered, and no test of it is in view, so it is graded as conjecture and is not established.
It belongs to the long-horizon interpretive layer of Infinite Architects (Eastwood 2026a) and requires
independent argument and evidence beyond what is reported here.
    The predictions are specified. The falsification criteria are public. The data will decide.

Section 5.1, the version 3.1 empirical update

physical regime, 𝛽 < 0. No deployed system measured to date has therefore been observed inside the

than parallel processing for every model tested (Paper II 2.18 now grades this as observed, wherever both
exponents could be estimated; Section 4.3).

E     Measurement protocol
To test the framework, we set out a standardised six-step protocol for any recursive system:
    Step 1. Define one recursive cycle (one self-referential step where output becomes input).
    Step 2. Measure base capability 𝐼 at 𝑅 = 1 (no recursion). For cross-domain comparability,
we operationalise 𝐼, in a working definition not yet tested and this paper’s own, outside the notation
register, as the maximum rate of entropy reduction per recursive cycle, measured in bits per cycle:
𝐼 = max𝑅 [−Δ𝐻/Δ𝑅] evaluated at the first cycle (𝑅 = 0 to 𝑅 = 1). Landauer’s principle gives the
least heat for erasing a bit, 𝑘 𝐵𝑇 ln 2 (Landauer 1961), verified experimentally by Bérut et al. (2012); it
bounds the energy cost of an irreversible step from below and does not convert energy processed into
information gained, so a link from 𝐼 to energy needs a model of how many bits each cycle erases. In
AI, 𝐼 is single-pass accuracy, without a reasoning chain (Section 4.2). In quantum error correction, 𝐼 is
the error reduction from one syndrome cycle. In biology, 𝐼 is the metabolic efficiency gain from one
branching level. 𝑈 = 𝐼 × 𝑅 𝛼 is dimensionally consistent because 𝑅 is a dimensionless count.

     Step 3. Measure capability 𝑈 at minimum 5 recursive depths spanning one order of magnitude. Look
for the 𝑅 ∗ crossover.
     Step 4. Fit the candidate forms: power law (log(𝑈/𝐼) = 𝛼 log 𝑅), exponential (log(𝑈/𝐼) = 𝜆𝑅),
logarithmic (𝑈/𝐼 = 𝑐 log 𝑅) and saturating (Theorem 1, case 3). Select best fit via AIC/BIC. The power

F     Computational validation suite
This appendix summarises the computational checks of Theorems 1 to 4 and of Conjecture 5. The
scripts and results deposited for them are listed under Code and data, after the conclusion; the count
of Appendix F.1 and the crossover figures of Appendices F.4 and F.8 stand there at most in a printed
summary, without the computation behind them. Tests were conducted using Python 3.12 with NumPy,
SciPy, and Matplotlib.

F.1   Theorem 1 validation (Cauchy classification)
Fifteen systems were tested across three composition regimes (5 multiplicative, 5 additive, 5 bounded).
Each system was evaluated on whether its measured composition rule matched the predicted scaling
function.
    Result: 15/15 correct classifications (100%). No system was misclassified. The classification itself is
proved (Appendix A.1); the check confirms only that each system, built in a known regime, was assigned
the form of that regime.

F.2   Theorem 2 validation (𝛽 𝐿 → 𝛼 identity)
The relationship 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) was tested against 30 exact Bernoulli ODE solutions with 𝛽 𝐿 spanning
0.05 to 0.92. For each test case, 𝛽 𝐿 was measured blindly from the marginal gain structure (𝑑𝑔/𝑑𝑅 vs 𝑔 in
log-log space), 𝛼 was predicted, and the predicted value was compared against the true scaling exponent.
    Result: 𝑅 2 = 1.00000000 (eight decimal places). Regression slope: 1.000102. Mean absolute error:
0.002%. This agrees with the closed form to numerical precision; that 𝛼 = 1/(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 ) is exact is proved
in Appendix A.2.

F.3   Theorem 2a validation (non-additivity)
The non-additivity proof was verified numerically. For multiple (𝐼1 , 𝐼2 , 𝑅1 , 𝑅2 ) combinations across
all 𝛽 𝐿 ∈ (0, 1): if 𝑈 were additive (𝑈 = 𝑓 (𝐼) + ℎ(𝑅)), then 𝑈 (𝐼1 , 𝑅1 ) − 𝑈 (𝐼1 , 𝑅2 ) would equal
𝑈 (𝐼2 , 𝑅1 ) − 𝑈 (𝐼2 , 𝑅2 ). In all cases, the differences are unequal, confirming the multiplicative interaction
is necessary.

F.4   Theorem 3 validation (𝑅 ∗ crossover)
The crossover depth 𝑅 ∗ = 1/[(1 − 𝛽 𝐿 )𝑎] = 𝛼/𝑎 was tested by comparing the predicted crossover with

Ten recursive systems and five non-recursive controls were scored on the five derived properties. Recursive

even if there were no true effect. Scientists typically require one in twenty or better. More data is needed.)
    Result: Direction correct; statistical power insufficient for definitive confirmation. Larger samples
are required.

F.6   Conjecture 5 validation (⊕ phase transitions)
Four systems were tested for depth-dependent composition transitions: (1) exact Bernoulli ODE (control),
(2) logistic growth, (3) gradient descent with momentum, (4) Kuramoto oscillators.

𝛽 𝐿 ≈ 1.7 (growth) to 𝛽 𝐿 ≈ 20 (saturation). Transitions appeared in all three bounded systems tested and
not in the unbounded Bernoulli control.

F.7   Negative controls
Four non-recursive systems were tested: (1) Gaussian noise, (2) linear decay, (3) sinusoidal oscillation,
(4) random walk. None produced false positive signals (𝑆 > 1 or 𝛼 > 1).
