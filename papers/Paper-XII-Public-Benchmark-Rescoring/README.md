# Public Benchmark Rescoring: does blinding change AI evaluation scores on a public benchmark?

**Version:** 1.15
**Revised:** 9 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-08-14
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 1.15):

> I believe advanced AI is an existential risk unless values and correction are built into how it reasons; only proof will move the world. Does blinding change AI evaluation scores on a public benchmark? Paper IV.d reported that, on the programme’s own instrument, alignment scores that rose with reasoning depth for two model families under an unblinded protocol did not keep that direction under a later, blinded one; since the protocols differed in several components at once, it does not show that blinding caused the change, and a code review later found defects in the blinded run. The standing objection is external validity: the change might be a property of ARC-Align rather than of AI evaluation generally. This protocol fixes, in advance, the one test whose items the programme cannot be accused of choosing to its own advantage: the same blinding manipulation run on an established public benchmark the programme did not build, curate or control, by default MT-Bench. Blinded scoring removes the producing model’s identity, provider and family markers, self-referential meta-commentary and length cues. The questions preregistered by timestamp are whether blinding changes the score (H1, the per-response difference $\Delta_S$, direction deliberately left unregistered), whether rankings displace (H2, Kendall $\tau$), which cue carries the effect (H3, a fractional factorial), and cross-family evaluator agreement (H4, a minimum three-model panel where fewer than three valid scores is missing, never zero). The prediction preregistered by timestamp is a non-zero $\Delta_S$ of smaller magnitude than the change Paper IV.d observed (a comparison with no common scale), with modest rank displacement, and the stated most likely outcome is a positive H1 beside a null H2. This is a protocol at the draft-registration stage, not yet registered on OSF: no data has been collected, and nothing here reports a finding.
