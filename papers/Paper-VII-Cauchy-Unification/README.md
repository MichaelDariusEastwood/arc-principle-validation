# Cauchy Unification: does the composition operator of a process determine the form of its scaling law?

**Version:** 3.21
**Revised:** 27 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-03-16
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 3.21):

> Why do the same scaling forms recur in systems that share no mechanism, from animal heat production to language-model loss? A rule that predicts the form of a scaling law from how a system composes, before its data are seen, would let a scaling claim be tested rather than fitted, including the power-law form that Law I of the ARC Theory, a law under test, gives recursive capability. Under a stated regularity condition, the non-zero solutions of Cauchy’s four functional equations form exactly four families: linear, power law, exponential and logarithmic. Read as composition rules in a declared variable, they give a power law where depth and output both multiply and an exponential where depth adds and output multiplies. A saturating form follows from none of them: it needs added assumptions, a ceiling on output and dynamics of its own, since a ceiling alone selects no form. The author classified 25 entries before comparing six standard curves, with a seventh for muscle force and velocity. The archived score uses a small-sample penalty on curve coefficients. The assignments and fits were deposited together. Version 3.19 set aside two formula checks and reported 17 matches in 23 rows (fixed-marginal permutation $p=5.1\times10^{-4}$), including 13 of 17 multiplicative and additive rows. One retained match, Brownian diffusion, reproduces the stated straight line to the precision printed. Its measured-point provenance is unresolved. These counts describe the archived inputs. The model set lacked logarithmic and pure linear candidates. Seven of 13 secondary metabolic estimates trace to the reported quantities. One further value matches two bird subgroups. A replication with independent classification and a four-family test are preregistered by timestamp and await submission.
