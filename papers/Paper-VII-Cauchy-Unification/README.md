# Cauchy Unification: does the composition operator of a process determine the form of its scaling law?

**Version:** 3.17
**Revised:** 9 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-03-16
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 3.17):

> Why do the same scaling forms recur in systems that share no mechanism, from a mouse’s heartbeat to neural networks? A rule that predicts the form of a scaling law from how a system composes, before its data are seen, would let a scaling claim be tested rather than fitted, including the power-law form that Law I of the ARC Theory, a law under test, gives recursive capability. Under a stated regularity condition, the non-zero solutions of Cauchy’s four functional equations form exactly four families: linear, power law, exponential and logarithmic. Read as composition rules in a declared variable, they give a power law where depth and output both multiply and an exponential where depth adds and output multiplies. A saturating form follows from none of them: it needs added assumptions, a ceiling on output and dynamics of its own, since a ceiling alone selects no form. In a structured prediction comparison under a publicly stated rule, the composition operator of each of 25 empirical domains was classified from known physics before the author fitted candidate models and selected among them by AICc. The predicted family was the one selected in 19 of 25 domains (marginal-conditioned permutation $p=7\times10^{-5}$), on a six-model candidate set with no logarithmic and no pure linear candidate, and in 14 of the 18 multiplicative and additive domains ($p=3.0\times10^{-4}$). Secondary tiers are reported separately and never blended: a baseline rerun gives 15 of 20; 13 published metabolic exponents sit nearest the dimensional comparator $d/(d+1)$, seven of them still without a per-row source; 6 analytic identities check the fitter. The predictions were not timestamped in advance of their own fits and the operators were classified by the author, so the result is not proven; a replication with independent classification and a four-family test are preregistered by timestamp and await submission.
