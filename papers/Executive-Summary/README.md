## Summary

From the abstract (version 3.29):

> I believe advanced, self-improving AI will eventually exceed external control. That belief is the ARC Theory’s own statement: control ends, by construction rather than decree, so the strategy is genesis, not control. Control is held for as long as it physically can be and never given up by choice. For anyone who believes control can be kept, genesis still holds as a contingency, in case it cannot; we test what would preserve useful, aligned behaviour if control were lost against our will. The ARC Theory is the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics. Its five named laws, each a law under test, are Law I, the ARC Principle; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; Law III, the ARC Ceiling; Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law; Law V, the ARC Embedding Law. What this programme adds is dated and under test: an equation written on 8 December 2024, which became Law I, the ARC Principle; a design that puts checks into every decision and makes ethics load-bearing; five laws with their mathematics; and registered tests. In Unit 83, P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis: the pooled performance lead widened, but only one engine individually cleared the tolerance. The result is family-dependent, its mechanism axis is not evaluable, and one call was retried under a disclosed recovery amendment. It does not establish persistent ethical alignment or the theory as a whole. Earlier alignment studies remain single-run evidence with protocol and code-review limitations; error-decay fits do not identify a capability-growth exponent. Eden specifies self-correction loops and an allow, hold or deny gate, while the working software is a partial implementation with unmeasured error rates. Published hardware concepts are design directions, not validated guarantees. The HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, remains a consultation proposal.

From The ARC Theory (version 7.10), on the status of Laws IV and V:

> Laws IV and V (the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law) were added on 24 September 2026 and registered as laws on 15 October 2026 in version 1.103, each a law under test; their mathematics, set out at version 1.2 of 29 October 2026, is in development.
