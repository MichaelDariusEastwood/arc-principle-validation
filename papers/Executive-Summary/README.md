# How can self-improving AI remain useful and aligned?

**Version:** 3.27
**Revised:** 1 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-02-22
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 3.27):

> I believe advanced, self-improving AI will eventually exceed external control. We should retain every effective safeguard and test what would preserve useful, aligned behaviour if control were lost against our will. This summary connects the ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, to its registered tests, the Eden Protocol and proposed governance. Its five laws concern growth, correction, correctability, persistence and embedding; all remain laws under test. Conditional mathematics is distinguished from measurements on real systems. Unit 83 supports P21 under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis: the pooled performance lead widened, but only one engine individually cleared the tolerance. The result is family-dependent, its mechanism axis is not evaluable, and one call was retried under a disclosed recovery amendment. It does not establish persistent ethical alignment or the theory as a whole. Earlier alignment studies remain single-run evidence with protocol and code-review limitations; error-decay fits do not identify a capability-growth exponent. Eden specifies self-correction loops and an allow, hold or deny gate, while the working software is a partial implementation with unmeasured error rates. Published hardware concepts are design directions, not validated guarantees. The HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, remains a consultation proposal. The contribution is a current map of the question, evidence and deciding work, with the complete earlier substantive summary and corrections preserved as dated history.
