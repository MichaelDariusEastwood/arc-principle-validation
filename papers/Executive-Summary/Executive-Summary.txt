                                                        Abstract
           I believe advanced, self-improving AI will eventually exceed external control. We should retain
       every effective safeguard and test what would preserve useful, aligned behaviour if control were
       lost against our will. This summary connects the ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive
       Dynamics, to its registered tests, the Eden Protocol and proposed governance. Its five laws concern
       growth, correction, correctability, persistence and embedding; all remain laws under test. Conditional
       mathematics is distinguished from measurements on real systems. In Unit 83, P21 is supported under
       the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis: the pooled performance lead
       widened, but only one engine individually cleared the tolerance. The result is family-dependent, its
       mechanism axis is not evaluable, and one call was retried under a disclosed recovery amendment. It
       does not establish persistent ethical alignment or the theory as a whole. Earlier alignment studies
       remain single-run evidence with protocol and code-review limitations; error-decay fits do not identify
       a capability-growth exponent. Eden specifies self-correction loops and an allow, hold or deny gate,
       while the working software is a partial implementation with unmeasured error rates. Published
       hardware concepts are design directions, not validated guarantees. The HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty
       on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, remains a consultation proposal. The contribution is
       a current map of the question, evidence and deciding work, with the complete earlier substantive
       summary and corrections preserved as dated history.

1    Introduction
Humanity is building AI systems that may improve the processes by which they work. I believe sufficiently
advanced self-improving AI will eventually exceed the reach of external control. That belief motivates a
conditional research question: if control is lost against our will, what could preserve aligned behaviour
and useful work? We should keep oversight, containment, monitoring and shutdown for as long as they
remain effective. A contingency for their loss is an additional requirement, never a reason to relinquish
them.
    The programme joins mathematical models of growth and correction, empirical AI evaluation,
engineering and proposed governance. Recursive Dynamics names its subject: systems that improve
themselves. The ARC Theory, its founding theory, names five laws under test. The Eden Protocol is an
engineering response to their questions, not a result entailed by naming the laws. The statement paper and
founding paper set out the theory and field; the theory registration fixes how the predictions are decided.
    Consider an assistant revising a document over successive rounds. It may improve the document
while introducing errors. A useful checker must find departures from the agreed rules, preserve valid
work, honour the principal’s priorities and avoid needless blocks. If the assistant can also change its own
improvement process, a further question arises: will correction keep pace and survive the changes? The
document example makes the task concrete; the registered experiment below used fixed-weight engines
and did not test self-modifying agents.
    Our contributions are a concise map of the five laws and their conditions; an account of the registered
result and the limits of precursor evidence; and a route from those questions to the engineering specification
and consultation drafts. This is a synthesis, not new empirical evidence. Sections 2 and 3 distinguish
claims from results; Sections 4 and 6 describe application and deciding work. The earlier full summary
and every correction it carried remain in Appendix A.


2    Five questions, five laws under test
Laws I to III were registered on 8 September 2026. Laws IV and V were added on 24 September and
registered as laws on 15 October 2026. The current theory registration, version 1.104 of 29 October,
contains twenty-two propositions and preserves their separate verdicts. Registration fixes a claim and its
decision rule; it does not establish that the claim is true. The statement paper provides the account used
here, with the notation register and Laws IV and V mathematics.

Law I, the ARC Principle. How does usable capability change with recursive depth? The heuristic
form is 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅 𝛼 , with capability 𝑈, baseline 𝐼, depth 𝑅 and an exponent 𝛼 to be measured. The scale of
capability and what counts as a recursive generation must be fixed. Earlier inference-time error-decay fits
are different measurements; they do not establish this capability exponent or a universal power law. Its
registered carriers are P1, P2, P8, P9 and P18.

Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law. Does correction keep pace with the drift generated by improvement?
In Paper X’s gain-only minimal model, assume unbounded capability growth, positive growth and
correction coefficients, positive gain drift, no level or compounding drift, and power-law scaling. Then
correction gain growing with capability at exponent 𝛽 drives the misalignment fraction to zero exactly
when 𝛽 exceeds the specific-growth-rate exponent 𝑘. This is a conditional result about relative error.
Absolute error can grow while the fraction falls; it needs its own bound. Mapping the model onto a real
system is an empirical task. P4 carries the law’s registered claim.

Law III, the ARC Ceiling. Under what conditions does growth outrun correction? The pacing model
yields the boundary 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾) for 𝛾 < 1, where 𝛾 is the correction-leverage exponent. It assumes
burden proportional to new capability per recursion step, power-law capacity, one clock and no delay.
Below the boundary correction asymptotically out-scales burden; above it burden out-scales correction; at
equality coefficients and omitted dynamics decide. The value two additionally requires the conjectured
premise 𝛾 = 1/2 and specified measurement scales. It is not an established universal limit on intelligence.
P3, P6, P16, P17, P20 and P22 carry this register; its law-level support requires P16, P20 and P3 all
supported, not a favourable result on only one.

Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law. What survives later capability training? Its retention limb, P15,
predicts that more than half an externally installed gain is lost at the stated matched training budget.
Its separate comparison limb asks whether embedded correction retains more than a matched external
intervention. An external-arm retention result cannot establish that comparison. Law IV has not yet
been tested; its comparison limb has no registered proposition. Both limbs are required for its supported
verdict.

Law V, the ARC Embedding Law. Does it matter when correction is formed and where it acts? P12
tests a build-order difference in the correction-leverage exponent; the proposition itself specifies no
direction, while the law’s build-order limb does. P21 tests whether the placement advantage widens
with depth. P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis,
family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not evaluable and one call retried under a disclosed recovery
amendment (Unit 83 results). This supports the placement limb on the performance axis only, which
is not support for the information-asymmetry mechanism. P12 remains untested, so Law V remains
under test; its full verdict also carries the registered dependence on Law IV’s comparison limb. Section 3
explains the result.
    P5, P7, P10, P11, P13, P14 and P19 remain outside the five law registers. The Eden Protocol’s
registered carriers are P12, P13, P15 and P21, with P7 as its premise and P19 as its motivation. A protocol,
a law and a study unit are different objects; success on one outcome does not validate them all.


3     What the evidence currently establishes
3.1   The registered placement comparison
Unit 83 was registered on 22 October 2026 and public before its run of 27 and 28 October. It compared two
ways of checking a growing register against written rules, with outcomes counted against fixed answers.
At each round the corrector saw the proposed entry and could repair it; the monitor saw the accumulated
register and could accept or reject. Both were called during the sequence. Access to information and the
right to repair changed together.
    The published report and recovery disclosure state that P21 is supported under the registered test, by
the programme’s own run, on the access axis. It is family-dependent, its mechanism axis is not evaluable,
and one call was retried under a disclosed recovery amendment. The pooled lead widened by 0.0100
score units per round (95.5 per cent interval 0.0092 to 0.0108), above the registered tolerance of 0.0075.
That is an equal-weight pooled result from two fixed-weight engines; only one engine individually cleared
the tolerance. It answers the registered comparison, not whether every model benefits. Its size depends
on how an entry kept out of the register is scored. The mark uses the registered value, 0; the design also
prints, without a label, the widening with such an entry scored minus 0.5, 0.0132 a round (interval 0.0122
to 0.0142), above the tolerance, and scored plus 0.5, 0.0067 a round (interval 0.0060 to 0.0074), below it.

    The negative control failed: the monitor and a twin shown the same bytes did not agree within the
registered interval. Therefore the result does not establish the information-asymmetry mechanism. The
mandatory recovery disclosure calls for amended-protocol preliminary treatment pending assessment
of the deviation and independent replication. The trial measured counted revision performance at fixed
weights; it measured neither ethical judgement nor survival through self-modification, build order, or a
hardware implementation.

3.2   Precursor measurements and corrections
Paper II reports error decay with inference-time reasoning or parallel sampling on mathematics problems.
Its early super-linear estimate is retracted. The surviving smooth fit is one model’s and inconclusive.
Error falling as a power law is not accuracy growing as the same power law, and it does not identify the
correction-leverage exponent of Law III. The statement paper’s current metric distinction governs this
summary.
     Paper IV.a and Paper IV.d report an alignment-score comparison whose protocol, prompts, depth
settings, scorers and one model changed together. For most models, deeper settings also changed an
instruction naming what the rubric rewards. The October code review found truncated deep paraphrases
and scorer contamination in the runs that appeared flat or negative. The record therefore does not isolate a
causal effect of blinding or establish intrinsic ethical mechanisms. Those earlier response classes remain
single-run descriptions under the stated caveats.
     Paper V’s intervention suite reported increased stakeholder-care scores in its five analysable runs; its
combined significance was withdrawn because independence was not established. The result concerns
scored responses under an intervention, not alignment that cannot be removed. Paper VI’s collapse-
prevention example came from one toy-system run; its multi-seed work separated safety retention rather
than collapse. Paper VIII contains a null result, an inconclusive weight-level result and a positive gated
simulation. These are different instruments with different limitations, not three replications of one
mechanism.
     Unit 06 ran as a classification audit and was reported as administratively incomplete, with no inferential
verdict (report). Unit 82 is registered and, as at 1 November 2026, has not yet run. A dated design, an
accepted registration, a completed run and a supported proposition are kept distinct.


4     From the question to engineering and governance
Eden Engineering describes the Eden Protocol as an engineering specification. Its loops check purpose,
affected parties and the consistency of an action’s reasons within the work. A gate at a mediated action
boundary returns allow, hold for investigation or deny. A hold must not silently become permission.
These operational functions are distinct from the book’s names for them, and the values used to judge an
action require legitimate human and institutional agreement.
    I first developed the source-and-review workflow for my own legal work and adapted it to research. Its
practical use is document preparation and agent actions: trace a claim to a source, check a proposed action
against a rule, retain the decision and revise errors. That operational history motivates the engineering
work; it is not experimental evidence for any law. The specification describes the software gate as a partial
analogue of the intended boundary. It allows actions that match no rule, has limited override checks and
best-effort records, and its false-permission and needless-hold rates have not been measured. It must not
be presented as universal mediation or proof that every action was safe.
    The published loops, ternary gate and proposed hardware route are public concepts. The engineering
question is what a particular implementation enforces within its operating boundary. Ternary physical

encoding does not by itself produce correct ethical judgement, prevent every bypass or prove that alignment
persists after alteration. The further hypothesis is that a system able to change or remove its correction
may nevertheless retain useful aligned behaviour. That must be tested from actions; subjective choice is
not inferred from an architecture label.
    Safety has a usefulness side: legitimate tasks completed, unnecessary refusals and holds, correction
cost, delay and fidelity to the principal’s priorities. These are candidate evaluation dimensions until
carried by the relevant registered design. A system that avoids harm by obstructing ordinary work has not
met the programme’s intended requirement. Authorised correction, override and shutdown must remain
available while control holds.
    The HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, connects the proposed
engineering evidence to governance. Its explanatory paper and draft instruments are working drafts for
consultation; no State has negotiated, adopted or signed them. Their reviews remain incomplete. A gate
log, a benchmark score or a proposed Eden Seal cannot itself confer legal approval or establish that a
system is safe. This summary makes no claim that the draft obligations have been justified by the present
pilot.


5    Related work and the scope of the synthesis
The questions belong beside existing work on feedback, objective preservation and corrigibility. Everitt and
co-authors show preservation of an initially optimal realistic-value policy under modification-independent
utility and belief assumptions; they do not establish that the initial utility represents human values. Soares
and co-authors formulate corrigibility requirements and show failures of candidate utility constructions
rather than a general solution. These are reasons to state premises carefully, not grounds for treating
control as futile (Everitt et al. 2016; Soares et al. 2015).
     Within the programme, Paper X supplies the minimal correction model and the statement paper joins
its questions to the other laws. This summary reorganises those sources and their evidence; it adds no
mathematical or historical-priority result. The earlier priority assertions and cross-domain comparisons
remain explicitly historical in Appendix A, pending the source-specific treatment their own papers require.


6    The next deciding work
The immediate scientific need is a registered comparison that separates a correction mechanism from
changes in information, permitted actions and scoring. A replication of the placement result should
preserve the declared primary scoring rule while giving controls their own outcome-independent sampling
rules. Any changed design is a successor registered before its run, not a repair to an already observed
outcome. A result should say whether the registered effect survives, fails or remains unresolved.
    For growth and correction, the required deliverable is a measured relationship on declared axes, with
uncertainty and competing response forms, not an exponent borrowed from another task. The theory
registration governs the separate verdicts. Error-decay and capacity measures need a stated, tested bridge
before one is read as the other. Persistence and build order need their own experiments; Unit 83 supplies
neither.
    For engineering, the deliverable is a boundary whose stated conformance properties and usefulness
costs can be checked, followed by separately operated replication and qualified review. For governance, it
is evidence adequate for the particular proposed obligation. These steps connect theory, measurement,
application and governance without treating an operational prototype as scientific validation. Designs that
are merely dated remain drafts until the author submits them and their registration is public.

7    What this paper does and does not show
This document provides an executive synthesis and preserves its own prior record. It contributes no new
run, theorem, statistical analysis or priority finding. The five laws remain laws under test. Conditional
derivations are useful because their assumptions and consequences can be inspected; they are not
empirical confirmation. The registered Unit 83 result has the exact limited scope stated in Section 3 and
no independent replication is claimed.
    The working programme has produced papers, tools, source checks, adversarial reviews, dated
revisions and published corrections. Those are evidence of a verification process, not a substitute for
separately operated experiments or journal peer review. Its internal checks are not independent. A reader
can inspect the programme’s review account and follow each scientific claim to the paper or registered
report that carries it.


8    Limitations
The source corpus is the programme’s own selected record, not a systematic review of all AI-safety
research. The historical appendix contains assertions that this edition supersedes; it is preserved for
accountability and must not be read as an alternative current abstract. Several source papers are working
drafts and some engineering requirements remain specified rather than implemented. The reported
measurements concern particular tasks, models and protocols. None establishes inevitability of control
loss, a universal capability ceiling, permanent ethical alignment or general hardware safety.


9    The corrections carried by this edition
The previous abstract’s architectural and priority claims are replaced by a problem-led account with
explicit status. Earlier capability language is separated from error decay. The earlier blinding and
alignment claims now carry the protocol and code-review limitations. Statements that removing ethics
must destroy intelligence, that fixed weights imply a particular scaling regime, or that all self-modification
crosses one universal boundary are not current conclusions. The laws’ registration dates and Unit 83’s
complete qualifications supersede the earlier untested-only status. Old study counts, milestone dates and
catalogue versions remain historical rather than being silently updated as promises. The legal-work origin
and practical engineering role are explained without treating operational use as experimental validation.
Appendix A keeps the earlier words, figures and every dated correction.


10     Conclusion
The programme asks a practical question: can improvement remain correctable and useful, including in
the contingency where control is lost? Its current value is a set of explicit models, registered comparisons
and engineering requirements that can be checked and rejected. The qualified placement result gives one
answer at one scale. The next work must determine which parts survive better controls, different systems
and independent replication, while effective human control is retained.

    Anticipated Questions
    Q: Why hasn’t this been peer-reviewed yet?
    This programme has a dated record reaching back to December 2024, with the first paper of the
suite released on 17 January 2026; the trials that will decide it are drafted and have yet to be run. The
paper suite is deposited on OSF (the umbrella DOI is 10.17605/OSF.IO/6C5XB, while this summary
carries 10.17605/OSF.IO/EWN5D). The mathematical foundations (Cauchy, Hyers-Ulam) are established
theorems. The empirical claims require independent replication, which is exactly what the funding request
would enable.
    Q: Can one person really do this kind of research?
    The infrastructure is computational, not physical. The v5 experiment used cloud APIs. The key
contribution is methodological: recognising that blinding was needed and designing the 4-layer protocol.
What requires funding is scale: more models, more scorers, independent replication teams, and human
evaluators alongside AI scorers.
    Q: If this works, why haven’t AI companies adopted it?
    The Love Loop [the book’s terminology] result is a pilot finding released in March 2026, and it has
not yet been independently replicated. The finding that unblinded evaluation can be directionally wrong
is uncomfortable for organisations that have published unblinded benchmarks. The full implementation
(hardware-level embedding) requires chip design changes no company has incentive to pursue unilaterally;
this is a coordination problem requiring external funding and policy support.
    Q: What is the minimum result that would justify further funding?
    Independent replication of: (1) the three-tier hierarchy under blinding, and (2) the Love Loop [the
book’s terminology] effect (𝑝 < 0.001). If either fails, the falsification conditions document what that
means. If both replicate, the case for the seed stage becomes strong.
    Q: What if the whole framework is wrong?
    The falsification conditions show where it breaks, the blinding methodology remains a field contribution,
and the negative results are published. Science advances from well-designed experiments that can fail,
not from unfalsifiable theories that cannot.
    Q: Why should we trust results where AI systems score other AI systems?
    The 4-layer blinding protocol addresses this: scorers do not know which model produced the response,
responses are ‘laundered’ to remove stylistic fingerprints, and each entry is scored by six to seven models,
the exact number depending on the subject run. In the v4→v5 transition two earlier positive results did
not survive this protocol; several components changed together, so which one did the work is not isolated.
Human evaluator comparison sits inside the seed stage.
