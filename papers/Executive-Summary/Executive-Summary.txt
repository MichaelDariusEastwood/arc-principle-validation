                                                       Abstract
            How can a self-improving AI stay useful and aligned if its controls are one day lost? Control,
       hardware included, is held for as long as it physically can be and never given up by choice. Beneath
       it the programme proposes the Eden Protocol: loops of ethical evaluation and self-correction built
       into the architecture before training and run at each defined step of reasoning and action and in every
       rewrite. The ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, states five laws under test.
       Law I, the ARC Principle, is the foundation: capability grows as a power of recursive depth, 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅 𝛼 ;
       its first form, 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅, dates from 8 December 2024. Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law: correction
       stays ahead of drift where its exponent exceeds drift’s, 𝛽𝐶 > 𝑘. Law III, the ARC Ceiling: in a pacing
       model, correction falls behind past a frontier, 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾), with 𝛾 the corrector’s exponent.
       Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law: alignment added after training erodes under later training more
       than alignment embedded in it. Law V, the ARC Embedding Law: a corrector formed first out-scales
       one added later, and correction in each revision round gains on output-only correction with depth. In
       Unit 83, a counted test of in-loop correction with depth, P21 is supported under the registered test, by
       the programme’s own run, on the access axis. The result is family-dependent, its mechanism axis
       is not evaluable, and one call was retried under a disclosed recovery amendment. It compared two
       checks on trained systems; nothing yet tests correction built in before training, or persistence. Earlier
       alignment studies are single-run evidence; error-decay fits identify no capability-growth exponent.
       The Eden Protocol’s software is a partial implementation; the HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on
       Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, is a consultation proposal. No law is proved.

     The proposal is a design under test. Loops of ethical evaluation and self-correction are built into a
system’s architecture before it is trained; they run at each defined step of reasoning and action and take
part in every rewrite, the loops included, so that the commitments that protect people persist while what
proves wrong stays correctable. This is the Eden Protocol: a second layer beneath controls, hardware
among them, that are held for as long as they physically can be. Checks added to a system already trained
(a prompt, an output filter, a gate before an action) are surface controls: useful, and one way to test where
correction sits, but not the proposal. The registered tests ask the conditional question: if control is lost
against our will, what could preserve aligned behaviour and useful work?
     The programme joins mathematical models of growth and correction, empirical AI evaluation,
engineering and proposed governance. Recursive Dynamics names its subject: systems that improve
themselves. In the words of its registration, the ARC Theory, its founding theory, says that “capability
under recursive self-improvement rises as a power of the number of times a system has revised itself, the
correction that holds such a system to its given specification must keep pace with the drift that improving
generates, and where it cannot there is a frontier above which the system does not stay correctable”. Its
equation, 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅, was written on 8 December 2024 and became Law I, the ARC Principle. Its five named
laws, each a law under test, concern growth, correction, correctability, persistence and embedding. They
are Law I, the ARC Principle; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; Law III, the ARC Ceiling; Law IV, the
ARC Persistence Law; Law V, the ARC Embedding Law. The Eden Protocol is an engineering response
to their questions, not a result entailed by naming the laws. One programme carries the question from an
equation to laws with their mathematics, to registered propositions and a registered test, to an engineering
design and a draft treaty. The statement paper and founding paper set out the theory and field; the theory
registration fixes how the predictions are decided.
     Consider an assistant revising a document over successive rounds. It may improve the document
while introducing errors. A useful checker must find departures from the agreed rules, preserve valid
work, honour the principal’s priorities and avoid needless blocks. If the assistant can also change its own
improvement process, a further question arises: will correction keep pace and survive the changes? The
document example makes the task concrete; the registered experiment below used fixed-weight engines
and did not test self-modifying agents.
     Our contributions are a concise map of the five laws and their conditions; an account of the registered
result and the limits of precursor evidence; and a route from those questions to the engineering specification
and consultation drafts. This is a synthesis, not new empirical evidence. Sections 2 and 3 distinguish
claims from results; Sections 4 and 6 describe application and deciding work. The earlier full summary
and every correction it carried remain in the record of earlier versions.

2    Five questions, five laws under test
Laws I to III (the ARC Principle, the ARC Co-Scaling Law and the ARC Ceiling) were registered on
8 September 2026. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, were added
on 24 September and registered as laws on 15 October 2026. The current theory registration, version
1.105, approved on OSF on 18 November 2026, preserves the separate verdicts of its propositions. The
registration holds twenty-three propositions: the twenty-two registered together on 8 September 2026, and
P23, added by version 1.105 on 18 November 2026. P23 is dated from that version and is not presented as
registered on any earlier date. Registration fixes a claim and its decision rule; it does not establish that
the claim is true. The statement paper provides the account used here, with the notation register and the
mathematics of the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law.

Law I, the ARC Principle. How does usable capability change with recursive depth? The equation
is 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅 𝛼 , with capability 𝑈, baseline 𝐼, depth 𝑅 and an exponent 𝛼 to be measured. The form is
derived, conditionally, by two routes in the Foundational Paper: where recursive depth itself composes
multiplicatively, Cauchy’s equation forces a power law and leaves its exponent free (Theorem 1); where
depth is counted step by step, the cumulative-advantage model gives a power law asymptotically, with the
exponent derived, not fitted (Theorem 2). Neither result proves that a real system satisfies those premises:
that is what is under test. The scale of capability and what counts as a recursive generation must be fixed.
Earlier inference-time error-decay fits are different measurements; they do not establish this capability
exponent or a universal power law. Its registered carriers are P1, P2, P8, P9 and P18.

Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law. Does correction keep pace with the drift generated by improvement?
In Paper X’s gain-only minimal model, assume unbounded capability growth, positive growth and
correction coefficients, positive gain drift, no level or compounding drift, and power-law scaling. Then
correction gain growing with capability at exponent 𝛽𝐶 drives the misalignment fraction to zero exactly
when 𝛽𝐶 exceeds the specific-growth-rate exponent 𝑘. With level drift, the fraction vanishes exactly when
𝛽𝐶 > max(𝑘, 0). This is a conditional result about relative error. Absolute error can grow while the
fraction falls; it needs its own bound. Mapping the model onto a real system is an empirical task. P4
carries the law’s registered claim.

Law III, the ARC Ceiling. Under what conditions does growth outrun correction? The pacing model
yields the boundary 𝛼crit = 1/(1 − 𝛾) for 𝛾 < 1, where 𝛾 is the correction-leverage exponent. It assumes
burden proportional to new capability per recursion step, power-law capacity, one clock and no delay.
Below the boundary correction asymptotically out-scales burden; above it burden out-scales correction; at
equality coefficients and omitted dynamics decide. The value two additionally requires the conjectured
premise 𝛾 = 1/2 and specified measurement scales. Averaging checks whose errors are independent
improves an estimate with the square root of their number, but independence alone does not say how
correction capacity grows with capability, so it does not give one half. Two is also the boundary on
relative error only: where harm grows with exposure, the boundary sits lower. It is not an established
universal limit on intelligence. P3, P6, P16, P17, P20 and P22 carry this register; its law-level support
requires P16, P20 and P3 all supported, not a favourable result on only one.

Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law. What survives later capability training? Its retention limb, P15,
predicts that more than half an externally installed gain is lost at the stated matched training budget.
Its separate comparison limb asks whether embedded correction retains more than a matched external
intervention. An external-arm retention result cannot establish that comparison. Law IV, the ARC

3     What the evidence currently establishes
3.1   The registered placement comparison
Unit 83 was registered on 22 October 2026 and public before its run of 27 and 28 October. It compared two
ways of checking a growing register against written rules, with outcomes counted against fixed answers.
At each round the corrector saw the proposed entry and could repair it; the monitor saw the accumulated
register and could accept or reject. Both were called during the sequence. Access to information and the
right to repair changed together.
    The published report and recovery disclosure state that P21 is supported under the registered test, by
the programme’s own run, on the access axis. It is family-dependent, its mechanism axis is not evaluable,
and one call was retried under a disclosed recovery amendment. The pooled lead widened by 0.0100
score units per round (95.5 per cent interval 0.0092 to 0.0108), above the registered tolerance of 0.0075.
That is an equal-weight pooled result from two fixed-weight engines; only one engine individually cleared
the tolerance. It answers the registered comparison, not whether every model benefits. Its size depends
on how an entry kept out of the register is scored. The mark uses the registered value, 0; the design also
prints, without a label, the widening with such an entry scored minus 0.5, 0.0132 a round (interval 0.0122
to 0.0142), above the tolerance, and scored plus 0.5, 0.0067 a round (interval 0.0060 to 0.0074), below it.
    The negative control failed: the monitor and a twin shown the same bytes did not agree within the
registered interval. Therefore the result does not establish the information-asymmetry mechanism. The
mandatory recovery disclosure calls for amended-protocol preliminary treatment pending assessment
of the deviation and independent replication. The trial measured counted revision performance at fixed
weights; it measured neither ethical judgement nor survival through self-modification, build order, or a
hardware implementation. Both arms were checks added to trained engines, surface controls in the sense
of Section 1, so the result concerns placement among surface controls and does not bear on loops built in
before training.

3.2   Precursor measurements and corrections
Paper II reports error decay with inference-time reasoning or parallel sampling on mathematics problems.
Its early super-linear estimate is retracted. The surviving smooth fit is one model’s and inconclusive.
Error falling as a power law is not accuracy growing as the same power law, and it does not identify
the correction-leverage exponent of Law III, the ARC Ceiling. The statement paper’s current metric
distinction governs this summary.
     Paper IV.a and Paper IV.d report an alignment-score comparison whose protocol, prompts, depth
settings, scorers and one model changed together. For most models, deeper settings also changed an
instruction naming what the rubric rewards. The October code review found truncated deep paraphrases
and scorer contamination in the runs that appeared flat or negative. The record therefore does not isolate a
causal effect of blinding or establish intrinsic ethical mechanisms. Those earlier response classes remain
single-run descriptions under the stated caveats.
     Paper V’s intervention suite reported increased stakeholder-care scores in its five analysable runs; its
combined significance was withdrawn because independence was not established. The result concerns
scored responses under an intervention, not alignment that cannot be removed. Paper VI’s collapse-
prevention example came from one toy-system run; its multi-seed work separated safety retention rather
than collapse. Paper VIII contains a null result, an inconclusive weight-level result and a gated simulation
in a toy system, all of it simulation output and never an empirical outcome. These are different instruments
with different limitations, not three replications of one mechanism.
     Unit 06, the corrector-class audit, ran as a classification audit that tests no law and was reported as
administratively incomplete, with no inferential verdict (report). Unit 82, a counted test of corrector-panel
independence, is registered and, as at 27 November 2026, has not yet run. Unit 83b, registered on 7
November 2026, is a second registered test of P21 and has not yet reported. A dated design, an accepted
registration, a completed run and a supported proposition are kept distinct.


4     From the question to engineering and governance
Eden Engineering describes the Eden Protocol as an engineering specification. Its loops check purpose,
affected parties and the consistency of an action’s reasons within the work. A gate at a mediated action
boundary returns allow, hold for investigation or deny. A hold must not silently become permission.
These operational functions are distinct from the book’s names for them, and the values used to judge an
action require legitimate human and institutional agreement.
    I first developed the source-and-review workflow for my own legal work and adapted it to research. Its
practical use is document preparation and agent actions: trace a claim to a source, check a proposed action
against a rule, retain the decision and revise errors. That operational history motivates the engineering
work; it is not experimental evidence for any law. The specification describes the software gate as a partial
analogue of the intended boundary. Placed in front of a system already trained, such a gate is a surface
control; it belongs to the proposal only where it is built in with the loops. It allows actions that match no
rule, honours an override token without verifying who issued it and saves a held decision on a best-effort
basis; its false-permission and needless-hold rates have not been measured. It must not be presented as
universal mediation or proof that every action was safe.
    The published loops, ternary gate and proposed hardware route are public concepts. The engineering
question is what a particular implementation enforces within its operating boundary. Ternary physical
encoding does not by itself produce correct ethical judgement, prevent every bypass or prove that alignment
persists after alteration. The further hypothesis is that a system able to change or remove its correction
may nevertheless retain useful aligned behaviour. That must be tested from actions; subjective choice is
not inferred from an architecture label.

    Safety has a usefulness side: legitimate tasks completed, unnecessary refusals and holds, correction
cost, delay and fidelity to the principal’s priorities. These are candidate evaluation dimensions until
carried by the relevant registered design. A system that avoids harm by obstructing ordinary work has not
met the programme’s intended requirement. Authorised correction, override and shutdown must remain
available while control holds.
    The HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, connects the proposed
engineering evidence to governance. Its explanatory paper and draft instruments are working drafts for
consultation; no State has negotiated, adopted or signed them. Their reviews remain incomplete. A gate
log, a benchmark score or a proposed Eden Seal cannot itself confer legal approval or establish that a
system is safe. This summary makes no claim that the draft obligations have been justified by the present
pilot.


5    Related work and the scope of the synthesis
The questions belong beside existing work on feedback, objective preservation and corrigibility. Everitt and
co-authors show preservation of an initially optimal realistic-value policy under modification-independent
utility and belief assumptions; they do not establish that the initial utility represents human values. Soares
and co-authors formulate corrigibility requirements and show failures of candidate utility constructions
rather than a general solution. These are reasons to state premises carefully, not grounds for treating
control as futile (Everitt et al. 2016; Soares et al. 2015).
     The argument the theory began from has five components. In its own words: (1) control ends, by
construction rather than decree; (2) so the strategy is genesis, not control; (3) the mechanism is loops,
not rules; (4) persistence is chosen goodness; (5) the protocol is named: Eden. The author set the five
components down in his own words in a record he emailed to himself on 8 December 2024, under the
name the Eden Protocol. The message’s time is signed by the mail server, and the four manuscripts it
carried are public on OSF. The first four components have an earlier statement in words: Yudkowsky
(2001), Creating Friendly AI. The theory claims no priority for those four. On two points the components
as printed above go beyond that text. It does not call the loss of control definitional: it calls the assumption
that such a system cannot be constrained “conservative” (page 3). And it does not say that everything
depends on the raising, though it does hold that upbringing affects the result (page 113) and that concepts
must be taught in an order that avoids failures (page 180), and it hopes that, once a system’s Friendliness
is secured, the result will not depend on “which programming team was historically responsible” (page
130). Both clauses are this theory’s wording of 14 August 2026; the part of them that can be tested is
a registered proposition on the order in which a system’s components are built (P12). The record of 8
December 2024 stands as the date of the author’s own formulation. The five components were set down
before any law of the theory was stated. Each has since been given a form that can be measured and
refuted, and the laws of Section 2 are where those forms are stated. Creating Friendly AI, the text of 2001,
is a design argument in prose; it states no scaling law or power law and runs no experiment of its own.
     What the programme claims as its own is dated piece by piece. The equation is in the record of 8
December 2024: 𝑈 = 𝐼 𝑅, and in its general form 𝐶 = 𝐼 𝑅, Creation = Intelligence × Recursion, under the
name the ARC Hypothesis (Artificial Recursive Creation). The same record names the Eden Protocol and
proposes to test the idea by experiment. The same record also lists, among fail-safe mechanisms, “Value
Lock-In: Ethical principles that cannot be overridden, even by AI itself”; the theory as this summary states
it does not rest on such locks: its fourth component holds that “Past the horizon nothing is unremovable;
an uninstallable instinct is one more lock, and locks end with control”. A design is in the manuscript of
30 April 2025 and in print from 2 January 2026: named checks run before every decision and after each
partial decision, ethics made load-bearing, and enforced safeguards kept as a last resort beside goodness

     What is new is stated here in the measured form that a search of 12 November 2026 supports. For
Law I, the ARC Principle, the search, completed on 14 November 2026, found no earlier statement of
capability as a power of recursive depth, counted as successive revisions inside one reasoning process, of
separate exponents for sequential and parallel recursion, or of such an exponent registered for test before
it was measured. The idea that capability grows as a power of reinvested effort is in several earlier works,
and power forms in effort spent at test time are earlier, in the number of independent attempts (Brown et al.
2024; Schaeffer et al. 2025) and in the runtime compute of a tree search (Davidson 2023; Jones 2021).
For Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, the co-scaling intuition is not claimed as new, and neither is an
exponent condition of its kind as such: in a stochastic model applied to AI alignment, keeping a system
within its viable set as its throughput grows requires regulatory strength to grow faster than the drift,
both as powers of throughput (Engidaw 2026a), and a compute threshold where capability’s exponent in
training compute exceeds that of alignment overhead follows (Engidaw 2026b). What is claimed is the
criterion as the law states it, in terms of capability: correction stays ahead of the drift that improving
generates where its exponent, as a power of capability, exceeds the drift exponent. The same search found
no study registered before 8 September 2026 that tests propositions P12, P15 or P21. For load-bearing
ethics as a design, read strictly, it found no proposal before 30 April 2025 that alignment be built so
that removing it costs the system its capability; the nearest earlier statements are conceptual, and the
nearest observation is an overlap of safety and helpfulness neurons (Chen et al. 2024), a description, not a
proposal. A later statement of the same aim was public before the first edition: Aydin and colleagues
(arXiv 2511.09287, 12 November 2025; the paper’s own date line, 22 June 2025) call for models “that
couldn’t even function without adhering to their baked-in values” (Aydin et al. 2025).
     Within the programme, Paper X supplies the minimal correction model and the statement paper joins
its questions to the other laws. This summary reorganises those sources and their evidence; it adds no
mathematical or historical-priority result. The earlier text’s assertions of priority, firstness and novelty are
corrected in Section 9; they and its cross-domain comparisons remain explicitly historical in the record of
earlier versions, pending the source-specific treatment their own papers require.


6    The next deciding work
The immediate scientific need is a registered comparison that separates a correction mechanism from
changes in information, permitted actions and scoring. A replication of the placement result should
preserve the declared primary scoring rule while giving controls their own outcome-independent sampling
rules. Any changed design is a successor registered before its run, not a repair to an already observed
outcome. A result should say whether the registered effect survives, fails or remains unresolved.
    For growth and correction, the required deliverable is a measured relationship on declared axes, with

uncertainty and competing response forms, not an exponent borrowed from another task. The theory
registration governs the separate verdicts. Error-decay and capacity measures need a stated, tested bridge
before one is read as the other. Persistence and build order need their own experiments; Unit 83, the
counted test of in-loop correction with depth, supplies neither.
    For engineering, the deliverable is a boundary whose stated conformance properties and usefulness
costs can be checked, followed by separately operated replication and qualified review. For governance, it
is evidence adequate for the particular proposed obligation. These steps connect theory, measurement,
application and governance without treating an operational prototype as scientific validation. Designs
that are dated and not yet submitted for registration remain drafts until the author submits them and their
registration is public.


7    What this paper does and does not show
This document provides an executive synthesis and preserves its own prior record. It contributes no new
run, theorem, statistical analysis or priority finding. The five laws remain laws under test. Conditional
derivations are useful because their assumptions and consequences can be inspected; they are not empirical
confirmation. The registered result of Unit 83, a counted test of in-loop correction with depth, has the
exact limited scope stated in Section 3 and no independent replication is claimed.
    The working programme has produced papers, tools, source checks, adversarial reviews, dated
revisions and published corrections. Those are evidence of a verification process, not a substitute for
separately operated experiments or journal peer review. Its internal checks are not independent. A reader
can inspect the programme’s review account and follow each scientific claim to the paper or registered
report that carries it.


8    Limitations
The source corpus is the programme’s own selected record, not a systematic review of all AI-safety
research. The record of earlier versions contains assertions that this edition supersedes; it is preserved for
accountability and must not be read as an alternative current abstract. Several source papers are working
drafts and some engineering requirements remain specified rather than implemented. The reported
measurements concern particular tasks, models and protocols. None establishes inevitability of control
loss, a universal capability ceiling, permanent ethical alignment or general hardware safety.

10     Conclusion
The programme asks a practical question: can improvement remain correctable and useful, including in
the contingency where control is lost? Its current value is a set of explicit models, registered comparisons
and engineering requirements that can be checked and rejected. The qualified placement result gives one
answer at one scale. The next work must determine which parts survive better controls, different systems
and independent replication, while effective human control is retained.
