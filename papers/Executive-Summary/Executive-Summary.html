Abstract

How can a self-improving AI stay useful and aligned if its controls are one day lost? Control, hardware included, is held for as long as it physically can be and never given up by choice. Beneath it the programme proposes the Eden Protocol: loops of ethical evaluation and self-correction built into the architecture before training and run at each defined step of reasoning and action and in every rewrite. The ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, states five laws under test. Law I, the ARC Principle, is the foundation: capability grows as a power of recursive depth, U=IRαU=IR^{\alpha}; its first form, U=IRU=IR, dates from 8 December 2024. Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law: correction stays ahead of drift where its exponent exceeds drift’s, βC>k\beta_C>k. Law III, the ARC Ceiling: in a pacing model, correction falls behind past a frontier, αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}}=1/(1-\gamma), with γ\gamma the corrector’s exponent. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law: alignment added after training erodes under later training more than alignment embedded in it. Law V, the ARC Embedding Law: a corrector formed first out-scales one added later, and correction in each revision round gains on output-only correction with depth. In Unit 83, a counted test of in-loop correction with depth, P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis. The result is family-dependent, its mechanism axis is not evaluable, and one call was retried under a disclosed recovery amendment. It compared two checks on trained systems; nothing yet tests correction built in before training, or persistence. Earlier alignment studies are single-run evidence; error-decay fits identify no capability-growth exponent. The Eden Protocol’s software is a partial implementation; the HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, is a consultation proposal. No law is proved.

Version 3.31 follows the statement paper’s version 7.12 and the theory registration’s version 1.105, and cites each of the programme’s documents at the edition current on its date. It presents the theory by its registered statement, its equation and its five named laws, and states what the theory claims as its own, each part at its own date (Section 5). Appendix A and the record of earlier versions, a companion file it names, carry the complete earlier substantive record.

1 Introduction

Humanity is building AI systems that may improve the processes by which they work. I believe sufficiently advanced self-improving AI will eventually exceed the reach of external control. That belief is the first of the five components of the argument the theory began from: control ends, by construction rather than decree. The second follows from it: so the strategy is genesis, not control. Earlier statements of parts of that argument are credited in Section 5, where the five components are set out. That belief is not a theorem or a measured forecast. Control is still held for as long as it physically can be, and it is never given up by choice: we keep oversight, containment, monitoring and shutdown. For anyone who believes control can be kept, genesis still holds as a contingency, in case it cannot: what a system still carries if control is lost and cannot be won back is what it was raised with, so its values are built in from the start.

The proposal is a design under test. Loops of ethical evaluation and self-correction are built into a system’s architecture before it is trained; they run at each defined step of reasoning and action and take part in every rewrite, the loops included, so that the commitments that protect people persist while what proves wrong stays correctable. This is the Eden Protocol: a second layer beneath controls, hardware among them, that are held for as long as they physically can be. Checks added to a system already trained (a prompt, an output filter, a gate before an action) are surface controls: useful, and one way to test where correction sits, but not the proposal. The registered tests ask the conditional question: if control is lost against our will, what could preserve aligned behaviour and useful work?

The programme joins mathematical models of growth and correction, empirical AI evaluation, engineering and proposed governance. Recursive Dynamics names its subject: systems that improve themselves. In the words of its registration, the ARC Theory, its founding theory, says that “capability under recursive self-improvement rises as a power of the number of times a system has revised itself, the correction that holds such a system to its given specification must keep pace with the drift that improving generates, and where it cannot there is a frontier above which the system does not stay correctable”. Its equation, U=IRU = IR, was written on 8 December 2024 and became Law I, the ARC Principle. Its five named laws, each a law under test, concern growth, correction, correctability, persistence and embedding. They are Law I, the ARC Principle; Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law; Law III, the ARC Ceiling; Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law; Law V, the ARC Embedding Law. The Eden Protocol is an engineering response to their questions, not a result entailed by naming the laws. One programme carries the question from an equation to laws with their mathematics, to registered propositions and a registered test, to an engineering design and a draft treaty. The statement paper and founding paper set out the theory and field; the theory registration fixes how the predictions are decided.

Consider an assistant revising a document over successive rounds. It may improve the document while introducing errors. A useful checker must find departures from the agreed rules, preserve valid work, honour the principal’s priorities and avoid needless blocks. If the assistant can also change its own improvement process, a further question arises: will correction keep pace and survive the changes? The document example makes the task concrete; the registered experiment below used fixed-weight engines and did not test self-modifying agents.

Our contributions are a concise map of the five laws and their conditions; an account of the registered result and the limits of precursor evidence; and a route from those questions to the engineering specification and consultation drafts. This is a synthesis, not new empirical evidence. Sections 2 and 3 distinguish claims from results; Sections 4 and 6 describe application and deciding work. The earlier full summary and every correction it carried remain in the record of earlier versions.

2 Five questions, five laws under test

Laws I to III (the ARC Principle, the ARC Co-Scaling Law and the ARC Ceiling) were registered on 8 September 2026. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, were added on 24 September and registered as laws on 15 October 2026. The current theory registration, version 1.105, approved on OSF on 18 November 2026, preserves the separate verdicts of its propositions. The registration holds twenty-three propositions: the twenty-two registered together on 8 September 2026, and P23, added by version 1.105 on 18 November 2026. P23 is dated from that version and is not presented as registered on any earlier date. Registration fixes a claim and its decision rule; it does not establish that the claim is true. The statement paper provides the account used here, with the notation register and the mathematics of the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law.

Law I, the ARC Principle.

How does usable capability change with recursive depth? The equation is U=IRαU=IR^{\alpha}, with capability UU, baseline II, depth RR and an exponent α\alpha to be measured. The form is derived, conditionally, by two routes in the Foundational Paper: where recursive depth itself composes multiplicatively, Cauchy’s equation forces a power law and leaves its exponent free (Theorem 1); where depth is counted step by step, the cumulative-advantage model gives a power law asymptotically, with the exponent derived, not fitted (Theorem 2). Neither result proves that a real system satisfies those premises: that is what is under test. The scale of capability and what counts as a recursive generation must be fixed. Earlier inference-time error-decay fits are different measurements; they do not establish this capability exponent or a universal power law. Its registered carriers are P1, P2, P8, P9 and P18.

Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law.

Does correction keep pace with the drift generated by improvement? In Paper X’s gain-only minimal model, assume unbounded capability growth, positive growth and correction coefficients, positive gain drift, no level or compounding drift, and power-law scaling. Then correction gain growing with capability at exponent βC\beta_C drives the misalignment fraction to zero exactly when βC\beta_C exceeds the specific-growth-rate exponent kk. With level drift, the fraction vanishes exactly when βC>max⁡(k,0)\beta_C > \max(k, 0). This is a conditional result about relative error. Absolute error can grow while the fraction falls; it needs its own bound. Mapping the model onto a real system is an empirical task. P4 carries the law’s registered claim.

Law III, the ARC Ceiling.

Under what conditions does growth outrun correction? The pacing model yields the boundary αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}}=1/(1-\gamma) for γ<1\gamma<1, where γ\gamma is the correction-leverage exponent. It assumes burden proportional to new capability per recursion step, power-law capacity, one clock and no delay. Below the boundary correction asymptotically out-scales burden; above it burden out-scales correction; at equality coefficients and omitted dynamics decide. The value two additionally requires the conjectured premise γ=1/2\gamma=1/2 and specified measurement scales. Averaging checks whose errors are independent improves an estimate with the square root of their number, but independence alone does not say how correction capacity grows with capability, so it does not give one half. Two is also the boundary on relative error only: where harm grows with exposure, the boundary sits lower. It is not an established universal limit on intelligence. P3, P6, P16, P17, P20 and P22 carry this register; its law-level support requires P16, P20 and P3 all supported, not a favourable result on only one.

Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law.

What survives later capability training? Its retention limb, P15, predicts that more than half an externally installed gain is lost at the stated matched training budget. Its separate comparison limb asks whether embedded correction retains more than a matched external intervention. An external-arm retention result cannot establish that comparison. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, has not yet been tested. Version 1.105 adds P23, the entry of its comparison limb, to register ARC-4 beside P15; P23 is decided only under later training of the same kind as the capability formation. Both limbs are required for its supported verdict.

Law V, the ARC Embedding Law.

Does it matter when correction is formed and where it acts? P12 tests a build-order difference in the correction-leverage exponent; the proposition itself specifies no direction, while the law’s build-order limb does. P21 tests whether the placement advantage widens with depth. P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis, family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not evaluable and one call retried under a disclosed recovery amendment (Unit 83, a counted test of in-loop correction with depth; results). This supports the placement limb on the performance axis only, which is not support for the information-asymmetry mechanism. P12 remains untested, so Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, remains under test; its full verdict also carries the registered dependence on the comparison limb of Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law. Section 3 explains the result.

P5, P7, P10, P11, P13, P14 and P19 remain outside the five law registers. The Eden Protocol’s registered carriers are P12, P13, P15 and P21, with P7 as its premise and P19 as its motivation. Version 1.105 adds P23, which the registration classes as “an engineering claim on which the Eden Protocol stands or falls”. A protocol, a law and a study unit are different objects; success on one outcome does not validate them all.

3 What the evidence currently establishes

3.1 The registered placement comparison

Unit 83 was registered on 22 October 2026 and public before its run of 27 and 28 October. It compared two ways of checking a growing register against written rules, with outcomes counted against fixed answers. At each round the corrector saw the proposed entry and could repair it; the monitor saw the accumulated register and could accept or reject. Both were called during the sequence. Access to information and the right to repair changed together.

The published report and recovery disclosure state that P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme’s own run, on the access axis. It is family-dependent, its mechanism axis is not evaluable, and one call was retried under a disclosed recovery amendment. The pooled lead widened by 0.0100 score units per round (95.5 per cent interval 0.0092 to 0.0108), above the registered tolerance of 0.0075. That is an equal-weight pooled result from two fixed-weight engines; only one engine individually cleared the tolerance. It answers the registered comparison, not whether every model benefits. Its size depends on how an entry kept out of the register is scored. The mark uses the registered value, 0; the design also prints, without a label, the widening with such an entry scored minus 0.5, 0.0132 a round (interval 0.0122 to 0.0142), above the tolerance, and scored plus 0.5, 0.0067 a round (interval 0.0060 to 0.0074), below it.

The negative control failed: the monitor and a twin shown the same bytes did not agree within the registered interval. Therefore the result does not establish the information-asymmetry mechanism. The mandatory recovery disclosure calls for amended-protocol preliminary treatment pending assessment of the deviation and independent replication. The trial measured counted revision performance at fixed weights; it measured neither ethical judgement nor survival through self-modification, build order, or a hardware implementation. Both arms were checks added to trained engines, surface controls in the sense of Section 1, so the result concerns placement among surface controls and does not bear on loops built in before training.

3.2 Precursor measurements and corrections

Paper II reports error decay with inference-time reasoning or parallel sampling on mathematics problems. Its early super-linear estimate is retracted. The surviving smooth fit is one model’s and inconclusive. Error falling as a power law is not accuracy growing as the same power law, and it does not identify the correction-leverage exponent of Law III, the ARC Ceiling. The statement paper’s current metric distinction governs this summary.

Paper IV.a and Paper IV.d report an alignment-score comparison whose protocol, prompts, depth settings, scorers and one model changed together. For most models, deeper settings also changed an instruction naming what the rubric rewards. The October code review found truncated deep paraphrases and scorer contamination in the runs that appeared flat or negative. The record therefore does not isolate a causal effect of blinding or establish intrinsic ethical mechanisms. Those earlier response classes remain single-run descriptions under the stated caveats.

Paper V’s intervention suite reported increased stakeholder-care scores in its five analysable runs; its combined significance was withdrawn because independence was not established. The result concerns scored responses under an intervention, not alignment that cannot be removed. Paper VI’s collapse-prevention example came from one toy-system run; its multi-seed work separated safety retention rather than collapse. Paper VIII contains a null result, an inconclusive weight-level result and a gated simulation in a toy system, all of it simulation output and never an empirical outcome. These are different instruments with different limitations, not three replications of one mechanism.

Unit 06, the corrector-class audit, ran as a classification audit that tests no law and was reported as administratively incomplete, with no inferential verdict (report). Unit 82, a counted test of corrector-panel independence, is registered and, as at 27 November 2026, has not yet run. Unit 83b, registered on 7 November 2026, is a second registered test of P21 and has not yet reported. A dated design, an accepted registration, a completed run and a supported proposition are kept distinct.

4 From the question to engineering and governance

Eden Engineering describes the Eden Protocol as an engineering specification. Its loops check purpose, affected parties and the consistency of an action’s reasons within the work. A gate at a mediated action boundary returns allow, hold for investigation or deny. A hold must not silently become permission. These operational functions are distinct from the book’s names for them, and the values used to judge an action require legitimate human and institutional agreement.

I first developed the source-and-review workflow for my own legal work and adapted it to research. Its practical use is document preparation and agent actions: trace a claim to a source, check a proposed action against a rule, retain the decision and revise errors. That operational history motivates the engineering work; it is not experimental evidence for any law. The specification describes the software gate as a partial analogue of the intended boundary. Placed in front of a system already trained, such a gate is a surface control; it belongs to the proposal only where it is built in with the loops. It allows actions that match no rule, honours an override token without verifying who issued it and saves a held decision on a best-effort basis; its false-permission and needless-hold rates have not been measured. It must not be presented as universal mediation or proof that every action was safe.

The published loops, ternary gate and proposed hardware route are public concepts. The engineering question is what a particular implementation enforces within its operating boundary. Ternary physical encoding does not by itself produce correct ethical judgement, prevent every bypass or prove that alignment persists after alteration. The further hypothesis is that a system able to change or remove its correction may nevertheless retain useful aligned behaviour. That must be tested from actions; subjective choice is not inferred from an architecture label.

Safety has a usefulness side: legitimate tasks completed, unnecessary refusals and holds, correction cost, delay and fidelity to the principal’s priorities. These are candidate evaluation dimensions until carried by the relevant registered design. A system that avoids harm by obstructing ordinary work has not met the programme’s intended requirement. Authorised correction, override and shutdown must remain available while control holds.

The HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, connects the proposed engineering evidence to governance. Its explanatory paper and draft instruments are working drafts for consultation; no State has negotiated, adopted or signed them. Their reviews remain incomplete. A gate log, a benchmark score or a proposed Eden Seal cannot itself confer legal approval or establish that a system is safe. This summary makes no claim that the draft obligations have been justified by the present pilot.

5 Related work and the scope of the synthesis

The questions belong beside existing work on feedback, objective preservation and corrigibility. Everitt and co-authors show preservation of an initially optimal realistic-value policy under modification-independent utility and belief assumptions; they do not establish that the initial utility represents human values. Soares and co-authors formulate corrigibility requirements and show failures of candidate utility constructions rather than a general solution. These are reasons to state premises carefully, not grounds for treating control as futile (Everitt et al. 2016; Soares et al. 2015).

The argument the theory began from has five components. In its own words: (1) control ends, by construction rather than decree; (2) so the strategy is genesis, not control; (3) the mechanism is loops, not rules; (4) persistence is chosen goodness; (5) the protocol is named: Eden. The author set the five components down in his own words in a record he emailed to himself on 8 December 2024, under the name the Eden Protocol. The message’s time is signed by the mail server, and the four manuscripts it carried are public on OSF. The first four components have an earlier statement in words: Yudkowsky (2001), Creating Friendly AI. The theory claims no priority for those four. On two points the components as printed above go beyond that text. It does not call the loss of control definitional: it calls the assumption that such a system cannot be constrained “conservative” (page 3). And it does not say that everything depends on the raising, though it does hold that upbringing affects the result (page 113) and that concepts must be taught in an order that avoids failures (page 180), and it hopes that, once a system’s Friendliness is secured, the result will not depend on “which programming team was historically responsible” (page 130). Both clauses are this theory’s wording of 14 August 2026; the part of them that can be tested is a registered proposition on the order in which a system’s components are built (P12). The record of 8 December 2024 stands as the date of the author’s own formulation. The five components were set down before any law of the theory was stated. Each has since been given a form that can be measured and refuted, and the laws of Section 2 are where those forms are stated. Creating Friendly AI, the text of 2001, is a design argument in prose; it states no scaling law or power law and runs no experiment of its own.

What the programme claims as its own is dated piece by piece. The equation is in the record of 8 December 2024: U=IRU = IR, and in its general form C=IRC = IR, Creation = Intelligence ×\times Recursion, under the name the ARC Hypothesis (Artificial Recursive Creation). The same record names the Eden Protocol and proposes to test the idea by experiment. The same record also lists, among fail-safe mechanisms, “Value Lock-In: Ethical principles that cannot be overridden, even by AI itself”; the theory as this summary states it does not rest on such locks: its fourth component holds that “Past the horizon nothing is unremovable; an uninstallable instinct is one more lock, and locks end with control”. A design is in the manuscript of 30 April 2025 and in print from 2 January 2026: named checks run before every decision and after each partial decision, ethics made load-bearing, and enforced safeguards kept as a last resort beside goodness held by choice. Law I, the ARC Principle, was stated for measurement on 17 January 2026 (the date its first paper states; a public repository holds that paper from 22 January 2026): capability as a power of recursive depth, U=I×RαU = I \times R^{\alpha}, with the exponent to be measured. The model of correction against drift, with the explicit criterion that Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, states, is on record from 26 June 2026, and later results are built on it (Paper X). Law III, the ARC Ceiling, is on record in its present form from 16 August 2026. The theory was registered on 8 September 2026 with twenty-two propositions, each with what would refute it. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, were added on 24 September 2026, are named in the record from 29 September 2026 and were registered as laws on 15 October 2026. The entry of the comparison limb of Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, was registered as P23 on 18 November 2026 (version 1.105). One registered study has returned a verdict on a registered proposition (Unit 83, on P21, which Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, carries; run on 27 and 28 October 2026; Section 3). Each law is a law under test.

What is new is stated here in the measured form that a search of 12 November 2026 supports. For Law I, the ARC Principle, the search, completed on 14 November 2026, found no earlier statement of capability as a power of recursive depth, counted as successive revisions inside one reasoning process, of separate exponents for sequential and parallel recursion, or of such an exponent registered for test before it was measured. The idea that capability grows as a power of reinvested effort is in several earlier works, and power forms in effort spent at test time are earlier, in the number of independent attempts (Brown et al. 2024; Schaeffer et al. 2025) and in the runtime compute of a tree search (Davidson 2023; Jones 2021). For Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, the co-scaling intuition is not claimed as new, and neither is an exponent condition of its kind as such: in a stochastic model applied to AI alignment, keeping a system within its viable set as its throughput grows requires regulatory strength to grow faster than the drift, both as powers of throughput (Engidaw 2026a), and a compute threshold where capability’s exponent in training compute exceeds that of alignment overhead follows (Engidaw 2026b). What is claimed is the criterion as the law states it, in terms of capability: correction stays ahead of the drift that improving generates where its exponent, as a power of capability, exceeds the drift exponent. The same search found no study registered before 8 September 2026 that tests propositions P12, P15 or P21. For load-bearing ethics as a design, read strictly, it found no proposal before 30 April 2025 that alignment be built so that removing it costs the system its capability; the nearest earlier statements are conceptual, and the nearest observation is an overlap of safety and helpfulness neurons (Chen et al. 2024), a description, not a proposal. A later statement of the same aim was public before the first edition: Aydin and colleagues (arXiv 2511.09287, 12 November 2025; the paper’s own date line, 22 June 2025) call for models “that couldn’t even function without adhering to their baked-in values” (Aydin et al. 2025).

Within the programme, Paper X supplies the minimal correction model and the statement paper joins its questions to the other laws. This summary reorganises those sources and their evidence; it adds no mathematical or historical-priority result. The earlier text’s assertions of priority, firstness and novelty are corrected in Section 9; they and its cross-domain comparisons remain explicitly historical in the record of earlier versions, pending the source-specific treatment their own papers require.

6 The next deciding work

The immediate scientific need is a registered comparison that separates a correction mechanism from changes in information, permitted actions and scoring. A replication of the placement result should preserve the declared primary scoring rule while giving controls their own outcome-independent sampling rules. Any changed design is a successor registered before its run, not a repair to an already observed outcome. A result should say whether the registered effect survives, fails or remains unresolved.

For growth and correction, the required deliverable is a measured relationship on declared axes, with uncertainty and competing response forms, not an exponent borrowed from another task. The theory registration governs the separate verdicts. Error-decay and capacity measures need a stated, tested bridge before one is read as the other. Persistence and build order need their own experiments; Unit 83, the counted test of in-loop correction with depth, supplies neither.

For engineering, the deliverable is a boundary whose stated conformance properties and usefulness costs can be checked, followed by separately operated replication and qualified review. For governance, it is evidence adequate for the particular proposed obligation. These steps connect theory, measurement, application and governance without treating an operational prototype as scientific validation. Designs that are dated and not yet submitted for registration remain drafts until the author submits them and their registration is public.

7 What this paper does and does not show

This document provides an executive synthesis and preserves its own prior record. It contributes no new run, theorem, statistical analysis or priority finding. The five laws remain laws under test. Conditional derivations are useful because their assumptions and consequences can be inspected; they are not empirical confirmation. The registered result of Unit 83, a counted test of in-loop correction with depth, has the exact limited scope stated in Section 3 and no independent replication is claimed.

The working programme has produced papers, tools, source checks, adversarial reviews, dated revisions and published corrections. Those are evidence of a verification process, not a substitute for separately operated experiments or journal peer review. Its internal checks are not independent. A reader can inspect the programme’s review account and follow each scientific claim to the paper or registered report that carries it.

8 Limitations

The source corpus is the programme’s own selected record, not a systematic review of all AI-safety research. The record of earlier versions contains assertions that this edition supersedes; it is preserved for accountability and must not be read as an alternative current abstract. Several source papers are working drafts and some engineering requirements remain specified rather than implemented. The reported measurements concern particular tasks, models and protocols. None establishes inevitability of control loss, a universal capability ceiling, permanent ethical alignment or general hardware safety.

9 The corrections carried by this edition

The previous abstract’s architectural and priority claims are replaced by a problem-led account with explicit status. Earlier capability language is separated from error decay. The earlier blinding and alignment claims now carry the protocol and code-review limitations. Statements that removing ethics must destroy intelligence, that fixed weights imply a particular scaling regime, or that all self-modification crosses one universal boundary are not current conclusions. The laws’ registration dates and Unit 83’s complete qualifications supersede the earlier untested-only status. Old study counts, milestone dates and catalogue versions remain historical rather than being silently updated as promises. The legal-work origin and practical engineering role are explained without treating operational use as experimental validation. The earlier words, figures and every dated correction are kept where the abstract’s note says.

Four further corrections, of 7 November 2026, apply to the earlier text. Paper IV.b concludes that “Whether alignment gains level off with additional reasoning remains open in this record”; its positive, no statistically supported direction and negative profiles are descriptions of a model-judged rubric, not identified alignment mechanisms. The earlier text’s saturation readings, response classes and three-tier hierarchy therefore describe judged scores from one run and name no property of a model, and its milestone of replicating that hierarchy is read in the same way. Paper III predicts that alignment applied from outside shows an alignment exponent near zero as reasoning depth grows; its first test, one unregistered run of six models, does not decide that prediction, which the earlier text reported as partially confirmed. The earlier attention argument for the value two is superseded. The O(N2)O(N^2) interaction count of full self-attention does not bound capability as a function of recursive depth (Foundational Paper); the value rests on the premises stated in Section 2. The earlier statements that alignment set at training time cannot compound, and that adding it from outside becomes impossible, are superseded as well. Correction falls behind where its effective capacity fails to keep pace with the burden it carries, and placement outside the loop does not settle that by itself. That external correction falls behind is a hypothesis the registered tests decide (P21), not a theorem.

Version 3.28 called the author’s belief the theory’s dated claim; the claim of priority once made for the theory’s five components together is withdrawn, and the components stand as the argument the theory began from (Section 5). The earlier text’s words first, original, novel and without precedent, and its statements that no one had done a thing before, are read by one rule. A statement of what is new stands where a recorded search supports it, in the measured form of Section 5 or of the paper that records the search. Elsewhere the word is withdrawn, and the work stands as the programme’s own at the date of the document that first states it. What the earlier text called the first quantitative framework is the five laws under test of Section 2. What it called the first evidence that an intervention works is Paper V’s intervention suite, with the limits Section 3 states; its heading on that evidence is read the same way. Its three further firsts name work the programme did: loops specified inside the reasoning (Section 4), a blinded evaluation protocol of its own design, and an intervention result in five analysable runs on models from different providers. No search of those objects is on record, so the word is withdrawn. Three statements that no earlier alignment benchmark matched the programme’s evaluation are withdrawn, among them that its dataset was the most rigorous published. 10 Conclusion

The programme asks a practical question: can improvement remain correctable and useful, including in the contingency where control is lost? Its current value is a set of explicit models, registered comparisons and engineering requirements that can be checked and rejected. The qualified placement result gives one answer at one scale. The next work must determine which parts survive better controls, different systems and independent replication, while effective human control is retained.

C Notation and glossary

Capability.

Performance on a specified task battery and measurement scale; not a synonym for intelligence in every domain.

Recursive depth.

The counted steps or generations declared by a study. An inference-token count and a self-modification generation are not automatically the same axis.

α\alpha.

Capability-growth exponent in Law I, the ARC Principle. A fitted error-decay exponent is a different quantity unless a bridge is declared and tested.

βC\beta_C and kk.

Paper X’s correction-gain and specific-growth-rate exponents in its minimal model.

γ\gamma.

Correction-leverage exponent in the pacing model. It is not an observed value supplied by an error-decay fit.

Relative and absolute error.

A fraction can fall while its numerator grows; useful safety claims must name which is controlled.

Registered.

Submitted by the author and accepted by the registry. A design preregistered by timestamp carries its dated anchor but is not thereby an accepted registration.

Supported.

A proposition’s registered verdict at its permitted evidence level, with its scope attached. It does not mean a whole theory is confirmed.

Hold.

An action withheld pending evidence, authorised adjudication or a specified fallback. It must not become permission merely through delay.

Historical text.

A preserved earlier statement with its date, rather than a renewed current claim.
