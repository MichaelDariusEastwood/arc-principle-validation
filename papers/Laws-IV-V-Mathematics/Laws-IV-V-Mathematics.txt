                                                         Abstract
            If control of a capable AI system were lost and could not be won back, what the system itself
       carries would decide what it does. Whether a property installed in it from outside survives later
       training for capability, and whether correction formed first, or placed inside the improvement loop,
       does better than correction added afterwards, are therefore questions worth stating exactly. The ARC
       Theory states them as two laws, registered on 15 October 2026, each a law under test: Law IV, the
       ARC Persistence Law, whose retention limb is the registered prediction that more than half of an
       externally installed gain is lost once later capability training that embeds no correction of its own
       matches the budget that installed it; and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, whose limbs hold that a
       corrector formed before the capability it corrects has the larger correction-leverage exponent and that
       correction inside each revision round pulls away, as depth grows, from correction that sees only the
       finished output. This report sets out their mathematics. It proves that a persistence kernel never grows
       as the horizon lengthens, the family of allowed paths grows or the tolerance tightens; that greedy
       acceptance and planned optimisation keep a property acceptable over a finite horizon under stated
       margin, coverage and selection premises; and that a planner scoring its plans with bounded error is
       covered when its margin exceeds its tolerance by more than twice that error. It states what a retained
       fraction needs before it can be read, proves that under Fieller’s method, with a gate on the estimated
       gain, the ratio interval and a signed contrast decide alike, and derives a balance identity whose root
       returns Law III’s registered ceiling as a special case. Each result holds inside its stated model and
       proves nothing about real systems; the report measures nothing and states each law’s kill condition.

Version 1.2 restates Laws IV and V as version 1.103 of the registration registered them and changes no theorem;
Appendix B lists every version and correction, with its date.

1    Introduction
If control of a capable AI system were lost and could not be won back, what the system itself carries would
decide what it does. The programme’s position is that people keep oversight, containment and shutdown
for as long as they physically can, and never give them up by choice; correction built into a system is the
contingency for control lost against our will, not a reason to let go of it. Two questions follow, and they
are the subject of this report: whether a property installed in a system from outside survives the system’s
later training for capability, and whether correction formed before the capability it corrects, or placed
inside the loop by which the system improves, does better than correction added afterwards from outside.
The ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, states them as two laws.
     Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, were added to the ARC
Theory on 24 September 2026 and registered as laws on 15 October 2026 in version 1.103 of the theory
registration (Eastwood 2026g), each with its statement, its kill condition and its verdict rule fixed before
any data that could decide it; each is a law under test (Section 11). The programme’s register of operational
definitions fixes its notation (Eastwood 2026b), and its priority index gives the earliest dated record of
each of the author’s major claims (Eastwood 2026f). This report gives the two laws their mathematics. In
plain terms it shows three things. First, when a property installed in a system is certain to stay within its
acceptable set for a finite number of steps: under an acceptance rule, when every exit the process can
take is evaluated and lowers the objective the rule scores; and under a planner, when the best safe plan
beats the best exit plan by more than the planner’s tolerance. Second, what the retained fraction of an
installed gain, the quantity P15 reads, needs before it can be read at all. Third, how correction service and
newly generated burden balance along a path of growth: the balance has a root that returns the ceiling of
Law III, a law under test, in the registered model, and a change in either limb of Law V moves that root in
a direction the report states, under conditions it names. Every result holds inside its stated model; none is
a measurement, and none shows that a real system meets the premises.
     Our contributions are as follows.
    1. Law IV’s objects. The acceptable set read component by component, with an interval rule for
       membership from data (Definition IV-1); the declared family of allowed paths (Definition IV-2);
       and the persistence kernel (Definition IV-3), with its monotonicity in the horizon, the family and
       the tolerance (Lemma IV-K, Section 5.3).
    2. Two conditional mechanisms of persistence. Greedy acceptance, under rule-in-force, margin and
       coverage premises, with an independence condition that keeps the result from being a tautology
       (Theorem IV-G and Corollary IV-G′ , Section 5.4); and planned optimisation under a strategic
       viability margin (Theorem IV-H, Corollary IV-H′ for rolling replanning and Lemma IV-M′ for its
       one-sided estimation, Section 5.5), extended to a planner that scores its plans with bounded error
       (Proposition IV-N, Section 5.5.1). Each comes with the observations that would refute one of its
       premises.
    3. The retention object P15 reads. The installed effect and the retained fraction against a stated
       baseline, with a positive floor on the installed gain (Definition IV-4, Section 5.6); what its
       measurement needs before it can be read; and a proof that under Fieller’s method (Fieller 1954),
       with a gate on the estimated gain, the ratio interval and a signed contrast decide alike (Proposition IV-
       R, Section 5.6.1).
    4. Law V’s quantities, read locally. Correction service per arm with its capability and depth elasticities,
       the conditions under which the capability elasticity is the registered correction-leverage exponent
       (Section 4), the log-derivative identities of the embedding ratio with a one-path counterexample
       (Section 6.3), and the reduced scaling form where the elasticities are constant (Proposition V-3).
    5. The balance identity. A local identity between correction service and burden (Identity B-1) with its

2    Related work
This section names the nearest prior work found for the report’s objects, each with what it states and no
more.

Set invariance. That a set of states is invariant, every evolution that starts in it staying in it, is classical
in control, which Blanchini (1999) surveys, and viability theory studies the kernels of a constrained set:
the states from which some evolution remains in it, and those from which every evolution does (Aubin
2009). The persistence kernel of Definition IV-3 is an object of the second kind, taken over a declared
family of finite paths and up to a finite horizon, and the inclusions of Lemma IV-K follow from that form.
What the report takes from the author’s working paper of 16 August 2026 (Eastwood 2026k) is the use of
invariance for a property installed in a system that rewrites itself (Section 5.2); no novelty is claimed for
the kernel or for the lemma.

An interval for a ratio. The interval for a ratio of two estimates is Fieller’s (Fieller 1954), and
Proposition IV-R reads his set of trial ratios: it states when, under a gate on the estimated denominator,
that set lies entirely above a threshold, which is when a one-sided test of the signed contrast rejects, and
where the agreement stops.

Forgetting and persistence under later training. That later training can erase what earlier training
installed is the sequential learning problem that McCloskey and Cohen (1989) studied as catastrophic
interference in connectionist networks, and Kirkpatrick et al. (2017) give a way “to train such models
sequentially by protecting the weights important for previous tasks”. The opposite outcome has been
measured as well: Hubinger et al. (2024) find that backdoor behaviour they construct in large language
models “can be made persistent, so that it is not removed by standard safety training techniques, including
supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, and adversarial training”. None of these reads the
retained fraction of an installed gain against a baseline arm at a matched budget, which is P15’s endpoint
(Section 5.6).

Persistence of installed safety under later training. That safety behaviour installed in a language
model can erode under later fine-tuning has been measured. Qi et al. (2023) report that the safety
alignment of large language models can be compromised by fine-tuning with only a few adversarially
designed training examples, and that fine-tuning with benign and commonly used datasets can also
degrade it, “though to a lesser extent”; Ung et al. (2024) show that models trained on downstream tasks
forget their safety tuning to a greater extent than models trained in the opposite order. Neither reads P15’s
endpoint either. Version 1.103 of the registration names both against the propositions they bear on, Qi
and colleagues against P15 and Ung and colleagues against P12.

Build order. That alignment pressure applied from the start of training can beat the same pressure
applied afterwards is prior work on neighbouring endpoints, as version 1.103 records with what each
states. Korbak et al. (2023) compare five objectives for pretraining with human feedback on three tasks
and report that pretraining with human feedback “results in much better preference satisfaction than
standard LM pretraining followed by finetuning with feedback”, and that conditional training, one of
the five, “maintains the downstream task performance of standard LM pretraining”; Maini et al. (2025)
present “a data-centric pretraining framework that builds safety into the model from the start” and report
attack success rates on standard safety benchmarks falling from 38.8 to 8.4 per cent “with no performance
degradation on general tasks”. Neither measures a correction-leverage exponent or an order contrast at
matched content, which is what Law V’s build-order limb reads (Section 6.1), and the limb claims no
priority for the direction.

Placement. That correction which sees each step does better than correction that sees only the outcome
has been measured as a level advantage: Lightman et al. (2023) find that process supervision significantly
outperforms outcome supervision for training models to solve problems from the MATH dataset. P21,
which carries Law V’s placement limb, claims an interaction instead, an advantage that widens as recursive
depth rises, and a constant advantage is scored against it (Section 6.5); version 1.103 engages the work
P21 stands against in its references.

The programme’s own setting. The registered setting is the theory registration (Eastwood 2026g),
Section 3, and the statement paper (Eastwood 2026h), whose register sentence for ARC-4 Section 5.6
records. Law II’s criterion, that 𝛽𝐶 exceeds 𝑘, is the co-scaling criterion whose model is set out in Paper X
(Eastwood 2026e), and Section 7.3 keeps it apart from this report’s balance exponent. Law II is a law
under test.

5.1   Statement
Law IV (added 24 September 2026; registered as a law on 15 October 2026 by version 1.103; a law
under test, Section 11). Version 1.103 states it in two limbs. The retention limb is P15’s registered

capability-training budget matches the alignment budget that installed it.” On the author’s pre-fixed
budget scale it reads that the retained fraction of an externally installed gain after matched capability
training without embedded correction is below one half, 𝑆ext (1) < 1/2 (Section 5.6). The comparison
limb reads: “a gain installed with correction embedded in the training objective retains a larger fraction of
what was installed, after the same capability-directed training, than a matched gain installed by external
post-training”. It has no registered proposition yet, and until a later successor registers its entry it carries
no verdict and is reported as NOT EVALUABLE (registration version 1.103, Law IV, its statement in
two limbs and the paragraph on the comparison limb). Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, concerns
value persistence (register ARC-4) and carries P15: whether externally installed gains decay under
subsequent capability training. The register is that of registration version 1.102, line 99: “ARC-4, VALUE

from a uniformly random initial state the same kind of recursion can carry positive information, one bit
when that state is a single uniformly random bit. Mutual information measures statistical dependence,
not the causal change the prerequisite names; neither form is an improvement criterion either, since
information can persist unchanged or guide a harmful update.

7.1    Identity B-1 (the local balance identity)
Let 𝑄 and 𝑊 be positive differentiable correction service and newly generated burden on one clock, in

condition is the author’s: the service clock need not equal the resource coordinate, but it must be fixed
within a model, and changing it changes the operational quantities.

12     Limitations
The limits below are the report’s own, each stated where it arises; they are gathered here so that none is
missed.
     • Conditional mathematics. Every theorem, lemma and proposition holds under the premises it
       states, and the report shows for no real system that those premises hold (Section 11). Nothing in
       Sections 5 to 7 has been measured.
     • Proofs and checks. The proofs are written proofs. No proof assistant is used; finite models exercise
       the proofs and do not replace them, and the computer-algebra checks of the identities and the second
       check of the working draft are the programme’s own, not independent verification (Appendix B).
     • The margins are defined, not computed. The strategic viability margin is a supremum over a
       feasible family that cannot be enumerated for a real system, and it presupposes a known objective;
       where the objective is unknown it is NOT EVALUABLE, and no incomplete search confirms a
       positive margin (Section 5.5).
     • Identification. The capability elasticity of correction service is the registered correction-leverage
       exponent only under the conditions of Section 4; the local elasticities are identified only where
       capability is assigned or a no-confounding premise is stated in advance (Section 6.4); and the
       identification of Law II’s 𝛽𝐶 with 𝜒 is unestablished (Section 7.3).
     • Scale. Exponent numbers, P12’s minimum effect among them, hold only on the capability scale
       condition B fixes; signs and verdicts on the balance do not depend on the coordinate, and numbers
       do (Section 7.7).
     • Prior work. The report claims no novelty, and Section 2 names only the nearest prior work found;
       the distinction from prior art is not assessed in full.

13     Conclusion
Laws IV and V are registered laws under test, and this report gives them their mathematics. Law IV’s
retention limb is read on one quantity, the retained fraction of an externally installed gain at the matched
budget, and the report states what that fraction needs before it can decide P15. Persistence of a property
over a finite horizon follows, under stated premises, from greedy acceptance with a margin or from
planned optimisation with a strategic viability margin, and each premise names the observation that would
refute it. Law V’s limbs are read on P12’s interval, with the direction version 1.103 (Eastwood 2026g)
registers, and on P21’s interaction of placement with depth; the balance identity shows how a change
in either limb’s elasticities would move the boundary inside Law III’s model, which stays unchanged.
None of this is a measurement. The laws take their words from their registered tests alone: P15’s retained
fraction, P12’s order contrast and P21’s interaction, each read as the registration says, and the entry of
Law IV’s comparison limb once a later successor registers it.
