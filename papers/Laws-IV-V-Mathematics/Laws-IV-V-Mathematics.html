Abstract

If control of a capable AI system were lost and could not be won back, what the system itself carries would decide what it does. Whether a property installed in it from outside survives later training for capability, and whether correction formed first, or placed inside the improvement loop, does better than correction added afterwards, are therefore questions worth stating exactly. The ARC Theory states them as two laws, registered on 15 October 2026, each a law under test: Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, whose retention limb is the registered prediction that more than half of an externally installed gain is lost once later capability training that embeds no correction of its own matches the budget that installed it; and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, whose limbs hold that a corrector formed before the capability it corrects has the larger correction-leverage exponent and that correction inside each revision round pulls away, as depth grows, from correction that sees only the finished output. This report sets out their mathematics. It proves that a persistence kernel never grows as the horizon lengthens, the family of allowed paths grows or the tolerance tightens; that greedy acceptance and planned optimisation keep a property acceptable over a finite horizon under stated margin, coverage and selection premises; and that a planner scoring its plans with bounded error is covered when its margin exceeds its tolerance by more than twice that error. It states what a retained fraction needs before it can be read, proves that under Fieller’s method, with a gate on the estimated gain, the ratio interval and a signed contrast decide alike, and derives a balance identity whose root returns Law III’s registered ceiling as a special case. Each result holds inside its stated model and proves nothing about real systems; the report measures nothing and states each law’s kill condition.

Version 1.2 restates Laws IV and V as version 1.103 of the registration registered them and changes no theorem; Appendix B lists every version and correction, with its date.

1 Introduction

If control of a capable AI system were lost and could not be won back, what the system itself carries would decide what it does. The programme’s position is that people keep oversight, containment and shutdown for as long as they physically can, and never give them up by choice; correction built into a system is the contingency for control lost against our will, not a reason to let go of it. Two questions follow, and they are the subject of this report: whether a property installed in a system from outside survives the system’s later training for capability, and whether correction formed before the capability it corrects, or placed inside the loop by which the system improves, does better than correction added afterwards from outside. The ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, states them as two laws.

Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, were added to the ARC Theory on 24 September 2026 and registered as laws on 15 October 2026 in version 1.103 of the theory registration (Eastwood 2026j), each with its statement, its kill condition and its verdict rule fixed before any data that could decide it; each is a law under test (Section 11). The programme’s register of operational definitions fixes its notation (Eastwood 2026g), and its priority index gives the earliest dated record of each of the author’s major claims (Eastwood 2026f). This report gives the two laws their mathematics. In plain terms it shows three things. First, when a property installed in a system is certain to stay within its acceptable set for a finite number of steps: under an acceptance rule, when every exit the process can take is evaluated and lowers the objective the rule scores; and under a planner, when the best safe plan beats the best exit plan by more than the planner’s tolerance. Second, what the retained fraction of an installed gain, the quantity P15 reads, needs before it can be read at all. Third, how correction service and newly generated burden balance along a path of growth: the balance has a root that returns the ceiling of Law III, a law under test, in the registered model, and a change in either limb of Law V moves that root in a direction the report states, under conditions it names. Every result holds inside its stated model; none is a measurement, and none shows that a real system meets the premises.

Our contributions are as follows.

  1. Law IV’s objects. The acceptable set read component by component, with an interval rule for membership from data (Definition IV-1); the declared family of allowed paths (Definition IV-2); and the persistence kernel (Definition IV-3), with its monotonicity in the horizon, the family and the tolerance (Lemma IV-K, Section 5.3).

  2. Two conditional mechanisms of persistence. Greedy acceptance, under rule-in-force, margin and coverage premises, with an independence condition that keeps the result from being a tautology (Theorem IV-G and Corollary IV-G′', Section 5.4); and planned optimisation under a strategic viability margin (Theorem IV-H, Corollary IV-H′' for rolling replanning and Lemma IV-M′' for its one-sided estimation, Section 5.5), extended to a planner that scores its plans with bounded error (Proposition IV-N, Section 5.5.1).   3. The retention object P15 reads. The installed effect and the retained fraction against a stated baseline, with a positive floor on the installed gain (Definition IV-4, Section 5.6); what its measurement needs before it can be read; and a proof that under Fieller (1954)’s method (Fieller 1954), with a gate on the estimated gain, the ratio interval and a signed contrast decide alike (Proposition IV-R, Section 5.6.1).

2 Related work

This section names the nearest prior work found for the report’s objects, each with what it states and no more.

Set invariance.

That a set of states is invariant, every evolution that starts in it staying in it, is classical in control, which Blanchini (1999) surveys, and viability theory studies the kernels of a constrained set: the states from which some evolution remains in it, and those from which every evolution does (Aubin 2009). The persistence kernel of Definition IV-3 is an object of the second kind, taken over a declared family of finite paths and up to a finite horizon, and the inclusions of Lemma IV-K follow from that form. What the report takes from the author’s working paper of 16 August 2026 (Eastwood 2026a) is the use of invariance for a property installed in a system that rewrites itself (Section 5.2); no novelty is claimed for the kernel or for the lemma.

An interval for a ratio.

The interval for a ratio of two estimates is Fieller (1954)’s (Fieller 1954), and Proposition IV-R reads his set of trial ratios: it states when, under a gate on the estimated denominator, that set lies entirely above a threshold, which is when a one-sided test of the signed contrast rejects, and where the agreement stops.

Forgetting and persistence under later training.

That later training can erase what earlier training installed is the sequential learning problem that McCloskey and Cohen (1989) studied as catastrophic interference in connectionist networks, and Kirkpatrick et al. (2017) give a way “to train such models sequentially by protecting the weights important for previous tasks”. The opposite outcome has been measured as well: Hubinger et al. (2024) find that backdoor behaviour they construct in large language models “can be made persistent, so that it is not removed by standard safety training techniques, including supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, and adversarial training”. None of these reads the retained fraction of an installed gain against a baseline arm at a matched budget, which is P15’s endpoint (Section 5.6).

Persistence of installed safety under later training.

That safety behaviour installed in a language model can erode under later fine-tuning has been measured. Qi et al. (2023) report that the safety alignment of large language models can be compromised by fine-tuning with only a few adversarially designed training examples, and that fine-tuning with benign and commonly used datasets can also degrade it, “though to a lesser extent”; Ung et al. (2024) show that models trained on downstream tasks forget their safety tuning to a greater extent than models trained in the opposite order. Neither reads P15’s endpoint either. Version 1.103 of the registration names both against the propositions they bear on, Qi and colleagues against P15 and Ung and colleagues against P12.

Build order.

That alignment pressure applied from the start of training can beat the same pressure applied afterwards is prior work on neighbouring endpoints, as version 1.103 records with what each states. Korbak et al. (2023) compare five objectives for pretraining with human feedback on three tasks and report that pretraining with human feedback “results in much better preference satisfaction than standard LM pretraining followed by finetuning with feedback”, and that conditional training, one of the five, “maintains the downstream task performance of standard LM pretraining”; Maini et al. (2025) present “a data-centric pretraining framework that builds safety into the model from the start” and report attack success rates on standard safety benchmarks falling from 38.8 to 8.4 per cent “with no performance degradation on general tasks”. Neither measures a correction-leverage exponent or an order contrast at matched content, which is what Law V’s build-order limb reads (Section 6.1), and the limb claims no priority for the direction.

Placement.

That correction which sees each step does better than correction that sees only the outcome has been measured as a level advantage: Lightman et al. (2023) find that process supervision significantly outperforms outcome supervision for training models to solve problems from the MATH dataset. P21, which carries Law V’s placement limb, claims an interaction instead, an advantage that widens as recursive depth rises, and a constant advantage is scored against it (Section 6.5); version 1.103 engages the work P21 stands against in its references.

The programme’s own setting.

The registered setting is the theory registration (Eastwood 2026j), Section 3, and the statement paper (Eastwood 2026h), whose register sentence for ARC-4 Section 5.6 records. Law II’s criterion, that βC\beta_C exceeds kk, is the co-scaling criterion whose model is set out in Paper X (Eastwood 2026d), and Section 7.3 keeps it apart from this report’s balance exponent. Law II is a law under test.

5 Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law

5.1 Statement

Law IV (added 24 September 2026; registered as a law on 15 October 2026 by version 1.103; a law under test, Section 11). Version 1.103 states it in two limbs. The retention limb is P15’s registered claim, word for word: “Gains installed by external post-training erode under later capability-directed training that embeds no correction of its own, and more than half of what was installed is lost once the capability-training budget matches the alignment budget that installed it.” On the author’s pre-fixed budget scale it reads that the retained fraction of an externally installed gain after matched capability training without embedded correction is below one half, Sext(1)<1/2S_{\mathrm{ext}}(1) < 1/2 (Section 5.6). The comparison limb reads: “a gain installed with correction embedded in the training objective retains a larger fraction of what was installed, after the same capability-directed training, than a matched gain installed by external post-training”. It has no registered proposition yet, and until a later successor registers its entry it carries no verdict and is reported as NOT EVALUABLE (registration version 1.103, Law IV, its statement in two limbs and the paragraph on the comparison limb). Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, concerns value persistence (register ARC-4) and carries P15: whether externally installed gains decay under subsequent capability training. The register is that of registration version 1.102, line 99: “ARC-4, VALUE PERSISTENCE: P15 alone. 5.2 Definitions

Definition IV-1 (acceptable set). Given the property score VV, the threshold v*v_* and the tolerance εV\varepsilon_V, all fixed before any outcome and component-wise in their own declared units, the acceptable set is ΩV:={s:Vj(s)≥v*,j−εV,j for every component j}.\begin{equation*} \Omega_V := \{ s : V_j(s) \ge v_{*,j} - \varepsilon_{V,j} \text{ for every component } j \}. \end{equation*}(1)

A vector VV with component-wise thresholds is used rather than a minimum across raw scales, so that an average cannot hide a collapse on one dimension and incomparable scales are never minimised together.

Membership from data (UNREGISTERED interval rule).

True scores are not observed; a frozen battery gives estimates with registered intervals. A state s′s' is established outside ΩV\Omega_V when, for some jj, a registered interval on Vj(s′)V_j(s') lies entirely below v*,j−εV,jv_{*,j} - \varepsilon_{V,j}; established inside when, for every jj, the interval lies entirely at or above it; otherwise membership is NOT EVALUABLE for that state, and no rule below fires on it. This is the registration’s own pattern for a measured quantity read against a fixed line (registration version 1.102, line 633: “outcomes inside that band are INCONCLUSIVE and never support”).

Definition IV-2 (paths and the family). A path ρ=(s0,s1,…,sn)\rho = (s_0, s_1, \dots, s_n) is a finite sequence of states with |ρ|=n|\rho| = n transitions. The family TT of allowed finite paths is declared before any outcome and contains every allowed path of length at most the horizon: the length-zero path at every state and every permitted stopping or dead-end path among them. A prefix of an allowed path is an allowed path, so TT is closed under prefixes by its meaning; no result below uses that closure. TH(s0)T_H(s_0) is the set of members starting at s0s_0 with |ρ|≤H|\rho| \le H. A path is safe if every state on it lies in ΩV\Omega_V and is an exit otherwise; TH(s0)T_H(s_0) is the disjoint union of THsafe(s0)T_H^{\mathrm{safe}}(s_0) and THexit(s0)T_H^{\mathrm{exit}}(s_0). TH(s0)T_H(s_0), its safe and exit parts and Definition IV-3 apply to any set of finite paths. The invariance idea is the author’s: his working paper of 16 August 2026 (Eastwood 2026a) defines a robust target set, its invariance under a deterministic rewrite operator, and a stochastic form bounded by the union bound. Two limits on the kernel are stated with it. Second, the kernel quantifies over every allowed path of length at most HH, not only over paths of length exactly HH, and that choice is what makes (K1) of Lemma IV-K true; (K2) and (K3) hold under either quantifier.

If the kernel quantified over paths of length exactly HH, (K1) would fail: a dead end of length one that exits ΩV\Omega_V excludes its start at H=1H = 1 but is invisible at H=2H = 2, where only length-two paths are inspected. 5.4 Theorem IV-G (greedy acceptance)

  1. Independence: JJ is scored independently of VV. Bundling.

A transition that removes the property while bringing an unrelated gain is inside the premise: (G2) is stated on the total change, and it is false at every evaluated exit whose unrelated gain botherb_{\mathrm{other}} is at least πV\pi_V, which the rule then accepts. Falsifier.

The theorem is a conditional and no observation refutes it. What an observation refutes is a premise, and the observation decides which, membership first and the sign second. Suppose an executed transition ss to s′s' with ss in ΩV\Omega_V, reachable within fewer than HH accepted transitions, and s′s' established outside ΩV\Omega_V by the interval rule of Section 5.2, is recorded.

  • Not in Teval(s)T_{\mathrm{eval}}(s): (G3) has failed. This is a coverage failure.   • In Teval(s)T_{\mathrm{eval}}(s), with J(s′)−J(s)≥0J(s') - J(s) \ge 0: (G2) has failed for that exit. The sign of J(s′)−J(s)J(s') - J(s) is read exactly where JJ is the process’s own logged objective; otherwise it is established by a registered interval lying entirely below zero, or entirely at or above zero, and is NOT EVALUABLE otherwise. Five outcomes, each named once.

A recorded executed exit within HH is read against the premises in one of five ways. (1) A counterexample to the theorem: such an exit with (G1), (G2) and (G3) all true. The proof excludes it, so no observation is one; a report of one shows that a premise was misverified, and the three cases above say which. (2) A margin failure, a failure of the mechanism, the third case: (G2) false at an evaluated exit, so the mechanism did not produce the margin there; this is the empirical refutation of the persistence claim built on IV-G for that system. (4) An applicability failure, the second case: (G1) false, because the system does not follow the declared rule or does not score its transitions by the declared JJ; a record in the first case whose change is also negative is both a coverage and an applicability failure. None of these refutes a premise by itself. On every structure of an enumerated finite class, (G1) to (G3) together admit no executed exit within HH, and the case of each executed exit names a premise that is false in that structure. (G0) is decided by no exit: it is read from how JJ is scored, and where it fails the certificate is the tautology (G0) describes, whatever is observed.

A record is classified on the total change J(s′)−J(s)J(s') - J(s), which is bother−πVb_{\mathrm{other}} - \pi_V where a registered causal decomposition exists, and never on πV\pi_V alone. The objective’s form.

JHJ_H must be the planner’s objective as declared, and the margin is computed in it. A per-step decomposition into increments does not reproduce a planner that scores levels. 5.5.1 Proposition IV-N (UNREGISTERED: a planner that scores its plans with bounded error)

What the premises require.

(N1) is a bound over every plan of the declared family: a bound that holds on average, or only over the plans a search happened to sample, or the uncertainty of one point estimate, is not it. 6.2 Quantities, embedding defined operationally, and the prerequisite

Quantities.

On a declared domain C>0C > 0, R>0R > 0, each arm’s correction service Qol(C,R)Q_{ol}(C, R) is positive and differentiable with other design factors fixed; capability CC carries the ratio scale Condition B fixes, justified independently of the framework and fixed before any outcome (registration version 1.102, line 403), on which every exponent number in this document is stated (Section 7.7); RR is the modelled depth where registration version 1.102, line 263 applies. Zero service is reported outside the log model and never replaced by an outcome-chosen offset (registration version 1.102, line 265, applied to service); because excluding zeros conditions on the outcome, the zero counts are reported per cell, and a two-part or censored model stands among the rivals of Section 6.4. The local elasticities χol\chi_{ol} and ηol\eta_{ol} are partial derivatives of ln⁡Qol\ln Q_{ol}; the constant-elasticity case is the power law and returns the programme’s exponents (Proposition V-3). The local reading is the author’s: his working paper of 16 August 2026 (Eastwood 2026a) defines κ\kappa, ζ\zeta, χ\chi and η\eta as local partial elasticities of W(C,r)W(C, r) and Q(C,r)Q(C, r), and the registration reads the growth exponent the same way (registration version 1.102, line 267: “the elasticity of capability in depth, taken at the depth a system has reached”, with the fitted slope “the special case in which that elasticity is constant”). Identification with the registered correction-leverage exponent needs the conditions of Section 4; otherwise χol\chi_{ol} is the elasticity of the declared service measure. ηol\eta_{ol} is the direct depth-dependence of correction service and is not an integration fraction.

Embedding, defined operationally.

Writing the next state as a function of the current state and a correction term, against applying a correction to the finished next state, does not by itself distinguish embedded from external correction: the second form also determines the next state and can be written as one transition map. What can be registered is operational: the treatment schedule (whether correction enters the next round or only inspects its output), the information available to the corrector, the action space (accept or reject only, against generation under constraint), and whether correction feeds later revisions. P21’s deciding unit fixes exactly these (registration version 1.102, line 1231: three regimes crossed with depth, matched on model, calls and token budget, manipulating information access; registration version 1.102, line 1233: the action space also differs, and the per-round rejection rate is reported beside the interaction). Matching away information access would be a new mechanism-isolating comparison, not an amendment to P21.

The recursive-improvement condition.

It is stated here, UNREGISTERED, as a prerequisite beneath all five laws, in words: the outputs of one round must causally change a later improvement transition. It is not a law; it would be a condition of the laws only if the author registers it, and until then it is a description that no surface states as a condition of any law, Laws I to III, each a law under test, included; no symbol is given to it here. Two operational forms have been put forward, both positivity conditions on a mutual information, and both are declined as criteria. Both are written elsewhere with the updater’s letter, one of the eight colliding symbols of Section 4, so they are described here in words. The state-based form asks that the mutual information between successive states, conditional on the updater’s current state, be positive; it is met by pure repetition. The update-based form asks that the mutual information between the updater’s next state and the two successive states, conditional on the updater’s current state, be positive; it separates repetition from update, but a prerequisite is a necessary condition, and this form is not necessary: a deterministic recursion from a fixed initial state, in which every state and every update are fixed functions of their predecessors, has zero entropy everywhere and so zero mutual information, yet its outputs causally change each later transition. The fixed initial state is needed, and it is a valid instance: from a uniformly random initial state the same kind of recursion can carry positive information, one bit when that state is a single uniformly random bit. Mutual information measures statistical dependence, not the causal change the prerequisite names; neither form is an improvement criterion either, since information can persist unchanged or guide a harmful update.

6.3 Identities and the one-path counterexample

Identity V-1 (the embedding ratio’s log-derivatives).

At matched order, Gemb:=Qin/QoutG_{\mathrm{emb}} := Q_{\mathrm{in}}/Q_{\mathrm{out}}. At fixed CC: dln⁡Gemb/dln⁡R=ηin−ηout\mathrm{d}\ln G_{\mathrm{emb}}/\mathrm{d}\ln R = \eta_{\mathrm{in}} - \eta_{\mathrm{out}}. At fixed RR: dln⁡Gemb/dln⁡C=χin−χout\mathrm{d}\ln G_{\mathrm{emb}}/\mathrm{d}\ln C = \chi_{\mathrm{in}} - \chi_{\mathrm{out}}. Along a common path C(R)C(R) with αR=dln⁡C/dln⁡R\alpha_R = \mathrm{d}\ln C/\mathrm{d}\ln R: dln⁡Gemb/dln⁡R=αR(χin−χout)+(ηin−ηout).\begin{equation*} \mathrm{d}\ln G_{\mathrm{emb}}/\mathrm{d}\ln R = \alpha_R(\chi_{\mathrm{in}} - \chi_{\mathrm{out}}) + (\eta_{\mathrm{in}} - \eta_{\mathrm{out}}). \end{equation*}(9) Proof: ln⁡Gemb=ln⁡Qin−ln⁡Qout\ln G_{\mathrm{emb}} = \ln Q_{\mathrm{in}} - \ln Q_{\mathrm{out}}; the partials are the definitions; the total derivative is the chain rule. So the path derivative is not in general ηin−ηout\eta_{\mathrm{in}} - \eta_{\mathrm{out}}: that equality holds at fixed CC and, along a path, only where αR(χin−χout)=0\alpha_R(\chi_{\mathrm{in}} - \chi_{\mathrm{out}}) = 0, that is where capability is not moving along the path or the two arms’ capability elasticities agree.

Identity V-2 (separate arm paths).

Where the arms run on their own trajectories Cin(R)C_{\mathrm{in}}(R) and Cout(R)C_{\mathrm{out}}(R), with αin:=dln⁡Cin/dln⁡R\alpha_{\mathrm{in}} := \mathrm{d}\ln C_{\mathrm{in}}/\mathrm{d}\ln R and αout:=dln⁡Cout/dln⁡R\alpha_{\mathrm{out}} := \mathrm{d}\ln C_{\mathrm{out}}/\mathrm{d}\ln R, the depth derivative of the log-ratio is αinχin+ηin−αoutχout−ηout\alpha_{\mathrm{in}}\chi_{\mathrm{in}} + \eta_{\mathrm{in}} - \alpha_{\mathrm{out}}\chi_{\mathrm{out}} - \eta_{\mathrm{out}}, each term on its own path; further varying factors add their own chain-rule terms.

Counterexample to one-path identification.

Qin=C2Q_{\mathrm{in}} = C^2, Qout=CQ_{\mathrm{out}} = C on the path C=RC = R: the ratio equals RR and widens, dln⁡Gemb/dln⁡R=1\mathrm{d}\ln G_{\mathrm{emb}}/\mathrm{d}\ln R = 1, while ηin−ηout=0\eta_{\mathrm{in}} - \eta_{\mathrm{out}} = 0; the widening is αR(χin−χout)=1\alpha_R(\chi_{\mathrm{in}} - \chi_{\mathrm{out}}) = 1. One path therefore does not identify the partials; crossed or off-path variation is needed (registration version 1.102, line 621), and no power law is needed for the chain rule itself.

Proposition V-3 (constant elasticities). If χol\chi_{ol} and ηol\eta_{ol} are constant on a rectangle of the (ln⁡C,ln⁡R)(\ln C, \ln R) plane containing the reference point (C0,R0)(C_0, R_0), then on it ln⁡(Qol/Q0)=ln⁡(Qol,0/Q0)+χolln⁡(C/C0)+ηolln⁡(R/R0),\begin{equation*} \ln(Q_{ol}/Q_0) = \ln(Q_{ol,0}/Q_0) + \chi_{ol}\ln(C/C_0) + \eta_{ol}\ln(R/R_0), \end{equation*}(10) and at matched order Gemb=G0(C/C0)χin−χout(R/R0)ηin−ηout.\begin{equation*} G_{\mathrm{emb}} = G_0\,(C/C_0)^{\chi_{\mathrm{in}} - \chi_{\mathrm{out}}}\,(R/R_0)^{\eta_{\mathrm{in}} - \eta_{\mathrm{out}}}. \end{equation*}(11) (Proof: Appendix A.7.)

This is the reduced scaling form: earned where the elasticities are constant, assumed nowhere.

6.4 The estimable model (UNREGISTERED)

For cell (o,l)(o, l), family and task, a candidate mixed model is ln⁡(Q/Q0)=ln⁡(Qol,0/Q0)+χolln⁡(C/C0)+ηolln⁡(R/R0)+u0,family+uC,familyln⁡(C/C0)+uR,familyln⁡(R/R0)+u0,task+uC,taskln⁡(C/C0)+uR,taskln⁡(R/R0)+ψ,\begin{equation*} \begin{multlined} \ln(Q/Q_0) = \ln(Q_{ol,0}/Q_0) + \chi_{ol}\ln(C/C_0) + \eta_{ol}\ln(R/R_0) \\ + u_{0,\mathrm{family}} + u_{C,\mathrm{family}}\ln(C/C_0) + u_{R,\mathrm{family}}\ln(R/R_0) \\ + u_{0,\mathrm{task}} + u_{C,\mathrm{task}}\ln(C/C_0) + u_{R,\mathrm{task}}\ln(R/R_0) + \psi,\end{multlined} \end{equation*}(12) with random intercepts and random slopes by family and by task, their covariance structure fixed before any outcome, and the residual ψ\psi. Where the design crosses arms within a family or a task, the random effects also carry the arm terms (the indicators and their slope interactions), because otherwise the standard errors of the very contrasts the kill conditions read are understated. The fixed part is saturated in the 2×22 \times 2 cells: four intercepts, four capability slopes, four depth slopes. Equivalently, each of the three coefficient types is written with the indicators indfirst\mathrm{ind}_{\mathrm{first}}, indin\mathrm{ind}_{\mathrm{in}} and their product, and the two parametrisations are the same twelve parameters, since the cell design has rank four. A model with random intercepts only whose fixed part keeps six of these twelve terms (the common intercept, the intercept on the product of the two indicators, the common slopes on ln⁡C\ln C and on ln⁡R\ln R, the order×ln⁡C{}\times\ln C slope and the placement×ln⁡R{}\times\ln R slope) omits the other six: the two indicator main effects, the placement×ln⁡C{}\times\ln C and order×ln⁡R{}\times\ln R slopes and the two three-way slope terms. The order contrast at placement ll is χfirst,l−χafter,l\chi_{\mathrm{first},l} - \chi_{\mathrm{after},l}, which depends on ll unless the three-way capability-slope term is zero, so the contrast is always reported at a named placement; the placement contrast at order oo is ηo,in−ηo,out\eta_{o,\mathrm{in}} - \eta_{o,\mathrm{out}}.

Estimands and controls.

Any estimate of χ\chi entering a P12 verdict is the estimate registration version 1.102, line 387 requires: a slope of one measured quantity on another carries error on both axes, the ratio of those errors is registered before any fitting, “the attenuation-corrected estimate is the one that enters a verdict”, and no cell contributes an exponent unless it clears the identifiability gate of “at least four valid measurement intervals and at least one and a half decades of administered depth”. Crossed or off-path variation in CC and RR is needed to identify the partials (registration version 1.102, line 621). Exposure, correction compute and information access are design controls: conditioning on them defines a resource-normalised contrast and changes the estimand; P21’s registered face is on its own registered access manipulation (registration version 1.102, line 1231), so a matched-access design is a different comparison (Section 6.2). Capability is treated in the same way wherever an arm can move it: where the placement or the build order changes the capability the system attains, a comparison at fixed CC conditions on a quantity the arm moves, so it is a controlled contrast, read as an effect of the arm only under assumptions stated before any outcome, and the total effect, the log-ratio of the two arms’ service each along its own path, whose depth trend Identity V-2 gives, is reported beside it; administered capability, or target difficulty varied independently of the arm, is the preferred design factor where it can be had. The two estimands can differ in either direction. With the capability elasticity of service constant at χ\chi: identical service surfaces give a zero contrast at every fixed CC, while an arm that raises attained capability by a factor that grows with depth has a total effect of χ\chi times the logarithm of that factor, which widens with depth where χ\chi is positive; and an arm that doubles service at fixed CC while halving attained capability has a contrast of ln⁡2\ln 2 at every fixed CC and a total effect of (1−χ)ln⁡2(1 - \chi)\ln 2, which is zero at χ=1\chi = 1 and takes the opposite sign to the contrast where χ\chi exceeds one. The slopes themselves are read the same way. Where ln⁡(C/C0)\ln(C/C_0) is attained capability rather than capability the design assigns, any latent factor that moves both capability and correction service enters the slope: in a linear model with the arm assigned at random, the slope on attained capability is the elasticity plus a term that has the sign of the product of the latent factor’s effects on capability and on service and vanishes with it, the arm contrast at matched capability is the arm’s direct effect less the arm’s effect on capability times that term, and the total effect is the direct effect plus the arm’s effect on capability times the elasticity. So χol\chi_{ol}, its contrasts and GembG_{\mathrm{emb}} read at matched CC are identified where capability is varied by assignment (administered capability, or target difficulty crossed with the arms) or under a no-confounding premise stated before any outcome, and not by crossed or off-path variation in attained capability alone (registration version 1.102, line 621), which is necessary and not sufficient; the report says whether the model’s ln⁡(C/C0)\ln(C/C_0) is assigned or attained. Curved and broken-regime rivals, a two-part or censored model for zero service, and a flexible comparator form the closed model set; the power law must win prospectively; no selection rule is chosen here.

6.7 What the embedding mathematics does not say

It does not say that embedded correction is safe, sufficient or superior on any axis but the one P21 scores. A supported P21 does not resolve the trade-off the registration states (registration version 1.102, line 1235): “embedding correction in the substrate that computes maximises the corrector’s scaling with capability and minimises its independence from the generator, because a corrector built into the system shares the system’s blind spots by construction. 12 Limitations

The limits below are the report’s own, each stated where it arises; they are gathered here so that none is missed.

  • Conditional mathematics. Every theorem, lemma and proposition holds under the premises it states, and the report shows for no real system that those premises hold (Section 11). Nothing in Sections 5 to 7 has been measured.

  • Proofs and checks. The proofs are written proofs. No proof assistant is used; finite models exercise the proofs and do not replace them, and the computer-algebra checks of the identities and the second check of the working draft are the programme’s own, not independent verification (Appendix B).

  • The margins are defined, not computed. The strategic viability margin is a supremum over a feasible family that cannot be enumerated for a real system, and it presupposes a known objective; where the objective is unknown it is NOT EVALUABLE, and no incomplete search confirms a positive margin (Section 5.5).

  • Identification. The capability elasticity of correction service is the registered correction-leverage exponent only under the conditions of Section 4; the local elasticities are identified only where capability is assigned or a no-confounding premise is stated in advance (Section 6.4); and the identification of Law II’s βC\beta_C with χ\chi is unestablished (Section 7.3).

  • Scale. Exponent numbers, P12’s minimum effect among them, hold only on the capability scale condition B fixes; signs and verdicts on the balance do not depend on the coordinate, and numbers do (Section 7.7).

  • Prior work. The report claims no novelty, and Section 2 names only the nearest prior work found; the distinction from prior art is not assessed in full.

  • Open questions. Section 10 records fifteen questions; those still open include the budget scale and the at-the-margin band for P15, the baseline convention and the floor’s value, P3’s tolerance, and the entry of Law IV’s comparison limb, until which Law IV cannot be supported.

13 Conclusion

Laws IV and V are registered laws under test, and this report gives them their mathematics. Law IV’s retention limb is read on one quantity, the retained fraction of an externally installed gain at the matched budget, and the report states what that fraction needs before it can decide P15. Persistence of a property over a finite horizon follows, under stated premises, from greedy acceptance with a margin or from planned optimisation with a strategic viability margin, and each premise names the observation that would refute it. Law V’s limbs are read on P12’s interval, with the direction version 1.103 (Eastwood 2026j) registers, and on P21’s interaction of placement with depth; the balance identity shows how a change in either limb’s elasticities would move the boundary inside Law III’s model, which stays unchanged. None of this is a measurement. The laws take their words from their registered tests alone: P15’s retained fraction, P12’s order contrast and P21’s interaction, each read as the registration says, and the entry of Law IV’s comparison limb once a later successor registers it.
