# The mathematics of Laws IV and V: when do installed values fade with training, and does building them in first change that?

**Version:** 1.4
**Revised:** 22 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-10-01
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 1.4):

> Does a property installed in an AI survive later capability training, and does correction built in first, or in each revision round, beat correction added later? If control is lost against our will, what a system carries decides what it does. The ARC Theory states two laws on them, registered on 15 October 2026, each a law under test. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, predicts that more than half of an externally installed gain is lost once later capability training without embedded correction matches the installing budget, and that one installed with correction embedded in training keeps a fraction larger by at least a tenth. Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, holds in two limbs that a corrector formed before the capability it corrects has the larger correction-leverage exponent and that in-round correction pulls away with depth from correcting the finished output. The report gives their mathematics: a persistence kernel never grows as horizon lengthens, paths multiply or tolerance tightens. Under stated premises, greedy acceptance and planning keep a property acceptable for a finite horizon, as does a planner whose margin exceeds its tolerance by over twice its scoring error. Neither reaches gradient-step training. In a minimal training model a gain decays at rates the later loss’s curvature sets; under later training of the same kind, one installed with the capability loss in the objective keeps at least as much as one added later. Minimal models of the two limbs show what tests must measure: in-round detection by depth beside the outside checker’s two rates, and an early corrector’s retention of coverage. It sets conditions for reading retained fractions, proves that under a gate the ratio interval and a signed contrast decide alike, and returns Law III, the ARC Ceiling, as a balance identity’s root. It measures nothing, proves nothing of real systems and states each law’s kill condition.

From the paper (version 1.4):

> A report of the ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics: it sets out the mathematics of Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law (register ARC-4, carried by P15 and P23), and Law V, the ARC Embedding Law (register ARC-5, carried by P12 and P21), both added on 24 September 2026 and registered as laws on 15 October 2026 in version 1.103 of the theory registration, each a law under test, their mathematics: conditional theorems with proofs, identities and kill conditions, a minimal training model for Law IV and two minimal models for Law V (the report’s own models; not registered), and fifteen questions, four of them settled by the registration and six more in part (Section 10).
