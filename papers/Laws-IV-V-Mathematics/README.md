# The mathematics of Laws IV and V: when do installed values fade with training, and does building them in first change that?

## Summary

From the abstract (version 1.3):

> Does a property installed in an AI survive later capability training, and does correction built in first, or inside each revision round, beat correction added later? If control is lost against our will, what a system carries decides what it does. The ARC Theory states two laws on these questions, registered on 15 October 2026, each a law under test. Law IV, the ARC Persistence Law, predicts that more than half of an externally installed gain is lost once later capability training without embedded correction matches the installing budget. Law V, the ARC Embedding Law, holds in two limbs that a corrector formed before the capability it corrects has the larger correction-leverage exponent and that correction inside each round pulls away with depth from correction of the finished output. This report gives their mathematics: a persistence kernel never grows as the horizon lengthens, the allowed paths multiply or the tolerance tightens. Under stated premises, greedy acceptance and planning keep a property acceptable for a finite horizon, as does a planner scoring with bounded error if its margin exceeds its tolerance by over twice that error. Neither reaches gradient-step training. In a minimal training model a gain decays at rates the later loss’s curvature sets; under later training of the same kind, one installed with the capability loss in the objective keeps at least as much as one added later. Two minimal models of those limbs show what tests must measure: the in-round corrector’s detection by depth beside the outside checker’s two rates, and how well an early corrector keeps its coverage. It sets conditions for reading a retained fraction, proves that under a gate the classical ratio interval and a signed contrast decide alike, and returns Law III, the ARC Ceiling, as a balance identity’s root. It measures nothing, proves nothing of real systems and states each law’s kill condition.
