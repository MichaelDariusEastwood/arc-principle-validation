> Humanity is building minds that will improve themselves. Once such a mind exceeds us, what determines what it does? This form states the ARC Theory, the founding theory of Recursive Dynamics, on two printed pages. The theory is its registered statement and five laws: capability rises as a power of the number of times a system has revised itself, correction must keep pace with the drift that improving generates, and where it cannot there is a frontier above which the system does not stay correctable. It began from the author's five-part argument of 8 December 2024, printed here with its credit. Control is held for as long as engineering allows; the controls cannot be the whole plan. Each of the five laws is a law under test. Law I, the ARC Principle, $U = I \times R^{\alpha}$, is a form derived under stated axioms, its exponent to be measured, never assumed. Law II, the ARC Co-Scaling Law, $\beta > k$, is proved for positive gain drift in a minimal model, for relative drift. Law III, the ARC Ceiling, $\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma)$, is derived under stated assumptions, its value two, the ARC Bound, conjectured. Laws IV and V, the ARC Persistence Law and the ARC Embedding Law, were registered as laws on 15 October 2026; Law IV is not yet tested. Unit 83, the programme's first registered study to reach a verdict, tested P21 under Law V: P21 is supported under the registered test, by the programme's own run, on the access axis, family-dependent, with its mechanism axis not evaluable and one call retried under a disclosed recovery amendment. The form gives the dated record of that argument, what would refute each of the theory's five registers and what falls with it, the programme's public retraction, and an entry point a laboratory can estimate from logged series it already has.
