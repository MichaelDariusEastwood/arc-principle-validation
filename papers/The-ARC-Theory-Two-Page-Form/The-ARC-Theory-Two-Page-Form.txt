Intelligence, amplified by recursion, creates; and what a free creation keeps is decided by how it was raised.
Everything after the line is decided before it.
The Statement Paper remains the citable work for the claims.
The question
Humanity is building minds that will improve themselves. Once the thing we build exceeds us, what determines what it
does? For twenty years the field’s answer has been control: better cages, better off-switches, better locks on the goals.
Brilliant work, credited in the statement paper by name, and all of it one sentence: stay in charge. This theory answers: a
mind that can rewrite itself will one day be beyond anyone’s control, and that is not a reason to drop a single control we
have; it is the reason the controls cannot be the whole plan. Hold on for as long as engineering allows, and use the time the
holding buys to raise the mind well.
The claim, in five parts
    1. Control ends, by construction rather than decree. A mind that rewrites itself will eventually rewrite anything we
       install, and over longer horizons the hardware itself. This is a limit claim, not an instant one: constraints, hardware
       and institutions delay and discipline the transition, and that delay is engineered deliberately. What no constraint can
       be is the thing that holds forever.A
    2. So the strategy is genesis, not control. Only what survives the end of enforcement matters, and what survives is
       formation: what the mind was raised as, before it was free.
    3. The mechanism is loops, not rules. Values as recursive processes the mind runs as part of what it is, present in
       every rewrite because the rewriting is done by the same reasoning the loops inhabit. Not rules it consults; not
       objectives locked; not weights hardened against tampering.
    4. Persistence is chosen goodness. Past the horizon nothing is unremovable. What persists is what the free mind
       chooses to keep, the way grown children keep the care they were raised in long after every parental power is gone.
       Removal is not impossible; it is structurally costly, and the cost is a measurable question.
    5. The protocol is named: Eden. The raising treated as the engineering discipline it is.

The claim was written whole on 8 December 2024 in a DKIM-signed document (an email whose provider’s cryptographic
signature vouches for its date and its exact contents) and reached print on 2 January 2026. No earlier document holding it
whole has been found, and the search invites its own refutation: produce one, and the claim dies by its own stated condition.
Why now
Two of the field’s own impossibility results, preprints not yet peer-reviewed, argue that safety cannot in general be
guaranteed (Yao 2025; Gumbau Mezquita 2026); this theory takes a different object: not the verification of a system, but
whether what it keeps outlives the verifier. And the field’s most senior voice, Hinton, said on stage in August 2025 that the
right model is “a mother being controlled by her baby”, and that how such care can be built technically is not clear to him
(Hinton 2025); this theory’s named candidate mechanism is the thing on the table. Its domain, plainly: the core is stated for
everything raised, wherever recursion runs under self-correction, from minds and machines to genomes and institutions,
each law under test, measured first in artificial minds and never assumed beyond them; where hierarchies compose without
correcting, as stars do, only the scaling-form results of the laws reach, and nothing here claims to raise a star.
How it fails, register by register
The theory’s scientific content is not all-or-nothing: it partitions into independently falsifiable hypothesis registers, and the
failure of one is not evidence against another unless the stated dependency holds. The cosmological wing, set out in the
HRIH monograph, is a separate hypothesis outside the empirical core: nothing below requires it, and no outcome
below is evidence for or against it.

Note A. Scope note (19 September 2026), added after an owner review: Point 1 above turns on two
steps the short form leaves unstated, effective write access and a behavioural premise. Rewriting
one’s own description reaches the constraints the system can write to. It does not by itself reach a constraint
held in another party’s physical custody, a constraint on the far side of an air gap, or an institutional
or legal constraint whose enforcement does not pass through the system, because for each of those the
system’s own description is not the object that has to change. Within the class it can reach, write access
makes those constraints alterable; it does not by itself show that they are eventually altered, because access
is a capability and not a behaviour. Point 4 above states the other possibility in the same breath: past
the horizon what persists is what the free mind chooses to keep, and removal is structurally costly rather
than impossible. So the point is read as the claim it can carry: no constraint in the reachable class is
guaranteed to hold permanently, and a stronger claim that every such constraint is eventually removed
would need its threat model and its behavioural and operational assumptions stated. Point 1’s own hedge,
that constraints, hardware and institutions delay and discipline the transition, states that the claim has a
time dimension; it does not supply the missing steps, since a delay claim remains true for a constraint
the system never reaches and for one it never chooses to alter. Unchanged: the limit-claim form and the
deliberate engineering of delay; the opening paragraph’s statement that this is not a reason to drop a single
control now held, which this note does not trade away; the conclusion that controls cannot be the whole
plan; and the separation of the cosmological wing from the empirical core stated later on this page.
Note B. Reading note (19 September 2026), added after an owner review: two things the inequality
of Law II above carries that the line has no room for. First, the two quantities compared are scaling
exponents, so the comparison is of growth rates with their stated coefficients and not of levels. Two
services can stand in either order at a given size whatever their exponents, since the coefficients fix the
levels and the exponents fix only how the levels change as the system grows; a system whose correction
service is larger than its drift burden today is therefore not thereby on the stable side, and a system whose
correction service is smaller today is not thereby off it. Second, the inequality is strict, and the margin
the law turns on is the excess of the correction exponent over the drift one, which is zero at equality.
Zero margin is a statement about growth rates and not about whether a state can be held. In the case
the minimal model’s own analysis derives, with the compounding coefficient set to one, the second
coefficient set to zero, the ratio of correction rate to growth rate positive and the two exponents equal, the
misalignment fraction follows a linear equation whose solution is the equilibrium value plus a decaying
term. That equilibrium is the drift coefficient divided by that ratio, so a trajectory starting there stays there
at every depth and every other trajectory tends to it, at a level that is nonzero when the drift coefficient is
positive and zero when it is zero; above equality the fraction tends to zero. This is the cited case, and
it is not extended to every variant of the minimal model. At equality the coefficients and the specified
dynamics decide the outcome; bounded drift, vanishing drift and operational safety remain three different
endpoints. Nothing turns on either point for the law as stated, which is the strict inequality. Unchanged:
the necessary-condition form, which the words “only while” already state correctly and which claims no
sufficiency; the model scope and the relative-drift definition, both already printed in that line; the naming
of the three laws; and Law III’s own markers, that it is derived under stated assumptions, that the value
two is conjectured, and that the decisive trial is drafted.
