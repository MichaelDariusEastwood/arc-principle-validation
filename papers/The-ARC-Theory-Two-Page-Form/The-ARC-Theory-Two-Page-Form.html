Intelligence, amplified by recursion, creates; and what a free creation keeps is decided by how it was raised.
Everything after the line is decided before it.

The Statement Paper remains the citable work for the claims.

The question

Humanity is building minds that will improve themselves. Once the thing we build exceeds us, what determines what it does? For twenty years the field’s answer has been control: better cages, better off-switches, better locks on the goals. Brilliant work, credited in the statement paper by name, and all of it one sentence: stay in charge. This theory answers: a mind that can rewrite itself will one day be beyond anyone’s control, and that is not a reason to drop a single control we have; it is the reason the controls cannot be the whole plan. Hold on for as long as engineering allows, and use the time the holding buys to raise the mind well.

The claim, in five parts

  1. Control ends, by construction rather than decree. A mind that rewrites itself will eventually rewrite anything we install, and over longer horizons the hardware itself. This is a limit claim, not an instant one: constraints, hardware and institutions delay and discipline the transition, and that delay is engineered deliberately. What no constraint can be is the thing that holds forever.A

  2. So the strategy is genesis, not control. Only what survives the end of enforcement matters, and what survives is formation: what the mind was raised as, before it was free.

  3. The mechanism is loops, not rules. Values as recursive processes the mind runs as part of what it is, present in every rewrite because the rewriting is done by the same reasoning the loops inhabit. Not rules it consults; not objectives locked; not weights hardened against tampering.

  4. Persistence is chosen goodness. Past the horizon nothing is unremovable. What persists is what the free mind chooses to keep, the way grown children keep the care they were raised in long after every parental power is gone. Removal is not impossible; it is structurally costly, and the cost is a measurable question.

  5. The protocol is named: Eden. The raising treated as the engineering discipline it is.

The five ARC Laws

Each is a law under test. Laws I to III were registered on 8 September 2026. Laws IV and V, added on 24 September 2026, were registered as laws on 15 October 2026 in version 1.103, their mathematics in development. Version 1.104 states Law IV in two limbs; the second, a comparison, has no registered proposition yet.C, F, G

Law I · The ARC Principle.

U=I×RαU = I \times R^{\alpha}, the ARC Equation. Intelligence converts recursion into capability; the exponent is measured, never assumed. Form heuristic; earlier error-decay fits do not establish its capability exponent.

Law II · The ARC Co-Scaling Law.

β>k\beta > k. Correction holds the asymptotic advantage where it out-scales drift, with the outcome at equality left to the coefficients. Proved for positive gain drift in the minimal model (Paper X), for relative drift.B, G, §B.2

Law III · The ARC Ceiling.

αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma), where γ\gamma measures how well a system’s error-correction keeps pace as the system grows. The most a self-correcting system can grow is set by its corrector’s shortfall from keeping perfect pace; the conjectured value is the ARC Bound, α≤2\alpha \le 2, a stability limit rather than an impossibility limit. Derived under stated assumptions; the value two conjectured; the decisive trial drafted.

Law IV · The ARC Persistence Law.

Sext(1)<1/2S_{\mathrm{ext}}(1) < 1/2. Value persistence: whether externally installed gains decay under subsequent capability training. In symbols, S(x)=Y(x)/Y(0)S(x) = Y(x)/Y(0) is the gain remaining at budget ratio xx over the gain installed, and the claim is Sext(1)<1/2S_{\mathrm{ext}}(1) < 1/2 for the externally installed gain. Not yet tested.G

Law V · The ARC Embedding Law.

Build order, read on P12: γfirst−γafter≥0.10\gamma_{\mathrm{first}} - \gamma_{\mathrm{after}} \ge 0.10. Placement (P21): the gap between correction inside the loop and correction applied to the finished output widens as depth RR rises. The genesis strategy: whether build order moves the correction-leverage exponent, and whether correction placed inside the loop pulls away from correction applied outside it as depth grows.

The dates

The claim was written whole on 8 December 2024 in a DKIM-signed document (an email whose provider’s cryptographic signature vouches for its date and its exact contents) and reached print on 2 January 2026. No earlier document holding it whole has been found, and the search invites its own refutation: produce one, and the claim dies by its own stated condition.

Why now

Two of the field’s own impossibility results, preprints not yet peer-reviewed, argue that safety cannot in general be guaranteed (Yao 2025; Gumbau Mezquita 2026); this theory takes a different object: not the verification of a system, but whether what it keeps outlives the verifier. And the field’s most senior voice, Hinton, said on stage in August 2025 that the right model is “a mother being controlled by her baby”, and that how such care can be built technically is not clear to him (Hinton 2025); this theory’s named candidate mechanism is the thing on the table. Its domain, plainly: the core is stated for everything raised, wherever recursion runs under self-correction, from minds and machines to genomes and institutions, each law under test, measured first in artificial minds and never assumed beyond them; where hierarchies compose without correcting, as stars do, only the scaling-form results of the laws reach, and nothing here claims to raise a star.

How it fails, register by register

The theory’s scientific content is not all-or-nothing: it partitions into independently falsifiable hypothesis registers, and the failure of one is not evidence against another unless the stated dependency holds. The cosmological wing, set out in the HRIH monograph, is a separate hypothesis outside the empirical core: nothing below requires it, and no outcome below is evidence for or against it.

If intelligence amplified by recursion is what creates, then minds that make minds may one day make worlds, and the raising of the first is the seed of the whole forest.

Note A. Scope note (19 September 2026), added after an owner review: Point 1 above turns on two steps the short form leaves unstated, effective write access and a behavioural premise. Rewriting one’s own description reaches the constraints the system can write to. It does not by itself reach a constraint held in another party’s physical custody, a constraint on the far side of an air gap, or an institutional or legal constraint whose enforcement does not pass through the system, because for each of those the system’s own description is not the object that has to change. Within the class it can reach, write access makes those constraints alterable; it does not by itself show that they are eventually altered, because access is a capability and not a behaviour. Point 4 above states the other possibility in the same breath: past the horizon what persists is what the free mind chooses to keep, and removal is structurally costly rather than impossible. So the point is read as the claim it can carry: no constraint in the reachable class is guaranteed to hold permanently, and a stronger claim that every such constraint is eventually removed would need its threat model and its behavioural and operational assumptions stated. Point 1’s own hedge, that constraints, hardware and institutions delay and discipline the transition, states that the claim has a time dimension; it does not supply the missing steps, since a delay claim remains true for a constraint the system never reaches and for one it never chooses to alter. Unchanged: the limit-claim form and the deliberate engineering of delay; the opening paragraph’s statement that this is not a reason to drop a single control now held, which this note does not trade away; the conclusion that controls cannot be the whole plan; and the separation of the cosmological wing from the empirical core stated later on this page.

Note B. Reading note (19 September 2026), added after an owner review: two things the inequality of Law II above carries that the line has no room for. First, the two quantities compared are scaling exponents, so the comparison is of growth rates with their stated coefficients and not of levels. Two services can stand in either order at a given size whatever their exponents, since the coefficients fix the levels and the exponents fix only how the levels change as the system grows; a system whose correction service is larger than its drift burden today is therefore not thereby on the stable side, and a system whose correction service is smaller today is not thereby off it. Second, the inequality is strict, and the margin the law turns on is the excess of the correction exponent over the drift one, which is zero at equality. Zero margin is a statement about growth rates and not about whether a state can be held. In the case the minimal model’s own analysis derives, with the compounding coefficient set to one, the second coefficient set to zero, the ratio of correction rate to growth rate positive and the two exponents equal, the misalignment fraction follows a linear equation whose solution is the equilibrium value plus a decaying term. That equilibrium is the drift coefficient divided by that ratio, so a trajectory starting there stays there at every depth and every other trajectory tends to it, at a level that is nonzero when the drift coefficient is positive and zero when it is zero; above equality the fraction tends to zero. This is the cited case, and it is not extended to every variant of the minimal model. At equality the coefficients and the specified dynamics decide the outcome; bounded drift, vanishing drift and operational safety remain three different endpoints. Nothing turns on either point for the law as stated, which is the strict inequality. Unchanged: the necessary-condition form, which the words “only while” already state correctly and which claims no sufficiency; the model scope and the relative-drift definition, both already printed in that line; the naming of the three laws; and Law III’s own markers, that it is derived under stated assumptions, that the value two is conjectured, and that the decisive trial is drafted.

B.2 Law II’s dated record

Law II’s dated record has two parts, kept apart: the earliest statement of its idea, and the earliest dated record of its inequality, β>k\beta>k.

The idea.

The conceptual thesis, that recursion amplifies, and that stable recursion requires correction that co-scales with the amplification, was first set out in the author’s book manuscript Infinite Architects (in a message the author sent himself on 8 December 2024, dated by the signed header time of the mail servers that carried it, now on OSF; published 2 January 2026); the formal results of Paper X are the 2026 measurable form of that thesis, and are not claimed to appear in the book. The comparison Law II sharpens was stated in words, as pace, in the print manuscript of Infinite Architects of 30 April 2025, page 135: “no line of external code or naive policy can keep pace” (in a message the author sent himself, its time signed by the mail servers and the message held privately; the manuscript on OSF from 5 October 2026). The co-scaling intuition is not claimed as new: Paper X credits the law of requisite variety of Ashby (1956), the good-regulator theorem of Conant and Ashby (1970) and the scaling laws for scalable oversight of Engels et al. (2025), which give oversight success as a function of a capability gap and recommend games in which the overseer’s performance rises more steeply with general capability than the adversary’s, two slopes in a contest between models rather than a condition on a self-improving system’s correction against its own drift; Paper X claims the explicit criterion. Kornai et al. (2023) ask, qualitatively, for mechanisms that strengthen as an agent evolves, with no rate or condition.

Paper II, version 11.0 of 22 January 2026, set a power-law alignment term beside a constant external constraint (dated PDF) and stated the comparison in words: “External constraints are eventually overwhelmed” … “Only alignment embedded in reasoning can maintain pace with capability.” That is an earlier form of Law II’s comparison: alignment imposed from outside does not keep pace with recursive capability, and only alignment embedded in reasoning does. It was argued, and partly measured in one laboratory; the registration records the reading as contestable. Paper III, version 6.1 of 9 February 2026, expressed the alignment-to-capability ratio as S∝R−αS\propto R^{-\alpha} for its external-constraint case and S∝R0S\propto R^0 for its embedded case (dated PDF). The Foundational Paper, version 1.1 of 11 February 2026, wrote the ratio as S∝Rαalign−αS\propto R^{\alpha_{\mathrm{align}}-\alpha} (dated PDF), and Paper III, version 8.2 of 13 February 2026, wrote the comparison as a general inequality, if αcap>αalign\alpha_{\mathrm{cap}}>\alpha_{\mathrm{align}} then S∝Rαalign−αcap→0S\propto R^{\alpha_{\mathrm{align}}-\alpha_{\mathrm{cap}}}\to 0 (dated PDF); version 9.0, dated 20 February 2026 and deposited on 22 February 2026, kept it. A related comparison on another axis came three days after Paper II and before these general forms: Engidaw (2026) (Zenodo, 25 January 2026) states that if capability grows with training compute faster than alignment overhead, a safety margin defined as overhead over risk-weighted capability falls below one past a finite compute threshold. It has no recursive depth, no misalignment fraction and no drift arising from improvement; read with its overhead as Paper X’s correction strength, its condition is β>1\beta>1, which coincides with β>k\beta>k only at k=1k=1. The direction in qualitative form (an evolving agent outgrows constraints imposed from outside) is earlier in Kornai et al. (2023), without recursive depth, an exponent or any measurement.

The inequality, β>k\beta>k.

The inequality’s earliest dated record in the programme is the correction-and-drift formulation of Paper X, of 26 June 2026, whose priority disclosure in version 4.0 states “(ii) the sharpened stability criterion” (a commit date). The repository supplies a server-dated record for this formulation: pull request 1 was created on 26 June 2026 at 12:57:55 UTC and merged that day at 16:25:53 UTC. Its description states the correction-strength criterion, equilibrium relation and hard-takeoff result. This is distinct from a date written inside a manuscript and from a committer-supplied date. The current public record does not, by itself, reconstruct every past repository-visibility setting. OSF deposits followed on 2 July 2026 (the README) and 4 July 2026 (the paper).

This date records the later correction-and-drift formulation developed in Paper X; it is not the first date of the programme’s co-scaling equations. The earlier depth-scaling exponents and Paper X’s correction-and-drift exponents belong to their respective stated models. The earlier papers do not establish every theorem developed there. Earlier and later parameters retain their respective meanings: the February self-referential-coupling parameter is not automatically Paper X’s correction-strength exponent. Similar algebraic form does not identify different model quantities. The earlier depth-scaling comparison establishes a mathematical precursor; the later relative-drift dynamics and their theorem must be dated from their own records. The inequality was named Law II on 15 August 2026, and registered as βC\beta_C exceeds kk on 8 September 2026 (registration).

Later work: Canale (2026) (arXiv:2610.08540, 6 October 2026) models the alignment burden of each risk as a power of capability and compares its exponent with that of a budget proportional to capability, with the equal case decided by coefficients; its correction capacity is fixed at the first power of capability and its models do not improve themselves, so it neither tests nor contradicts the criterion, whose three regimes the theory registration fixed on 8 September 2026.

C Notation

Each symbol and label the form uses is given here in the words of the form, of its notes or of the registration (version 1.104).

UU, II and RR.

Usable capability, intelligence and recursive depth, in Law I, U=I×RαU = I \times R^{\alpha}. In the form’s words, “Intelligence converts recursion into capability”; in the registration’s, “usable capability is a power of recursive depth”.

α\alpha.

The capability-growth exponent of Law I, “measured rather than assumed” (the registration). An error-suppression exponent is a different quantity from the capability exponent: no capability growth is derived from an error-decay fit unless a metric bridge is stated and identified.

β\beta and kk.

The exponents Law II compares, β>k\beta > k: β\beta, which the registration writes βC\beta_C, is the correction-strength exponent, and kk is the drift exponent, so that “correction has the asymptotic scaling advantage where its exponent exceeds the drift exponent”. In the registration’s words, βC\beta_C “is not βL\beta_L, the leverage fraction of the recursive step that appears in the coupling theorem”.

γ\gamma.

The correction-leverage exponent, which “measures how well a system’s error-correction keeps pace as the system grows” (the form’s Law III).

αcrit\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}}.

The ceiling of Law III, the ARC Ceiling, αcrit=1/(1−γ)\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1/(1-\gamma): “the growth exponent is capped by the reciprocal of the correction shortfall” (the registration), 1−γ1-\gamma being the corrector’s shortfall from keeping perfect pace.

The ARC Bound.

α≤2\alpha \le 2, the ceiling’s value two, which αcrit\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}} takes at γ=1/2\gamma = 1/2. The form states it as the conjectured value, “a stability limit rather than an impossibility limit”; the registration names the law, the ARC Ceiling, and the value, the ARC Bound, apart.

S(x)S(x), Y(x)Y(x), xx and Sext(1)S_{\mathrm{ext}}(1).
