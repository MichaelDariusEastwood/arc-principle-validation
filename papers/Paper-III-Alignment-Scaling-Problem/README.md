# The alignment scaling problem: do external AI safety approaches keep pace with recursive capability?

**Version:** 1.19
**Revised:** 1 November 2026
**First published:** 2026-02-09
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 1.19):

> Does alignment applied from outside an AI system’s reasoning keep pace with the capability that reasoning produces? If capability compounds with recursive depth while safeguards outside the loop (human-feedback training, constitutional rules, output filters, monitoring) do not, the safety ratio falls with depth whatever the starting margin. We define the alignment scaling exponent $\alpha_{\text{align}}=\partial\log A/\partial\log R$, show that the safety ratio $S\propto R^{\alpha_{\text{align}}-\alpha_{\text{cap}}}$ tends to zero whenever $\alpha_{\text{align}}<\alpha_{\text{cap}}$, and predict $\alpha_{\text{align}}\approx0$ for external approaches and $\alpha_{\text{align}}\approx\alpha_{\text{cap}}$ for values embedded in the loop; the first, P19 of the theory registration, carries the author’s expectation of a value above zero but short of keeping pace. A framework derives $U=I\times g(R)$ with $\alpha=1/(1-\beta)$ from a Bernoulli equation, ties power-law, exponential and saturating forms to how recursive gains compose, and states the ARC Bound, $\alpha\le2$, as a conjectured safety boundary, argued for attention-based recursion; Paper X later supersedes that framing, without retracting the bound, with the criterion of Law II, a law under test. Across four domains, the biological, quantum error-correction and time-crystal forms do not alone test the framework. No time-crystal growth exponent has been measured. A first test, one unregistered run of six frontier models, gives depth contrasts in three tiers, positive, flat and negative; an internal code review in October 2026 found defects in exactly the flat and negative runs, so the tiers stand as a single-run result. An early error-decay estimate is retracted; the surviving fit, about 0.49 from one model, is inconclusive and does not identify capability growth. Thirteen criteria state what would refute the framework.
