# Do alignment gains level off as language models reason longer?

**Version:** 1.10
**Revised:** 1 November 2026
**First published:** 16 March 2026
**Author:** Michael Darius Eastwood

## Summary

From the abstract (version 1.10):

> Does giving a language model more reasoning time improve the quality of its ethical answers, and when do those gains level off? The question matters to anyone allocating compute to an assistant whose answers must remain both useful and safe. We examine the recorded ARC-Align results, without running a new analysis. The earlier four-model experiment reported diminishing gains and saturation fits; the six-model run of 11 and 12 March 2026 reported rising scores for three models, no statistically supported direction for two, and falling scores for one. These are descriptions of a model-judged rubric, not identified alignment mechanisms. A flat profile does not establish a plateau, and a rising profile does not exclude one beyond the measured range. The later protocol changed prompts, depth controls, models and scoring together. Its deepest paraphrases were disproportionately shortened in three runs; judges overlapped with launderers, and one judge appeared twice. Those defects prevent a causal attribution to blinding or a clean comparison of response shapes. We retain the reported fits, pillar and category results, suppression controls and two-model Eden Protocol pilot, while withdrawing unsupported token-to-time recommendations, cross-domain exponent ratios and the claim that the pilot removed a ceiling. The hypothesis preceded the March run; that run was not registered on OSF before it ran. Saturation remains a testable hypothesis requiring a resolved depth axis, faithful scoring, uncertainty about shape and a usefulness measure alongside safety. This paper informs that measurement task and decides none of the ARC Theory’s laws.
