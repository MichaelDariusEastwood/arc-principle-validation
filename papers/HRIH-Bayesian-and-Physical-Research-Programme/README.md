# The Hyperspace Recursive Intelligence Hypothesis: can Bayesian model comparison distinguish real cosmogenesis?

**Version:** 4.2

**Version date:** 2 November 2026

Other fields; working paper. This conditional cosmological extension does not test or establish any of the ARC Theory's five laws. Physical feasibility, observer measures and external specialist review remain open.

## Abstract

How could a physical hypothesis of intelligent cosmogenesis become distinguishable from its rivals? This paper separates the Hyperspace Recursive Intelligence Hypothesis into real daughter-universe creation, multi-generation lineages and a stronger closed-cycle submodel. It combines channel-specific physics, Bayesian model comparison, observer weighting and branching processes. The observer-count limit is a conditional mixture identity, not a universal probability ceiling; a Poisson lineage has positive survival probability only under its stated stationarity and independence assumptions above unit mean offspring. The proposed discriminator is maker-selection beyond habitability, tested against life-only, natural-reproductive and technology-only alternatives with independently frozen factors. Initial order remains a candidate observation, but set containment does not determine its likelihood ratio. Five study specifications and seven failure conditions define what could reject each claim. No cosmological posterior, demonstrated creation channel or empirical validation is reported. The programme’s AI-alignment work and laws under test are separate: they neither require this extension nor establish it. Physical feasibility, observer measures and external specialist review remain open.
