Abstract

How could a physical hypothesis of intelligent cosmogenesis become distinguishable from its rivals? This paper separates the Hyperspace Recursive Intelligence Hypothesis into real daughter-universe creation, multi-generation lineages and a stronger closed-cycle submodel. It combines channel-specific physics, Bayesian model comparison, observer weighting and branching processes. The observer-count limit is a conditional mixture identity, not a universal probability ceiling; a Poisson lineage has positive survival probability only under its stated stationarity and independence assumptions above unit mean offspring. The proposed discriminator is maker-selection beyond habitability, tested against life-only, natural-reproductive and technology-only alternatives with independently frozen factors. Initial order remains a candidate observation, but set containment does not determine its likelihood ratio. Five study specifications and seven failure conditions define what could reject each claim. No cosmological posterior, demonstrated creation channel or empirical validation is reported. The programme’s AI-alignment work and laws under test are separate: they neither require this extension nor establish it. Physical feasibility, observer measures and external specialist review remain open.

1 Introduction

The programme begins with a practical question: if control of advanced AI is lost against our will, what could preserve useful and aligned behaviour? Human control should be retained for as long as possible. This paper addresses a separate, conditional extension: whether an intelligence could instantiate a physically real daughter cosmology, and what evidence could distinguish that account from its rivals. A cosmologist assessing a candidate transition channel, or a statistician comparing selection models, can use the definitions and failure conditions below without accepting the hypothesis. No ARC law or result establishes the extension.

The paper connects cosmology and quantum gravity, Bayesian model comparison, observer selection, branching processes and thermodynamics. The long-form HRIH edition preserves the broader hypothesis and its dated record; this companion isolates the physical and statistical questions. The introduction states the three nested propositions; Sections 6 and 8 specify the channel and selection requirements, and Section B gives the proposed studies. Their outcomes have not been obtained here.

1.1 Contributions

The contribution is a reviewable research architecture: nested real-cosmogenesis, lineage and closed-cycle models; channel-specific feasibility conditions; a conditional branching threshold; maker-selection beyond habitability; explicit observer weighting; and comparison with natural and simulation families. It supplies no empirical posterior. Section 2 identifies earlier work on these components; priority for their precise conjunction remains unresolved pending the systematic review listed in Section 9.

1.2 The origin question that remains after early-universe cosmology

Modern cosmology describes the evolution of an early hot, dense universe with extraordinary success. Inflationary and quantum-cosmological proposals address important features of the early state and, in some models, remove an ordinary temporal boundary. They do not all answer the same question. A model may describe how a universe can emerge without specifying why this set of laws, constants and boundary data is realised. HRIH targets that selection question.

The scientifically meaningful form of the question is not simply “why is there something rather than nothing?” No current physical theory eliminates all primitives. The tractable question is:

Why do the laws and initial conditions of our universe permit a history containing observers, technological intelligence and, perhaps, access to cosmogenic processes; and is the joint permission of those outcomes better predicted by deliberate maker-selection than by non-deliberate cosmology or observer selection alone?

1.3 The nested hypothesis

HRIH is not one indivisible proposition. It is a hierarchy (Figure 1):

  1. Real intelligent cosmogenesis. At least one physically admissible process allows an advanced agent in a parent history to instantiate a physically real daughter cosmology.

  2. Recursive lineage. Real intelligent cosmogenesis occurs in more than one generation and can be represented as a branching lineage.

  3. Closed-cycle HRIH. The complete cosmogenic history satisfies a global fixed-point or periodic-boundary condition, so the Big Bang is an internal temporal boundary of a self-consistent whole rather than an unexplained first event in an external linear chain.

Evidence for R1 does not entail R2; evidence for R2 does not entail R3. A confirmed baby-universe channel would therefore be scientifically important without proving the closed-loop ontology.

1.4 What “hyperspace” means in this paper

HRIH is retained as the historical name of the hypothesis. In scientific propositions, hyperspace does not assert extra dimensions, a particular metric, a superluminal channel or a known quantum-gravitational mechanism. It denotes an as-yet-unspecified cosmogenic channel. A physical submodel becomes scoreable only when it supplies a state space, dynamics or action, boundary conditions, parameter domain and an observational map. Until then, the neutral term unknown cosmogenic channel is used.

1.5 The strongest claim and its honest limit

The strongest HRIH claim is that a globally self-consistent cosmogenic history could explain why the Big Bang need not be an uncaused first event in a linear sequence. This is a genuine explanatory structure. It does not answer why the space of possible laws, the global consistency rule or the admissible solution exists at all. The paper therefore claims a candidate closure of a causal regress, not an explanation without primitives.

Withdrawal of an invalid inference. The existence of intelligence in our universe does not imply a prior creator, an infinite lineage or a closed loop. Recursion alone does not remove the need for a base case, boundary condition, fixed point or global consistency rule. Closed-cycle HRIH is a postulate to be tested for existence, stability, physical admissibility and predictive value.

2 Related work

2.1 Intelligent and reproductive cosmology

HRIH belongs to a small but real lineage of cosmological proposals involving universe reproduction or intelligent participation. Smolin (Smolin 1992) proposed cosmological natural selection through black holes. Crane’s meduso-anthropic principle (Crane 1994), Harrison’s intelligent-life selection proposal (Harrison 1995), Gardner’s Biocosm (Gardner 2003), and Vidal’s inquiry into whether natural or artificial selection could hold on a cosmic scale (Vidal 2014) discuss intelligence and universe creation in different forms. These are antecedents, not embarrassments. HRIH’s defensible novelty must be stated as a precise remainder after they are credited.

2.2 Quantum cosmology and no-boundary proposals

Hartle-Hawking no-boundary cosmology (Hartle and Hawking 1983), Vilenkin’s tunnelling proposal (Vilenkin 1982), and past-eternal or null-boundary models such as Aguirre-Gratton (Aguirre and Gratton 2003) show that the absence of an ordinary initial temporal boundary is not unique to HRIH, while the positive averaged-expansion condition in the theorem of Borde, Guth and Vilenkin implies past geodesic incompleteness (Borde, Guth, and Vilenkin 2003). HRIH’s distinctive ambition is not merely to remove a boundary mathematically, but to model selected initial conditions through intelligent cosmogenesis and, in R3, global closure.

2.3 Anthropic reasoning and observer measures

Carter (Carter 1974), Weinberg (Weinberg 1987) and later multiverse work developed observer-selection arguments; Srednicki and Hartle (Srednicki and Hartle 2010) made explicit that a theory in a large universe requires a xerographic distribution. HRIH therefore cannot treat “we observe a life-permitting universe” as direct evidence of deliberate creation. Its maker-selection claim must predict something beyond observer permission.

2.4 Simulation

Bostrom’s argument (Bostrom 2003), Chalmers’s philosophy of virtual reality (Chalmers 2022), Kipping’s Bayesian model (Kipping 2020) and physical critiques (Vazza 2025) provide a mature comparison literature. HRIH should use that literature as a methodological benchmark, not as an opponent to defeat rhetorically.

The six-part contribution is stated once in Section 1.1. The comparisons above identify adjacent work, not a completed priority search.

3 Transition, lineage and closure, stated as mathematics

3.1 Universe histories and cosmogenic transitions

Let XgX_g denote the physically relevant state of a universe history in generation gg. It can include laws and constants θg\theta_g, initial quantum state ψg\psi_g, low-energy effective fields, and any inherited data admitted by a particular physical channel. Let AgA_g denote properties of a cosmogenic agent or civilisation arising in XgX_g. A candidate transition kernel is Xg+1∼𝒯χ(⋅∣Xg,Ag,ϕg),\begin{equation*} X_{g+1}\sim \mathcal{T}_{\chi}\!\left(\,\cdot\mid X_g,A_g,\phi_g\right), \end{equation*}(1) where χ\chi identifies the physical channel and ϕg\phi_g its parameters. Equation (1) is not evidence that such a transition exists. It is the minimum mathematical object needed to state the hypothesis without converting a metaphor into a conclusion.

The transition decomposes into at least six contingent events:

  1. intelligence arises;

  2. it reaches relevant cosmogenic capability;

  3. the proposed physical channel is real and accessible;

  4. the agent chooses real cosmogenesis rather than alternatives;

  5. the resulting daughter is viable;

  6. the creator system remains sufficiently stable to complete the act.

Each event requires an explicit probability or physical model. None may be assigned probability one by definition.

3.2 A closed-cycle submodel

A closed-cycle history can be represented as a fixed point X⋆=𝒞(X⋆;ϕ),\begin{equation*} X^{\star}=\mathcal{C}\!\left(X^{\star};\phi\right), \end{equation*}(2) or as a periodic orbit Xg+L=Xg\begin{equation*} X_{g+L}=X_g \end{equation*}(3) for some L≥1L\geq1, with generation g+Lg+L identified with generation gg, so that the history closes on itself rather than repeating without end. Here 𝒞\mathcal{C} includes both ordinary physical evolution and the cosmogenic transition. This formulation does not require a signal travelling backwards through local laboratory time. It treats the complete history as a boundary-value solution whose regions are jointly constrained.

For Eq. (2) or (3) to become physics, a submodel must establish:

  1. existence: at least one admissible solution;

  2. measure: non-zero amplitude or probability under the theory;

  3. stability: robustness to perturbations;

  4. causal consistency: no contradiction with the channel’s causal structure;

  5. observational content: a likelihood distinguishable from rival cosmologies.

Self-consistency inside an assumed loop proves none of these by itself.

3.3 Occurrence is not constructibility

The occurrence of our universe establishes that a universe history exists. It does not establish that agents inside a universe can reproduce the relevant boundary conditions. Natural events frequently depend on regimes inaccessible to internal engineering. The constructibility hypothesis is therefore channel-specific: Hconstruct(χ):P(successful daughter cosmology∣A,χ)>0.\begin{equation*} H_{\mathrm{construct}}(\chi):\quad P(\text{successful daughter cosmology}\mid A,\chi)>0. \end{equation*}(4) A single counterexample to one channel does not exclude all channels; conversely, merely naming an unknown channel is not a rescue. Each scored channel must carry an explicit physical model and a complexity penalty (Simpson, Rue, Riebler, Martins, and Sø rbye 2017).

3.4 Ability is not preference

Even if real cosmogenesis is feasible, a capable agent may prefer simulation, virtual worlds, observation, reversible experiments, or no creation at all. Let pW=P(agent chooses physically real cosmogenesis∣capable, informed).\begin{equation*} p_W=P(\text{agent chooses physically real cosmogenesis}\mid\text{capable, informed}). \end{equation*}(5) HRIH does not set pW=1p_W=1. Preference is a behavioural and normative uncertainty that must be modelled, bounded or measured. This term sharply distinguishes real intelligent cosmogenesis from a simulation argument.

4 Bayesian model space and conditional observer counting

4.1 Top-level families

For a specified inference, first name the focal experienced world and the level of explanation. The following families distinguish its immediate implementation or origin: HN:directly physical history, not deliberately seeded,\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} H_{\mathrm{N}}&: \text{directly physical history, not deliberately seeded},\end{aligned} \end{equation*}(6) HS:experienced world implemented by external computation,\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} H_{\mathrm{S}}&: \text{experienced world implemented by external computation},\end{aligned} \end{equation*}(7) HR:directly physical daughter, deliberately instantiated.\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} H_{\mathrm{R}}&: \text{directly physical daughter, deliberately instantiated}. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}(8) These are categories for the chosen focal level, not an exhaustive ontology. Simulation and real cosmogenesis can coexist: a simulation may run inside a deliberately created physical universe. Such ancestry belongs in an explicit hybrid submodel. If the focal definitions do not make alternatives disjoint, compare joint submodels indexed by implementation and origin rather than adding overlapping family probabilities. The sums below require mutually exclusive model assignments, normalised weights and a declared treatment of omitted possibilities.

Each family must contain explicit submodels. HNH_{\mathrm{N}} may include a single-history model, inflationary or quantum-cosmological boundary proposals, multiverse selection (Bousso and Polchinski 2000; Garriga and Vilenkin 2008), and natural cosmological reproduction (Smolin 1992). HSH_{\mathrm{S}} must state the simulated reference class, computational substrate and fidelity. HRH_{\mathrm{R}} includes one-generation cosmogenesis, recursive lineages and the closed-cycle submodel.

An unspecified “unknown mechanism” has no scoreable likelihood. Flexibility is handled through declared, proper priors and normalised likelihoods, not by an automatic penalty assigned in prose.

4.3 Comparison with the simulation argument

Bostrom’s simulation argument (Bostrom 2003) is a conditional trilemma about civilisation survival, willingness to run ancestor simulations and observer counts; it is not a direct measurement of whether we are simulated. Bayesian treatments such as Kipping’s (Kipping 2020) obtain near-balanced odds only inside specified partitions and priors. Physical analyses such as Vazza’s (Vazza 2025) impose resource constraints on simulations of this universe. HRIH is different: it requires a physical cosmogenesis channel and a maker-selection likelihood. A number from the simulation literature cannot be transferred to HRIH, and the two hypotheses cannot be ranked responsibly by counting how many independent authors have assigned them percentages.

The proper comparison is Eq. (9), with each family carrying its own physical constraints, reference class and complexity cost. At present, HRIH has no registered empirical posterior. This paper deliberately supplies a model, not a favourable number.

5 How observers are weighted can change the posterior

5.1 Why physical theory is not enough

In a large or branching cosmology, a physical theory can generate many observers or observer-moments compatible with some data. Prediction then requires a rule for locating “us” among them (Hartle and Srednicki 2007). Let 𝒪j\mathcal{O}_j be the observer set under HjH_j and ξj(o)\xi_j(o) a xerographic distribution (Srednicki and Hartle 2010). Then p(D∣Hj,ξj)=∑o∈𝒪jξj(o)p(D∣o,Hj).\begin{equation*} p(D\mid H_j,\xi_j)=\sum_{o\in\mathcal{O}_j}\xi_j(o)\,p(D\mid o,H_j). \end{equation*}(11) Different ξj\xi_j can materially change the posterior. This is not a technical nuisance. It is one of the central uncertainties in multiverse, simulation and recursive-lineage inference.

5.2 Reference classes to freeze

The model-space study must decide, in the protocol it registers, whether it weights:

  • observers or observer-moments;

  • civilisations or individuals;

  • biological, artificial or both kinds of intelligence;

  • root and descendant universes equally or by physical measure;

  • exact duplicates separately;

  • simulated and physically embodied observers in a shared or separate class.

No choice is neutral. HRIH should therefore report a multiverse of observer measures and identify which conclusions survive all reasonable variants.

5.3 Indexical evidence

Observing a life-permitting universe is conditioned by the existence of an observer. Its evidential strength still depends on the normalised rival likelihoods and the observer measure; selection alone does not establish that the evidence is weak or neutral. A proposed discriminator should identify what each rival predicts after applying the same declared observer condition. This motivates maker-selection in Section 8.

6 Physical feasibility is scored one channel at a time

6.1 Why a physical channel is indispensable

A cosmogenic hypothesis acquires scientific content only through a concrete channel. The fact that general relativity, semiclassical gravity or speculative quantum gravity contains unusual solutions does not establish that an internal agent can reach them. A channel is scoreable only if it specifies:

  1. the relevant fields and state variables;

  2. the action, Hamiltonian or transition rule;

  3. boundary and initial conditions;

  4. parameter ranges and approximation regime;

  5. resource requirements accessible to the agent;

  6. the mapping from parent controls to daughter observables;

  7. failure modes and a likelihood function.

6.2 False-vacuum bubble and tunnelling channels

Farhi and Guth (Farhi and Guth 1987) showed a classical obstruction to creating an inflating universe in the laboratory under their assumptions. Later work by Farhi, Guth and Guven (Farhi, Guth, and Guven 1990), and by Fischler, Morgan and Polchinski (Fischler, Morgan, and Polchinski 1990a; Fischler, Morgan, and Polchinski 1990b), examined tunnelling and Hamiltonian treatments of false-vacuum bubbles. These papers are serious antecedents, not confirmations of HRIH. They expose precisely the problems the hypothesis must confront: singularity theorems, topology change, pseudomanifold constructions, boundary conditions, negative-energy or mass issues, and the validity of semiclassical approximations.

For a semiclassical tunnelling submodel the minimum quantitative object is a rate of the form ΓV=Aexp⁡(−Bℏ),\begin{equation*} \frac{\Gamma}{V}=A\exp\!\left(-\frac{B}{\hbar}\right), \end{equation*}(12) where BB is an appropriate Euclidean action difference and AA a prefactor. HRIH-B, the proposed physical-channel study, must choose one channel, state AA, BB, boundary conditions and validity range, and permit the resulting rate to become negligibly small. If the calculation cannot be completed without an uncontrolled construction, that submodel remains unscored.

6.3 Black-hole and bounce channels

Cosmological natural selection (Smolin 1992) and meduso-anthropic proposals (Crane 1994) consider black holes as potential reproductive sites. These ideas motivate, but do not establish, parameter inheritance or agent control. A maker-selection model needs an explicit map θg+1∼Kχ(θg+1∣θg,ag)\begin{equation*} \theta_{g+1}\sim K_{\chi}(\theta_{g+1}\mid\theta_g,a_g) \end{equation*}(13) showing which parent variables can influence which daughter variables, with what noise. If no information or control can traverse the relevant boundary, maker-selection is physically inoperative even if a daughter region exists.

6.4 Chronology and global closure

The closed-cycle submodel is more demanding than a lineage. It may be formulated as a global boundary condition without local backwards signalling, but it still requires a physical theory admitting the complete solution. Closed timelike curve models and quantum consistency analyses (Deutsch 1991) provide mathematical precedents, while Hawking’s chronology-protection analysis (Hawking 1992) supplies a serious counterpressure through quantum backreaction. HRIH does not claim that chronology violation is established or required. The closed-cycle model must be separately penalised and separately killable.

6.5 Physical resource accounting

Simulation and cosmogenesis have different resource profiles. Vazza’s astrophysical constraints (Vazza 2025) apply to computing a sufficiently detailed simulation on a physical substrate; real cosmogenesis may instead attempt to seed a state that evolves under its own dynamics. That distinction can reduce one cost while introducing much harder gravitational constraints. HRIH must not use the resource cost of simulation as evidence for real cosmogenesis. It must calculate the cost and feasibility of the chosen real channel.

Useful constraints include entropy and information bounds, thermodynamic costs, available free energy, stability of the parent vacuum, concentration of energy, and causal access to the relevant region. Bekenstein, holographic and computational bounds (Bekenstein 1981; Susskind 1995; Lloyd 2002) are boundary conditions, not evidence that a universe can be engineered. 6.6 Duration-dependent costs and autonomous instantiation

Vazza’s Planck-resolution Earth calculation gives 2.55×10592.55\times10^{59} erg at the cosmic microwave background temperature. He compares this with roughly 5.6×10595.6\times10^{59} erg for the rest-mass energy of Palomar 2, with mass about 3.3×1053.3\times10^5 solar masses, and estimates a similar expenditure per simulation timestep; approximately 10610^6 such timesteps reach the Milky Way comparison (Vazza 2025). These are his model-specific resource estimates, not a bound on every possible simulation or every possible host physics.

A conditional cost model makes the distinction precise. Suppose a host pays c>0c>0 per simulated timestep of fixed duration Δt\Delta t, and a hypothetical autonomous daughter requires only a one-off parent cost EseedE_\mathrm{seed}. In a continuous interpolation, Esim(T)=E0+cT/Δt,∂Esim∂T=c/Δt>0,\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} E_\mathrm{sim}(T)&=E_0+cT/\Delta t, & \frac{\partial E_\mathrm{sim}}{\partial T}&=c/\Delta t>0, \end{aligned} \end{equation*} Einst(T)=Eseed,∂Einst∂T=0.\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} E_\mathrm{inst}(T)&=E_\mathrm{seed}, & \frac{\partial E_\mathrm{inst}}{\partial T}&=0. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}(14) The second line is an assumed parent-cost boundary, not a demonstrated cosmogenesis channel. It requires no continued support, control, maintenance or duration-dependent resource demand from the daughter. Relaxing any of those assumptions changes the model. The first line also assumes fixed fidelity, positive expenditure per step and unchanged accounting; it is not a technology-independent theorem. The daughter’s own energy and entropy accounting remains part of its physics.

Faizal and colleagues argue that limits on algorithmic completeness preclude a fully algorithmic account of reality and draw an anti-simulation conclusion (Faizal, Krauss, Shabir, and Marino 2025). That conclusion is their argument. Formal undecidability alone does not establish that a specified finite observation cannot be simulated. Nor does a constraint on computation establish or exclude an agent-accessible cosmogenesis channel. This paper therefore transfers neither their conclusion nor Vazza’s resource calculation into evidence for HRIH.

The comparison retains two distinct burdens: simulation must specify fidelity, substrate and computational resources; instantiation must specify an admissible and accessible gravitational channel, its one-off cost and any continuing costs. The singularity and semiclassical obstacles above remain. No ordering of the families’ probabilities follows from comparing an energy estimate with an unresolved channel.

7 Lineage survival in a conditional branching model

7.1 Definition

Let the expected number of viable daughter universes generated by one universe history be μR=pApCpUpWpVpKmR,\begin{equation*} \mu_R=p_Ap_Cp_Up_Wp_Vp_Km_R, \end{equation*}(15) where:

pAp_A

probability that relevant intelligence arises;

pCp_C

probability that it reaches cosmogenic capability;

pUp_U

probability that an accessible real-universe channel exists;

pWp_W

probability that capable agents choose to use it;

pVp_V

probability that a generated daughter is viable;

pKp_K

probability that the creator system remains sufficiently stable;

mRm_R

expected number of attempted daughters, conditional on successful parent preparation and before applying per-daughter viability.

Equation (15) is a factorised baseline, not a measurement or an identity for arbitrary joint distributions. Here mRm_R counts attempted daughters conditional on successful parent preparation; pVp_V is the per-daughter viability probability, so viability is counted once. Under conditional sequential probabilities, the parent-event product can instead be written by the chain rule; otherwise dependencies require an explicit joint expectation. Correlation between yield and viability cannot be discarded. No factor is fixed to one by definition.

7.2 Extinction and survival

Proposition 2 (Poisson lineage threshold). Start from one ancestor in a single-type Galton-Watson process. Assume independent offspring counts with one stationary Poisson distribution of finite mean μR\mu_R for every parent and generation. The extinction probability qq is the smallest solution in [0,1][0,1] of q=exp⁡[μR(q−1)].\begin{equation*} q=\exp\!\left[\mu_R(q-1)\right]. \end{equation*}(16) Thus μR≤1⇒q=1,\begin{equation*} \mu_R\leq1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q=1, \end{equation*}(17) and a lineage becomes extinct almost surely. If μR>1\mu_R>1, survival has positive probability 1−q1-q (Figure 3).

This standard branching-process result (Harris 1963) is derived for the stated Poisson model in Appendix A. It establishes no physical reproduction process. Shared environments, multiple types and changing offspring laws require a different analysis; a marginal average above one is not then a sufficient survival condition.

7.3 What the threshold contributes

The threshold converts a vague narrative of recursive creation into a set of measurable or constrainable terms. A theory can fail because the channel is physically impossible, because capable agents rarely choose it, because daughters are non-viable, because creator civilisations are unstable, or because the expected yield is too low. The model is valuable even if the posterior mass ends up concentrated below one.

7.4 Closed cycles are not required for branching

A recursive lineage may have a finite or unknown origin. The branching programme therefore survives rejection of the closed-cycle submodel. Conversely, μR>1\mu_R>1 does not supply a closed cycle: a closed cycle requires a compatible global boundary condition and a path through state space returning to the required conditions. These are distinct hypotheses and must never be reported as one confirmation.

8 Maker-selection beyond habitability is the principal discriminator

8.1 The discriminator

Let θ\theta denote physical laws, constants and boundary parameters. Let p0(θ)p_0(\theta) denote a baseline distribution supplied by a natural-cosmology submodel. Define: L(θ)=observer or life permission,\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} L(\theta)&=\text{observer or life permission},\end{aligned} \end{equation*}(18) C(θ)=accessibility of cosmogenic technology,\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} C(\theta)&=\text{accessibility of cosmogenic technology},\end{aligned} \end{equation*}(19) S(θ)=probability of stable technological ascent,\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} S(\theta)&=\text{probability of stable technological ascent},\end{aligned} \end{equation*}(20) K(θ)=expected viable daughter yield.\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} K(\theta)&=\text{expected viable daughter yield}. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}(21) A simplified life-conditioned natural model has p(θ∣HN,obs)∝p0(θ)L(θ),\begin{equation*} p(\theta\mid H_{\mathrm{N}},\mathrm{obs})\propto p_0(\theta)L(\theta), \end{equation*}(22) whereas a simplified HRIH maker-selection model predicts p(θ∣HR,obs)∝p0(θ)L(θ)C(θ)S(θ)K(θ).\begin{equation*} p(\theta\mid H_{\mathrm{R}},\mathrm{obs})\propto p_0(\theta)L(\theta)C(\theta)S(\theta)K(\theta). \end{equation*}(23) Both expressions require finite, positive normalising integrals over the same declared parameter measure. With M(θ)=C(θ)S(θ)K(θ)M(\theta)=C(\theta)S(\theta)K(\theta) and normalisers ZNZ_N and ZRZ_R, their pointwise density ratio on common support is M(θ)ZN/ZRM(\theta)Z_N/Z_R. Extra positive factors alone therefore imply no evidential advantage at the observed parameters.

The distinctive claim is not that our universe permits life. It is that the realised parameter region is unusually favourable to technologically accessible, stable and viable cosmogenesis beyond what life-only, black-hole-production and technology-only selection models predict.

8.2 Independence requirements

The functions CC, SS and KK must be frozen before inspecting the held-out target relationships. They may use training data to define physically meaningful proxies, but they cannot be reverse-engineered from the constants they are supposed to explain. A valid study should:

  1. predefine parameter ranges and rival models;

  2. select creator-enabling proxies using physical theory independent of the held-out outcomes;

  3. fit on a training subset of parameter relationships;

  4. predict held-out relationships or sensitivity directions;

  5. compare out-of-sample likelihood against life-only, black-hole-selection and technology-only models;

  6. report model and prior sensitivity.

8.3 Candidate creator-enabling factors

Potential factors include long-lived free-energy gradients; stable complex chemistry; compact objects and high-energy environments; metastable but sufficiently durable vacuum structure; laws permitting information processing and error correction; and physical access to a cosmogenic channel. These are candidates, not a confirmed list. Some are already selected by ordinary habitability, and others may be generic consequences of broad parameter ranges. HRIH earns evidence only from the residual predictive contribution after those overlaps are modelled.

8.4 Rival selection models

Maker-selection is adjacent to established and speculative predecessors:

  • anthropic and observer selection (Carter 1974; Weinberg 1987);

  • Smolin-style cosmological natural selection through black-hole production (Smolin 1992);

  • Crane, Harrison, Gardner and Vidal-style intelligence-related cosmogenesis (Crane 1994; Harrison 1995; Gardner 2003; Vidal 2014);

  • a technology-only selection model, defined here as a rival, in which laws favour increasingly capable observers.

The paper’s potentially distinctive remainder is the joint inclusion of creator-system stability and a quantitative comparison of maker-selection against both life-only and non-intelligent reproductive models. Priority for that precise remainder remains a literature-search question, not a premise of the physics.

8.5 Initial order as a proposed discriminator

Penrose’s estimate of the specialness of the initial condition, one part in 101012310^{10^{123}}, is conditional on his phase-space account (Penrose 2004). His earlier argument asks why an observer-producing fluctuation should account for the much larger observed entropy imbalance; his proposed initial Weyl-curvature condition is a distinct answer (Penrose 1979), developed later in conformal cyclic cosmology (Penrose 2010). This paper uses neither the estimate nor that objection as a measured probability for HRIH.

Maker-selection asks a further question: does producing a long-lived technological lineage require a distribution of initial order distinguishable from that required by the observer condition alone? It is plausible that sustained free-energy gradients, cumulative selection and stable information processing matter. Landauer’s principle bounds heat dissipation in logically irreversible erasure under its physical assumptions (Landauer 1961); it does not supply a lower bound on the initial entropy of an entire maker-producing universe, or a cost for every learning operation.

Let EE be a pre-specified measurable event or finite-resolution region for initial order, and define Λℛ(E)=P(E∣Hmak)P(E∣Hobs),P(E∣Hobs)>0.\begin{equation*} \Lambda_{\mathcal{R}}(E)=\frac{P(E\mid H_\mathrm{mak})}{P(E\mid H_\mathrm{obs})}, \qquad P(E\mid H_\mathrm{obs})>0. \end{equation*}(24) Every maker-containing history contains observers under the chosen definition. That set inclusion, even combined with a higher minimum order for makers, does not establish Λℛ(E)>1\Lambda_{\mathcal{R}}(E)>1 for the observed region. Two normalised distributions over histories and an observation model are needed. Appendix A gives a finite counterexample: containment can coexist with a ratio below, equal to or above one.

Defeaters retained for a future specified model.
E1

If a rival observer-selection model predicts the observed order as well as the maker model, the proposed discriminator supplies no advantage.

E2

If a maker can arise without more initial order than the comparator requires, the proposed ordering premise fails.

E3

If a dynamical initial-condition model predicts the observation, compare that model explicitly; its success may remove the proposed selection advantage. Inflation alone is not assumed to settle this question.

The likelihood ratio is presently undetermined against both fluctuation-only and inflationary observer-selection alternatives. The former edition’s strict inequality is withdrawn. The physical question and the three defeaters remain; no numerical posterior or favourable sign is inferred.

9 What this paper does and does not show

9.1 Evidence clusters

The Bayesian programme should divide evidence into clusters whose dependencies can be inspected:

  1. Physical feasibility: channel-specific theoretical and experimental constraints.

  2. Parameter distribution: fine-tuning and held-out maker-selection predictions.

  3. Astrophysical structure: black-hole production, vacuum stability and energy resources.

  4. Technological trajectory: evidence bearing on pCp_C, pWp_W and pKp_K.

  5. Indexical evidence: observer and reference-class information.

  6. Counterevidence: constraints that exclude or suppress candidate channels.

Evidence reused in more than one cluster must be explicitly linked so it does not update the model twice.

9.2 Current evidence status

At present:

  • no accepted physical channel has been shown to permit agent-controlled creation of a viable daughter universe;

  • no observation identifies a parent universe or deliberate parameter selection;

  • no registered maker-selection model has outpredicted life-only or natural reproductive rivals;

  • no observer measure is uniquely justified;

  • no lower-level ARC result supplies a likelihood ratio for HRIH’s cosmological ontology.

No HRIH-specific likelihood has been supplied for broad ARC observations. The defensible action is to make no cosmological update from them. A neutral factor can record that convention, BFR/Nbookkeeping(DARC):=1,\begin{equation*} \mathrm{BF}^\mathrm{bookkeeping}_{R/N}(D_\mathrm{ARC}) := 1, \end{equation*}(25) but it is not an estimated Bayes factor or a theorem that the unknown likelihoods are equal. A specified generative bridge, fixed before the relevant analysis, is needed to make that comparison empirical.

HRIH may be compatible with general relativity, quantum mechanics, evolution, information theory and diverse philosophical or religious traditions. Compatibility means that no immediate contradiction has been identified at the stated level. It does not increase the probability of HRIH. Support requires a new derivation, quantitative prediction or likelihood not already supplied by the established theory.

10 Five predictions, each a hypothesis with what would count against it

Each of P1 to P5 is a hypothesis to be decided by the studies of Section B, stated with what would count against it; none is a forecast of a study’s outcome, and this paper attaches no confidence to any, because no registered model yet exists to supply one.

10.1 Primary maker-selection prediction

P1: held-out maker-selection. A model using independently defined C(θ)C(\theta), S(θ)S(\theta) and K(θ)K(\theta) predicts held-out relations among physical parameters better than life-only anthropic selection, cosmological natural selection and technology-only rivals. Failure to outperform those models counts against HRIH; redefinition of the factors after failure is prohibited.

10.2 Physical-channel prediction

P2: at least one scoreable channel. At least one physically specified cosmogenesis channel retains non-negligible transition probability over an agent-accessible parameter region after singularity, topology, backreaction, resource and validity constraints are imposed. If every well-specified channel is excluded or negligibly suppressed across defensible ranges, real intelligent cosmogenesis is strongly disfavoured.

10.3 Branching prediction

P3: supercritical region. For the stationary independent Poisson lineage in Proposition 2, indefinite survival requires a non-negligible parameter region with μR>1\mu_R>1. If the posterior mass for μR>1\mu_R>1 becomes negligible under all defensible priors and dependencies, indefinite survival in those specified submodels is disfavoured even if isolated cosmogenesis remains possible.

10.4 Closed-cycle prediction

P4: admissible closure. The closed-cycle submodel requires at least one stable, non-zero-measure solution of Eq. (2) or (3) in a specified physical theory and at least one observational consequence not shared by the corresponding open lineage. Failure removes the closed-cycle explanation without removing R1 or R2.

10.5 Preference prediction

P5: real-creation choice cannot be assumed. HRIH’s real-cosmogenesis posterior must integrate over pWp_W. Evidence that capable agents overwhelmingly prefer simulation, abstention or non-cosmogenic goals lowers the real-lineage probability. The paper makes no claim that ability entails preference.

11 What would count against HRIH, and what would not

11.1 Scientific kills and model-specific kills

A scientific theory must be allowed to lose probability. The following outcomes have different scopes and must not be conflated:

  1. Channel exclusion. Mature physical analysis excludes every specified agent-accessible real-cosmogenesis channel, or drives its transition rate to negligible values across the declared parameter domain. This kills the scored real-cosmogenesis submodels, not every logically imaginable mechanism.

  2. Maker-selection failure. Independently frozen maker-selection factors fail to improve held-out prediction beyond life-only, natural reproductive and technology-selection rivals, with an equivalence region ruling out a scientifically meaningful advantage. This kills the distinctive maker-selection claim.

  3. Subcritical lineage. Within the stationary independent Poisson model, the posterior probability of μR>1\mu_R>1 becomes negligible under every defensible joint parameter model. This kills the corresponding indefinite-survival claim while leaving isolated creation, and lineages of finitely many generations, possible.

  4. Closure exclusion. The closed-cycle model has no admissible, stable, non-zero-measure solution, or has no observational distinction from an open lineage after complexity penalties. This kills R3.

  5. Observer-measure fragility. HRIH is favoured only under a narrow, outcome-selected xerographic distribution. This counts against a robust cosmological inference.

  6. Prior-volume fragility. Apparent support disappears under modest and defensible prior or model-space changes. This counts against a claimed posterior rather than being hidden in a supplement.

  7. Preference suppression. Empirical or theoretical evidence drives pWp_W close to zero. This can make real intelligent cosmogenesis rare even if the physical channel exists.

11.2 What is not a scientific kill

An earlier document containing the same idea would defeat a priority claim but would not falsify HRIH. Failure of an ARC experiment would alter only the bridge or stability factors it actually informs. Rejection of the cosmological closed loop would not refute a one-generation physical cosmogenesis channel. These distinctions are essential to prevent both overclaiming and artificial survival by moving between claim types.

11.3 No branch may turn every outcome into success

A fired registered kill condition kills the claim as stated. It may motivate a new hypothesis, but the new hypothesis must receive a new identifier, date and registration. A null result is not support; a confidence interval containing the null is not equivalence; and a statistically detectable but scientifically negligible effect does not establish the proposed mechanism.

12 Limitations

12.1 Physics is incomplete

Quantum gravity is unresolved. Semiclassical calculations may fail in the regimes most relevant to cosmogenesis. HRIH cannot bridge those gaps by naming hyperspace or by invoking future intelligence. Unknown physics can keep a possibility open; it cannot make it probable.

12.2 Priors and measures dominate

Model priors, parameter priors and observer measures can dominate cosmological posteriors; for anthropic predictions, Starkman and Trotta argue that the answer depends on how probabilities are assigned to possible universes (Starkman and Trotta 2006). The paper’s remedy is sensitivity and registration, not a claim that one choice is uniquely correct.

12.3 One observed universe

We have one observed universe and strong selection effects. Many proposed discriminators may remain underpowered or underdetermined. Maker-selection must therefore focus on structured, held-out predictions rather than visual closeness to familiar constants.

12.4 Technological forecasting

Cosmogenic capability depends on existential risk, coordination, energy, time and a feasible physical channel. Near-term AI progress constrains none of these by itself.

12.5 Normative uncertainty

pWp_W may be small. Advanced agents may reject real cosmogenesis for ethical or practical reasons. HRIH cannot assume that creation is inevitable merely because it is possible.

12.6 The closed loop may remain metaphysical

If R3 produces no observational distinction from an open lineage or a natural boundary model, it cannot become an empirically preferred cosmological theory even if it remains logically coherent. The paper permits that outcome.

13 Conclusion

HRIH is not established cosmology. It does not presently show that intelligence created our universe, that the Big Bang was engineered, or that a beginningless intelligent loop is physically real. Its central intuition nevertheless survives in a scientifically stronger form.

A physically real daughter-universe channel is a legitimate question at the frontier of gravitation and quantum cosmology. If intelligence can access such a channel, cosmogenic histories can be represented as branching processes. If creator-enabling properties affect the distribution of daughter constants, maker-selection can in principle be compared with life-only and natural reproductive selection. If a specified global theory admits a stable fixed point or cycle, the Big Bang can become an internal boundary of a self-consistent history rather than an unexplained first event in a linear chain.

The work ahead is explicit: choose one physical channel; freeze a fair model space; specify observer measures; compute branching survival; define maker-selection factors independently; and permit every central claim to lose. The most defensible HRIH is therefore not a declaration that the origin problem has been solved. It is a Bayesian and physical research programme whose strongest submodel supplies a candidate solution and whose lower rungs can be tested separately.
