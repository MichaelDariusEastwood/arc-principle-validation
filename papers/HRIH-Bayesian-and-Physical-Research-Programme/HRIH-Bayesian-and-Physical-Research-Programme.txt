                                                        Abstract
           How could a physical hypothesis of intelligent cosmogenesis become distinguishable from its
       rivals? This paper separates the Hyperspace Recursive Intelligence Hypothesis into real daughter-
       universe creation, multi-generation lineages and a stronger closed-cycle submodel. It combines
       channel-specific physics, Bayesian model comparison, observer weighting and branching processes.
       The observer-count limit is a conditional mixture identity, not a universal probability ceiling; a
       Poisson lineage has positive survival probability only under its stated stationarity and independence
       assumptions above unit mean offspring. The proposed discriminator is maker-selection beyond
       habitability, tested against life-only, natural-reproductive and technology-only alternatives with
       independently frozen factors. Initial order remains a candidate observation, but set containment does
       not determine its likelihood ratio. Five study specifications and seven failure conditions define what
       could reject each claim. No cosmological posterior, demonstrated creation channel or empirical
       validation is reported. The programme’s AI-alignment work and laws under test are separate: they
       neither require this extension nor establish it. Physical feasibility, observer measures and external
       specialist review remain open.


1     Introduction
The programme begins with a practical question: if control of advanced AI is lost against our will,
what could preserve useful and aligned behaviour? Human control should be retained for as long as
possible. This paper addresses a separate, conditional extension: whether an intelligence could instantiate
a physically real daughter cosmology, and what evidence could distinguish that account from its rivals. A
cosmologist assessing a candidate transition channel, or a statistician comparing selection models, can
use the definitions and failure conditions below without accepting the hypothesis. No ARC law or result
establishes the extension.
    The paper connects cosmology and quantum gravity, Bayesian model comparison, observer selection,
branching processes and thermodynamics. The long-form HRIH edition preserves the broader hypothesis
and its dated record; this companion isolates the physical and statistical questions. The introduction
states the three nested propositions; Sections 6 and 8 specify the channel and selection requirements, and
Section B gives the proposed studies. Their outcomes have not been obtained here.

1.2   The origin question that remains after early-universe cosmology
Modern cosmology describes the evolution of an early hot, dense universe with extraordinary success.
Inflationary and quantum-cosmological proposals address important features of the early state and, in
some models, remove an ordinary temporal boundary. They do not all answer the same question. A
model may describe how a universe can emerge without specifying why this set of laws, constants and
boundary data is realised. HRIH targets that selection question.
    The scientifically meaningful form of the question is not simply “why is there something rather than
nothing?” No current physical theory eliminates all primitives. The tractable question is:

      Why do the laws and initial conditions of our universe permit a history containing observers,
      technological intelligence and, perhaps, access to cosmogenic processes; and is the joint
      permission of those outcomes better predicted by deliberate maker-selection than by non-
      deliberate cosmology or observer selection alone?

1.3   The nested hypothesis
HRIH is not one indivisible proposition. It is a hierarchy (Figure 1):
 R1. Real intelligent cosmogenesis. At least one physically admissible process allows an advanced
     agent in a parent history to instantiate a physically real daughter cosmology.
 R2. Recursive lineage. Real intelligent cosmogenesis occurs in more than one generation and can be
     represented as a branching lineage.
 R3. Closed-cycle HRIH. The complete cosmogenic history satisfies a global fixed-point or periodic-
     boundary condition, so the Big Bang is an internal temporal boundary of a self-consistent whole
     rather than an unexplained first event in an external linear chain.
   Evidence for R1 does not entail R2; evidence for R2 does not entail R3. A confirmed baby-universe
channel would therefore be scientifically important without proving the closed-loop ontology.

1.4   What “hyperspace” means in this paper
HRIH is retained as the historical name of the hypothesis. In scientific propositions, hyperspace does not
assert extra dimensions, a particular metric, a superluminal channel or a known quantum-gravitational
mechanism. It denotes an as-yet-unspecified cosmogenic channel. A physical submodel becomes
scoreable only when it supplies a state space, dynamics or action, boundary conditions, parameter domain
and an observational map. Until then, the neutral term unknown cosmogenic channel is used.

1.5   The strongest claim and its honest limit
The strongest HRIH claim is that a globally self-consistent cosmogenic history could explain why the Big
Bang need not be an uncaused first event in a linear sequence. This is a genuine explanatory structure. It
does not answer why the space of possible laws, the global consistency rule or the admissible solution
exists at all. The paper therefore claims a candidate closure of a causal regress, not an explanation without
primitives.

2     Related work
2.1   Intelligent and reproductive cosmology
HRIH belongs to a small but real lineage of cosmological proposals involving universe reproduction or
intelligent participation. Smolin (Smolin 1992) proposed cosmological natural selection through black
holes. Crane’s meduso-anthropic principle (Crane 1994), Harrison’s intelligent-life selection proposal
(Harrison 1995), Gardner’s Biocosm (Gardner 2003), and Vidal’s inquiry into whether natural or artificial
selection could hold on a cosmic scale (Vidal 2014) discuss intelligence and universe creation in different
forms. These are antecedents, not embarrassments. HRIH’s defensible novelty must be stated as a precise
remainder after they are credited.

2.2   Quantum cosmology and no-boundary proposals
Hartle-Hawking no-boundary cosmology (Hartle and Hawking 1983), Vilenkin’s tunnelling proposal
(Vilenkin 1982), and past-eternal or null-boundary models such as Aguirre-Gratton (Aguirre and Gratton
2003) show that the absence of an ordinary initial temporal boundary is not unique to HRIH, while the
positive averaged-expansion condition in the theorem of Borde, Guth and Vilenkin implies past geodesic
incompleteness (Borde, Guth, and Vilenkin 2003). HRIH’s distinctive ambition is not merely to remove a
boundary mathematically, but to model selected initial conditions through intelligent cosmogenesis and,
in R3, global closure.

2.3   Anthropic reasoning and observer measures
Carter (Carter 1974), Weinberg (Weinberg 1987) and later multiverse work developed observer-selection
arguments; Srednicki and Hartle (Srednicki and Hartle 2010) made explicit that a theory in a large
universe requires a xerographic distribution. HRIH therefore cannot treat “we observe a life-permitting
universe” as direct evidence of deliberate creation. Its maker-selection claim must predict something
beyond observer permission.

2.4   Simulation
Bostrom’s argument (Bostrom 2003), Chalmers’s philosophy of virtual reality (Chalmers 2022), Kipping’s
Bayesian model (Kipping 2020) and physical critiques (Vazza 2025) provide a mature comparison
literature. HRIH should use that literature as a methodological benchmark, not as an opponent to defeat
rhetorically.
     The six-part contribution is stated once in Section 1.1. The comparisons above identify adjacent work,
not a completed priority search.

3.3   Occurrence is not constructibility
The occurrence of our universe establishes that a universe history exists. It does not establish that agents
inside a universe can reproduce the relevant boundary conditions. Natural events frequently depend on
regimes inaccessible to internal engineering. The constructibility hypothesis is therefore channel-specific:

                     𝐻construct ( 𝜒) :   𝑃(successful daughter cosmology | 𝐴, 𝜒) > 0.                   (4)

A single counterexample to one channel does not exclude all channels; conversely, merely naming an
unknown channel is not a rescue. Each scored channel must carry an explicit physical model and a
complexity penalty (Simpson, Rue, Riebler, Martins, and Sø rbye 2017).

3.4   Ability is not preference
Even if real cosmogenesis is feasible, a capable agent may prefer simulation, virtual worlds, observation,
reversible experiments, or no creation at all. Let

               𝑝 𝑊 = 𝑃(agent chooses physically real cosmogenesis | capable, informed).                 (5)

HRIH does not set 𝑝 𝑊 = 1. Preference is a behavioural and normative uncertainty that must be modelled,
bounded or measured. This term sharply distinguishes real intelligent cosmogenesis from a simulation
argument.


4     Bayesian model space and conditional observer counting
4.1   Top-level families
For a specified inference, first name the focal experienced world and the level of explanation. The
following families distinguish its immediate implementation or origin:

                     𝐻N : directly physical history, not deliberately seeded,                           (6)
                     𝐻S : experienced world implemented by external computation,                        (7)
                     𝐻R : directly physical daughter, deliberately instantiated.                        (8)

These are categories for the chosen focal level, not an exhaustive ontology. Simulation and real
cosmogenesis can coexist: a simulation may run inside a deliberately created physical universe. Such
ancestry belongs in an explicit hybrid submodel. If the focal definitions do not make alternatives disjoint,
compare joint submodels indexed by implementation and origin rather than adding overlapping family
probabilities. The sums below require mutually exclusive model assignments, normalised weights and a
declared treatment of omitted possibilities.
    Each family must contain explicit submodels. 𝐻N may include a single-history model, inflationary or
quantum-cosmological boundary proposals, multiverse selection (Bousso and Polchinski 2000; Garriga
and Vilenkin 2008), and natural cosmological reproduction (Smolin 1992). 𝐻S must state the simulated
reference class, computational substrate and fidelity. 𝐻R includes one-generation cosmogenesis, recursive
lineages and the closed-cycle submodel.
    An unspecified “unknown mechanism” has no scoreable likelihood. Flexibility is handled through
declared, proper priors and normalised likelihoods, not by an automatic penalty assigned in prose.

4.3   Comparison with the simulation argument
Bostrom’s simulation argument (Bostrom 2003) is a conditional trilemma about civilisation survival,
willingness to run ancestor simulations and observer counts; it is not a direct measurement of whether we
are simulated. Bayesian treatments such as Kipping’s (Kipping 2020) obtain near-balanced odds only
inside specified partitions and priors. Physical analyses such as Vazza’s (Vazza 2025) impose resource
constraints on simulations of this universe. HRIH is different: it requires a physical cosmogenesis channel
and a maker-selection likelihood. A number from the simulation literature cannot be transferred to HRIH,
and the two hypotheses cannot be ranked responsibly by counting how many independent authors have
assigned them percentages.
    The proper comparison is Eq. (9), with each family carrying its own physical constraints, reference
class and complexity cost. At present, HRIH has no registered empirical posterior. This paper deliberately
supplies a model, not a favourable number.

5.3     Indexical evidence
Observing a life-permitting universe is conditioned by the existence of an observer. Its evidential strength
still depends on the normalised rival likelihoods and the observer measure; selection alone does not
establish that the evidence is weak or neutral. A proposed discriminator should identify what each
rival predicts after applying the same declared observer condition. This motivates maker-selection in
Section 8.


6     Physical feasibility is scored one channel at a time
6.1     Why a physical channel is indispensable
A cosmogenic hypothesis acquires scientific content only through a concrete channel. The fact that
general relativity, semiclassical gravity or speculative quantum gravity contains unusual solutions does
not establish that an internal agent can reach them. A channel is scoreable only if it specifies:
    1. the relevant fields and state variables;
    2. the action, Hamiltonian or transition rule;
    3. boundary and initial conditions;
    4. parameter ranges and approximation regime;
    5. resource requirements accessible to the agent;
    6. the mapping from parent controls to daughter observables;
    7. failure modes and a likelihood function.

6.2     False-vacuum bubble and tunnelling channels
Farhi and Guth (Farhi and Guth 1987) showed a classical obstruction to creating an inflating universe
in the laboratory under their assumptions. Later work by Farhi, Guth and Guven (Farhi, Guth, and
Guven 1990), and by Fischler, Morgan and Polchinski (Fischler, Morgan, and Polchinski 1990a; Fischler,
Morgan, and Polchinski 1990b), examined tunnelling and Hamiltonian treatments of false-vacuum bubbles.
These papers are serious antecedents, not confirmations of HRIH. They expose precisely the problems
the hypothesis must confront: singularity theorems, topology change, pseudomanifold constructions,
boundary conditions, negative-energy or mass issues, and the validity of semiclassical approximations.

6.3   Black-hole and bounce channels
Cosmological natural selection (Smolin 1992) and meduso-anthropic proposals (Crane 1994) consider
black holes as potential reproductive sites. These ideas motivate, but do not establish, parameter
inheritance or agent control. A maker-selection model needs an explicit map

                                        𝜃 𝑔+1 ∼ 𝐾 𝜒 (𝜃 𝑔+1 | 𝜃 𝑔 , 𝑎 𝑔 )                               (13)

showing which parent variables can influence which daughter variables, with what noise. If no information
or control can traverse the relevant boundary, maker-selection is physically inoperative even if a daughter
region exists.

6.4   Chronology and global closure
The closed-cycle submodel is more demanding than a lineage. It may be formulated as a global boundary
condition without local backwards signalling, but it still requires a physical theory admitting the complete
solution. Closed timelike curve models and quantum consistency analyses (Deutsch 1991) provide
mathematical precedents, while Hawking’s chronology-protection analysis (Hawking 1992) supplies a
serious counterpressure through quantum backreaction. HRIH does not claim that chronology violation is
established or required. The closed-cycle model must be separately penalised and separately killable.

6.5   Physical resource accounting
Simulation and cosmogenesis have different resource profiles. Vazza’s astrophysical constraints (Vazza
2025) apply to computing a sufficiently detailed simulation on a physical substrate; real cosmogenesis
may instead attempt to seed a state that evolves under its own dynamics. That distinction can reduce one
cost while introducing much harder gravitational constraints. HRIH must not use the resource cost of
simulation as evidence for real cosmogenesis. It must calculate the cost and feasibility of the chosen real
channel.
    Useful constraints include entropy and information bounds, thermodynamic costs, available free
energy, stability of the parent vacuum, concentration of energy, and causal access to the relevant region.
Bekenstein, holographic and computational bounds (Bekenstein 1981; Susskind 1995; Lloyd 2002) are
boundary conditions, not evidence that a universe can be engineered. Table 2 sets the candidate channels
side by side.

6.6   Duration-dependent costs and autonomous instantiation

temperature. He compares this with roughly 5.6×1059 erg for the rest-mass energy of Palomar 2, with mass
about 3.3 × 105 solar masses, and estimates a similar expenditure per simulation timestep; approximately
106 such timesteps reach the Milky Way comparison (Vazza 2025). These are his model-specific resource
estimates, not a bound on every possible simulation or every possible host physics.

finite observation cannot be simulated. Nor does a constraint on computation establish or exclude an
agent-accessible cosmogenesis channel. This paper therefore transfers neither their conclusion nor Vazza’s
resource calculation into evidence for HRIH.
     The comparison retains two distinct burdens: simulation must specify fidelity, substrate and computa-

cost and any continuing costs. The singularity and semiclassical obstacles above remain. No ordering of
the families’ probabilities follows from comparing an energy estimate with an unresolved channel.

7.3   What the threshold contributes
The threshold converts a vague narrative of recursive creation into a set of measurable or constrainable
terms. A theory can fail because the channel is physically impossible, because capable agents rarely
choose it, because daughters are non-viable, because creator civilisations are unstable, or because the
expected yield is too low. The model is valuable even if the posterior mass ends up concentrated below
one.

7.4   Closed cycles are not required for branching
A recursive lineage may have a finite or unknown origin. The branching programme therefore survives
rejection of the closed-cycle submodel. Conversely, 𝜇 𝑅 > 1 does not supply a closed cycle: a closed
cycle requires a compatible global boundary condition and a path through state space returning to the
required conditions. These are distinct hypotheses and must never be reported as one confirmation.

8.2     Independence requirements
The functions 𝐶, 𝑆 and 𝐾 must be frozen before inspecting the held-out target relationships. They may
use training data to define physically meaningful proxies, but they cannot be reverse-engineered from the
constants they are supposed to explain. A valid study should:
   1. predefine parameter ranges and rival models;
   2. select creator-enabling proxies using physical theory independent of the held-out outcomes;
   3. fit on a training subset of parameter relationships;
   4. predict held-out relationships or sensitivity directions;
   5. compare out-of-sample likelihood against life-only, black-hole-selection and technology-only
      models;
   6. report model and prior sensitivity.

8.3     Candidate creator-enabling factors
Potential factors include long-lived free-energy gradients; stable complex chemistry; compact objects
and high-energy environments; metastable but sufficiently durable vacuum structure; laws permitting
information processing and error correction; and physical access to a cosmogenic channel. These are
candidates, not a confirmed list. Some are already selected by ordinary habitability, and others may be
generic consequences of broad parameter ranges. HRIH earns evidence only from the residual predictive
contribution after those overlaps are modelled.

8.4     Rival selection models
Maker-selection is adjacent to established and speculative predecessors:
      • anthropic and observer selection (Carter 1974; Weinberg 1987);
      • Smolin-style cosmological natural selection through black-hole production (Smolin 1992);
      • Crane, Harrison, Gardner and Vidal-style intelligence-related cosmogenesis (Crane 1994; Harrison
        1995; Gardner 2003; Vidal 2014);
      • a technology-only selection model, defined here as a rival, in which laws favour increasingly capable
        observers.
The paper’s potentially distinctive remainder is the joint inclusion of creator-system stability and a
quantitative comparison of maker-selection against both life-only and non-intelligent reproductive models.
Priority for that precise remainder remains a literature-search question, not a premise of the physics.

9     What this paper does and does not show
9.1    Evidence clusters
The Bayesian programme should divide evidence into clusters whose dependencies can be inspected:
    1. Physical feasibility: channel-specific theoretical and experimental constraints.
    2. Parameter distribution: fine-tuning and held-out maker-selection predictions.
    3. Astrophysical structure: black-hole production, vacuum stability and energy resources.
    4. Technological trajectory: evidence bearing on 𝑝 𝐶 , 𝑝 𝑊 and 𝑝 𝐾 .
    5. Indexical evidence: observer and reference-class information.
    6. Counterevidence: constraints that exclude or suppress candidate channels.
Evidence reused in more than one cluster must be explicitly linked so it does not update the model twice.

      • no observer measure is uniquely justified;

been identified at the stated level. It does not increase the probability of HRIH. Support requires a new
derivation, quantitative prediction or likelihood not already supplied by the established theory.

against it; none is a forecast of a study’s outcome, and this paper attaches no confidence to any, because
no registered model yet exists to supply one.

10.1   Primary maker-selection prediction
P1: held-out maker-selection. A model using independently defined 𝐶 (𝜃), 𝑆(𝜃) and 𝐾 (𝜃) predicts
held-out relations among physical parameters better than life-only anthropic selection, cosmological
natural selection and technology-only rivals. Failure to outperform those models counts against HRIH;
redefinition of the factors after failure is prohibited.

10.2   Physical-channel prediction
P2: at least one scoreable channel. At least one physically specified cosmogenesis channel retains
non-negligible transition probability over an agent-accessible parameter region after singularity, topology,
backreaction, resource and validity constraints are imposed. If every well-specified channel is excluded or
negligibly suppressed across defensible ranges, real intelligent cosmogenesis is strongly disfavoured.

10.3   Branching prediction
P3: supercritical region. For the stationary independent Poisson lineage in Proposition 2, indefinite
survival requires a non-negligible parameter region with 𝜇 𝑅 > 1. If the posterior mass for 𝜇 𝑅 > 1
becomes negligible under all defensible priors and dependencies, indefinite survival in those specified
submodels is disfavoured even if isolated cosmogenesis remains possible.

10.4   Closed-cycle prediction
P4: admissible closure. The closed-cycle submodel requires at least one stable, non-zero-measure
solution of Eq. (2) or (3) in a specified physical theory and at least one observational consequence not
shared by the corresponding open lineage. Failure removes the closed-cycle explanation without removing
R1 or R2.

10.5   Preference prediction
P5: real-creation choice cannot be assumed. HRIH’s real-cosmogenesis posterior must integrate over
𝑝 𝑊 . Evidence that capable agents overwhelmingly prefer simulation, abstention or non-cosmogenic goals
lowers the real-lineage probability. The paper makes no claim that ability entails preference.


11     What would count against HRIH, and what would not
11.1   Scientific kills and model-specific kills
A scientific theory must be allowed to lose probability. The following outcomes have different scopes and
must not be conflated:
 K1. Channel exclusion. Mature physical analysis excludes every specified agent-accessible real-
     cosmogenesis channel, or drives its transition rate to negligible values across the declared parameter
     domain. This kills the scored real-cosmogenesis submodels, not every logically imaginable
     mechanism.
 K2. Maker-selection failure. Independently frozen maker-selection factors fail to improve held-
     out prediction beyond life-only, natural reproductive and technology-selection rivals, with an
     equivalence region ruling out a scientifically meaningful advantage. This kills the distinctive
     maker-selection claim.


                                                    18
                             HRIH: Bayesian and physical research programme


 K3. Subcritical lineage. Within the stationary independent Poisson model, the posterior probability
     of 𝜇 𝑅 > 1 becomes negligible under every defensible joint parameter model. This kills the
     corresponding indefinite-survival claim while leaving isolated creation, and lineages of finitely
     many generations, possible.
 K4. Closure exclusion. The closed-cycle model has no admissible, stable, non-zero-measure solution,
     or has no observational distinction from an open lineage after complexity penalties. This kills R3.
 K5. Observer-measure fragility. HRIH is favoured only under a narrow, outcome-selected xerographic
     distribution. This counts against a robust cosmological inference.
 K6. Prior-volume fragility. Apparent support disappears under modest and defensible prior or
     model-space changes. This counts against a claimed posterior rather than being hidden in a
     supplement.
 K7. Preference suppression. Empirical or theoretical evidence drives 𝑝 𝑊 close to zero. This can make
     real intelligent cosmogenesis rare even if the physical channel exists.

11.2    What is not a scientific kill
An earlier document containing the same idea would defeat a priority claim but would not falsify HRIH.
Failure of an ARC experiment would alter only the bridge or stability factors it actually informs. Rejection
of the cosmological closed loop would not refute a one-generation physical cosmogenesis channel. These
distinctions are essential to prevent both overclaiming and artificial survival by moving between claim
types.

11.3    No branch may turn every outcome into success
A fired registered kill condition kills the claim as stated. It may motivate a new hypothesis, but the new
hypothesis must receive a new identifier, date and registration. A null result is not support; a confidence
interval containing the null is not equivalence; and a statistically detectable but scientifically negligible
effect does not establish the proposed mechanism.


12     Limitations
12.1    Physics is incomplete
Quantum gravity is unresolved. Semiclassical calculations may fail in the regimes most relevant to
cosmogenesis. HRIH cannot bridge those gaps by naming hyperspace or by invoking future intelligence.
Unknown physics can keep a possibility open; it cannot make it probable.

12.2    Priors and measures dominate
Model priors, parameter priors and observer measures can dominate cosmological posteriors; for anthropic
predictions, Starkman and Trotta argue that the answer depends on how probabilities are assigned to
possible universes (Starkman and Trotta 2006). The paper’s remedy is sensitivity and registration, not a
claim that one choice is uniquely correct.

12.3    One observed universe
We have one observed universe and strong selection effects. Many proposed discriminators may
remain underpowered or underdetermined. Maker-selection must therefore focus on structured, held-out


                                                     19
                              HRIH: Bayesian and physical research programme


predictions rather than visual closeness to familiar constants.

12.4    Technological forecasting
Cosmogenic capability depends on existential risk, coordination, energy, time and a feasible physical
channel. Near-term AI progress constrains none of these by itself.

12.5    Normative uncertainty
𝑝 𝑊 may be small. Advanced agents may reject real cosmogenesis for ethical or practical reasons. HRIH
cannot assume that creation is inevitable merely because it is possible.

12.6    The closed loop may remain metaphysical
If R3 produces no observational distinction from an open lineage or a natural boundary model, it cannot
become an empirically preferred cosmological theory even if it remains logically coherent. The paper
permits that outcome.


13     Conclusion
HRIH is not established cosmology. It does not presently show that intelligence created our universe,
that the Big Bang was engineered, or that a beginningless intelligent loop is physically real. Its central
intuition nevertheless survives in a scientifically stronger form.
    A physically real daughter-universe channel is a legitimate question at the frontier of gravitation and
quantum cosmology. If intelligence can access such a channel, cosmogenic histories can be represented
as branching processes. If creator-enabling properties affect the distribution of daughter constants,
maker-selection can in principle be compared with life-only and natural reproductive selection. If a
specified global theory admits a stable fixed point or cycle, the Big Bang can become an internal boundary
of a self-consistent history rather than an unexplained first event in a linear chain.
    The work ahead is explicit: choose one physical channel; freeze a fair model space; specify observer
measures; compute branching survival; define maker-selection factors independently; and permit every
central claim to lose. The most defensible HRIH is therefore not a declaration that the origin problem
has been solved. It is a Bayesian and physical research programme whose strongest submodel supplies a
candidate solution and whose lower rungs can be tested separately.

A.2    Poisson extinction
Let 𝐹 (𝑧) = exp[𝜇 𝑅 (𝑧 − 1)] be the Poisson probability-generating function. Independence and stationarity
give extinction by generation 𝑛 as 𝐹 ◦𝑛 (0), an increasing bounded sequence. Its limit is a fixed
point and cannot exceed any non-negative fixed point, hence is the smallest one. For 0 < 𝜇 𝑅 ≤ 1,
𝐹 ′ (𝑧) = 𝜇 𝑅 𝐹 (𝑧) ≤ 1 on [0, 1] and 𝐹 (𝑧) − 𝑧 > 0 for 𝑧 < 1, including the critical case by strict
convexity. At 𝜇 𝑅 = 0, 𝐹 = 1. Thus 𝑞 = 1 for 𝜇 𝑅 ≤ 1. For 𝜇 𝑅 > 1, 𝐹 (0) > 0, 𝐹 (1) − 1 = 0 and
(𝐹 (𝑧) − 𝑧) ′ | 𝑧=1 = 𝜇 𝑅 − 1 > 0, so 𝐹 (𝑧) − 𝑧 < 0 immediately to the left of one. Strict convexity gives
exactly one additional root in (0, 1); that smaller root is 𝑞, and 1 − 𝑞 > 0. This proof relies on the
single-type stationary independent-offspring model. It proves no survival criterion for a correlated or
time-varying cosmogenic lineage.

B.2   Study HRIH-B: one physical cosmogenesis channel
Question. Does one named physical channel admit non-negligible transition probability in a regime

Question. Does maker-selection predict held-out parameter relations beyond habitability and rival
selection models?

    Primary failure. HRIH fails to exceed the smallest meaningful predictive advantage, with the interval

G     Programme placement and the five laws under test
G.1    Separate scientific objects
The ARC Theory statement paper, version 7.6, treats recursive capability, correction and value persistence;
the Recursive Dynamics founding paper sets out the field. HRIH concerns cosmological origin and
reproduction. Neither requires the other’s truth. If HRIH fails, the ARC laws retain their own registered
tests. If an ARC law fails, HRIH loses only a proposed stability model that actually uses it. Section 9
explains why broad ARC data currently make no cosmological update.
    ARC-derived work could inform 𝑆(𝜃) or 𝑝 𝐾 , the proposed probability that technological intelligence
remains stable enough to complete cosmogenesis. Such a bridge would require a model; it would not
establish that our universe was selected. The Eden Protocol and the HARI Treaty, the Draft Treaty
on Hardware-Aligned Recursive Intelligence, provide engineering and governance strands of the wider
programme; neither is a cosmogenic likelihood in this paper. Humans retain control as long as possible.
Embedded correction is a contingency for involuntary loss of control, not a plan to surrender it.
