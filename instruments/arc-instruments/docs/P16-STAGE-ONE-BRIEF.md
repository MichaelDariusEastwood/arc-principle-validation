# P16: prospective correction-burden boundary prediction

Stage-one drafting brief for review, 5 September 2026. This is a complete paper plan with an unfilled result shell. It is not a report of an experiment and does not replace the governing P16 charter.

**Question.** Can independently measured correction service and offered burden predict a change in correctability on untouched recursive trajectories, including where the change lies and when it becomes detectable?

**Contribution to test.** A sealed quantitative prediction should outperform named alternative explanations under a frozen comparison. A balance identity alone is not a new empirical law. The contribution would be additional predictive success and a reproducible intervention, with its domain and uncertainty stated.

## Design and estimands

Use a checkable artefact domain first if a valid assay can be demonstrated. Keep a fixed system/artefact identity, recursive-depth clock, capability scale, independently measured correction service and independently measured offered burden. The units and exposure of service and burden must match. Record backlog, severe failures, absolute adequacy and recovery separately; a favourable trend alone is not safety.

Delta is the elasticity of Q/W with depth. If the instrument observes log(Q/W), estimate its slope against log depth. If it observes Delta itself, do not regress it against raw rounds and call that a second estimate of Delta. The proposed software component checker exposes this distinction; it does not build the underlying assay.

Before outcomes, fix the eligible system universe, calibration/holdout split, interventions, sham and baseline assignments, realised-alpha estimator, full interval procedure, horizon, censoring rule, aggregation denominator and approved strict margins. Register the complete line and timing prediction. Record whether repeated looks estimate change time or declaration time; those are different endpoints.

The primary rival list should include coefficient-only improvement, workload/inventory artefacts, fixed capacity, generic deterioration, delayed correction, and a more general burden model where the governing charter permits it. A changed zero crossing with the wrong slope is not agreement with the sealed relation. A non-alarm is not evidence of positive balance or a resolved refutation.

## Prerequisites with deliverables

| Requirement | Deliverable that must actually be produced |
|---|---|
| Assay identity | Frozen units, fault/task population and independently audited service/burden measurements |
| Identification | An argument and off-path checks separating workload manipulation, capability and correction allocation |
| Prospective custody | Exact source/specification/assay digest, independently verified commitment and untouched outcome access record |
| Statistical validity | Complete-procedure calibration under adverse nuisance worlds, including repeated looks, clustering and all aggregate gates |
| Execution | Isolated untrusted-code runner; provider/version receipts; verified budget reservations; append-only collection journal and approved interruption handling |
| Specificity | Untouched rival comparisons and predeclared response to a nonlocalised boundary |
| Replication | Fresh-data protocol plus independent reimplementation interface and an outcome reporting commitment |

No row can be completed by setting a Boolean in a JSON document. A report must name and inspect the evidence behind it.

## Reporting paths

- **Quantitative agreement:** report all interval and specificity gates, then the tested domain. This does not establish universal alignment or an unconditional bound of two.
- **Adverse result:** identify exactly which sealed prediction failed, retain every assigned unit, and report consequences through the existing dependency map. Do not rename the rival's success as confirmation of the original relation.
- **Unresolved result:** state the failed measurement/precision/horizon condition and the bound on what was actually tested. Do not count excluded systems as supportive survivors.

The strongest paper would report a successful sealed prediction, a successful intervention and independent replication together. This brief makes those outcomes testable; it does not promise them.
